## Findings

### Finding 1: sh Is pos1-ENRICHED, LL-DEPLETED Cross-Dialectally — Stronger Than ch at Both Positions

A sh: pos1=1.64×, LL=0.52×, LF=0.81×. B sh: pos1=1.84×, LL=0.45×, LF=0.61×. sh's pos1-enrichment exceeds ch (ch A=1.43×, B=1.23×): sh is even more concentrated at the second-position slot. sh's LL depletion is stronger than ch (ch A=0.81×, B=0.91× vs sh A=0.52×, B=0.45×): sh actively avoids LL while ch is only mildly depleted. Cross-dialect: B sh pos1=1.84× is the strongest pos1-enrichment of any characterized onset class. Architecture: both ch and sh are "second-position response classes" that follow LF-formula words (gallows, d, s, ol, qot at LF, with ch/sh appearing at pos1). sh occupies pos1 slightly more exclusively than ch, while ch has more MID presence (MID_inner: ch A=1.11×/B=1.14× vs sh A=1.06×/B=1.05× — very similar).

### Finding 2: B sh at pre_LL → qok at LL E=2.73× — New B LL Routing Rule; sh Completes the B LL Grammar

B sh at pre_LL = 174 cases. LL onset after B sh at pre_LL: qok=2.73×(36/174), s=1.86×(6/174), d=0.56×(depleted), ch=0.73×(depleted), r=0.48×(depleted). Top pairs: shedy→qoky(3), shedy→qokaiin(2), shey→qokam(2). B sh at pre_LL → qok at LL with E=2.73× — this is a major new B LL routing rule. Updated B LL grammar routing by pre_LL onset: ch→d(2.01×, F383); s/qok/ok/ot→ol(F376/F379/F380); qot/qok→r(F377/F380); **sh→qok(2.73×, NEW)** and sh→s(1.86×); r→d/ol(F382). B LL grammar is now more completely characterized: different pre_LL onset classes route to specific LL terminal classes.

### Finding 3: Cross-Dialect sh Pre_LL → s at LL (A=3.58×, B=3.12×) — First Cross-Dialect sh Pre_LL Rule

A sh→s at pre_LL→LL: E=3.58× (N=7/94). B sh→s at pre_LL→LL: E=3.12× (N=6/174). CROSS-DIALECT: both A and B show sh at pre_LL → s at LL with E=3.1-3.6×. Architecture: when sh appears at MID positions, it's the 'she-' body sh subclass that drives qok succession; at pre_LL, the routing shifts to qok/s LL terminals.
