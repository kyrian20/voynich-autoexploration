## Interpretation

**H_new_W ANSWERED:**

1. **ch+edy within-line is FLAT (B)**: B chedy→qot within-line E=1.05×. The B ch+edy→qot CL coupling is NOT an extension of a within-line rule. It is a distinct cross-line grammatical coupling with no within-line precedent.

2. **B ch+edy → qot CL CONFIRMED: E=2.36×(N=7), F442 candidate.** B ch at LL predicts qot at next line start. This is a B-specific cross-line rule: qot onset class follows ch-terminal lines when those ch lines end with edy-body words.

3. **A/B ch cross-line INVERSION:**
   - B: ch+edy at LL → qot (E=2.36×/N=7), qot=0.00×↓ in A
   - A: ch+edy at LL → ch (E=2.96×/N=13), ch=1.07× in B
   - Same body class (ch+edy at LL), completely different cross-line routing by dialect

4. **F441 A ch+edy→ch CL CONFIRMED and STRENGTHENED: E=2.96×(N=13)** (from 2.79×).

5. **H_new_U supported — aiin-body is the d-bridge across dialects**: B ch+aiin→d CL (E=1.88×/N=6); A ch+aiin→sh CL (E=3.73×/N=4, dir). In A dialect, ch+aiin drives sh instead of d — consistent with A's broader sh usage and d-role difference (A d is LL-enriched, ch+aiin→sh makes sense as connecting to the A sh-framing structure).

6. **Within-line A ch+aiin → y (E=2.31×/N=6, dir)**: A ch+aiin words precede y within-line (though N is directional). This is distinct from the cross-line A ch+aiin→sh rule.

**Mechanism interpretation:** The ch+edy cross-line routing (qot in B, ch in A) reflects the dialect's grammar mode for "what follows a ch-terminal edy line." In B, ch-terminal-edy lines precede qot-opening lines — suggesting a paragraph-level ch/qot alternation in B. In A, ch-terminal-edy lines precede ch-opening lines (self-succession). These are different paragraph grammar strategies across dialects.
