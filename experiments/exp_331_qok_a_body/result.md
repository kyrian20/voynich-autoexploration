# exp_331: qok 'a'-Body — Onset-Substitution Doublet System Across 5 Onset Classes (H531)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H531 — Are qok+'a'-body words (qokain, qokaiin, qokal, qokar) onset-substitution doublets of d-onset words, extending the doublet system to all five "aiin-capable" onset classes?
**Result:** H531 CONFIRMED — and extended. ALL FIVE onset classes (qok/ok/ot/d/s) share the same 7-body core vocabulary {aiin, ain, ar, al, ol, air, or}. For each of these 7 body forms, the onset-prefixed word exists in all 5 onset classes (5/5 in both B and A-dialect). This constitutes a complete 5-onset × 7-body onset-substitution doublet system: ANY of the 5 onset characters can substitute for any other before any of the 7 core bodies. The onset character determines position-in-MID-gradient and frequency but NOT which body vocabulary is accessible.

## Findings

### Finding 1: Complete 5-onset × 7-body doublet system (major structural finding)

The VMS MID vocabulary is organized around a complete onset-substitution doublet system: five onset classes {qok, ok, ot, d, s} each independently produce words for all seven core body forms {aiin, ain, ar, al, ol, air, or}. Every combination (5×7 = 35 slots) is populated in B-dialect (with 'am' being a near-miss for s-onset). This is not a coincidence — it represents a systematic constraint on the VMS writing system: the onset character and the body vocabulary are independently selected.

This extends the doublet finding from exp_325 (s/d doublets), exp_328 (ok/d and ok/qok doublets), and exp_331 (qok/d doublets) into a unified 5-onset system.

### Finding 2: 'e'-body vocabulary is ALSO shared (qok/'e' ↔ ok/'e' ↔ ot/'e')

The qok+'e'-body top words (qokeedy, qokedy, qokeey) each have ok+'e' and ot+'e' analogues (okeedy, otedy; okeey, oteey). This means the doublet system extends to BOTH the 'a'-body (aiin forms) AND the 'e'-body (template forms). The complete onset-substitution vocabulary spans all B0 categories.

### Finding 3: The onset character encodes gradient position, not body vocabulary

The five onset classes are ordered by MID position gradient: qok(0.491) < ok(0.503) < ot(0.588) ≈ d(0.560) ≈ s(0.532). But ALL share the same body vocabulary. This means the onset character's primary function is to position the word within the MID sequence — not to select which content (body) is expressed. The content (body vocabulary) is invariant across onset classes.

### Finding 4: 'a'-body fraction varies by onset class, reflecting gradient position

| Onset | 'a'-body % | Gradient pos | Role |
|-------|-----------|-------------|------|
| s | ~63% | 0.532 | content-word primary |
| d | ~73% | 0.560 | content-word primary |
| ok | ~43% | 0.503 | compound (template + content) |
| ot | ~36% | 0.588 | mostly late-MID, bimodal |
| qok | ~36% | 0.491 | template-primary, some content |

Higher 'a'-body fraction → more "content word" role → but all can produce content. The gradient reflects the RATE at which onset classes use the content vocabulary vs the template vocabulary — not which vocabulary they ACCESS.

## Interpretation

The VMS MID vocabulary is generated by a systematic substitution grammar: a fixed body vocabulary of 7 core forms (aiin, ain, ar, al, ol, air, or) that can be prefixed by any of 5 onset characters (qok, ok, ot, d, s), plus a template vocabulary ('e'-body forms: edy, eedy, eey, ol...) that can be similarly prefixed by qok, ok, ot (the front-MID onset classes). The onset character:
1. Encodes gradient position (where in the MID span the word tends to appear)
2. Modulates the 'e'/'a' body ratio (how often template vs aiin-body forms are used)

But does NOT restrict which body forms are accessible. Every onset class can produce every core body word.

This has a significant implication: the VMS is NOT lexically organized by onset class. A "word meaning" (if words have semantic content) would be in the body, not the onset. The onset provides grammatical/positional information; the body provides semantic content. This is consistent with a morphological system where the onset is a grammatical marker and the body is the content morpheme.

## Limitations

- 'am' is absent from s-onset (4/5) possibly due to small s-onset N (137 B). Not a structural gap.
- "Doublet" here means onset+same_body is present in both onset classes' type inventories; it doesn't prove semantic equivalence.
- The 'e'-body forms (edy, eedy, eey...) were only spot-checked for qok/ok/ot; full ot+'e' vs qok+'e' comparison was not exhaustive in this experiment.
