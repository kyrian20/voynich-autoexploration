## H320: WHAT FOLLOWS ychor@LF AND ycheor@LF

| cth   | 2 | 15.4% | 4.3%     | **3.62×**  |

Second-position words: chor (2), cthy (2), chshoty (1), oky (1), qokchol (1), s (1), ykchey (1), ckhor (1), odol (1), ols (1), sheol (1)

**[ychor][cthy@second] appears 2 times** (both long lines):
- `ychor cthy che'eky cheo otor oteol` (n=6 words; cthy at i=1 = second)
- `ychor cthy chol qokom sy sa,ykchom` (n=6 words; cthy at i=1 = second)

This is notable: cthy is normally LL-enriched (1.63×) and second-depleted (0.93×). But after ychor@LF, cthy appears at second (i=1) in 2/13=15.4% of cases (3.62× enriched). The [ychor@LF][cthy@second] construction places cthy in an atypical position — second word rather than last word. This may represent a different functional use of cthy when preceded by ychor.

**ychor full lines** (all 13):
All 13 lines begin with ychor; most have ch/cth/other words at second. Line lengths range from 3-14 words. Two lines have the [ychor cthy...] formula.

## H322: GRAMMAR OF y-OPENED LINES IN A-DIALECT (n=254 lines)

### Second-position onset after y@LF:

| Onset | n  | rate  | baseline | enrichment |
|-------|----|-------|----------|------------|
| ch    | 43 | 16.9% | 20.8%    | 0.81×      |
| other | 36 | 14.2% | 12.2%    | 1.17×      |
| sh    | 24 | 9.4%  | 12.9%    | 0.73×      |
| d     | 23 | 9.1%  | 8.6%     | 1.05×      |
| y     | 17 | 6.7%  | 4.0%     | **1.69×**  |
| k     | 15 | 5.9%  | 4.5%     | 1.31×      |
| cth   | 11 | 4.3%  | 4.3%     | 1.02×      |

**y-opened lines are near-baseline at second position.** No strong enrichment. y-self-succession (6.7%, 1.69×) is the only notable pattern — y-words can follow y-words at second.

y-opened lines do NOT enriched cth at second (cth=1.02× baseline). In contrast, d-opened lines show cth at second = 7.9% (1.86×). This confirms that the [d@LF][cth@second] construction is d-specific, not a general y/d opener construction.

### LL onset in y-opened lines:

| Onset | n  | rate  | baseline | enrichment |
|-------|----|-------|----------|------------|
| d     | 64 | 25.2% | 24.9%    | 1.01×      |
| other | 42 | 16.5% | 12.7%    | 1.30×      |

**ch is depleted at LL in y-opened lines (0.59×)**. y-opened lines tend to avoid ch-words at line-end. ot-words and y-words are mildly enriched at LL in y-opened lines.

### For comparison: d-opened lines (n=266):

After d@LF, cth at second = 7.9% (1.86×) — significantly enriched vs y@LF (4.3%, 1.02×). This confirms the [d@LF][cth@second] construction is concentrated in d-opener lines, likely driven by daiin (the dominant d-LF word) introducing cth at second in [daiin@LF][cth@second] cases (F164).

## Summary of New Findings (F167)

1. **[ychor@LF][cthy@second] appears 2 times** (3.62×): ychor specifically introduces cthy at second position — an atypical cthy position (normally LL). This may be a specific A-herbal formula phrase. ycheor@LF → chor/chol at second (2.40×).

2. **y-opened lines have near-baseline second-position composition**: No onset class is strongly enriched at second after y@LF (highest: y-self 1.69×). y-opened lines do NOT specifically introduce cth at second (1.02× baseline).

3. **ch is depleted at LL in y-opened lines (0.59×)**: y-opened lines tend to end with d/ot/y-class words, not ch-class. This is a systematic feature of y-line grammar: y-openers associate with ot/y/d-class endings.

4. **d@LF lines → cth@second = 1.86×** (vs y@LF → cth@second = 1.02×): The [d@LF][cth@second] construction is significantly more common in d-opened lines than y-opened lines. This is consistent with [daiin@LF][cth@second] from F164.
