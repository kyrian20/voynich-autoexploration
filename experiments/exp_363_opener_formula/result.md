# exp_363: Folio-Opener and Folio-Closer Word Formulas (H565)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H565 — What specific word pairs appear at folio-opener LF+pos1? Is there a two-word formula?
**Result:** H565 ANSWERED (trivially). No folio-opener LF+pos1 word pair repeats in either dialect. ALL LF+pos1 pairs are unique (N=1) in both A and B dialects. The folio-opener grammar operates PURELY at the onset-class level, not the word-pair level. A opener: LF hapax rate=88-94%; B opener=90-96%. The structure is: gallows-onset LF (A: pchor/kooiin/chor top words; B: tchedy/pchedar dominant) → diverse pos1. A pos1 is sh-dominated (shey=5, shor=4 — sh most common individual pos1 words). B pos1 is diverse (or/ytedy/qokedy/shey/dar each count=2). FOLIO-CLOSER: A ycheor(3) is the only repeated LF word; ch-onset dominates pos1 (chor/chol/cheor top pos1). B closer: ol(3) is the only repeated LF word; B closer LL is completely hapax (all count=1).

## Findings

### Finding 1: No Folio-Opener LF+pos1 Word Pair Repeats (N=1 for ALL Pairs)

In both A (N=121) and B (N=73) dialect folio-opener lines, every single LF+pos1 word pair is unique (count=1). This confirms that the folio-opener grammar is PURELY onset-class-level — the system selects onset classes according to the grammar rule (gallows LF, then diverse pos1) but word choice within the class is hapax-dominated. Architecture implication: no specific word formula needs to be memorized; only the onset-class constraint (gallows at LF; sh-elevated at A pos1; diverse at B pos1) is required.

### Finding 2: A-Dialect Folio-Opener pos1 — sh Tokens Dominate (shey=5, shor=4, sheol=3)

The most common pos1 words in A-dialect folio-opener are all sh-onset: shey(5), shor(4), sheol(3), totaling 12/121=9.9% as just three sh words. This is consistent with F359 (gallows→sh rule elevates sh at pos1 after gallows LF). The word-level pattern is sh-body diversity: shey/shor/sheol/shol/shear are all different sh-body words.

### Finding 3: B-Dialect Folio-Opener LF Is p-Onset Dominant (34%) With edy/al Suffix Compounds

B-dialect folio-opener LF has p-onset as dominant (pchedar(2), pchedal(2), psheoky, pdair, pchey, pchedain, pdsairy, pcheodchy, psheor — all p-onset). Many have edy or al-suffix (B-dialect characteristic). This is consistent with F345-F346 (gallows LF at folio-opener) and F358 (B-dialect only f-gallows at pos1). The B opener is more p-dominant than A opener (which has k/t/f also).

### Finding 4: A-Dialect Folio-Closer ycheor(3) Is the Only Multi-Token Closer LF Word

ycheor appears 3 times at folio-closer LF — the most repeated word in A-dialect folio-closer LF. daiin also appears 3 times. Together they suggest the A-dialect folio-closer has slight formula tendency (y-onset and d-onset LF) but the overall hapax rate remains very high. ycheor is consistently followed by ch-onset pos1 words (ycheor→chol, ycheor→chor, ycheor→ksho — 2 of 3 pairs start ch at pos1).

### Finding 5: A-Dialect Folio-Closer pos1 ch-Dominance Is Word-Level Diverse

ch-onset pos1 words in A-dialect closer: chor(4), chol(3), cheor(3), chear(2), chokal(2), cheol(2). All different ch-body words (chor/chol/cheor/chear/chokal/cheol). The ch-dominance at closer pos1 (F357: E=1.34×) is spread across multiple ch body-types, confirming it's an onset-class constraint, not a specific ch-word formula.

## Summary

H565 ANSWERED. No two-word formula at folio-opener — ALL LF+pos1 pairs are unique (N=1). The folio-opener grammar is onset-class only: gallows LF → sh-elevated A pos1, diverse B pos1. A-dialect: pchor(3) is the most repeated opener LF word (2.5%). B-dialect: tchedy(3) is most repeated (4.1%). Both are hapax-dominated (88-96% LF hapax rate). A folio-closer ycheor(3) is the closest to a formula word (appearing at LF+pos1→ch pattern). Architecture can implement folio-opener/closer constraints as onset-class rules without memorizing any specific word pairs.
