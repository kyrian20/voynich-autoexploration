# exp_322: y+Gallows Compound Structure Confirmation (H523)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H523 — Confirm that y-onset words are y+gallows compounds at the word-type level; identify gallows rank order; check if B0='c' = 'ch' compound.
**Result:** H523 CONFIRMED with full detail. 92-94% of y-onset MID words are y+gallows compounds. Bare gallows (k/t/p/f) = 89.5% (B) and 88.2% (A); compound gallows (ch) = 4.6% (B) and 4.2% (A). Bare 'c' at B0 = 0.0% (B) and 0.3% (A) — the B0='c' from exp_321 is ENTIRELY the ch compound, not bare 'c'. THREE ADDITIONAL FINDINGS: (1) y-onset A/B suffix split mirrors full corpus: B y-words end in -eedy/-edy (B-only: ykeedy, yteedy, ykedy); A y-words end in -ol/-or/-al (A-only: ytor, ykol, ykaly, ykchor). (2) y+p/f forms include triple compounds: ypchedy, yfchdy — y(onset)+p/f(gallows)+ch(body[1]). (3) All-positions analysis shows y+ch is 13.6% when LF/LL included (vs 4.6% MID-only), confirming ch compounds concentrate at LF positions (paragraph-grammar formulas).

## Structural Findings

### Finding 1: y+gallows = confirmed compound class (94% of MID tokens)

The word-type inventory confirms H523: 94.1% (B) and 92.7% (A) of y-onset MID words have a gallows character at body position 0. The remaining ~6-8% ("other") includes ydaiin, ydy, ysain-type words where the body begins with a non-gallows character.

There is no bare 'c' at B0 in B-dialect MID (0.0%); the exp_321 'c'=4.6% was entirely the 'ch' compound (y+ch forms). A-dialect has 1 bare 'c' out of 306 words (0.3%) — effectively zero.

**Gallows rank order is k > t > p > f >> ch (compound):** This matches the known EVA gallows frequency in the broader corpus. y+k and y+t together cover 80% of y-onset MID tokens in both dialects.

### Finding 2: A/B dialect suffix split applies within y-onset

The B-only types (ykeedy, yteedy, ykedy, ypchedy, ycheedy) all end in -eedy/-edy. The A-only types (ytor, ykol, ykaly, ykchor, ykeor, ypchor) all end in -ol/-or/-al. This is the same A/B suffix paradigm that differentiates B-qok (edy/eedy-dominant) from A-qok (ol/or-dominant) (F321), and B-ch/sh (e-body) from A-ch/sh (o-body) (F323).

y-onset is NOT structurally different from other onset classes in this respect — the A/B dialect split permeates every onset class's paradigm.

Shared words (ykaiin, ytaiin, ykar, ytar, ykeey, yteey) are the universal "core" y-onset vocabulary, present in both dialects. The 81% non-overlap is explained by dialect-specific suffix preferences, not onset-class differences.

### Finding 3: y+p/f forms are triple compounds (y+p/f+ch)

y+p forms: ypchedy, ypchdy, ypchey (combining y+p+ch). y+f forms: yfchdy, yfchey, yfchedy (combining y+f+ch). These are triple-compound words: y(onset) + p/f(gallows) + ch(body continuation) + suffix. The pattern y+{p,f}+ch mirrors the paragraph-grammar gallows+ch formulas (F131-F133: bare gallows NEVER appear at PS; only gallows+ch compounds function as para-starters). Within y-onset, p and f gallows prefer ch-extension in their body, consistent with the gallows+ch coupling identified in paragraph grammar.

y+k and y+t do NOT use p+ch or t+ch as frequently — k/t gallows have direct body sequences (kaiin, teedy, kar, tar), while p/f gallows use ch extension.

### Finding 4: y+ch concentrates at LF/LL positions

All-positions data shows y+ch rises from 4.6% (MID) to 13.6% (all positions). y+k and y+t drop from 44% and 36% (MID) to 36% and 30% (all). This means y+ch compounds are preferentially used at LINE-FIRST or LINE-LAST positions, not in the middle of lines.

This is consistent with the paragraph grammar (F131-F133): gallows+ch compounds are para-start markers. y+ch words (ycheedy, ychedy, ychol, ychor) at LF positions are y-prefixed versions of the paragraph-initial gallows+ch formulas.

## Interpretation

**y-onset is a gallows-compound onset class:** The y character functions as a prefix that combines with gallows characters (k/t/p/f/ch) to form compound words. The basic unit is y+gallows, with the body then following the standard body sequences for those gallows classes. This reframes y-onset from "a vowel/connecting character" to "a gallows-compound prefix."

**Why y-onset is a LF grammar-word class:** The LF enrichment of y-onset (y_lf = 3.506×, one of the battery metrics) is explained by y+ch compounds concentrating at LF positions (13.6% all-position vs 4.6% MID). y+ch LF forms are the y-prefixed paragraph-start formulas. y+k/t words appear at all positions but are diluted at MID by the high frequency of non-y onset classes.

**The gallows+ch coupling extends to y-onset:** y+p+ch and y+f+ch triple compounds show that the p/f gallows characters prefer ch extension whether at paragraph positions (bare pch/fch as PS markers) or within y-onset (ypch/yfch compounds). This may represent a systematic coupling rule: p and f gallows are high-energy markers that require ch extension.

**Cross-dialect convergence at the structural level:** Both A and B dialects use y+gallows compound structure (92-94% gallows at B0). The dialect difference is in the suffix selection (A: -ol/-or/-al; B: -edy/-eedy). The structural skeleton (y+gallows+body) is cross-dialect invariant; only the body completion differs.

## Limitations

- "other(s)" at 7.7% (all positions) likely represents ys-forms (y+s), which would extend the compound-onset analysis to y+s as well. This is not a gallows character but may be a related grammatical element.
- ydaiin (5 tokens in A-dialect): y+d forms are body[0]='d' which is not gallows. These may be y+daiin compound words (a full word embedded as y's body).
- The analysis treats 'y' as a single-character onset; if y is itself part of a compound glyph in some VMS readings, this analysis may need revision.
- y-onset MID N=306 for both dialects — moderate sample for 128-144 type estimation.
