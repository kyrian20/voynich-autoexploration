## Findings

### Finding 1: B s→sh Is LF-POSITION-SPECIFIC (LF→pos1 E=2.47×, 83% of All Hits)

B-dialect s→sh E=1.98× overall is almost entirely driven by LF→pos1: 62/75 (83%) of all s→sh hits are at LF→pos1 (E=2.47×, N=62/260). MID_inner is near-baseline (E=1.15×). Pre_LL→LL is DEPLETED (E=0.43×). The rule is: **B-dialect s-onset words at LF (line opening) → sh-onset words at pos1 (second word)**. This is a LINE-OPENING formula coupling, not a general succession rule.

### Finding 2: B s→sh Shares the 'she-' Body sh Hub With B d→sh (F373)

The sh successor words in B s→sh are: shedy(14), shey(9), sheedy(5), sheckhy(5), sheey(4), shol(3), sheol(3). The first five are all 'she-' body sh words — the same subclass that receives B d→sh (F373: daiin/dal/dar → shedy/shey) and sends to qok (F372). The 'she-' body sh class is now a **TRIPLE HUB** in B-dialect grammar:
1. **Receives** from d-onset predecessors (F373: pos1→pos2, E=2.58×)
2. **Receives** from s-onset predecessors (F374: LF→pos1, E=2.47×)
3. **Sends** to qok-onset successors (F372: sh→qok, E=1.97×)

### Finding 3: B s→sh Is s-CLASS-GENERAL (All Major s-Words Show Elevated sh-Follow Rates)

Every major B s-onset word shows elevated P(sh|s): sor=33.3%, sal=25.0%, sair=25.0%, sain=22.9%, saiin=20.0%, sol=14.8%, sar=15.2%, soiin=25.0%. All rates are > baseline (9.6%). This is not driven by a specific formula word (e.g., saiin exclusively) — the rule applies s-class-generally. The rule is: B s-CLASS word at LF → sh-onset at pos1.

### Finding 4: B s→ol Is Pre_LL-CONCENTRATED (E=4.46×, N=6) with Multiposition Baseline Elevation

B s→ol (E=1.78× overall) decomposes differently from s→sh: pre_LL→LL E=4.46× (N=6, directional), LF→pos1 E=1.58×, MID_inner E=1.65×. The dominant mechanism is **s at pre_LL → ol (bare word) at LL**. The top ol successor is the bare word "ol" (17/39=44%). This is consistent with B LL={d,ol,s} from F368: ol is a B-dialect LL terminal word, and s-onset at pre_LL forecasts this terminal. Architecture: B s→ol pre_LL→LL is a sub-case of the B ol-LL formula (not a general s-class rule).

### Finding 5: A sh→qot Is POSITION-GENERAL (E=1.58-1.83× All Positions) and sh-CLASS-GENERAL

A-dialect sh→qot E=1.63× (N=29) is elevated at ALL three position categories: LF→pos1 (1.83×), MID_inner (1.58×), pre_LL→LL (1.70×). No position peak. No dominant word pairs (all N=1). No specific sh-word driver (sheor=9.5%, shey=4.9%, shol=3.5% — distributed). This is a **position-general, class-level A-dialect rule**: sh-onset words predict qot-onset successors throughout continuation lines.

### Finding 6: A sh→t Is POSITION-GENERAL with Pre_LL Peak (E=2.56×)

A-dialect sh→t E=2.04× (N=30) is elevated at all positions (LF=1.77×, MID=2.00×, pre_LL=2.56×). The pre_LL→LL peak (E=2.56×, N=5/94) connects to A-dialect pre_LL→LL intensification pattern (also seen in A d→cth pre_LL=1.76×, F373). A sh predicts t-onset (gallows-t class: tol, tchol, tcheey, tey) throughout the line with peak near line-end.

### Finding 7: A/B Dialect Bigram Rule Positional Architecture Contrast

A systematic contrast emerges across the last 4 experiments (exp_369-372):
- **B-dialect bigram rules**: LF/pos1-specific or LL-specific (B ch→daiin: MID+pre_LL; B d→sh: pos1→pos2; B s→sh: LF→pos1; B s→ol: pre_LL→LL)
- **A-dialect bigram rules**: position-general (A ch→daiin: all 1.13-1.43×; A d→cth: all 1.51-1.76×; A sh→qot: all 1.58-1.83×; A sh→t: all 1.77-2.56×)

This dialect difference suggests: B-dialect grammar has more REGISTER-SPECIFIC formula couplings (different rules for LF/MID/LL); A-dialect grammar has REGISTER-NEUTRAL coupling rules that apply throughout the line.
