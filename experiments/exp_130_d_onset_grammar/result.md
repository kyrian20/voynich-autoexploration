### d-Chain Analysis: No Strong Prediction

When a line ends with a d-word (692 cases), the next line's first-word onset distribution:
- ch: 1.39× enriched (weak)
- qot: 1.67× enriched (weak)
- p: 0.61× (depleted — p-word lines do NOT follow d-word closers)

No onset class exceeds 1.70× enrichment. The d-chain (d-closer on line N → d+ch opener on line N+1) is NOT a strong pattern — d-word line-endings do NOT systematically predict the next line's opener. The chain was perhaps an artifact or too weak to survive this analysis.

### H203 Verdict: CONFIRMED — d-onset has THREE clear sub-classes: (1) d+ch/sh compound OPENERS (60-100% line-first; dialect-split: dshedy/dchedy=100%B, dchor/dchol=A-dominant); (2) d-CLOSING particles (dan=100%LL, dam=67.9%LL, dy=40.3%LL; short words with -am/-an/-aly/-y suffixes); (3) balanced medial FUNCTION words (daiin=61.5%MID, dol=80%MID; the determiners and reference words); d+gallows (dk/dt) is vanishingly rare (23 tokens); d-chain is WEAK (no onset class >1.67× enriched on next line after d-closer); d-grammar is a POSITIONAL system (not a chain system)

## H202: CONFIRMED PARTIAL — ok/ot/ol Body Words Are Predominantly B-Dialect MEDIAL CONTENT Words (81-82% MID); Low Vocabulary Overlap with Labels (ok Jaccard=0.197, ot=0.139, ol=0.062); Stars/Cosmo Concentrated (ok=38%, ot=45%, ol=40%); Some Sub-Forms Are Line-Closers (oly=81.2%LL, otam=62.8%LL); Body ok/ot ≠ Label ok/ot Vocabulary

**LOW Jaccard for all three** — body ok/ot/ol words are largely DISTINCT from label ok/ot/ol words. The body text creates its own ok/ot vocabulary, not simply recycling label words.
