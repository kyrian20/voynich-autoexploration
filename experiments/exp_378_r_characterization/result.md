## Findings

### Finding 1: r Is LL-Enriched in Both Dialects (A: LL=2.95×; B: LL=2.48×) — Third Confirmed LL-Terminal Class

A r: LL=2.95× (10/21 r tokens at LL, N=10/1387 LL positions), LF=0.27× (1/21 at LF). B r: LL=2.48× (79/256 tokens at LL, 3.6% of all LL positions), LF=0.30×. Both dialects: r strongly concentrates at LL and is almost absent from LF. Cross-dialect LL enrichment: d-class (A=2.08×, B r-equivalent not applicable — d is LF in B), ol (A=1.43×, B=1.59×), r (A=2.95×, B=2.48×). r has the strongest LL enrichment of any confirmed LL-terminal class. Architecture: A-dialect r is so rare (0.25% of words) that it functions as an exceptional/rare LL token rather than a systematic LL terminal. B-dialect r (1.4% of words, 3.6% of LL positions) is a genuine LL-terminal class.

### Finding 2: A-Dialect r Is Effectively Absent From Grammar (21 Tokens, 88% Hapax) — r Is a B-Dialect-Specific Grammatical Class

A r: 21 tokens, 17 types, 15 hapax (88%), raiin(4)/ry(2) the only multi-occurrence forms. This is the lowest-N characterization in the template class series (B qot N=714 by comparison). 88% hapax means every A r occurrence is essentially a unique lexical item — there is no systematic A r vocabulary. A r self-succession E=0.00× (0 hits from N=11 outgoing bigrams). A r outgoing bigrams are dominated by zeroes (qok=0, ok=0, qot=0, ol=0, r=0). Architecture: r-onset words in A-dialect are sporadic, individually distinct, and grammatically isolated (no sequential coupling). Contrast: B r has 256 tokens, structured vocabulary (raiin dominant), and multiple coupling rules. r is essentially a B-dialect grammatical class.

### Finding 3: B r Self-Succession Is MID-Concentrated (E=1.96×), LF-Flat (0.00×) — r Chains in Middle-Line But Not at Openings

B r→r: LF→pos1=0.00× (flat), MID_inner=1.96× (N=4/129), pre_LL→LL=1.71× (N=1/37). r chains at MID positions in B (when r appears mid-line, it tends to produce another r-class word), but never at LF. Compare with ol (B ol→ol LF-flat=1.02×, pre_LL=2.55×) and ot (B ot→ot = closed cluster). r occupies a distinct succession profile: MID-chaining, LL-isolated. Top B r→r pairs: rchey→ral(1), rol→rol(1) — all class-level (different words).

### Finding 4: B pre_LL→r Is qot-Dominated and qok-Supplemented; ok/ot/sh/s Are Depleted

B pre_LL onset → r at LL: qot=2.27×(9/78), qok=1.31×(14/78), d=1.30×(6/78), y=1.46×(2/78). Depleted: ok=0.52×(3/78), ot=0.72×(5/78), sh=0.48×(3/78), s=0.00×(0/78). Top pairs at B pre_LL→r: shdalo→ry(1), chekeedy→ror(1), dy→rol(1), shedy→rshedy(1). The B pre_LL→r rule is **qot/qok-specific** (E=1.31-2.27× for {qot,qok}) while ok/ot/sh/s are all suppressed. This refines the earlier generalization ({qot,qok,ol}→r at LL, F377/F380/F381): ol→r pre_LL was directional (N=4/105 from ol's perspective, E=2.41×; from r's perspective: ol=4/78=1.06×=baseline). The ol→r rule holds from ol's perspective but ol is a small pre_LL class (4.9% baseline), so ol accounts for only 5.1% of r-at-LL events.

Revised B pre_LL→r generalization: **PRIMARY drivers = {qot, qok}** (E=1.31-2.27×); SECONDARY = {d, y} (E=1.30-1.46×, directional); DEPLETED = {ok, ot, sh, s} (E=0.00-0.72×). Architecture: B r-onset LL words are the canonical successors of qot/qok at line-end — NOT the successors of ok/ot (which preferentially end with ol/d, F378/F379).

### Finding 5: B r→ch Is MID-Elevated (E=1.65×) — r→ch Is the Primary r Outgoing Coupling in B

B r→ch: overall E=1.35×, MID_inner=1.65× (N=37/129), pre_LL→LL=0.47× (depleted near LL). Top pairs: raiin→chey(3), ral→cheey(2). When B r appears in mid-line positions, its most likely successor is ch-onset — the most frequent B-dialect onset class. The MID r→ch coupling (1.65×) creates a B-dialect pattern: if r appears mid-line (not at LL), it likely transitions to a ch-onset word. B r also mildly elevates sh→sh (E=1.48×, MID=1.53×). Outgoing depleted classes: qok=0.72×, ok=0.53×, ot=0.64×, s=0.00×.

### Finding 6: B r→s Completely Absent (0/175) — r and s Are Mutually Exclusive in B Succession

B r→s: E=0.00× (N=0/175 outgoing r bigrams). When r appears anywhere in a B-dialect continuation line, it is never followed by s-onset. This complements B s→r (not explicitly computed but s→r would be part of the B LL grammar picture). s-onset is a LF-formula class in B (elevated at LF), and r-onset is a LL-terminal class — they occupy opposite ends of lines and appear to avoid each other in sequential succession.

### Finding 7: B r at pre_LL → d and ol at LL (Both E≈2.0×) — r Participates in the B LL Routing System as BOTH Terminal and Pre-Terminal

B r→d at pre_LL→LL: E=2.01× (N=4/37). B r→ol at pre_LL→LL: E=1.93× (N=4/37). When r appears at pre_LL in B-dialect (N=37 cases), it tends to produce d-class (2.01×) or ol-class (1.93×) at LL — the same two LL terminals that appear after ok/ot at pre_LL (F379/F378). This means r is both (a) a preferred LL target when qot/qok is at pre_LL, AND (b) a source class that predicts d/ol at LL when r itself is at pre_LL. r is mid-position in the B LL terminal hierarchy: it is produced by higher-tier template classes (qot/qok) and itself produces lower-tier LL terminals (d/ol).

### Finding 8: A r at LL = ch-Dominated Pre_LL Predecessor (5/10 = 2.18×) — Directional A Pre_LL→r Rule

A r at LL = 10/1206 (0.8%). Pre_LL predecessors: ch=5/10 (50%, E=2.18×), qot=1/10 (E=3.35×), qok=1/10 (E=1.95×). The N=10 is too small for strong claims, but it is notable that A-dialect r-at-LL is predominantly preceded by ch-onset at pre_LL. Compare B: ch at pre_LL → r at LL: E=0.77× (depleted in B). This is a **cross-dialect contrast**: A ch → r (directional, small N); B ch → daiin (F371, E=1.68×). Architecture: A-dialect ch at pre_LL leads to r at LL (not daiin), while B-dialect ch at pre_LL leads to daiin at LL. The A-dialect LL grammar (ch-onset dominant at pre_LL per F366: ch=22.9% of pre_LL positions) routes ch → r in A vs ch → daiin in B.
