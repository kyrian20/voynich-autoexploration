# exp_337: Is 'eey' a Terminal Template Form — LF/LL Position Distribution (H540)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H540 — 'eey' is a terminal template form, appearing at LF or LL positions more than 'edy'/'eedy', explaining its 7/7 onset coverage in A-dialect.
**Result:** H540 REFUTED. 'eey' is NOT a terminal form — it is the MOST MID-concentrated of all 'e'-body template suffixes (B: LL_enr=0.427×; A: LL_enr=0.346×). ALL 'e'-body templates are LL-DEPLETED (LL_enr < 0.67× in both dialects). The true LL-terminal form is 'am' (B: LL_enr=6.49×; A: LL_enr=3.52×). 'eol'/'eor' have the highest LF enrichment (B: 1.48×/1.61×). BONUS: d+eey words (deey) are 100% LF in B-dialect (N=17, LF_enr=7.80×) — deey is a dedicated LF formula word. A-dialect 'aiin' suffix is LL-enriched (1.225×) vs B-dialect 'aiin' LL-depleted (0.634×) — dialect divergence in aiin positional grammar.

## Findings

### Finding 1: ALL 'e'-body templates are LL-DEPLETED — they are MID or LF forms, not LL forms

Every 'e'-body suffix (eey, edy, eedy, ey, eol, eor) has LL enrichment < 0.67× in both dialects. 'eey' is the MOST LL-depleted (B: 0.427×; A: 0.346×). H540 is comprehensively refuted: there is no 'e'-body suffix that serves as a terminal form. The MID position is the canonical home for all 'e'-body template words.

### Finding 2: 'am' is the exclusive LL-terminal suffix (B: 6.49×; A: 3.52×)

The -am suffix is overwhelmingly concentrated at LL positions (B: 83.2% LL; A: 61.2% LL). This confirms the foundational finding (am_ll enrichment = 6.811×) that distinguishes VMS from position-blind mechanisms. The 'am' suffix is the sole high-LL template form in the inventory.

### Finding 3: d+eey (deey) is a dedicated LF formula word in B-dialect

In B-dialect, all 17 deey occurrences appear at LF (E=7.80×). This is the strongest positional concentration in the entire dataset. A-dialect deey also strongly LF-enriched (47%, E=2.68×). dedy similarly LF-dominant (B: 53% LF, E=4.15×). d-onset + e-body words appear specifically at LINE START in B-dialect. This is a new finding: d-onset, which is primarily a content-body onset in MID positions (daiin/dain/dar dominate in MID), also has a LF formula function when combined with e-body (deey/dedy = d-onset LF template words).

### Finding 4: 'eol'/'eor' are the most LF-enriched 'e'-body suffixes

'eol' (B: LF_enr=1.478×) and 'eor' (B: LF_enr=1.611×) show the highest LF enrichment among e-body templates. These forms appear at the beginning of lines more than average. A-dialect eor also LF-enriched (1.230×). 'aiin' is also LF-enriched in both dialects (B: 1.434×; A: 1.186×).

### Finding 5: A-dialect 'aiin' suffix is LL-enriched (1.225×) vs B-dialect LL-depleted (0.634×)

A-dialect words ending in 'aiin' appear at LL positions more than baseline (21.3% LL vs baseline 17.4%). B-dialect aiin appears at LL near-baseline or depleted (8.1% vs 12.8% baseline = 0.634×). This is a dialect-level positional divergence for the aiin body form. A-dialect daiin can appear at line terminal positions; B-dialect daiin primarily appears in LF and MID. This is consistent with A-dialect having weaker positional constraints on d-onset words (A-dialect d-onset is more distributed than B-dialect).

### Finding 6: The 7/7 onset coverage of 'eey' in A-dialect reflects broad MID distribution, not terminal specialization

'eey' achieves 7/7 onset coverage because it is a general-purpose MID template word used by all 7 onset classes. Its near-absence from LL (0.346×) confirms it is not a terminal form. The A-dialect shift from edy/eedy (rare in A) to eey as the primary e-body template reflects a suffix vocabulary shift, not a positional change.

## Interpretation

The template vocabulary has a three-way positional structure:
1. **LF forms**: d+eey (deey/dedy: E=4.15-7.80×), 'eol'/'eor' onset (1.5-1.6×), 'aiin' body (1.43×) — these appear at line start
2. **MID forms**: all 'e'-body templates (eey/edy/eedy/ey) — depleted at both LF and LL, concentrated in MID span
3. **LL forms**: 'am' only (E=6.49×) — the terminal marker

'eey' is a MID-only template form. Its universality (7/7 onset coverage in A-dialect) reflects the need for every onset class to have a MID template word — not a terminal function.

## Limitations

- The suffix analysis counts all words ending in the target suffix regardless of onset class, then the per-onset breakdown excludes words where len(w) ≤ len(onset)+3. This means short forms like seey (4 chars) may be excluded from some per-onset analyses but counted in the aggregate summary. Numbers are consistent within each analysis section.
- d+eey LF dominance in B-dialect (N=17): small sample, but 100% LF concentration is striking and consistent with A-dialect pattern (47% LF).
