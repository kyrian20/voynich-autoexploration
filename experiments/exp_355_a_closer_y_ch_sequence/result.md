# exp_355: A-Dialect Folio-Closer y→ch Onset Sequence (H562)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H562 — Is the 40% ch at pos1 in A-dialect folio-closer lines specifically driven by y-onset LF words followed by ch, or is it a general folio-closer rule?
**Result:** H562 ANSWERED. The elevated ch at pos1 (37.3%) is a GENERAL FOLIO-CLOSER PROPERTY, not y-onset-specific. All LF onset types show elevated ch at pos1 in folio-closer vs continuation: y-LF→ch(36.8% vs 23.2%), d-LF→ch(43.8% vs 25.4%), s-LF→ch(45.5% vs 34.1%), o-LF→ch(60.0% vs 28.4%), k-LF→ch(66.7% vs 16.7%). NEW FINDING: sh at folio-closer pos1 = 2.4% (E=0.16× vs continuation 15.0%) — sh is strongly depleted at pos1 of the A-dialect folio-closer line. The folio-closer structure is: anti-gallows + anti-sh LF AND pos1, with ch filling the pos1 slot.

## Findings

### Finding 1: ch Enrichment at pos1 Is a General Folio-Closer Property, Not y-Onset-Specific

The folio-closer elevated ch at pos1 (37.3% vs 27.8% continuation = E=1.34×) is NOT driven by y-onset LF words specifically. Every LF onset class shows elevated ch at pos1 in folio-closer vs continuation: d-LF 43.8% vs 25.4% (E=1.72×), s-LF 45.5% vs 34.1%, o-LF 60% vs 28.4%, k-LF 67% vs 17%. The ch elevation at pos1 is a structural property of the short A-dialect folio-closing line. The line tends to: {any non-gallows onset LF} → {ch-onset pos1} → ... → {d-onset LL}.

### Finding 2: sh Is Strongly Depleted at Folio-Closer pos1 (E=0.16×)

A-dialect folio-closer pos1: sh=2.4% (2/83) vs continuation pos1 sh=15.0% (220/1471), E=0.16×. sh is the most depleted onset at folio-closer pos1 (even more depleted than at folio-closer LF where sh=1.2%, E=0.16×). The folio-closer anti-sh constraint extends to at least pos1: the second word of the A-dialect folio-closing line is strongly non-sh. Note: sh=7.4% at continuation LF and sh=15% at continuation pos1; folio-closer LF sh=1.2% (E=0.16×) AND folio-closer pos1 sh=2.4% (E=0.16×) — the same depletion factor at both positions.

### Finding 3: y→ch Is Enriched in Folio-Closer But NOT in Continuation (where y→ch=0.86×)

In continuation lines, y→ch is BELOW expectation (E=0.86×). In folio-closer lines, y→ch is slightly enriched (E=1.29×). This is consistent with the general folio-closer ch-enrichment at pos1 (which applies to all LF onsets). The bigram data shows y→ch is not a specific grammar rule; instead, ch is elevated at all folio-closer word transitions due to the general folio-closer structure.

### Finding 4: cth Completely Absent from Folio-Closer pos1 (0/83, E=0.00×)

cth-onset = 0/83=0% at folio-closer pos1 vs 5.1% in continuation (E=0.00×). cth-onset is absent from the second word of A-dialect folio-closing lines. This combines with: cth depleted at folio-closer LF (from F351/F355: cth at folio-last LF≈0%). The folio-closer line systematically excludes cth from both the first AND second position.

### Finding 5: Full Folio-Closer Pattern — {anti-gallows, anti-sh, anti-cth LF} → {ch-dominant, anti-sh pos1} → {d/ch body} → {d LL}

A-dialect folio-closer line structure, characterized:
- LF: anti-gallows (p=0%), anti-sh (E=0.16×), anti-cth (?), y/d/ch/s onset
- pos1: ch dominant (37.3%), sh depleted (E=0.16×), cth=0%
- pos2-MID: ch dominant (29%), d present
- LL: d dominant (28.9%), daiin=8.4%

The folio-closer is a 3-5 word line with consistent anti-sh/cth throughout and ch filling body positions. The two most common folio-closer line patterns appear to be: {y-LF, ch, d-or-ch, daiin-LL} and {d-LF, ch, daiin-LL}.

## Summary

H562 ANSWERED. The 40% ch at folio-closer pos1 is NOT y-onset-specific. It is a general A-dialect folio-closer property: ch is elevated and sh is depleted at pos1 across all LF onset types. The folio-closer anti-sh constraint extends to pos1 (E=0.16× at both LF and pos1). The line structure is anti-gallows/anti-sh/anti-cth throughout, with ch dominant at body positions and daiin at LL.
