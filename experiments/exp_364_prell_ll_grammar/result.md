## Findings

### Finding 1: A-Dialect LL Is d-Onset Dominated; B-Dialect LL Is Distributed

A-dialect top 6 LL words are all d-onset (daiin/dy/dam/dal/dar/dain) plus cthy(cth-onset). Contrast with B-dialect where chedy(ch), ol(ol), oly(ol), am, qoky(qok) are top 5. The A-dialect LL layer is primarily a d-onset zone, while B-dialect distributes across multiple onset classes. The A-dialect d-LL dominance reflects the A daiin=content-LL (F341: A daiin LL=1.37×) and other d-body words in terminal position.

### Finding 2: A-Dialect qot→daiin E=2.52× Pre-LL CONFIRMED; qot Words Diverse (No Formula)

qot-onset accounts for 7.5% of pre-daiin positions (7/93) vs 3.0% baseline — E=2.52×. The qot words preceding daiin: qotchey(2), qotychor, qotchod, qotomodydchol, qotchor, qotal — no single word dominates. The qot→daiin rule is onset-class level (not a specific word pair). qot→daiin is the strongest E-value pre-daiin predictor in A-dialect.

### Finding 3: B-Dialect d→daiin Pre-LL E=5.19× (N=7) — CONFIRMED, d-Onset Words Before daiin at LL

In B-dialect, d-onset accounts for 31.8% of pre-daiin positions (7/22) vs 6.1% baseline — E=5.19×. This is the strongest single pre-LL bigram rule by E in the full pre-LL→LL analysis. The mechanism: in B-dialect, when daiin appears at LL (rare event: 1.0% of LL), it is almost always preceded by another d-onset word. This suggests B-dialect daiin-at-LL is a specific sub-construction (d-onset word cluster at line end), consistent with daiin's broader d-class membership.

### Finding 4: B-Dialect sh Pre-LL → qok-Onset LL (sh→qok at Line-Terminal Position)

When sh-onset occupies the pre-LL position in B-dialect continuation lines, the LL word tends to be qok-onset: qoky(3.2%), qokal(2.7%), qokeedy/qokam/qokaiin(2.1% each). The sh→qok coupling (F336: E=1.97× at all positions) manifests specifically at the line-terminal bigram. This is a new data point confirming that sh→qok is not just a MID-position rule but extends to the pre-LL→LL transition.

### Finding 5: B-Dialect qot→daiin at LL = 0/111 (Complete Contrast with A-Dialect 15.9%)

In B-dialect, not a single qot-onset pre-LL word is followed by daiin at LL. This is a strong contrast with A-dialect (15.9%, E=2.52×). The qot→daiin rule is A-dialect-specific. In B-dialect, qot as pre-LL onset leads to diverse LL words (dar=2.7%, lol=2.7%, lo=2.7%, chedy=2.7%) — no single dominant LL word.

### Finding 6: A-Dialect ch Before LL Consistently Predicts d-Onset LL Words (daiin=9.0%, dal=2.6%, dy=2.0%, dam=1.5%)

When ch-onset is at pre-LL in A-dialect, the LL word is d-onset 9.0%+2.6%+2.0%+1.5% = 15.1% daiin+dal+dy+dam combined (top 4 all d-onset). Plus cthy(2.0%). The ch-onset word strongly predicts d-onset LL words in A-dialect, extending the ch→daiin rule (F362) to a broader ch→d-LL pattern.
