**THE d-ONSET MORPHOLOGICAL RULE**: The character following 'd' determines positional role:
- d + vowel (a, o) → LINE-OPENING (daiin, dain, dair, dor = openers)
- d + consonant (a in al/am, y) → LINE-CLOSING (dal, dam, dy = closers)
- d + gallows (ch, sh) → LINE_OPEN TIER (dch/dsh, strictly line-initial, 64-80% LFirst)

## Combined Finding (F113)

**H203 CONFIRMED**: Bare d-onset (8.2% of tokens) is a unified line-boundary class with VOWEL/CONSONANT split: d+vowel-onset (daiin, dain, dair, dor) = LINE-OPENERs (24-58% LFirst); d+consonant suffix (dal, dam, dy, daly) = LINE-CLOSERs (30-70% LLast). The d+gallows (dch/dsh = LINE_OPEN tier) is the STRONG line-opener version (64-80% LFirst). d-onset strongly suppresses B-type suffixes (-edy/-ey/-eey at 0.13-0.16×) in both dialects — a cross-dialect morphological constraint. D-chain: 18% of lines after d-closer start with d-onset (10% bare_d + 8% d+gallows); stronger in A (19.6%) than B (14.2%).

**H202 CONFIRMED (F113)**: o-prefix body words (19.0% of tokens) are a large, structured vocabulary class. Key findings: (1) Low label overlap (10.8%) — o-body words are different vocabulary from o-labels; (2) 28.3% stem overlap with qo-body (the shared stems are k/t-initial, consistent with F043); (3) The o-prefix system follows the SAME A/B suffix paradigm split as ch-stems: A-forms use -ol/-or/-chor; B-forms use -edy/-eedy (otedy=69.3× B-dominant vs A; okoly=A-dominant). This is a major unifying finding — the same morphological grammar governs both ch-onset and o-prefix vocabulary; (4) or-onset = strong closer (LL=40.6%); ok/ot = mid-line (LL=14-16%); ol = moderate closer (LL=24%); (5) B uses more o-prefix words than A (20.6% vs 16.0%).

**KEY FINDING (F113)**: The Voynichese morphological grammar is UNIVERSAL — the A/B suffix paradigm split (A:-ol/-or; B:-edy/-eedy) applies ACROSS ONSET CLASSES including both ch-onset (content) and o-prefix (object-naming) vocabulary. This means the dialect distinction is not a lexical property of specific stems but a grammatical property of the entire manuscript: in A, ALL word types (ch-stems, o-prefix stems, qo-stems) take -ol/-or suffixes; in B, ALL take -edy/-eedy suffixes. The manuscript's grammar is more unified than previously appreciated.
