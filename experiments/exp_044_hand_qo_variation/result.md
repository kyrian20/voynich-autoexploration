# Experiment exp_044: Scribal Hand Variation in qo-Rate and Suffix Preferences
Date: 2026-04-09

## Question (H050)
Do scribal hands 1-5 vary in qo-prefix rate independently of section/dialect?

## Results

### Main Hand Comparison Table

| Hand | Folios | Tokens | Currier | qo-rate | Top 3 suffixes |
|------|--------|--------|---------|---------|----------------|

**Hand 4 is a dramatic outlier:** only 5.9% qo-rate in Currier B (vs 17.7-20.6% for Hands 2 and 3). Hand 4 writes the astro/cosmo section.

**Hand 4 is morphologically anomalous among Currier B hands:**

- Hand 4's suffix profile (-ol dominant, -edy near-absent) resembles Currier A more than other Currier B hands

### Suffix-Section Confound Confirmed

**Hand 3 (Special section, Currier B)**: -aiin=11.4% — highest of any Currier B hand.  
This directly mirrors exp_038's finding that -aiin concentrates in the 'S' (special) section (57%). Hand 3 IS the special section scribe, and the -aiin preference is a hand/section feature simultaneously.

**Hand 2 (bio+herbal, Currier B)**: -edy=16.4% — matches bio section's -edy dominance (exp_038: -edy 42% of bio section suffix tokens).

### Within-Hand Variation in qo-Rate

Hand 2 (bio section) shows enormous within-hand qo-rate variation across folios:
- f33v: qo=0.000 (zero qo-words in 81 tokens!)
- f31v: qo=0.041
- f77r: qo=**0.368** (highest in the hand)
- mean=0.174, std=0.090

This within-hand variation (std=0.090 within Hand 2) is almost as large as the between-hand variation (Hand 2=0.206 vs Hand 4=0.059). **qo-rate is driven by content (section, folio topic) not purely by scribal habit.**

### Hand 4: Intermediate Between A and B?

Hand 4 (folios f67r1, f67r2, f67v2, f68r1, f68r2, f68v2, f68v3, f69r, f70r2):
- Currier B designation but qo-rate (5.9%) close to Hand 1/Currier A (10.7%)
- -edy near-absent (0.4%) — opposite to all other B hands
- -ol elevated (8.3%) — resembles Currier A's dominant suffix
- Section: astro/cosmo diagrams with circular text (some folios have constrained text in ring structures)

This may indicate: (a) the astro/cosmo section uses a different morphological register intermediate between A and B, (b) Hand 4 is a scribe with A-dialect morphological habits writing Currier B-assigned folios, or (c) the circular text format constrains vocabulary differently from prose.

## Finding (F051)
**Scribal hands vary in qo-rate and suffix preferences, but with strong section confound:**
- Hand 4 (astro/cosmo section, Currier B) is a major outlier: qo=5.9% (vs 17-21% for Hands 2-3), -edy=0.4% (vs 10-16%), -ol=8.3% — morphologically resembling Currier A rather than other Currier B hands
- Hand 3 (Special section): -aiin dominant (11.4%), consistent with exp_038's -aiin→Special section concentration
- Hand 2 (bio section): -edy dominant (16.4%), consistent with exp_038's -edy→bio concentration
- Within-Hand 2 qo-rate variation (std=0.090) approaches between-hand variation — qo-rate is primarily content-driven, not scribe-driven
- The astro/cosmo section (Hand 4) may represent a morphologically intermediate variety between Currier A and B

## H050 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED
Hands do vary in qo-rate and suffix preferences, but not independently of section — section and hand are strongly confounded (each hand writes specific sections). The one clear hand outlier is Hand 4 (astro/cosmo), which has A-like morphological features despite a B Currier designation. This raises a new hypothesis (H056): the astro/cosmo section is morphologically intermediate between A and B.
