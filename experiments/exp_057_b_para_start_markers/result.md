# Experiment exp_057: Currier B Paragraph-Start Markers
Date: 2026-04-09

## Question (H064)
Does -dy mark paragraph starts in Currier B, as it does in Currier A (4.39×, F061)? Full cross-dialect comparison of paragraph-level positional grammar.

## Results

### -dy Enrichment at Para-starts: A vs B

| Dialect | Para-starts | -dy at PS% | -dy baseline% | Ratio |
|---------|-------------|-----------|--------------|-------|
| A | 134 | 12.7% | 3.8% | **3.36×** |
| B | 130 | 7.7% | 5.0% | **1.53×** |

**-dy is enriched at para-starts in both dialects, but the effect is 2× stronger in A.** (Note: prior exp_054 reported 4.39× for A — slight discrepancy likely from different locus type parsing; current figure 3.36× is consistent with the same direction.)

### y-Initial Suppression at Para-starts

| Dialect | y-initial at PS | y-initial baseline | Ratio |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|-------|
| A | 0/134 = 0.0% | 14.7% | **0.00× (complete)** |
| B | 5/130 = 3.8% | 13.6% | **0.28×** |

y-initial is suppressed at para-starts in both dialects — completely in A, strongly but not absolutely in B (0.28×).

### Other Currier B Para-start Markers

| Suffix | PS% | Baseline% | Ratio |
|--------|-----|-----------|-------|
| -chy | 5.4% | 1.8% | **2.94×** |
| -ar | 17.7% | 8.4% | **2.11×** |
| -dy | 7.7% | 5.0% | **1.53×** |
| -or | 7.7% | 5.2% | **1.48×** |
| -ol | 10.0% | 7.6% | **1.32×** |

In Currier B, **-chy (2.94×) and -ar (2.11×) are stronger para-start markers than -dy (1.53×)**. The dominant para-start suffixes in B are -ar and -chy, not -dy.

### Currier B Para-start Suppressions

| Suffix | PS% | Baseline% | Ratio |
|--------|-----|-----------|-------|
| -aiin | 5.4% | 14.4% | **0.37×** |
| -ain | 2.3% | 7.5% | **0.31×** |

**-aiin and -ain are strongly suppressed at B para-starts** (0.37× and 0.31× respectively). In A, -aiin was at baseline (0.96×), so the -aiin para-start suppression is a **Currier B-specific feature**.

### Top Para-start Words by Dialect

**Currier A** (all hapax in para-start corpus): pchor, kooiin, pcheol... — no strong recurrent formula.

**Currier B** (some repetition): pchedar (×3), tchedy (×3), pchedal (×2). B para-start words cluster around p/t onset + consonant cluster + suffixed form. These are rare elsewhere in the corpus (0.4% and 0.9% overall), suggesting **B para-starts use more formulaic opener words** than A.

### Onset Distribution at B Para-starts

B para-start first words strongly favor p- and t- onset words (pchedar, tchedy, pchedal, pdair). The common qo-/o-/ch-/sh- onsets are underrepresented. This onset preference was not observed in Currier A para-starts.

## Finding (F064)

Currier B has paragraph-start markers, but a distinct set from Currier A:
- **-dy is a weak para-start marker in both dialects** (A: 3.36×, B: 1.53×)
- **y-initial suppression exists in both** (A: complete 0×, B: partial 0.28×)
- **B-specific para-start markers**: -chy (2.94×), -ar (2.11×) — not elevated in A
- **B-specific para-start suppression**: -aiin (0.37×), -ain (0.31×) — not suppressed in A
- **B para-starts cluster around p/t-onset formulaic words** (pchedar, tchedy, pchedal)
- The two dialects share the same underlying grammar type (para-start marking) but use different morphological signatures to implement it

## H064 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED
-dy marks paragraph starts in both Currier A and B, but with sharply different strength (3.36× in A vs 1.53× in B). B has additional, stronger para-start markers (-chy: 2.94×, -ar: 2.11×) that are absent in A. y-initial suppression is complete in A but partial in B. The two dialects share a paragraph-grammar system but implement it with different marker morphemes — suggesting dialect-specific morphological conventions within a shared grammatical framework.
