## H340: cth SELF-SUCCESSION PAIRS (A-dialect, n=23)

All 23 cth→cth pairs are unique (no repeat word pair). The dominant pattern is:

**[cth-non-cthy] → cthy**: ~8-10 cases:
- ctheey→cthy (f17), cthol→cthy (f7), cthody→cthy (f21), cthoinn→cthy (f15), cthai{ih}ar→cthy (f8), cth[a:o]r→cthy (f6), cthar→cth[a:o]r (f6), cthody→cthy

These confirm: **medial cth-words (cthol, ctheey, cthody, cthor-variants) introduce cthy at the terminal position**. The [cth-MID][cthy@LL] pair is the dominant cth self-succession pattern.

**cthy → [other cth]**: 3 cases where cthy is NOT the last word and is followed by another cth-form:
- cthy→cthd (f7), cthy→ctheo,r (f22), cthy→cthe (f24), cthy→cthy<$> (f44)
These are cases where cthy appears in non-LL positions (consistent with the F159 finding that cthy sometimes appears at second after ychor).

**cthol→cthol (1 case, f28)**: exact reduplication in `qotchaiin shor cthol cthol shor`.

**cthar→cthar (1 case, f1)**: `sory ckhar or,y kair chtaiin shar ase cthar cthar,dan` — cthar appears twice, second occurrence is a compound `cthar,dan` (corpus notation for cthar+dan).

**Key finding**: The [cth-MID][cthy@LL] construction appears ~8× in cth self-succession — this is the dominant cth→cth pattern and represents the systematic [medial cth-nominal → terminal cthy] grammatical sequence.

## H339: qok→cth DEPLETION

**qok→cth: 10 observed, 18.5 expected, enrichment = 0.54×** — confirmed structural depletion.

**qok successor onset distribution reveals qok's preferred successors**:
- qok→l = 2.44× (highest enrichment — qok specifically introduces l-onset words)
- qok→ok = 1.77×, qok→qok = 1.62× (self-succession enrichments)
- qok→ch_e = 1.31×, qok→qot = 1.31×
- qok→cth = 0.54× (depleted)
- qok→t = 0.41×, qok→s = 0.43× (strongly depleted)

**Structural explanation**: qok selects k/t-initial stems (F037). cth selects o/y/e-initial stems (F047). The morphological selectional constraints are incompatible — qok-words and cth-words belong to different morphological stem classes, so they systematically avoid adjacent positioning. The 0.54× depletion is structural, not frequency-driven.

**qok→l=2.44× (highest qok successor enrichment)** — in A-dialect, qok-words specifically introduce l-onset words. This is the same pattern as qok→l=2.70× from exp_164. l-onset words are predominantly B-dialect vocabulary (F147), but they appear in late A-herbal and pharma. qok→l in A is concentrated in late A-herbal/pharma where B-type l-words appear.

## H338: B-DIALECT cthedy/cthdy ARE GENUINE B-VOCABULARY

**cthedy (n=11)**: Avg folio edy-rate = **0.216** (consistently B-dialect). Spread: herbal=4, bio=4, stars=3. Predecessors: ch_e (2), qot (2), sh, s, d, ot (1 each) — diverse B-vocabulary onsets. The folios containing cthedy (f26, f31, f57, f75, f76, f104, f107, f114, f116) all have edy-rates 8.9-34.0% — genuine B-dialect folios.

**cthdy (n=7)**: Avg edy-rate = 0.222. Predecessors: ch (3, dominated by ch-forms). Sections: herbal, bio, cosmo, stars — cross-section. All from genuine B-dialect folios.

**cthey (n=10)**: Avg edy-rate = 0.198. Cross-section distribution (herbal=5, bio=1, stars=4). Predecessors and successors are diverse B-vocabulary.

**CONCLUSION**: cthedy, cthdy, cthey in B-dialect are genuine B-vocabulary items appearing in consistently B-dialect folios with typical B bigram contexts. They are NOT scribal errors or A-herbal borrowings. The cth- prefix operates in BOTH dialects but takes dialect-specific suffixes:
- A-dialect: cth + A-suffixes (cthor=-or, cthol=-ol, cthy=-y)
- B-dialect: cth + B-suffixes (cthedy=-edy, cthdy=-dy, cthey=-ey)

This is the strongest evidence yet that cth- is a true cross-dialect prefix morpheme with morphological productivity in both A and B.

In A-dialect, cth-words are positionally constrained (never opens lines, specifically terminal and second-position). The different positional profiles suggest cth-words serve DIFFERENT grammatical roles in A vs B, despite sharing the cth- prefix morpheme.
