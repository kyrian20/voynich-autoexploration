# exp_343: Folio-Opener Formula Words — Characterizing the LF of Folio-First Lines

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** Follow-up to F344 (exp_342). daiin avoids folio-first LF. What DOES appear there? Predicted: gallows-bearing onset words (p/t/k/f) from inline exp_342 analysis.
**Result:** CONFIRMED AND EXTENDED. Folio-first LF is dominated by p/k/t/f-onset (gallows-bearing) words in both dialects (~67–70%), with 3.3–5.2× enrichment over continuation-line LF. d/y/qok/s onsets are strongly depleted cross-dialectally. The gallows-formula distinction is a cross-dialect conserved structural feature of folio-opening positions.

## Findings

### Finding 1: Folio-first LF = gallows-onset formula words (cross-dialect conserved)

Both B-dialect (67.4%) and A-dialect (69.3%) folio-first lines open with p/k/t/f-onset words. The convergence at ~68% is striking given the substantial vocabulary differences between dialects. The enrichment is 3.28× (B) and 5.23× (A) relative to continuation-line LF.

The dominant onset is p in both dialects: B p=43.5% of folio-first LF vs 9.8% in continuation (E=4.46×); A p=37.3% vs 2.6% (E=14.15×). The A-dialect p-onset enrichment is extreme (14×) because continuation-line LF in A-dialect is even lower than B.

### Finding 2: d/y/qok/s onsets are conserved DEPLETIONS at folio-first LF

Cross-dialect depleted from folio-first LF:
- d-onset: B E=0.14×, A E=0.08× (effectively absent, consistent with F344 daiin exclusion)
- y-onset: B E=0.00×, A E=0.00× (completely absent from folio-first LF in both dialects)
- qok-class: B E=0.00×, A E=0.12× (absent/severe depletion)
- s-onset: B E=0.19×, A E=0.20× (strong depletion, near-identical)

These five classes — d, y, qok, s, sh(B) — form the "continuation LF grammar" class. They dominate continuation lines (together >55% of continuation LF) but are essentially absent from folio-opening lines.

### Finding 3: B-dialect folio-first LF is hapax-dominated; A-dialect less so

B-dialect top word is pchedar (2 tokens, 4.3%); 18 of 20 top words appear once (2.2% each). No single formula word dominates. N=46 tokens, 39 unique word types (85% hapax rate among folio-first LF words). 

A-dialect: kooiin(2.7%), shol(2.7%), tol(2.7%), pcheol(2.7%) tied at top. N=75 tokens, fewer hapaxes, but still diverse. The folio-opener formula is onset-class constrained (must use p/k/t) but the specific word is not fixed — unlike continuation lines where daiin alone captures 3.8%/5.1%.

### Finding 4: A-dialect vs B-dialect divergence in folio-first onset profile

A-dialect folio-first has sh-onset mildly present (E=1.32×, 7 tokens), which is absent in B-dialect (E=0.00×). A-dialect has qo-onset enriched (E=3.66×). B-dialect has f-onset strongly enriched (E=11.19×, though only 2 tokens). Both have t-onset: B at baseline (E=0.97×), A enriched (E=2.43×). Despite these differences, the dominant signal — p/k dominance, d/y/qok/s depletion — is conserved.

## Interpretation

The VMS has a two-register LF grammar: (1) **folio-opener register** (p/k-onset dominant, ~68% gallows class), and (2) **continuation register** (d/qok/s/y dominant, ~60% of these classes combined). daiin's LF enrichment (F341 E=2.57×) belongs entirely to the continuation register — it is the canonical continuation-opener but is absent from folio openers. The folio-opener formula requires a gallows-bearing word, which in EVA corresponds to the pc-, pk-, tc-, tk- compound structures (pchedar, pchedy, tol, etc.).

This dichotomy may reflect a structural/semantic distinction: folio openers signal topic boundaries with different grammatical markers than continuation-within-folio lines.
