## H105: Pharma A Has A-Type Vocabulary but B-Strength Sequential Grammar

### Onset-Class Distribution

| Onset | Herbal A | Pharma A | Currier B |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|
| qo | 10.1% | 13.2% | **18.6%** |
| ch | 20.7% | 19.1% | 15.0% |
| sh | 9.4% | 7.9% | 8.8% |
| d | 15.7% | 13.3% | 6.8% |
| o | 15.2% | **22.1%** | 23.2% |

Pharma A's qo-rate (13.2%) is intermediate between Herbal A (10.1%) and B (18.6%). Pharma A's o-rate (22.1%) approaches B's (23.2%), while Herbal A is 15.2%.

### Suffix Profile

| Suffix | Herbal A | Pharma A | Currier B |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| -edy | 0.1% | 0.2% | **12.8%** |
| -ol | 13.4% | **18.2%** | 5.2% |
| -aiin | 12.8% | 10.2% | 9.6% |
| -ey | 4.7% | **7.9%** | 6.1% |
| -dy | 5.3% | **8.6%** | 6.5% |

**Pharma A is clearly A-type in suffix profile**: -edy=0.2% (vs B's 12.8%), -ol=18.2% (highest of any dialect), -aiin=10.2%. Despite having a B-strength sh→qo coupling (2.44×), Pharma A uses pure A-type morphology.

### Vocabulary Jaccard

| Comparison | Jaccard | Shared | Total |
|-----------|---------|--------|-------|
| Pharma A ↔ Currier B | **0.070** | 463 | 6,598 |
| Herbal A ↔ Currier B | 0.100 | 813 | 8,117 |
| Pharma A ↔ Herbal A | 0.109 | 382 | 3,517 |
| A global ↔ B global | 0.120 | 1,063 | 8,846 |

**Pharma A is FARTHER from B (Jaccard=0.070) than Herbal A is (0.100)**. Pharma A and Herbal A share more vocabulary with each other (0.109) than either does with B. Pharma A is unambiguously Currier A in vocabulary.

### Pharma A sh→qo Word Pairs

Top pairs (all counts ≤ 2): sheey→qokol (2), sheol→qokol (2), and 13 hapax pairs. The coupling is NOT driven by any single high-frequency pair — it's distributed across diverse sh-words and qo-words.

### H105 Verdict: SUPPORTED WITH PARADOX — Pharma A Has A-Type Vocabulary but B-Strength Sequential Grammar

Pharma A has: (1) pure A-type suffix profile (-edy=0.2%, -ol=18.2%); (2) vocabulary closer to Herbal A (Jaccard=0.109) than to B (Jaccard=0.070); (3) sh→qo sequential coupling of 2.44× — exceeding B's 1.97×. The sequential grammar (sh→qo coupling) is NOT a vocabulary effect — it operates independently of morphological dialect. **Pharma A represents a Currier A dialect text with B-strength word-ordering grammar.** This dissociation of morphological dialect from sequential grammar suggests that the two phenomena are independent.

## H106: Aiin-Dom d→qo=1.55× Is Almost Entirely Driven by daiin→qo (2.38×)

### daiin vs Other-d as qo Predecessor

| Context | → qo rate | Baseline qo | Enrichment |
|---------|----------|------------|-----------|
| aiin-dom: **daiin**→qo | **18.9%** (10/53) | 7.9% | **2.38×** |
| aiin-dom: other-d→qo | 9.9% (14/142) | 7.9% | 1.24× |
| y-dom: daiin→qo | 8.8% (16/181) | 10.9% | **0.81×** |
| y-dom: other-d→qo | 8.8% (32/364) | 10.9% | **0.81×** |

**daiin specifically precedes qo in aiin-dominant Herbal A at 2.38× enrichment.** Other d-words in aiin-dom show only 1.24× (modest). In y-dominant Herbal A, daiin→qo is SUPPRESSED (0.81×) — the opposite direction.

Top word pairs: daiin → qotchy (2), daiin → qodaiin (1), daiin → qoky (1), daiin → qokcho (1), daiin → qotchey (1) — all hapaxes except one doublet. The coupling is spread across diverse qo-words.

### H106 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — daiin→qo (2.38×) Drives aiin-dom d→qo=1.55×

The aiin-dominant Herbal A d→qo enrichment (1.55× total) is almost entirely driven by daiin specifically → qo-onset words (2.38×). Other d-words in aiin-dom are only modestly enriched (1.24×). This creates a paradox: in aiin-dominant A, daiin (the most frequent word) precedes qo at 2.38×, while in y-dominant A, daiin→qo is suppressed (0.81×). The two registers have opposite daiin-qo coupling.

**Cross-register daiin→qo asymmetry:**
- aiin-dom: daiin is a qo-PREDICTOR (2.38×) — "daiin announces a qo-class word follows"
- y-dom: daiin avoids qo (0.81×) — "daiin is in a complementary position to qo"
- B: d-class avoids qo (0.76×) — more like y-dom A

This reveals a fundamentally different grammatical role for daiin in the two A registers.

## H107: B Special Section "Other" Line-Final Words Are a/l/r-Initial — Same Composition as Non-Special B

### First-Character Distribution of "Other" Line-Final Words

| First char | Special B | Non-Special B |
|-----------|----------|--------------|
| 'a' | 40.5% | 38.2% |
| 'l' | 37.3% | 39.9% |
| 'r' | 15.8% | 13.8% |
| 'c', 'q', 'f', other | 5.4% | 7.5% |

**The composition of "other" line-final words is nearly identical between Special B and non-Special B.** The anomaly in Special section is not WHAT words end lines (a/l/r-initial in both), but HOW MANY: Special section has 28.7% "other" onset at line-ends vs the typical ~15-18%.

Top Special B "other" line-final words: am (15), al (10), ram (7), lchedy (7), lol (5), lchdy (4).

### H107 Verdict: SUPPORTED (MECHANISM CLARIFIED) — Special Section Has More a/l/r-Onset Line-Final Words

The "other" onset in B Special section is dominated by a/l/r-initial words — exactly the same as non-Special B. Special section line-ends are unusual not in kind (a/l/r is the norm for "other") but in quantity: Special section simply has MORE "other"-onset words at line-ends (28.7% vs ~15%). This suggests Special section has a larger proportion of line-ending words that begin with a, l, or r — and correspondingly avoids d-onset at line-ends (0.85× vs 1.50-2.87× in other sections).

## H108: Para-Final vs Para-Interior Line Endings Are Significantly Different

### B: Para-Final vs Para-Interior Last-Word Onset

| Onset | Para-final (n=130) | Para-interior (n=2437) | PF enrichment | PI enrichment |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|--------------|--------------|
| **qo** | 3.1% | 11.6% | **0.17×** | 0.62× |
| sh | 8.5% | 3.4% | 0.97× | 0.39× |
| **o** | **40.0%** | 28.2% | **1.72×** | 1.22× |
| d | 8.5% | 10.4% | 1.24× | **1.52×** |
| ch | 18.5% | 13.2% | 1.23× | 0.88× |

**B paragraph endings (last word of last paragraph line) are dominated by o-onset (40.0%, 1.72×)** — much stronger than within-para line-final o enrichment (28.2%, 1.22×). qo is near-absent at para-final (3.1%, 0.17×). d enrichment is STRONGER within para (1.52×) than at para-final (1.24×).

The B paragraph-closing word is almost always an o-onset word.

### A: Para-Final vs Para-Interior Last-Word Onset

| Onset | Para-final (n=134) | Para-interior (n=1429) | PF enrichment | PI enrichment |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|--------------|--------------|
| qo | 2.2% | 6.2% | **0.21×** | 0.57× |
| sh | 4.5% | 5.2% | 0.49× | 0.57× |
| o | 20.9% | 17.3% | 1.21× | 1.00× |
| d | 23.9% | 25.5% | **1.62×** | **1.73×** |
| **ch** | **23.9%** | 16.4% | **1.20×** | 0.83× |

A para-final: d (1.62×) and ch (1.20×) are both enriched. Within A para-interior, ch is SUPPRESSED (0.83×) but d is enriched (1.73×).

**In A, ch appears at para-final endings (1.20×) but is suppressed within-para (0.83×).** d is the dominant line-final class in both contexts. In B, o-onset is the para-final marker; in A, ch plays a parallel role.

### H108 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — Para-Final Last Word Differs Significantly From Within-Para Last Word

B para-final: o-onset 1.72× (vs 1.22× within-para), qo 0.17× (vs 0.62× within-para). **B paragraphs close with o-onset words (40.0%) — a paragraph boundary marker.** A para-final: ch 1.20× (vs 0.83× within-para), d 1.62× (vs 1.73× within-para). A paragraphs also close with ch more often than mid-para line endings (ch is suppressed within-para). Both dialects show strong qo suppression at para-final (B: 0.17×, A: 0.21×).

## Combined Finding (F083)

**H105 PARADOX CONFIRMED**: Pharma A has B-strength sequential grammar (sh→qo=2.44×) but pure A-type vocabulary (Jaccard with B=0.070, LOWER than Herbal A's 0.100; -edy=0.2%, -ol=18.2%). The sh→qo coupling is distributed across diverse word pairs (no single high-freq pair drives it). Sequential grammar and morphological dialect are dissociated in Pharma A. **H106 STRONGLY SUPPORTED**: Aiin-dominant Herbal A d→qo=1.55× is driven by daiin specifically → qo at 2.38×; other d-words only 1.24×. In y-dom A, daiin→qo is SUPPRESSED (0.81×). The two A registers have OPPOSITE daiin-qo coupling, revealing different grammatical roles for daiin across registers. **H107 CLARIFIED**: Special section "other" line-final words are a/l/r-initial — same composition as non-Special B. The anomaly is quantity (28.7% vs ~15-18% elsewhere), not kind. Special section avoids d-onset at line-ends (0.85×) while all other B sections enrich it (1.76-2.87×). **H108 STRONGLY SUPPORTED**: B paragraph-closing words are strongly o-onset (40.0%, 1.72×); within-para line-final favors d (1.52×). A paragraph-closing words show ch enrichment (1.20× para-final vs 0.83× within-para). Both dialects show near-complete qo suppression at para-final (B: 0.17×, A: 0.21×). Para-boundaries are grammatically marked by onset class in both dialects.
