# exp_395: B t→{pos1}→{pos2} Full Trigram Analysis (H609/H610)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis tested:** H609/H610 — Is t the third gallows LF formula type? What does t at LF produce at pos1 and pos2?
**Outcome:** ANSWERED — B t is NOT a third distinct gallows formula type. B t→pos1 is NEAR-BASELINE (ch=0.96×, sh=1.02×) — unlike p (p→sh=5.90×) and y (y→y=2.13×, y→sh=0.57×). B t LF grammar is "open" — distributes approximately proportionally to all pos1 classes. A t→ch DEPLETED at pos1 (0.66×); A t→ot elevated (2.77×).

## 5. Interpretation

**H609 ANSWERED: t is NOT a third distinct gallows LF formula type.** The F364 pattern ({k,t}→ch) applies at MID positions but does NOT operate at LF→pos1 — t at LF does not preferentially route to ch at pos1 in B (E=0.96×). At the trigram level, B t→sh and B t→ch lines both show mild distributed effects at pos2 with no single dominant routing comparable to p→sh→qot or y→sh→ch.

**Three LF grammar types now fully characterized for B gallows classes:**
- p: strongly directed (p→sh=5.90×); gallows-formula (p→sh→qot=2.11×)
- f: mildly directed (f→sh=1.69× in B, directional)
- t: position-open (both sh/ch near-baseline); no formula-type trigram grammar
- k: position-general in continuation lines (F397 — folio-level class)

The k and t continuation-line LF openness suggests that k and t are structurally distinct from p/f within the gallows class: p/f are "strong formula LF classes" while k/t are "weak or absent LF formula classes." This is consistent with F397 (k is folio-level not line-level) and the present finding (t at LF distributes openly).
