# Experiment exp_063: B Para-Start pch/tch Onset Class Analysis
Date: 2026-04-09

## Question (H073)
Are pch/tch onset words in Currier B a distinct structural vocabulary class concentrated at paragraph-starts?

## Results

### Para-Start Enrichment

| Onset | PS_first/130 | PS_first% | All_words% | Ratio |
|-------|-------------|----------|-----------|-------|
| pch | 18 | 13.8% | 0.81% | **17.19×** |
| tch | 8 | 6.2% | 0.57% | **10.81×** |
| p_other | 24 | 18.5% | 1.01% | **18.21×** |
| t_other | 17 | 13.1% | 1.95% | 6.71× |

**pch words are 17× enriched at B para-starts; tch words are 11× enriched.** Note: p_other (non-pch p-onset) is even more enriched (18.21×), suggesting the entire p-onset class is the B paragraph-opener class.

### Line-Positional Profile

pch words are almost exclusively LINE-INITIAL:
- Line-first: 112/2567 = **4.4%** of all B lines
- Para-start first: 18/130 = **13.8%**
- Continuation first: 92/2416 = **3.8%**
- **Line-last: 3/2567 = 0.1%** — nearly complete line-final avoidance

tch words: similar profile (line-first: 3.0%, line-last: 0.1%).

### Label Absence

**0 pch words in labels (0/781). 0 tch words in labels.** Complete absence from the label corpus is consistent with pch/tch being structural/positional markers rather than content words.

### Suffix Distribution: -edy Preference

| Suffix | pch% | tch% | B marginal% |
|--------|------|------|------------|
| -edy | **19.0%** | **25.2%** | 12.8% |
| -dy | 11.5% | 14.6% | 6.5% |
| -al | 7.5% | 2.4% | 5.1% |
| -aiin | 6.3% | 6.5% | 9.6% |

pch and tch words strongly prefer -edy (1.5-2× above B baseline) — a distinctive suffix pattern not shared with the non-pch p_other or t_other classes.

### Top pch and tch Words

Most common **pch** words: pchedy (29), pchedar (10), pchdy (7), pcheol (6), pchedal (5), pchdair (5)
Most common **tch** words: tchedy (28), tchdy (12), tchey (9), tcheol (3), tcheody (3)

Both classes show a clear pattern: the most common form is always pch/tch + edy (pchedy=29, tchedy=28). These compound onset+suffix forms are the dominant realization of the class.

### Section Distribution

pch and tch words appear across all major sections (Special=1.01%, Herbal=0.85-0.91%, Bio=0.47-0.58%) but are **completely absent from Astro**.

## H073 Verdict: SUPPORTED

pch/tch onset words in Currier B are a distinct structural vocabulary class:
1. **Massively enriched at para-starts** (pch: 17×, tch: 11×)
2. **Almost exclusively line-initial** (line-last avoidance ~98%)
3. **Completely absent from labels** (0/781)
4. **Distinctive suffix preference**: -edy (1.5-2× above baseline)
5. **Canonical form**: pch+edy (pchedy) and tch+edy (tchedy) are the most common realizations

The full p-onset class (both pch and p_other) is the B paragraph-start marker class. The p-onset para-start enrichment (17-18×) is much stronger than the -dy enrichment in A (3.36×) or the B -chy enrichment (2.94×) found in exp_057. This suggests that in Currier B, the **p-onset** is the dedicated paragraph-opening marker, not the suffix class.

## Finding (F070)
pch-initial and tch-initial words in Currier B are structural paragraph-opening markers: 17× enriched at para-starts (pch), 11× (tch), completely absent from labels (0/781), and almost exclusively line-initial (line-last rate <0.15%). The most common forms are pchedy (29 tokens) and tchedy (28 tokens) — both use the -edy suffix. The broader p-onset class (all p-initial B words) is even more para-start enriched (18.21×), making p-onset the primary B paragraph-opening class. This is structurally analogous to -dy in Currier A (3.36× para-start enrichment), but the B marker operates on ONSET rather than SUFFIX — the dialectal difference in positional grammar is: A marks paragraph-starts with a suffix class (-dy), while B marks them with an onset class (p-onset). Both achieve the same structural function through different morphological slots.
