## Findings

### Finding 1: Both Folio Registers Are Hapax-Dominated (80-90% vs 35-40% Continuation)

The folio-opener AND folio-closer LF positions share a structural property: both use highly unique, non-repeated vocabulary (80-90% hapax token rate). Continuation LF is only 35-40% hapax tokens (many formula words like daiin, chedy repeat frequently). This confirms: the three-register grammar operates as an **onset-class constraint on diverse vocabulary**, not as a formula word constraint. No formula word dominates the folio registers.

### Finding 2: B-Dialect Folio-Closer Top LF Word = ol (the Bare Terminal Morpheme)

The most common B-dialect folio-last LF word is **ol** (3/48=6.2%) — the bare terminal morpheme. Other frequent words: lchey (2), daiin (2). The folio-closer formula (l/ol/ot enriched) uses line-terminal onset words as line-openers — a structural inversion: line-terminal morphemes (l/ol/ot, which are enriched at LL in normal continuation) appear at the LF position of the folio-closing line.

### Finding 3: B-Dialect Folio-Closer LL Is sh-Enriched (E=3.45×) But Hapax — sh+ckhy Compound Class

B folio-closer LL: sh=14.6% (E=3.45×). All 48 LL tokens are unique words. The sh-onset words at folio-closer LL include: shckhdy, shckhar, shdy, shckhy — this is the sh+ckhy compound class (F352: shckhy is 96-97% MID in continuation lines). These sh+ckhy words appear at LL of folio-closing lines specifically, not as MID words. B folio-closer LL also: l-onset depleted (E=0.22×, depleted despite l being the folio-closer LF formula). l appears at LF; LL uses ch/sh.

### Finding 4: A-Dialect Folio-Closer LL = daiin Dominant (8.4%)

A-dialect folio-closer LL: daiin=7/83=8.4% — the single most common LL word. A-dialect daiin is a content word appearing at LL (F341: A daiin LL=1.37× in continuation). At folio-closer LL, daiin appears even more prominently. The A-dialect folio-closing line pattern: y-onset LF, ch-body middle, daiin LL.

### Finding 5: A-Dialect Folio-Closer pos1 = ch Dominant (40%)

A-dialect folio-closer lines: position 1 (second word) = ch-onset in 40% of cases. This is striking: overall A-dialect ch-onset is ~7.4% of continuation LF words. At folio-closer pos1, ch=40% — a 5.4× enrichment. This suggests a y→ch onset bigram sequence within the A-dialect folio-closer line: after the y-onset LF word (ycheor, ykeey), the second word is frequently a ch-onset word. The folio-closer line structure in A-dialect appears to be: {y/d} LF → {ch×40%} → {ch} → daiin LL.

### Finding 6: B-Dialect Folio-Closer MID Has 0% Gallows

B-dialect folio-closer MID_rest positions: 0/55=0% gallows (0 gallows words in any internal position). The anti-gallows constraint in B-dialect folio-closer extends to ALL positions (LF=12.5%, pos1=7.0%, pos2=2.7%, MID=0.0%, LL=2.1%). The folio-closer anti-gallows gradient tightens through the line, reaching 0% in internal MID positions.
