# exp_360: ch→daiin Sequential Rule (H571)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H571 — Is ch-onset the dominant predecessor of daiin? Is ch→daiin a cross-dialect rule with E significantly above baseline?
**Result:** H571 CONFIRMED. ch→daiin is a cross-dialect onset-class rule: A E=1.30× (N=85), B E=1.45× (N=39). Effect is strongest at pre-LL→LL in BOTH dialects: A E=1.69×, B E=2.02×. In A-dialect, chol is the specific dominant ch-word preceding daiin (25/85 = 29% of ch→daiin pairs). NEW FINDING: B-dialect d→daiin E=5.57× at pre-LL→LL (N=7, 33.3% vs 6.0% baseline) — a very strong but N-limited finding. A-dialect qot→daiin E=2.50× at pre-LL→LL. Both dialects: ch is the onset class most strongly predicting daiin at LL in absolute frequency.

## Findings

### Finding 1: ch→daiin Is a Cross-Dialect Rule (A: E=1.30×, B: E=1.45×), Strongest at pre-LL→LL

Both dialects show ch-onset word followed by daiin at elevated rates. A-dialect: ch→daiin E=1.30× (N=85, robust). B-dialect: ch→daiin E=1.45× (N=39, adequate). The effect is consistent across all positions but STRONGEST at the pre-LL→LL transition: A E=1.69×, B E=2.02×. This is the clearest sequential rule linking an onset class to daiin as a terminal formula word.

### Finding 2: chol→daiin Is the Dominant A-Dialect Word-Level Rule (29% of ch→daiin Pairs)

In A-dialect, 25 of 85 ch→daiin pairs involve chol as the predecessor (chol is an ol-body ch-onset word meaning it has the body "-ol"). chol→daiin is a recurring word-level bigram, concentrating the ch→daiin signal. chor is second (8/85 = 9%). Together chol+chor account for 39% of A-dialect ch→daiin. The remaining 61% are spread across various ch words (chy, cheey, chey, etc.).

### Finding 3: B-Dialect chedy→daiin Is the Dominant B-Dialect ch→daiin Pair (26%)

In B-dialect, chedy (the edy-suffix variant of ch-onset) accounts for 10/39 = 26% of ch→daiin pairs. chey (4/39), chckhy (3/39), cheey (3/39) follow. The B-dialect ch→daiin is more concentrated on edy-body ch words than A-dialect (A's dominant ch→daiin words are ol-body: chol, chor).

### Finding 4: B-Dialect d→daiin E=5.57× at pre-LL→LL (N=7; High E, Limited N)

In B-dialect, when daiin appears at LL, the pre-LL word is d-onset in 33.3% of cases (7/21), vs 6.0% d-onset baseline at pre-LL positions — E=5.57×. This is a very strong enrichment if real. B-dialect d→daiin at ALL positions: E=1.69× (N=18). The pre-LL effect may be driving the overall B d→daiin enrichment. The mechanism is likely specific d-onset words (dar, dain, etc.) forming word-level pairs with daiin at line end. Note: N=7 makes this directional; the high E should be verified.

### Finding 5: A-Dialect qot→daiin E=2.50× at pre-LL→LL (N=6; Moderate Signal)

qot-onset is the STRONGEST E at pre-LL→LL in A-dialect (E=2.50×, N=6). qot is a template-class onset (ot-class with q-augment). This qot→daiin rule adds a second specific onset class (beyond ch) that strongly predicts daiin at LL in A-dialect. sh and d are DEPLETED before daiin at LL (E=0.94×, 0.79×).

### Finding 6: ch Is the Dominant Daiin Predecessor by Absolute Count (Not E)

Despite qot and cth having higher E values (1.91×, 1.56× in A), ch has the highest ABSOLUTE count of daiin predecessors in A-dialect (85 pairs) because ch is a very common onset class (N_prev=1415 vs qot N=215). In B-dialect, ch has the second highest count of daiin predecessors (39 pairs) after qok (27 pairs). By count, ch is the most practically important daiin predecessor.

## Summary

H571 CONFIRMED. ch→daiin is a cross-dialect sequential rule: A E=1.30×, B E=1.45×. Strongest at pre-LL→LL (A: 1.69×, B: 2.02×). A-dialect specific: chol→daiin (29% of ch→daiin pairs), qot→daiin E=2.50× at pre-LL→LL. B-dialect specific: chedy→daiin (26%), d→daiin E=5.57× at pre-LL→LL (N=7, directional). The ch→daiin rule is architecturally important: it connects the ch-body word class (chol, chor in A; chedy in B) to the LL-terminal daiin formula, providing a sequential constraint for the simulation model.
