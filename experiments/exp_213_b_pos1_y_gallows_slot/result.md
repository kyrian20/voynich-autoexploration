## H404: y+Gallows Slot at pos=1?

**Result: H404 NOT SUPPORTED — but a NEW FINDING identified**

y+gallows-compound words are NOT concentrated at pos=1 in B:
- pos=0 (PS): y+gal = 1/101 (0.99%, 0.44×) — depleted
- pos=1: y+gal = 2/98 (2.04%, 0.91×) — near-baseline

The hypothesis that they occupy a two-step formula [PS] → [pos=1: y+gallows] is not supported. The y+gallows vocabulary is a within-first-line class without a dedicated positional slot.

## Self-Audit

**Target-awareness:** No target-aware elements. This is a corpus extraction experiment.

**Feature-destroying implementation:** No feature-destroying details. The y+gallows detection is conservative (ypch/ytch/ykch/yfch prefix matching). One or two words with y+gallows structure might be missed by this criterion (e.g., if word contains a different gallows), but the pattern is robust at the count level.

**Extrapolation:** The finding that p+t+k = 0/98 at pos=1 in B is a strong pattern (expected ~65 words if distributed like PS; observed 0; p<10⁻³⁰ by binomial). The claim "p/t/k strictly excluded from B pos=1" is supported by n=98 with 0/98 from these onset classes. This is not sensitive to sample size.

**Negative-result scope:** H404 (y+gallows at pos=1) is not supported. The correct conclusion is: "y+gallows words are not concentrated at pos=1; they appear diffusely in B first lines, enriched at pos=3+ (1.38×)." This does not rule out the vocabulary class itself (F212 finding stands) but corrects the positional hypothesis.

**Control behavior:** A-dialect comparison serves as control. A shows a parallel (but softer) anti-PS pattern at pos=1, supporting that B's pattern is an extension of a shared A/B structural rule rather than a B-specific artifact.

## Conclusion

**H404 NOT SUPPORTED**: y+gallows-compound words are not concentrated at B first-line pos=1. They appear at pos=3+ (1.38× enrichment), distributed across later first-line positions. No dedicated y+gallows slot at pos=1 exists.

**NEW FINDING (H405 generated)**: B pos=1 (immediately after PS) is the "anti-PS slot" — the three onset classes that dominate PS (p/t/k: 67% of PS) are strictly excluded from pos=1 (0/98). B pos=1 is dominated by sh(1.59×)/qok(2.22×)/ch(1.12×)/ol(1.66×)/d(1.13×). This anti-PS pattern is also present in A (sh/ch dominate pos=1; p/t nearly absent) but is more absolute in B.

**Implication**: B first-line structure is:
- PS: p/t/k (67% of words) — gallows-compound para-openers
- pos=1: sh/qok/ch (the anti-PS vocabulary) — hard constraint, no p/t/k
- pos=2+: more variable, gradual return toward corpus baseline

This alternating onset structure within B first lines (PS=gallows → pos=1=anti-gallows) is the strongest evidence of within-line positional onset grammar in B-dialect, comparable to A-dialect's PS→sh transition (F207) but with a full anti-gallows vocabulary at pos=1 rather than a single class (sh).
