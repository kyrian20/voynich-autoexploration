## H344: [daiin][cthol][cthy] TRIGRAM

**[daiin][cthol][cthy] = 1 occurrence** (f7 herbal):
`chochy cthy daiin qoky ch{cphh}y daiin cthol cthy cthd`

The f7 line has this structure:
- Position 0: chochy (LF)
- Position 1: cthy (second)
- Position 2: daiin
- Position 3: qoky
- Position 4: ch{cphh}y
- Position 5: daiin (second daiin — the one introducing cthol)
- Position 6: cthol (follows daiin)
- Position 7: cthy (follows cthol — confirming [cthol][cthy])
- Position 8: cthd (line ends with another cth fragment)

The [daiin cthol cthy] trigram exists and appears once. The line also has a DOUBLE DAIIN structure (daiin at positions 2 and 5). The [cthol][cthy] bigram (1 case only in A-dialect) is confirmed to be preceded by daiin.

**[daiin][cthol] bigrams (n=6)**: After cthol, the next word is:
- cthy: 1× (f7 — the triggering case)
- ctholo: 1× (f22 — cthol followed by a cthol variant)
- dol: 1× (f6)
- doiin: 1× (f30)
- chos: 1× (f56)
- daiin: 1× (f89)

Only 1/6 [daiin cthol] sequences lead to cthy. The dominant continuation after [daiin cthol] is diverse — cthol does not consistently select cthy as its successor when daiin is the precursor.

**[daiin][cthor][chol]: 2 occurrences** (the [daiin cthor chol] formula from F165): f15 `daiin cthor chol chor` and f19 `daiin cthor chol ykchor chordy`. This is more recurrent than [daiin cthol cthy] (2× vs 1×).

**Dominant cth trigram patterns (onset-level, A-dialect)**:
- [ch][d][cth] = 17 (highest): ch-word → d-word → cth-word
- [d][cth][d] = 16: d surrounds cth (daiin before and after cth-word)
- [ch][ch][cth] = 13: two consecutive ch-words then cth
- [ch][cth][d] = 10, [ch][cth][ch] = 8

**[d][cth][d]=16** is the second-highest trigram: cth-words are frequently surrounded by d-words (daiin primarily) on both sides. This suggests the pattern: [daiin][cth-word][daiin] is common — cth-words appear between two daiin tokens in A-herbal lines.

**MAJOR FINDING: B-dialect cthedy and cthey are LF-ENRICHED (1.53× and 1.69×) while standard B-suffix words (chedy, shedy, qokedy) are LF-DEPLETED (0.26-0.52×)**. B's cthedy/cthey specifically OPEN LINES at higher-than-baseline rate. This is the opposite of chedy/shedy which are predominantly medial.

**B-dialect cthdy is LL-ENRICHED (2.41×)** — it closes lines. This parallels A-dialect cthy (LL=1.81×).

**B-dialect cth-words have an internal positional paradigm**:
- B-cthedy/cthey = LF-enriched (line-opening role)
- B-cthdy = LL-enriched (line-closing role)

This parallels A-dialect's internal cth paradigm (cthor=second-enriched, cthol=MID, cthy=LL). The positional specialization within cth-words is cross-dialect — both A and B cth-words show internal positional paradigm.

**However, the roles DIFFER between A and B**:
- A: cth-words are second/MID/LL (NEVER LF); cthy=LL-closer
- B: cth-words CAN be LF-openers (cthedy=1.53×, cthey=1.69×); cthdy=LL-closer

The F173 finding that "B cth is positionally neutral overall" (LF=0.97×) was the AVERAGE of LF-enriched (cthedy, cthey) and MID-dominant (cthdy) forms. The individual words have strong positional specialization.

## Summary of New Findings (F174)

1. **[daiin cthol cthy] trigram = 1 occurrence** (f7 herbal) (H344 CONFIRMED): The [determiner][medial-nominal][terminal-nominal] grammatical chain exists in A-herbal. The f7 line has [daiin cthol cthy cthd] — two cth-words ending the line (cthy at position 7, cthd at 8). After [daiin cthol], next word is mostly diverse (cthy in only 1/6 cases).

2. **[d][cth][d]=16 is the 2nd most common cth onset-trigram**: cth-words are frequently surrounded by d-words (daiin) on both sides — [daiin][cth-word][daiin] is a frequent structural pattern.

3. **B-dialect cth-words have INTERNAL POSITIONAL PARADIGM** (H342 CONFIRMED WITH MAJOR SURPRISE): cthedy (1.53×) and cthey (1.69×) are **LF-ENRICHED** — they open lines. cthdy is **LL-enriched (2.41×)** — it closes lines. This is opposite to standard B-suffix words (chedy=LF 0.26×, shedy=LF 0.52×, qokedy=LF 0.86×). B's cth-words play grammatically distinct positional roles from other -edy words. The F173 finding of "near-baseline B cth" was the average of this LF-opener / LL-closer internal split.

4. **qok→l=7 pairs all unique, concentrated in late A-herbal and A-pharma** (H343 CONFIRMED): qok-words precede B-type l-vocabulary (lkeedy, lchedy) in the B-adjacent zones of the herbal section. This is consistent with qok being A-domain vocabulary that encounters B-type l-words in the A/B boundary zone.
