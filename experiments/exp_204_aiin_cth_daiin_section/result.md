**H378 SUPPORTED WITH NUANCE — -aiin class is enriched (1.92×) but enrichment is NOT uniform; concentrated in onset-specific subset**:

The -aiin suffix class as a whole has 1.92× cth enrichment (8.37% vs 4.36% baseline), confirming it is a genuine cth-introduction class in A-dialect. However, the enrichment is DIVIDED:

**High-cth -aiin words** (all >8%): shaiin(28.57%/6.56×), okaiin(15.15%/3.48×), daiin(12.76%/2.93×), chodaiin(9.52%/2.19×), otaiin(8.00%/1.84×). These words have ONSET CLASSES that are themselves cth-attractors: sh-onset (shaiin), ok-onset (okaiin), d-onset (daiin), ch-onset (chodaiin), ot-onset (otaiin).

**Zero-cth -aiin words** (all 0%): aiin(58), odaiin(26), qokaiin(24), saiin(23), ykaiin(15). These have onset classes that are NOT cth-attractors: standalone/o-onset (aiin, odaiin, oaiin), qok-onset (qokaiin), s-onset (saiin), y-onset (ykaiin, ytaiin, ydaiin).

**Conclusion**: The -aiin suffix does NOT independently confer cth-attraction. Rather, -aiin words that have cth-attracting onset classes (sh, d, ok, ch) inherit the cth-attraction of their onset. aiin and qokaiin (-aiin forms of non-cth-attracting onsets) have zero cth successors. The "daiin→cth construction" (F165) is more precisely: "d-onset -aiin words → cth," with daiin as the dominant member. More broadly, the cth-attracting -aiin words are: any -aiin word whose onset already has cth-attraction (enrichment is onset-class driven, not suffix-driven). Within d-onset: daiin=2.93× >> dain=1.61× > dal≈baseline > dar=0.41× — the cth-attraction is specific to the word 'daiin', not all d-onset -aiin words.

**H379 SUPPORTED — cth-words introduce d-onset(1.33×)/cth(1.49×)/ot(1.48×)/qo(1.64×); sh is DEPLETED (0.57×)**:

The cth→daiin enrichment (1.50×) is moderate — daiin is enriched as a cth-successor but not dramatically. cth→cth is also enriched (1.49×), meaning cth-word chains are grammatical. sh is DEPLETED after cth-words (0.57×), consistent with A-dialect's cth/sh mutual exclusion. ch is at baseline (1.01×) after cth-words.

**Grammar cluster established**: The A-dialect constructs a sequence: [ch-word] → daiin → [cth-word] → daiin/dain/d-word. The chain is: ch precedes daiin (1.18-1.61×); daiin precedes cth (2.93×); cth precedes daiin/d-onset (1.33-1.50×). This forms a loop: ch → daiin → cth → d-onset → (ch again). The sh depletion at both ends (before cth: sh=1.02× near-baseline in A-herbal; after cth: sh=0.57×) suggests sh and cth are grammatically segregated in A-dialect.
