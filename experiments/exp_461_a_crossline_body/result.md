## Interpretation

**H_new_V ANSWERED:**

1. **A ok→y CL is body-class-distributed** (not edy-body specific): c-body(3.75×/N=5), edy-body(1.45×), aiin-body(1.41×) all elevated. Unlike B where aiin-body drives specific CL couplings (F441), A ok→y is class-level. The F438 "class-level" characterization is confirmed for A ok→y.

2. **A d+aiin cross-line is FLAT** (E≈1.0× for sh/y/ch/d, N=160): B d+aiin→sh (F441: E=1.99×/N=14) is B-specific. In A, d is so common at LL (A d is LL-enriched, F302) that it doesn't carry specific cross-line routing — it's a "neutral" LL class in A. This is the clearest A/B grammar difference at the cross-line body-conditional level.

3. **A sh+edy → qok CL (E=3.45×/N=4, dir):** A shedy words at LL route to qok at next LF. B shedy words at LL route to d (exp_457). Another A/B sh+edy CL inversion:
   - A: shedy→qok CL (directional); within-line shedy→qo (F439/A)
   - B: shedy→d CL (E=1.54×); within-line shedy→qok (F439/F372)
   
4. **edy-body CL pattern consolidation** (H_new_U extension):
   - sh+edy: A→qok CL; B→d CL — dialectal inversion in CL target
   - ch+edy: A→ch CL (F442); B→qot CL (F442) — dialectal inversion
   - ok+edy: A→sh CL (dir); B→y CL — different targets
   - In every case tested, edy-body at LL generates a different CL target between A and B. edy-body's cross-line routing is DIALECT-SPECIFIC, not a universal rule.
