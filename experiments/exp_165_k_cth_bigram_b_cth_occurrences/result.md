## H335: k→cth BIGRAMS IN A-DIALECT (n=18)

All 18 k→cth pairs are **unique** (no word pair repeats). The k-words are diverse:
- ky: 2×, kchor: 3×, kaiin: 1×, koshey, kar, ksheo, keeor, keey, kain, kchy, keeey, keeokechy, kodaiin, key (1× each)
- Most k-words precede diverse cth-words (cthy×5, cthor×2, cthol×2, other cth-words×9)

**Positional slot of k-word**: MID=12 (67%), second=4 (22%), LF=2 (11%). k-words in the MIDDLE of lines introduce cth-words at the next position — this is a MID→MID sequential pattern, not a line-opening construction.

**k→cth is a minor construction without a dominant formula**: no specific [k-word][cth-word] pair is recurrent. k-words as cth-introducers are spread across diverse k-forms and all contribute ≤1 case each (except kchor=3×: all different cth-words though).

**kchor→cth**: kchor appears 3× as cth-preceder (cthaiin, cthol, cthy). kchor at second (2/3 cases): [ychor→kchor@second→cthaiin/cthol/cthy@MID]. This may be a minor sub-variant of the y-opener constructions.

**k-onset is now the 6th cth-introduction onset class** in A-dialect (1.37× enriched), adding to the 5-construction model from F165: ch (1.27×), d (1.36×), k (1.37×), cth-self (1.62×), sh (1.12×), qo (1.22×).

## H337: B-DIALECT cth OCCURRENCES — MAJOR SURPRISE

**Total B-dialect cth-word occurrences: 52** (from 49 unique lines across B-herbal, B-bio, B-stars, B-cosmo).

**B-dialect cth vocabulary — dominated by B-SUFFIX FORMS**:

| cth-word | n  | suffix type |
|----------|----|-------------|
| cthedy   | 10 | **B-suffix** (-edy) |
| cthey    | 10 | ambiguous (-ey: mild B) |
| cthdy    | 6  | **B-suffix** (-dy) |
| cthy     | 4  | A-suffix (-y) |
| cthar    | 3  | ambiguous (-ar) |

**cthedy (10 tokens) and cthdy (6 tokens) are B-dialect forms of cth — they take the B-suffix paradigm (-edy, -dy)**. This is a MAJOR FINDING: cth- is NOT purely A-dialect morphology. In B-dialect, cth-words take B-suffixes (cthedy, cthdy, ctheey) rather than A-suffixes (cthor, cthol). The cth prefix is cross-dialect; only the SUFFIX PARADIGM differs between A and B.

**Section distribution of B cth occurrences**:
- B-herbal: 18 (f26, f31, f33, f34, f43, f46, f48, f57, f66)
- B-bio: 17 (f75-f84, f116)
- B-stars: 15 (f104-f116)
- B-cosmo: 2 (f86)

B cth is NOT concentrated in any single section — it appears across all B sections. The cthedy/cthey forms are present in herbal, bio, AND stars.

**B-dialect ch→cth bigrams (n=8)**:
All 8 cases — 6 from B-herbal (f46, f48, f66), 1 from B-cosmo (f86), 1 from B-stars (f113):
- f46: `chdy → cthy`, `chody → cthdy`, `chody → cthey`
- f48: `chdy<->ytchedal → cthey`
- f66: `chady → cthdy`
- f86: `chol,tal → cthdy`
- f113: `chcheey → cthes`, `chor → cthol`

The B ch→cth bigrams show ch-prefix words (chdy, chody, chady) specifically preceding B-type cth-words (cthdy, cthey). This parallels A-dialect [ch][cth] but uses B-vocabulary in both positions. The pattern is specifically in B-herbal (f46=largest contributor with 3 cases).

**f46 is a B-herbal folio with anomalously concentrated cth-words**: f46 has 4 cth-word occurrences in 3 lines (cthy, cthdy, cthy, cthey) — more cth-concentration than typical B-herbal.

## H336: ch→r SPECIFIC PAIRS (A-dialect, n=15)

ch→r pairs: All 15 pairs unique. **cho-forms dominate as the ch-word** (cho×6, cha×2, chor×1, chotain×1, chocthy×1, others). The r-words are diverse (raiin, ry, r, ro, rol, ror, roaiin, rchealcham, rchseesy, rcheey — all different).

**cho specifically precedes r-onset** (6/15=40% of ch→r pairs are cho→r-word). `cho` is a very short, possibly function-like ch-word. The pattern [cho][r-word] may reflect a specific syntactic linkage.

**r→ch (n=8)**: All from bare `r` before ch-words. The pattern r→ch and r→ch_e together (6+8=14 r→ch/ch_e bigrams) show that bare `r` specifically introduces ch-class words (both ch and ch_e). `r` is a minimal linking token that bridges to the next ch-word.

## H334: t→ot SPECIFIC PAIRS (A-dialect, n=16)

All 16 t→ot pairs unique. t-words are diverse: tchy (3×), and 13 other t-words (1× each). **8/16 t-words are at LF** — these are t-opened lines where the second word is ot-class. The ot-words are diverse (otal, otchl, otchoky, otol, oty, otar, otaiin, etc.).

**The t→ot=1.85× enrichment is driven by t@LF opening lines that have ot-words at second position** (8 of 16 cases). This is a new minor line-opening construction: [t@LF][ot@second]. Folios: spread across A-herbal (f1-f58), A-astro (f68), A-cosmo (f87), A-pharma (f90).

## Summary of New Findings (F172)

1. **k→cth=18 cases (1.37×), all unique word pairs** (H335 CONFIRMED): k-onset is the 6th enriched onset class for cth-introduction in A-dialect. No fixed formula; k-words in MID position (67% of cases) precede diverse cth-words. kchor is the most common k-word (3 cases).

2. **B-DIALECT cth VOCABULARY DOMINATED BY B-SUFFIX FORMS** (H337 CONFIRMED — MAJOR): cthedy (10×), cthey (10×), cthdy (6×) are the top B-dialect cth words. cthedy takes the canonical B-suffix (-edy), cthdy takes -dy. The cth- prefix is NOT A-exclusive — it appears in B-dialect with B-suffix paradigm. This challenges F047's characterization of cth as "A-exclusive." B-cth appears in all B-sections (herbal=18, bio=17, stars=15). f46 is the B-herbal folio most concentrated in cth.

3. **cho specifically precedes r-onset in A** (H336 CONFIRMED): 6/15 ch→r pairs are cho→r-word. `cho` is the dominant ch-word before r-onset. The bare `r` token specifically introduces ch/ch_e words in both directions (r→ch=8 cases, r→ch_e=6 cases).

4. **t→ot=1.85×: 8/16 cases from t@LF → ot@second** (H334 CONFIRMED): t-opened lines have ot-words at second position at enriched rate. New minor line-opening construction: [t@LF][ot@second].

5. **Expanded cth introduction model**: cth-self (1.62×), k (1.37×), d (1.36×), ch (1.27×), qo (1.22×), sh (1.12×) all enriched. qok (0.51×) and s (0.43×) are DEPLETED before cth — qok and s never introduce cth. cth self-succession (n=23) is a significant minor cth-introduction source.
