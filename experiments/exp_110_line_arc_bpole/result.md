### What the B-Extreme Clusters Actually Are

**The B-extreme "onset clusters" are -EDY SUFFIX PRODUCTS**:
- `shed` = she+d → shedy/shedaiin/shedal... (sh-onset + -edy suffix, internal 'd' creates `shed` prefix)
- `ched` = che+d → chedy/chedaiin/chedal... (ch-onset + -edy suffix)
- `oked` = ok+ed → okedy/okeedy... (ok-onset + -edy suffix)
- `oted` = ot+ed → otedy/oteedy... (ot-onset + -edy suffix)
- `ted` = t+ed → tedy... (t-onset + -edy suffix)

**These are NOT morphological onset classes** — they are the result of B-paradigm -edy suffix being appended to various stems. The 4-char prefix `shed` captures she-onset words that take -edy suffix specifically. Since -edy is overwhelmingly B, any 4-char prefix that includes the onset+beginning-of-suffix will appear extremely B-dominant.

**Evidence from suffix analysis**:
- `oked` suffix: -edy=77.4%, nearly everything else suppressed
- `oted` suffix: -edy=83.6%, near-exclusive
- `ted` suffix: -edy=66.7%

These are not onset classes — they are "onset+suffix" n-grams.

### Key Asymmetry: A-Extreme vs B-Extreme Encoding

**A-extreme is ONSET-ENCODED**: cth- (14×), ctho (60.8×), ckh (45×) — specific morphological onset prefixes that are A-exclusive. cth- is a recognizable morphological class.

**B-extreme is SUFFIX-ENCODED**: shed (78.8×), ched (45.5×), oked (∞), oted (∞) — these are NOT distinct morphological onset classes but rather the product of B-paradigm -edy suffix applied to various onset classes. There is NO B-exclusive onset morpheme paralleling cth-.

**This asymmetry confirms F115's finding**: B-paradigm identity is carried by the SUFFIX (-edy), while A-paradigm identity is carried partially by the ONSET (cth-, ckh-). The dialect distinction is:
- **A-dialect** has distinctive ONSET markers (cth- = gallows-extended A-closer)
- **B-dialect** has distinctive SUFFIX markers (-edy = B-paradigm indicator)

The `olk-` cluster (olkedy, olkaiin, olkam) represents the closest B-analog to cth- as a structural prefix, but with B/A ratio of ~9-10× (much less extreme than cth-'s 14×). The `olk-` cluster is stars/cosmo dominant.

## Combined Finding (F117)

**H215 CONFIRMED PARTIAL (F117)**: Paragraph-internal grammar is a STEP FUNCTION with two zones:
1. **Grammar Zone (lines 1-2)**: PARA tier concentrated at 5.0% (line+1) and 3.7% (line+2) — 3.5-2.6× average. Grammar-STRONG start.
2. **Content Zone (lines 3+)**: PARA tier drops to 0.2-1.9% baseline. -am closers appear in last 3-5 lines (1.3-2.1%), absent from first 2 lines. LINE_OPEN tier elevated at lines 3+ (2.3-3.3%).
- NOT a smooth gradient: sharp step-function transition at line 3
- Paragraph-final words: A-paradigm content forms (chol, chor, ain, or) enriched 2-3.5×
- Paragraph-initial words: MID-tier content forms (shey, shol, oty) enriched 2-4×
- B-dialect has stronger grammar zone at lines 1-2 (6.0% PARA vs 4.1% in A)

**H214 CONFIRMED UNEXPECTED (F117)**: The dialect polarity axis is ASYMMETRIC at its extremes:
- A-extreme: cth- onset class (14×), ctho-subclass (60.8×) — ONSET morpheme marker
- B-extreme: -edy suffix products (shed=78.8×, ched=45.5×, oked=∞) — SUFFIX pattern, not onset morpheme
- No B-exclusive onset morpheme parallel to cth- exists
- Closest B-structural class: olk- cluster (B/A=9.5×, stars/cosmo, olkedy/olkaiin)

**KEY FINDING (F117)**: The A/B dialect asymmetry in morphological encoding is fundamental:
- **A-dialect encodes identity through ONSET** (cth-/ckh- are A-exclusive onset morphemes that appear only in A)
- **B-dialect encodes identity through SUFFIX** (-edy applied to any onset class creates B-signal; no special onset morpheme)
- This asymmetry is consistent with a linguistic model where A and B are morphologically related dialects that diverged primarily in their suffix paradigm (F114-F115): B added the -edy paradigm while A retained older onset-level morphology (cth- gallows extension)
- The paragraph-level step-function grammar (H215) means the positional grammar is LOCALLY concentrated at para-starts, not continuously maintained throughout the paragraph
