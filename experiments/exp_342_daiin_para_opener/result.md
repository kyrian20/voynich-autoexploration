# exp_342: Is B-Dialect daiin a Paragraph/Folio-Opening Formula Word? (H544)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H544 — B-dialect daiin LF enrichment (E=2.57×) is driven by daiin appearing specifically as the first word of paragraphs/folios.
**Result:** H544 REFUTED. B-dialect daiin appears at LF of folio-FIRST lines at rate 0/73 (0.0%); continuation lines show 87/2316 (3.8%, E≈2.97×). daiin specifically AVOIDS folio-opening line positions. The LF enrichment is driven by continuation lines within folios, not folio/paragraph openers. A-dialect shows the same pattern: first-line daiin LF = 0.8% (1/121); continuation lines = 5.1% (80/1554). daiin is a CONTINUATION-LINE LF formula — it opens lines WITHIN folios/paragraphs, not at folio boundaries.

## Findings

### Finding 1: B-dialect daiin AVOIDS folio-first lines (0/73 = 0.0%)

Not a single B-dialect folio opens with daiin as the first word. This is a strong constraint: daiin is specifically excluded from the folio-opening line formula. Instead, daiin appears within folios as a line-first word on continuation lines (3.8% of continuation lines start with daiin).

### Finding 2: daiin is a CONTINUATION-LINE LF formula

The daiin LF enrichment (overall E=2.57×) is driven entirely by continuation lines (E≈2.97× within continuation). Folio-first lines use different formula words for LF. This suggests that the VMS line-first position has at least two registers: (1) folio-opener LF (other words, not daiin) and (2) continuation LF (daiin-enriched).

### Finding 3: A-dialect shows the same folio-opener exclusion pattern

A-dialect: folio-first daiin LF = 0.8% (1/121); continuation = 5.1% (80/1554). The LL-enrichment of A-dialect daiin (F341, 1.37×) may similarly be concentrated in continuation lines. The folio-opener exclusion is cross-dialect conserved.

---

## Interpretation

daiin's LF function is "mid-folio continuation opener" not "folio/paragraph opener." This is consistent with a model where daiin serves as a section-continuation formula (like "furthermore" or "also" in natural language) appearing at the start of middle lines.
