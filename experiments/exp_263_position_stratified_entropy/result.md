# exp_263: Position-Stratified Character Entropy (LF/MID/LL)

**Date**: 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis**: H464 — Separating by line position (LF/MID/LL) should show LF has stronger onset constraint (lower pos-0 entropy) and LL has stronger suffix constraint (lower last-k entropy).
**Method**: Positional entropy h(c_i) computed separately for LF, MID, and LL word pools in A- and B-dialect. VMS corpus only (no simulation).

## Interpretation

### Finding 1: MID Words Are the Most Constrained at Both Onset and Suffix Zones
MID words have the lowest pos-0 entropy (A: 2.8353, B: 2.8804) AND the lowest last-1 entropy (A: 2.4343, B: 2.0800) across both dialects. This contradicts the hypothesis direction: neither LF onset constraint nor LL suffix constraint is stronger than MID.

The mechanism: MID words are generated by the copy buffer, which heavily recycles onset classes. In A-dialect, ch-onset (starts with 'c') constitutes 31.3% of MID pos-0 vs 6.9% at LF — buffer cycling pushes c/ch dominance. Similarly, y-suffix terminal (38–49%) dominates MID because buffer copies preserve suffix patterns.

### Finding 2: LF Is More Diverse, Not More Constrained
LF has the highest pos-0 entropy in both dialects (A: 2.9777, B: 2.9793). The LF cipher (cipher_lf using onset_lf distribution) draws from a more uniform onset distribution — y, s, d, o, q are all 13–18% at A-LF. The LF onset enrichment pattern (y, s, t enriched as positional signal) increases LF diversity rather than reducing it, because these onset classes are adding to LF where ch/qok would otherwise dominate.

### Finding 3: LL -am Adds Diversity, Not Constraint
The LL -am suffix (8.18% A, 12.64% B) introduces 'm' as a terminal character where MID has near-zero 'm'. This elevates LL last-1 entropy above MID, not below. The LL suffix grammar is strongly position-specific (F037, F258) but its character-level effect is diversity-increasing.

### Finding 4: B-LL Has Highest Onset Diversity of All Strata
B-LL pos-0 = 3.0275, highest among all 6 strata. B-LL onset distribution: o=27.4%, q=14.4%, c=14.2%, l=11.0%, a=8.5%, d=8.5% — notably, 'l' (ol-onset) and 'a' are elevated at B-LL relative to B-MID, reflecting B-dialect LL onset grammar that differs from MID. The LL generation mechanism draws from a different onset distribution than MID, producing higher diversity rather than onset-class concentration.

### Architecture Validation
This result is consistent with the copy-buffer architecture: the buffer concentrates on a few onset classes (ch, qok in A-MID) via self-succession and onset-Markov rules, creating the low-entropy MID signature. LF and LL escape this concentration via dedicated generation mechanisms (cipher_lf, cipher_ll). The entropy stratification is explained by mechanism, not by intrinsic positional grammar.

## Limitations

- Paragraph lines only (ltype @P). Header lines and other types excluded.

- Small LF/LL strata at later positions (N<100 at pos 9-10); results there have noise.

## Conclusion

H464 NEGATIVE (hypothesis direction wrong). Position stratification reveals that MID words are the most constrained at both onset (pos-0) and suffix (last-1) zones in both dialects. LF words have the most diverse onset distribution; LL words have elevated 'm' terminal from -am suffix (which increases diversity relative to y-dominated MID). This pattern is fully consistent with the copy-buffer architecture: MID words recycle buffer content (concentrating ch/qok onsets and y-endings), while LF and LL use dedicated generation mechanisms. The hypothesis that "LF has stronger onset constraint" was incorrect — onset enrichment at LF (y, s, t) represents a diversification relative to MID's ch/qo concentration, not an additional constraint.
