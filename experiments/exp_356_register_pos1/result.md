## Findings

### Finding 1: t-Onset Excluded from Folio-Opener pos1 in Both Dialects (Cross-Dialect Grammar Rule)

t=0/121 (A) and 0/73 (B) at folio-opener pos1. This extends the previously-found paragraph-level t-exclusion (t=0/258 at paragraph-first line pos=1, from paragraph grammar analysis) to the folio level. The t-exclusion at pos1 is a universal grammar rule: after any LF word (whether folio-opener or paragraph-opener), t-onset never appears at the second word position.

### Finding 2: B-Dialect Excludes p AND k from Folio-Opener pos1 (p=0/73, k=0/73)

B-dialect folio-opener pos1: p=0/73=0.0%, k=0/73=0.0%, t=0/73=0.0%. After the p/k gallows LF word (B folio-opener LF is 44% p-onset, 11% k-onset), the next word is categorically non-{p,k,t}. Only f (4.1%) is the non-excluded gallows at pos1. B folio-opener pos1 is dominated by o=15.1%, sh=15.1%, qok=13.7%, ch=12.3%, d=11% — a diverse, non-gallows profile. This means the B folio-opener two-word sequence is: {p-gallows or k-gallows LF} → {o/sh/qok/ch/d at pos1}.

### Finding 3: A-Dialect Folio-Opener pos1 = ch+sh Balanced (25.6%+25.6%=51%)

After the A folio-opener gallows LF word (p/k/t/f dominant), the second word is ch or sh at exactly equal rates (25.6% each). This is distinct from A-dialect continuation pos1 (ch=27.8%, sh=15.0%, ratio=1.86) where ch dominates. The folio-opener second word grammar: A = ch+sh balanced; B = o/sh/qok/ch/d diverse.

### Finding 4: B-Dialect Folio-Closer pos1 sh Also Depleted (H564 CONFIRMED, E=0.49×)

B folio-closer pos1: sh=4.7% vs cont 16.8%, E=0.49× (depleted). H564 CONFIRMED: both dialects show sh depletion at folio-closer pos1, but with different magnitudes (A: E=0.16×, B: E=0.49×). B folio-closer ch at pos1 = 18.6% vs cont 19.3% (not elevated — unlike A where ch is elevated to 37.3%). The B folio-closer anti-sh at pos1 is primarily sh-depletion without ch-elevation.

### Finding 5: B-Dialect sh "Migrates" from pos1 to LL in Folio-Closer

B folio-closer: sh depleted at pos1 (4.7%, E=0.49×) but enriched at LL (14.6%, E=3.45×). The sh-at-LL signal in B folio-closer comes from sh being absent from early positions (LF: 2.1%, pos1: 4.7%) and concentrated at the final position (LL: 14.6%). The folio-closer appears to "save" sh for the final word position. This contrasts with A-dialect where sh is depleted at both pos1 AND is near-baseline at LL (E=1.01×).

### Finding 6: Cross-Register ch/sh Ratio Gradient Is a Structural Signature

The ch/sh ratio at pos1 shows a cross-dialect gradient across registers:
- **A**: opener=1.00 → cont=1.86 → closer=15.50 (monotonic increase)
- **B**: opener=0.82 → cont=1.15 → closer=2.00 (monotonic increase)

Both dialects show the same direction: folio-opener pos1 is the most ch/sh-balanced position, while folio-closer pos1 is the most ch-dominant (least sh). The opener is the most "even" register; the closer is the most ch-specialized.
