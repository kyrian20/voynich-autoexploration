New: A-dialect ch→d-class at MID is near-baseline (E=0.98×); the ch→daiin MID elevation (E=1.19×) is DAIIN-SPECIFIC (d-other depleted to compensate). A ch at LF→d-class at pos1 E=1.57× (new LF-position ch→d coupling).

## Findings

### Finding 1: A-Dialect ch→daiin Is POSITION-GENERAL (E=1.13-1.43× at All Positions)

A-dialect ch→daiin is elevated at every position category: LF→pos1=1.30×, pos1→pos2=1.13×, MID_inner=1.19×, pre_LL→LL=1.43×. The pre-LL position is the peak (1.43×) but the rule operates everywhere. This is a general bigram rule, not a positional formula. Architecture implication: ch→daiin should be modeled as a position-independent bigram rule (with possible pre-LL amplification), not as a pre-LL-specific positional constraint.

### Finding 2: B-Dialect ch→daiin Is POSITION-SPECIFIC (MID and pre_LL Only)

B-dialect ch→daiin at LF→pos1=1.07× and pos1→pos2=1.08× (both near-baseline). Only MID_inner (E=1.54×) and pre_LL→LL (E=1.68×) are elevated. In B-dialect, the ch→daiin rule is active in the middle of lines and at line end, but not at line beginning. This is consistent with B-dialect daiin being a LF-formula word (F341: daiin LF=2.57×) — daiin rarely follows ch at LF because daiin is ALREADY enriched there by a separate LF formula. The B-dialect position-specificity of ch→daiin is a downstream effect of daiin's competing LF grammar.

### Finding 3: A-Dialect ch→d-class at MID Is Near-Baseline (E=0.98×); ch→daiin MID Elevation Is DAIIN-SPECIFIC

A-dialect: ch→d-class at MID_inner E=0.98× (near-baseline). But ch→daiin at MID E=1.19×. This apparent contradiction resolves: ch→daiin at MID is elevated, but ch→d-other-words at MID is DEPLETED — the two effects cancel at the class level. So the ch→d enrichment at MID is specifically a ch→daiin effect, not a broader ch→d-class rule. Contrast with pre_LL and LF positions where ch→d-class is elevated (1.16×, 1.57×).

### Finding 4: A-Dialect ch at LF Predicts d-Class at pos1 (E=1.57×)

When ch-onset words appear at LF position in A-dialect continuation lines, the next word (pos1) is d-class at E=1.57×: 15/109=13.8% vs baseline 8.8%. This LF-position ch→d coupling is new. Given that A-dialect LF is 17.1% d-onset overall (F368: d LF E=1.44×) and ch LF is depleted (0.34×), this pattern says: when ch does appear at LF (uncommon), it predicts d-onset at pos1 more than baseline.

### Finding 5: ch-Onset Successor Profile — Only d-Class and Self-Succession Are Elevated; sh Always Depleted

A-dialect: ch successor elevations: d=1.15× (daiin=1.30×, d-other=10.9%), ch=1.22× (self). Depletions: sh=0.72×, ok=0.82×, qok=0.81×, ol=0.81×, y=0.77×. B-dialect: d=1.23×, ch=0.94×; sh=0.60× (strong depletion). ch-onset words predict more ch-onset words (A) or more d-onset words (B) as successors. The consistent ch→sh depletion across dialects (F328 bidirectional avoidance) is confirmed (A=0.72×, B=0.60×).

## Summary

H582 ANSWERED. A-dialect ch→daiin = POSITION-GENERAL (E=1.13-1.43× at all 4 positions, peaking at pre_LL=1.43×). B-dialect ch→daiin = POSITION-SPECIFIC (MID=1.54×, pre_LL=1.68×; LF and pos1 near-baseline, because daiin's B LF-formula status creates a competing constraint). New: A-dialect ch→d-class at MID E=0.98× (near-baseline) — the MID ch→daiin elevation is daiin-specific, not d-class-wide. A ch at LF→d-class at pos1: E=1.57× (new LF coupling). Full ch-successor profile: d=1.15×, self=1.22×; sh depleted (0.72×/0.60×); all others near baseline. Architecture: model A ch→daiin as a position-general bigram rule (implement at all positions); B ch→daiin as MID+pre_LL-specific.
