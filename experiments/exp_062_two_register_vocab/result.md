# Experiment exp_062: Vocabulary Differences in Two-Register Herbal A Folios
Date: 2026-04-09

## Question (H068)
Do the aiin-dominant herbal A folios differ in vocabulary from y-dominant folios — or only in positional grammar?

## Key Finding: The Two Registers Are Lexically Almost as Distinct as Currier A vs Currier B

**Vocabulary overlap (Jaccard): 0.138**

## Onset Distribution: y-Initial Words Are Globally Suppressed

The most dramatic difference is in onset distribution:

| Onset | Aiin-dom% | y-dom% | Ratio |
|-------|----------|--------|-------|
| d | 18.5% | 11.5% | **1.61×** |
| ch | 26.0% | 17.3% | **1.50×** |
| sh | 10.8% | 7.1% | **1.52×** |
| **y** | **2.0%** | **9.3%** | **0.21×** |
| o | 12.4% | 19.8% | 0.63× |

**y-initial words are suppressed 5× across all positions in aiin-dominant folios** — not just at line-starts. This means y-initial words belong to a different register vocabulary, not just a different line-position preference.

## Interpretation

The two registers represent distinct vocabularies, not just positional grammar conventions:

1. **y-dominant register**: uses y-initial words pervasively (9.3% of ALL tokens), o-onset words, 'or' particle (4.04× enriched), compound forms like odaiin, qodaiin, shodaiin
2. **aiin-dominant register**: uses d-onset (1.61×) and ch-onset (1.50×) words, avoids y-initial globally (0.21×), concentrates on chaiin, dain, chey, chy forms

The vocabulary Jaccard (0.138) being near the A/B inter-dialect Jaccard (0.148) suggests the two registers may be more accurately described as **sub-dialects within Currier A Herbal**, not just scribal style variants. The registers differ in morphological class preferences, word composition, and line-level grammar.

## H068 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED

The aiin-dominant herbal A folios are lexically distinct from y-dominant folios at the vocabulary level, not just positionally. Jaccard=0.138 is near-identical to the A/B dialect gap. The core difference: y-initial words are globally suppressed in aiin-dominant folios (0.21× across all positions), while d-onset words are enriched (1.61×). The two registers have different word inventories, suggesting different scribal authors or traditions rather than just different grammatical stylistic preferences.

## Finding (F069)
Two herbal A sub-dialects with vocabulary Jaccard = 0.138 (near-identical to the A/B inter-dialect gap of 0.148): (1) y-dominant register: 9.3% y-initial words, enriched o-onset (1.59×), 'or' particle (4.04×), compound forms odaiin/shodaiin; (2) aiin-dominant register: y-initial globally suppressed (0.21×), d-onset enriched (1.61×), ch-onset enriched (1.50×), chaiin (11.75×), daiin (2.01×). The vocabulary difference extends beyond positional grammar to the entire word inventory, with 447 aiin-dominant exclusive types and 781 y-dominant exclusive types. This near-dialect-level lexical distinction combined with the quire clustering (Quires D and F are predominantly aiin-dominant) and the parallel grammatical register (competing line-continuation strategies: y-initial vs -aiin) suggests these two registers represent distinct scribal traditions within Currier A Herbal — possibly a different scribe who wrote quires D and F using an alternative morphological convention for expressing the same underlying grammatical categories.
