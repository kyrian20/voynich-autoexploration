### Interpretation

**sh-onset is NOT a pure line-opener.** Its LFirst% (A=20.7%, B=13.0%) is moderate — far below s-onset (45.9%/61.3%) or y-onset (40.7%/44.4%). The true line-opening onsets in both dialects are s and y (by LFirst%).

**The -am suppression for sh is better explained by low LLast%** (A=11.0%, B=6.8%) than by a grammatical incompatibility with -am. Since -am words are predominantly line-final (see H145 below), onsets that rarely appear at line-end naturally have low -am rates. Correlation between LLast% and AM%:
- d-onset: LLast=30.5%, AM=3.6% (high-high)
- ch-onset: LLast=17.0%, AM=1.1% (med-low)
- sh-onset: LLast=11.0%, AM=1.0% (low-low)

sh-onset IS enriched at PSFirst% in A (4.4% — highest among named onsets), confirming the para-start enrichment from F090. But this enrichment is about PARA-START position, not about being a general "line-opener."

The causal chain is: sh-words appear at para-starts (4.4× enriched) and have lower LLast% because they cluster at line-beginnings → they cannot take -am (a line-ender marker) → AM% suppressed for sh.

### H144 Verdict: PARTIALLY REFUTED — sh-Onset Is Not a Pure Line-Opener; -am Suppression Explained by Low LLast% (Not Grammatical Incompatibility)

sh-onset has moderate LFirst% (not dominant), not "pure line-opening." The -am suppression for sh is mechanistically explained by sh's low line-last rate (6.8-11%) rather than a direct incompatibility. The true line-opening onsets by LFirst% are s (61%/46%) and y (44%/41%). sh-onset is enriched at para-starts (PSFirst=4.4% in A) but not dominant as a general line-opener.

### Functional Word Classification

The data reveals a clear **three-tier within-para function-word system**:

**Tier 1 — PURE WITHIN-PARA LINE-CLOSERS (-am class):**
- {otam, qokam, dam, okam, cham, ram} — LLast% = 50-100%, WithinPara% = 90-100%, PSFirst% = 0%, PFLast% ≈ 0%
- These words mark WITHIN-PARAGRAPH line endings; never appear at para-starts; rarely at para-finals

**Tier 2 — PURE WITHIN-PARA LINE-CLOSERS (non-am):**
- {oly, oldy} in B — LLast% = 78-89%, WithinPara% = 93-97%, PSFirst% = 0%
- Same functional profile as -am class; different morphological form

**Tier 3 — PARA-BOUNDARY MARKER:**
- daiin in A — LLast% = 25.9%, PFLast% = 3.4%, LFirst% = 19.4% — spans multiple functions
- daiin in B — LFirst% = 34.0%, LLast% = 9.5% — behaves as LINE-OPENER in B!

**Critical observation**: daiin in Currier B is a LINE-OPENER (LFirst=34%), not a line-closer! This is opposite to daiin's role in Currier A (LFirst=19.4%, LLast=25.9%). The two dialects use daiin in completely different positional roles.

### H145 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — -am Core Words Are a Closed Class of Within-Para Line-Closers; Distinct From daiin (Para-Boundary) and Complementary to oly/oldy (Non-am Line-Closers)

The within-para line-closing function word class has two morphological sub-groups: {-am words: otam/qokam/dam/okam/cham/ram} and {oly/oldy}. Both have LLast%=73-100% and WithinPara%=90-100%. daiin in A is a para-boundary particle (spans line-final and para-final roles); daiin in B is a line-OPENER (LFirst=34%). The -am class and oly/oldy are functionally synonymous within-para line-closers occupying the same grammatical slot.
