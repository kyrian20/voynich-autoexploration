# exp_430: A-Dialect p-LF → 'opch' MID Embedding Cross-Dialectal Check

**Hypothesis (H648):** Does the A-dialect show the same p-LF → opch MID enrichment as B (F427: E=5.28×)?

**Method:** A and B dialect continuation lines. A-dialect: folio edy+eedy rate <5%. B-dialect: rate ≥5%. For each dialect, separate p-LF and non-p-LF lines. Count 'opch'-prefix and 'op'-prefix words at MID. Compare enrichment across dialects.

---

## Results

### B-dialect (confirmatory)

| Prefix | p-LF rate | non-p-LF rate | E | N_p |
|--------|-----------|---------------|---|-----|
| opch | 2.70% | 0.50% | **5.38×** | 40 |
| op | 4.04% | 0.67% | **6.05×** | 60 |
| o-onset MID | 6.87% | 3.40% | **2.02×** | — |

B-dialect opch vocab in p-LF MID: opchedy(13), opchdy(7), opchey(6), opchedaiin(2)

### A-dialect

| Prefix | p-LF rate | non-p-LF rate | E | N_p |
|--------|-----------|---------------|---|-----|
| opch | 2.31% | 0.16% | **14.58×** | **3** |
| op | 3.08% | 0.36% | **8.64×** | **4** |
| o-onset MID | 6.15% | 4.00% | 1.54× | — |

A-dialect opch vocab in p-LF MID (N_lines=35): opchey(2), opchaldy(1)

**A-dialect p-LF lines: N=35; A-dialect MID tokens in p-LF: N=130.**

### Cross-dialect comparison

| Metric | A-dialect | B-dialect |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| p-LF lines | 35 | 224 |
| Non-p-LF MID background | 0.16% opch | 0.50% opch |
| p-LF opch rate | 2.31% | 2.70% |
| p-LF opch E | 14.58× | 5.38× |

The higher A-dialect E reflects the lower A background (0.16% vs 0.50%), not necessarily stronger A signal — the raw rate (2.31% vs 2.70%) is comparable.

---

## Interpretation

**H648 ANSWERED: p-LF → opch enrichment IS PRESENT in A-dialect, but N=3 is completely directional.**

Both dialects show p-LF MID concentration of opch/op compounds:
- B: E=5.38×/N=40 (statistically meaningful)
- A: E=14.58×/N=3 (directional only)

The A raw rate (2.31%) is similar to B (2.70%), consistent with a cross-dialectal mechanism. The higher A E-value (14.58× vs 5.38×) is driven by the lower A background opch rate (0.16% vs 0.50%) — A-dialect has fewer opch compounds overall, making the p-LF concentration more striking in relative terms but based on only 3 instances.

**Conclusion**: The p-formula self-referential structure (p-gallows at LF + p-embedded 'opch' compounds at MID) appears to be a CROSS-DIALECTAL feature of p-formula lines. A-dialect p-LF lines produce opch compounds at MID at a rate comparable to B-dialect p-LF lines (~2.3-2.7%), despite A-dialect having a much lower background opch rate. This suggests the opch MID structure is tied to the p-formula line type independent of dialect.

The o-onset MID enrichment is weaker in A (E=1.54× vs B 2.02×), consistent with A's weaker global o/qo structure (A has less B-dialect's o-enrichment pattern in p-formula lines).

---

## Limitations

- A-dialect: N=3 opch instances in 35 p-LF lines, 130 MID tokens — completely directional.
- A p-LF lines (N=35) are far fewer than B (N=224) due to A-dialect size.
- A background rate (0.16%) is much lower than B (0.50%), which inflates the A enrichment ratio.
- The two A opch words (opchey×2 + opchaldy×1) are different from the dominant B types (opchedy/opchdy), suggesting possible vocabulary differences.
