## Key Finding — ot→ot Lexical Analysis (H448)

**ot→ot is grammatical, not lexical co-clustering.** HHI/null=1.23× — near-random, comparable to sh→qok (1.29×) and qok→ch (1.39×).

- Total ot→ot MID bigrams: 113
- Unique ot→ot pairs: 93
- HHI=0.0132, null=0.0108, ratio=**1.23×**
- Top-5 pairs: 11.5% of total (similar to qok→ch's 11.5%)
- After removing top-10 pairs: 77.9% remain

The ot→ot enrichment (E2=1.887) comes from dispersed vocabulary rather than specific repeated pairs. This is the same structure as sh→qok and qok→ch. H448 CONFIRMED: ot→ot is a genuine grammatical rule.

**ot→ot is concentrated in the "stars" section (N=88/113, 78%).** In the bio section, ot→ot E2=1.354 (N=12, still enriched). The grammar rule exists across sections but is most expressed in the stars/astrological text.

## Interpretation

The stars section (f103–117, astrological text with one diagram per line) has the strongest onset succession grammar for both sh→qok and ot→ot. This section's highly regular one-line-one-diagram structure — where each line is approximately 5–8 words — may produce more consistent onset grammar effects through the copy-buffer mechanism (short lines allow onset-succession rules to dominate over other variation sources).

The bio section (f75–84, bathing women) has high sh→qok (1.832) and qok→ch (1.363) enrichment with large N (165 and 127 pairs), confirming that onset succession grammar is present in the biological section, not only in stars.

The cosmo section (f85–86, cosmological rosettes) shows near-random values for all onset pairs but has small N — this is inconclusive. The cosmological rosette pages are visually distinct (radial text around circles) and may have genuinely different structural grammar, but N is too small to confirm.

**Theory implications:**
- The grammar rules (sh→qok, qok→ch, ot→ot) are NOT uniformly distributed across the manuscript. They are concentrated in specific sections.
- If VMS is a copy-buffer production system: sections with shorter, more regular lines (stars) would naturally show stronger buffer-mediated onset succession. The copy buffer's window-20 memory operates most effectively when lines are short and consistent.
- If VMS encodes different content by section: the stars section's stronger grammar may reflect a more systematic rule system in that section's content (e.g., a fixed astrological enumeration).
- Both theories predict section-level variation in grammar intensity; this result does not discriminate between them.

## Limitations

- Section boundaries are approximate folio ranges (f1-57, f103-117, etc.) — boundary folios may be misclassified.
- cosmo and pharma sections have N=3–9 for key pairs — E2 estimates unreliable.
- astro section (51 lines) has marginal N for most pairs (N=6–7).
- y→y analysis is too sparse for any section-level claim (max N=6 per section).
- Bio section qok→qok=0.909 (E2 below 1.0) is surprising but may reflect compositional differences (bio text may have different qok-onset vocabulary that creates natural depletion).

## Conclusion

H451 CONFIRMED: Onset succession grammar is section-specific. sh→qok and ot→ot are dominated by the stars section (f103–117, 45% of B-dialect), which shows the strongest enrichment (sh→qok=2.155, ot→ot=1.900). qok→ch is more uniformly enriched across sections (herbal 1.431, bio 1.363, stars 1.234). cosmo and pharma show near-random values but have insufficient N for conclusions.

H448 CONFIRMED: ot→ot is a grammatical rule (HHI/null=1.23×, 93 unique pairs, dispersed vocabulary), structurally identical to sh→qok (1.29×) and qok→ch (1.39×). The ot→ot mechanism gap identified in exp_246 is a genuine grammatical under-production, not a lexical artifact. Investigation of an ot→ot Markov mechanism is warranted (H447 remains open).

New finding: qok→qok MID enrichment is section-specific (stars=1.358, bio=0.909). The overall B-dialect qok→qok=1.169 masks opposite trends in different sections. This may explain why qok→qok is harder to model than qok→ch — it is not a universal B-dialect grammar rule.
