(4) Cross-onset B0 character matrix shows THREE DISTINCT GROUPS: {d,s}='a'-primary; {qok,ch,sh,ok}='e'-primary; {y}=gallows-primary. These groups are qualitatively different character sets.

## Four Major Findings

### Finding 1: d-onset Body Sequence Traces the daiin Word

d-onset body positions B0-B3 (B-dialect): a=73% → i=40% → i=42% → n=49%

This is the word "daiin" parsed as: d(onset) + a(B0) + i(B1) + i(B2) + n(B3). The character sequence is almost completely determined by the daiin template:
- B0='a': the 'a' in d-a-iin (73% dominant)
- B1='i': the first 'i' in daiin (40%), with 'l' (25%) and 'r' (21%) from dal/dar/dar forms
- B2='i': the second 'i' in d-a-i-in (42%), with 'n' (12%) from shorter d+a+i+n sequences
- B3='n': the terminal 'n' in d-a-i-i-n (49%)

A-dialect is even more concentrated: d B3='n' at 64.4% (vs B's 49%). The d-onset body entropy constraint extends 4 positions deep (B0-B3) in A because daiin-like forms are even more dominant there (consistent with F297: daiin=32.5% of A-d tokens vs 21.7% of B-d tokens).

The d→-aiin constraint (F289) operates at the character level: it's not just that d-onset takes the '-aiin' suffix — the character sequence a+i+i+n appears position by position starting at B0.

**Additional d-onset word patterns visible:**
- B0='a' then B1='l' (25%) → 'dal'-type words (d + al suffix direct)
- B0='a' then B1='r' (21%) → 'dar'-type words
- B0='y' (10%) → 'dy'-type words (d + y suffix = zero midfix)
- B0='o' (9.3%) → 'do'-type words (d + o midfix or onset)

### Finding 2: y-onset B0 = 80% Gallows Characters — y-Onset is y+Gallows

y-onset B0 distribution (B-dialect): k=43.8%, t=36.3%, p=7.2%, c=4.6%, f=2.3%

The characters k, t, p, f are the four EVA gallows characters. Combined gallows rate at y-onset B0 = 43.8+36.3+7.2+4.6+2.3 = 94.2% (B-dialect) and 43.1+37.6+6.2+4.6+3.6 = 95.1% (A-dialect). This means y-onset words consist of:
- y (onset) + gallows character (B0 = k/t/p/f/c) + vowel followers (B1 = e=44%, a=25%)

This is a qualitatively different character set from all other onset classes. No other onset class places gallows at B0 — ch/sh/qok use 'e', 'o', 'a'; d uses 'a'; s uses 'a'. The y-onset B0 = gallows means y-onset words are compound structures: y + gallows form a ligature-like unit.

**Interpretation:** y-onset words are y+gallows+body compounds. This explains:
1. Why y-onset is a LINE-FIRST (LF) grammar-word class (F293) — y+gallows compounds may be paragraph-positional markers or formulas, consistent with a grammar-word role
2. Why y-onset B1 entropy is LOWEST at B1 in B-dialect (2.216 bits) — after the gallows at B0, B1 is predictably 'e' (43.8%) or 'a' (25.3%), the standard vowel-followers of gallows in EVA
3. Why y-onset B0 entropy (2.035) is already lower than average — limited to 4-5 gallows characters, not the full EVA alphabet

The 'c' at B0 (4.6%) may represent 'ch' as a compound gallows (y+ch forms like ychaiin, ychedy).

### Finding 3: A-Dialect vs B-Dialect B0 Reversal for ch/sh

**B-dialect**: ch B0 = e=60%, o=13%; sh B0 = e=73%, o=10% (e-primary)
**A-dialect**: ch B0 = o=48%, e=30%; sh B0 = o=45%, e=39% (o-primary)

A-dialect ch/sh onset words begin with 'o' as the primary B0 character; B-dialect uses 'e'. This is a cross-dialect reversal specific to ch/sh onsets.

In EVA structure, this means:
- B-dialect ch-words: chedy, cheedy, cheol, cheaiin... (ch+e+suffix) are dominant
- A-dialect ch-words: chol, chor, chody, chotol... (ch+o+suffix) are dominant

This matches exp_319's finding that A-qok uses 'ol' suffix (20.7% of A-qok vs 1.9% in B) and B-qok uses 'edy' (14.0% in B) — A-dialect prefers 'o'-body forms while B-dialect prefers 'e'-body forms. The B0='o' in A ch/sh is the same pattern: A body uses 'o' where B uses 'e' as the primary midfix/body character.

The **B0 group structure** by dialect:

| B-dialect group | Onsets | Primary B0 |
|----------------|--------|------------|
| e-dominant | ch, sh, qok (partial) | e=60-73% |
| a-dominant | d, s, ok (partial) | a=63-73% |
| gallows-dominant | y | k+t=80% |

| A-dialect group | Onsets | Primary B0 |
|----------------|--------|------------|
| o-dominant | ch, sh | o=45-48% |
| e-dominant | qok, ok | e=37-39% |
| a-dominant | d, s | a=37-70% |
| gallows-dominant | y | k+t=81% |

The 'o'-primary group for ch/sh in A is a DIALECT MARKER — A-dialect writes ch/sh words with 'o' bodies (chol, chor, chody) while B-dialect uses 'e' bodies (chedy, cheedy, shedy).

### Finding 4: No Exclusive Characters — Distributional Not Categorical Boundary

The cross-onset exclusivity analysis shows ZERO characters that appear ONLY in d-onset body and NOWHERE else (at B0-B2). All characters used by d-onset also appear in at least one other onset class. This rules out a categorical character-class boundary (where onset A uses chars {set1} and onset B uses chars {set2}, disjoint).

Instead, the boundary is DISTRIBUTIONAL: all onset classes draw from the same EVA character set, but with dramatically different frequency profiles. d-onset uses 'a' 73× more than 'e' at B0; sh-onset uses 'e' 41× more than 'a'. The boundary is soft (probabilistic), not hard (categorical).

This is consistent with a morphological paradigm model where characters are shared across classes but their weights differ per class, rather than a strict morpheme-class partition.

## Interpretation

**d-onset body sequence confirms the daiin template as the structural backbone:** The character sequence a(73%), i(40%), i(42%), n(49%) at B0-B3 directly traces the daiin word letter by letter. The non-daiin d-onset words (dal, dar, dy) are smaller in volume but visible at B1 (l=25%, r=21%) and B0 (y=10%). The d→-aiin constraint (F289) is not just a suffix pattern — it's a character-by-character template that determines the body sequence.

**y-onset is a compound-gallows class, not a simple vowel onset:** y+gallows at B0-B1 means y-onset words structurally combine y with a gallows character to form compound formulas. This reframes y-onset as a gallows-compound class rather than a vowel-onset class, explaining its LF grammar-word role and compressed body entropy.

**A/B dialect distinction maps to 'o'-vs-'e' body preference:** The ch/sh B0 reversal (B: e-primary → A: o-primary) is the character-level manifestation of the same A/B distinction seen at the word level (B: chedy/shedy dominant → A: chol/shor dominant). The dialect difference is 'o'-body vs 'e'-body for the dominant suffix-connected midfix.

- The daiin-body tracing (a+i+i+n) is inferential — individual word-type confirmation would require word-type inventory
