# exp_328: ok-Onset Body Structure — Bimodal B0 and ok↔qok Coupling (H528)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H528 — Does ok-onset have a two-tier body structure corresponding to its bimodal B0 distribution ('e'=43.5%, 'a'=43.3%)? Is ok a compound onset class overlapping with both qok (template forms) and d-onset (aiin-body doublets)?
**Result:** H528 CONFIRMED — ok is a compound onset class with two structurally distinct sub-classes. (1) ok+'e'-body = template-copying forms (okeedy, okeey, okedy) structurally identical to qok+'e' forms. (2) ok+'a'-body = aiin-doublet forms (okaiin, okain, okar, okal) structurally identical to d-onset words. BONUS: ot-onset shows the same bimodal structure (B0: e=42.6%, a=36.3%). A-dialect ok shifts from 'a'-body (B=43.2%→A=24.3%) toward 'o'-body (B=2.6%→A=17.6%), following A's general 'o'-preference. A-dialect ok↔qok coupling (E=2.035/1.676×) is class-level, driven by ok+'e'-template words preceding qok.

## Findings

### Finding 1: ok = compound onset class with two structural sub-classes

ok-onset splits nearly equally into:
- **ok+'e'-body** (43%): template-copying forms identical in structure to qok+'e' forms (okeedy/qokeedy, okedy/qokedy, okeey/qokeey). The 'ok' onset replaces 'qok'.
- **ok+'a'-body** (43%): aiin-paradigm forms identical in structure to d-onset words (okaiin/daiin, okain/dain, okar/dar, okal/dal). The 'ok' onset replaces 'd'.

ok is the only major onset class simultaneously overlapping two paradigms (template-copying AND aiin-body). This explains the bimodal B0: 'e' signals template-form context, 'a' signals aiin-body context.

### Finding 2: ot-onset is structurally isomorphic to ok

ot shows the same bimodal B0 (e=42.6%, a=36.3%) with the same two-tier word inventory (otedy/oteedy = template forms; otaiin/otar/otain/otal = aiin-doublets). ot and ok form a paired compound class: together they constitute the 'o'-initial template + aiin-body paradigm. The distinction between ok and ot in EVA may correspond to different tokens in the VMS script, but at the structural level they are parallel.

### Finding 3: A-dialect ok de-emphasizes aiin-body, adds 'o'-body

B-dialect ok has a symmetric 43%/43% 'e'/'a' split. A-dialect shifts to 39%/24%/18% ('e'/'a'/'o'): the 'a'-body forms (okaiin, okain) are weaker and the 'o'-body forms (okol, okor) are stronger. This is consistent with A-dialect systematically using 'o'-body where B uses 'a'-body (same pattern for ch/sh: A 'o'-primary, B 'e'/'a'-primary). A-dialect ok+'e'-body also shifts to -ol/-or suffix (okeol, okeor) from B's -edy/-eedy/-ey suffix.

### Finding 4: A-dialect ok↔qok coupling is class-level

The A-dialect bidirectional coupling (E=2.03/1.68×) is driven by ok+'e'-template words preceding qok (okeey 29%, okey 29%), but also includes ok+'a'-body words (okal 14% P(→qok)). This is class-level: different ok sub-classes both participate. The absence of this coupling in B-dialect (ok→qok=0.96×) means the ok↔qok coupling is A-specific — perhaps A-dialect uses ok and qok in the same template-copying paragraph context where B-dialect uses qok alone (qok self-succession and qok→ch patterns).

## Interpretation

The bimodal B0 distribution of ok (and ot) reveals that these onset classes are morphological composites: they span the same vocabulary register as two other onset classes simultaneously. The onset character ('ok' or 'ot') does NOT specify the body class — it can introduce either a template body ('e') or an aiin-paradigm body ('a'). This is structurally unusual: qok is almost exclusively 'e'-body (only 36% 'a'-body), d-onset is exclusively 'a'-body. ok/ot are transition classes.

**Why ok/ot are bimodal**: In the VMS script, 'ok' likely represents an initial combination (o+k) and 'ot' represents (o+t), where 'o' can be followed by a 'benched character' (k or t = gallows). The resulting onset allows both 'e'-body words (where the o is followed by template suffix) and 'a'-body words (where the o-initial word substitutes for a d-initial word). The 'c' at B0 (6.5%) further confirms that ok-initial words sometimes use ch as their first body character (okchedy = template compound).

**A-dialect ok↔qok coupling**: The A-dialect coupling (2.03×) may reflect that A uses ok-onset as a "qok-substitute" in contexts where qok is highly enriched in B. A-dialect qok is larger and more diverse (3× bigger paradigm than B-qok); A-dialect ok participates in the same template-copying context, creating the bidirectional coupling.

## Limitations

- ot was analyzed for B-dialect only; A-dialect ot analysis was not run (would require expanding the script).
- The "class-level" A-dialect ok↔qok coupling claim is based on moderate N (28 ok→qok bigrams in A); per-word P values are noisy.
- The ok+'c'-body words (okchedy, okchey = 6.5%) were not analyzed as a separate sub-class; they may represent a third sub-type (template forms with ch-midfix).
