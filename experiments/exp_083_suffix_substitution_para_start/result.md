## H132: Liquid-Glide Substitution Is a Global Paradigm Shift, Not Concentrated in Specific Word Pairs

### Top Substitution Pairs

| Stem (onset+internal) | Aiin-dom n | Liq% | Gli% | Y-dom n | Liq% | Gli% | Score |
|----------------------|-----------|------|------|---------|------|------|-------|
| o+pch | 3 | 33% | 0% | 9 | 11% | 44% | 0.67 |
| o+ke | 3 | 100% | 0% | 14 | 57% | 21% | 0.64 |
| t+ch | 7 | 57% | 14% | 22 | 23% | 32% | 0.52 |
| **o+k** | **24** | **38%** | 12% | **105** | **12%** | **22%** | **0.35** |
| o+tch | 25 | 48% | 0% | 29 | 34% | 21% | 0.34 |
| o+t | 23 | 39% | 4% | 94 | 18% | 6% | 0.23 |

**Most high-scoring pairs have small n (3-9 in aiin-dom).** The largest reliable pair is o+k (n=24/105): aiin-dom 38% liquid vs y-dom 12% liquid, showing a 3× preference for liquid suffixes in aiin-dom for the same ok- stem. The o+tch (n=25/29) pair also shows consistent liquid preference in aiin-dom (48% vs 34%).

**Key stem pairs (ch and sh onset)** show INCONSISTENT or SMALL patterns for ch+k, ch+ok, ch+ot, sh+k — these specific pairs don't clearly demonstrate the substitution, likely because they are low-frequency in aiin-dom.

### Interpretation

The liquid-to-glide substitution is NOT concentrated in a few high-frequency word pairs — it's DISTRIBUTED across many different stem types at low rates. Each individual stem doesn't have enough aiin-dom tokens to show a clean pair (most aiin-dom stem n < 10). The GLOBAL -ol/-or rate difference (aiin-dom 25.8% vs y-dom 12.3%) observed in exp_082 is the aggregate of many small substitutions across diverse stems, not a few dominant word pairs.

### H132 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — Substitution Is Real But Distributed, Not Word-Pair Concentrated

The o+k stem (okol/okedy/okain...) shows the clearest pattern: aiin-dom 38% liquid vs y-dom 12%. This specific stem preferentially takes -ol/-or in aiin-dom and -edy/-aiin/-ey in y-dom. But most ch/sh stem pairs have insufficient aiin-dom tokens for clear pairwise substitution. The liquid-glide substitution is a REGISTER-LEVEL PARADIGM SHIFT affecting the overall suffix inventory, distributed across many stems rather than driven by a few key word-pair alternations.

**B paragraphs begin with LONG o-onset or sh-onset words ending in -dy/-edy/-ey.** The dominant B para-start words are complex compound-like forms (olfchedy, opchedy, olkey) — not the simple qo- or sh- words that dominate the corpus.

### Word Length at B Para-Starts

B para-start first words are LONGER than average: len=8 occurs 11.9% (vs NPS 5.7%); len=6 is similar; len=3 is suppressed (3.0% vs 8.5%).

### H133 Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED AS STATED — B Para-Starts ARE Morphologically Marked (Opposite Conclusion)

B para-starts are distinctly marked: o-onset (2.42×), sh-onset (1.72×), -dy/-edy/-ey suffixes, long words, and rare specific words (olfchedy, olkey). d/s/y/p/t-onset suppressed (0.00-0.65×). -aiin/-eey suppressed (0.21-0.24×). **Both B para-starts AND B para-finals use o-onset, but with different word profiles**: para-starts use long complex o-onset words (olfchedy, olkey) with -dy/-edy suffix; para-finals use medium o-onset words (okeedy, otaiin) with -eedy/-aiin suffix. The B positional grammar is fully characterized: [o/sh+long-edy-dy PARA-START] → [-am line-ends, 12.6%] → [o+med-eedy-aiin PARA-END].

## H135: A Para-Starts Use sh/ch-Onset; A Para-Finals Use daiin/ch; A -am Is Also Within-Para Only

### A First-Word Onset at Para-Starts

| Onset | PS rate | NPS rate | Enrichment |
|-------|---------|---------|-----------|
| **sh** | **24.1%** | 9.5% | **2.54×** |
| **ch** | **21.6%** | 9.6% | **2.25×** |
| other | 8.6% | 3.1% | **2.79×** |
| qo | 14.8% | 13.2% | 1.13× |
| o | 16.0% | 18.4% | 0.87× |
| **d** | 3.7% | 17.8% | **0.21×** |
| **y** | 5.6% | 14.5% | **0.38×** |
| **t** | 0.0% | 5.1% | **0.00×** |

**A para-starts strongly prefer sh-onset (2.54×) and ch-onset (2.25×) first words.** d-onset (0.21×), y-onset (0.38×), t-onset (0.00×) are suppressed. This is OPPOSITE to B (o-onset 2.42×, d-onset 0.65×) — B starts paragraphs with o-onset; A starts paragraphs with sh/ch-onset.

### daiin Positional Distribution in A

| Position | daiin rate | Interpretation |
|----------|-----------|---------------|
| para-start first word | **0.6%** | **STRONGLY SUPPRESSED** |
| non-para-start first word | 5.3% | baseline |
| within-para last word | 6.2% | slightly enriched |
| para-final last word | **8.6%** | **ENRICHED** |

**daiin is almost completely absent from A para-start positions (0.6%) but enriched at A para-final last words (8.6%).** This creates a clean directional gradient: daiin marks ENDINGS in A, not beginnings.

### A Para-Final Last Words

Top A para-final last words: **daiin (14, 8.6%)**, chol (7, 4.3%), chor (4, 2.5%), or (3, 1.9%), aiin (2), cheey (2), cheol (2), dar (2), dain (2).

**A para-final last word is dominated by daiin (8.6%) and ch-onset liquid words (chol 4.3%, chor 2.5%).** A para-final onset: ch=26%, o=22%, d=20% (including daiin), other=8%.

### A -am Suppression at Para-Final (NEW FINDING)

- A within-para -am last word: 97/1519 = **6.4%**
- A para-final -am last word: 3/162 = **1.9%**

**A also suppresses -am at para-final (1.9% vs 6.4%)** — exactly parallel to B's -am suppression (2.0% vs 12.6%). This is a CROSS-DIALECT finding: -am marks within-paragraph line-boundaries in BOTH A and B, and is suppressed at paragraph endings in both dialects.

### Top A Para-Start First Words

chor (5, 1.8×), **shey** (5, 4.4×), **sheol** (4, 5.7×), shol (3, 1.6×), shor (3, 2.8×), oty (3, 4.5×).

The top A para-start words are predominantly sh-onset (shey, sheol, shol, shor) — consistent with sh-onset's 2.54× enrichment at para-starts.

### H135 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — A Para-Starts Use sh/ch-Onset; daiin Marks Para-Finals Not Para-Starts; -am Is Cross-Dialect Within-Para Marker

A has a clear para-start morphological marker: **sh-onset (2.54×) and ch-onset (2.25×) first words**, suppressing d/y/t-onset. A para-starts are functionally OPPOSITE to B para-starts (B=o-onset 2.42×; A=sh-onset 2.54×). **daiin is a directional marker in A**: SUPPRESSED at para-start first words (0.6%) and ENRICHED at para-final last words (8.6%). **Cross-dialect finding**: -am is suppressed at para-final in BOTH A (1.9% vs 6.4%) and B (2.0% vs 12.6%), confirming -am is a within-para line-boundary marker across both dialects.
