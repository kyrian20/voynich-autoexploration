## H348: [ch][daiin][cth] = A COMPOUND PRECURSOR WITH 5.89× ENRICHMENT

**[ch][daiin] → cth enrichment = 5.89×** (12 observed vs 2.0 expected from baseline):

| bigram | → cth enrichment |
|--------|-----------------|
| [ch][daiin] | **5.89×** |
| [daiin] alone | 3.30× |
| baseline | 1.00× |

The [ch][daiin] sequence is a significantly stronger cth predictor than daiin alone (5.89× vs 3.30×). This means: when a ch-word is followed by daiin, that specific three-word context [ch][daiin][cth] occurs at nearly 6× the rate expected by chance.

**Specific ch-words before daiin→cth:**
- chol: 3× (most common — the medial nominal)
- chor: 2× (also medial nominal)
- diverse compounds/forms: choteol, ch{cphh}y, chy, chotchol, chocthy, chckhey (1× each)

**cth-words after daiin (in [ch][daiin][cth]):**
- cthy: 4× (dominant)
- cthol: 2×
- diverse: cthey<->choaiin, cthoiin, ctholdy, cthaiin<->daiin, ctheody, ctheol (1× each)

**Dominant sub-pattern: [chol/chor][daiin][cthy]** — the medial ch-nominals (chol, chor) specifically introduce the [daiin][cthy] terminal construction. The f3/f15 examples: `chol daiin cthy` and the f22/f45 examples: `chor daiin cthy`.

**Ch-word → daiin enrichment**: [ch] → daiin = 1.31× (mild enrichment). So ch-words slightly favor daiin as successor, but it's not highly enriched. The strong pattern is the CONDITIONAL: given [ch][daiin] already formed, cth follows at 5.89×.

**Grammatical interpretation**: [ch-nominal][daiin][cth-nominal] = a three-word chain where two nominals (a ch-type and a cth-type) are connected by daiin as a grammatical particle. This is consistent with daiin functioning as a linking/determinative element between two nominals.

**After [ch][daiin], the non-cth successor distribution:**
- cth: 12× (5.89×) — highest enrichment
- ch: 12× (1.54×)
- d: 11× (1.37×)
- ot: 4× (1.83×)
- sh: 4× (0.80×)

So the top successor after [ch][daiin] is NOT another onset class with a frequency advantage — cth wins by enrichment despite being rarer than ch or d in absolute terms.

## H349: cthol d-WORD ASYMMETRY — NOT CONFIRMED; BUT cthol SUCCESSOR ENRICHED FOR cth (2.41×)

**[d][cthol] before cthol (n=11):**
- daiin: 6/11 = 54.5%
- diverse other d-words: dshoy, dchol, dockhy, dal, compound (1 each)

**[cthol][d] after cthol (n=14):**
- daiin: 6/14 = 42.9%
- diverse: daimg, dol, dar, doiin, dam, dy, d-compounds (1 each)

**No asymmetry**: daiin is roughly equally common before (54.5%) and after (42.9%) cthol. The hypothesis that "daiin specifically left, non-daiin right" is NOT confirmed.

**NEW FINDING — cthol successor onset distribution (cthol n=49):**

| successor onset | count | rate  | baseline | enrichment |
|-----------------|-------|-------|----------|------------|
| **d**           | 14    | 31.8% | 14.8%    | **2.15×**  |

cthol specifically introduces FURTHER cth-words (2.41×) and d-words (2.15×) — this confirms the [cth-MID][cthy@LL] self-succession finding from F166 (cthol is the medial cth-word that introduces cthy at terminal). Also, cthol's most common specific successor is **daiin** (6 cases) and **chol** (3 cases). So:
- [cthol][daiin] = 6 cases — cthol introduces daiin (a new determiner phrase?)
- [cthol][chol] = 3 cases — cthol introduces a parallel ch-nominal

**cthol PREDECESSOR enrichments:** d=1.55×, qot=2.23×, cth=1.66×, ot=1.54× — cthol is preceded by qot-words (2.23×) and cth-words (1.66×) at enriched rates, suggesting cthol occupies medial positions in longer nominal chains.

## H350: cthey→ch_e AFFINITY — POSITIONAL (LF ONLY), NOT GENERAL

**cthey overall successor distribution (n=9 with successor):**
- ok: 2 (3.35×) — highest overall enrichment
- ch_e: 2 (2.47×)
- ol: 1 (2.01×)
- ch: 1 (1.75×), ot: 1 (1.69×)

**By position:**
- cthey@LF (n=2): 100% ch_e successors (chey, chedain) — **ch_e affinity is real at LF**
- cthey@second (n=2): d=1, ch=1 — diverse
- cthey@MID (n=5): ok=2, ot=1, other=1, ol=1 — ok-onset dominant, no ch_e

**The cthey→ch_e pattern is positionally specific**: at LF, cthey always introduces a ch_e-onset second word. At MID, cthey introduces ok-onset words. The overall ch_e enrichment (2.47×) is driven by the LF cases (n=2). Verdict: the pattern is real but confined to cthey@LF — n=2 is marginal.

**MAJOR BONUS FINDING — B-dialect cth-words have STRONG DIVERGENT SUCCESSOR ENRICHMENTS:**

| B-cth word | dominant successor onset | enrichment |
|------------|--------------------------|-----------|
| cthedy     | l-onset                  | **5.79×** |
| cthedy     | ot-onset                 | **5.07×** |
| cthey      | ok-onset                 | **3.35×** |

cthedy specifically introduces l-onset (5.79×) and ot-onset (5.07×) words — far higher enrichment than baseline. This is striking: B-cthedy, which is front-enriched (LF/second enriched), specifically introduces a narrow class of successors.

## Summary of New Findings (F176)

1. **[ch][daiin][cth] compound: [ch][daiin] → cth = 5.89×** (H348 CONFIRMED STRONGLY): The [ch][daiin] bigram is a compound predictor of cth at 5.89×, much stronger than daiin alone (3.30×). Dominant form: [chol/chor][daiin][cthy] — medial ch-nominals introduce the [daiin][cthy] terminal construction via a three-word chain. This represents [ch-nominal][particle][cth-nominal] grammatical structure.

2. **cthol d-word asymmetry NOT confirmed; cthol successor enriched for cth (2.41×)** (H349 NOT CONFIRMED AS HYPOTHESIZED): daiin appears equally left and right of cthol (~50% and ~43%). But cthol SUCCESSOR is enriched for cth (2.41×) and d (2.15×), confirming cthol's role as a medial cth-word that introduces further cth/d elements. [cthol][daiin]=6 (most common cthol successor) and [cthol][chol]=3.

3. **cthey→ch_e confined to LF; cthedy → l (5.79×) and ot (5.07×)** (H350 PARTIALLY CONFIRMED): cthey@LF always precedes ch_e, but at MID introduces ok-onset. The major new finding is cthedy's strong successor enrichments: l-onset (5.79×) and ot-onset (5.07×). B-cth words have divergent successor profiles: cthedy→l/ot, cthey→ok/ch_e, cthdy→qok.
