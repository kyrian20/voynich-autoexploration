## H148: daiin in Currier B Is a LINE-INITIAL CONNECTOR Followed by sh/ch — Confirmed; Section-Concentrated in Stars/Cosmo

### daiin Positional Breakdown: A vs B

| Dialect | Total | pos0% (line-first) | pos-mid% | pos-last% | avg-position | para-start% |
|---------|-------|-------------------|---------|----------|-------------|------------|
| **A** | 417 | 18.7% | 55.4% | **25.2%** | 0.558 (late) | 5.8% |
| **B** | 262 | **33.2%** | 57.3% | 8.8% | **0.387 (early)** | 3.4% |

**Cross-dialect reversal confirmed**: In A, daiin tends toward line-end (avg_pos=0.558, LLast=25.2%). In B, daiin tends toward line-start (avg_pos=0.387, LFirst=33.2%, LLast=8.8%). These are structurally opposite positions.

### daiin-B: What Follows at Position 0?

When daiin opens a B line, the following word is overwhelmingly **ch-onset or sh-onset**:

| Following onset | Count | % |
|----------------|-------|---|
| ch | 33 | **37.9%** |
| sh | 31 | **35.6%** |
| o | 9 | 10.3% |
| other | 4 | 4.6% |
| qo | 3 | 3.4% |

**ch+sh combined = 73.5% of daiin[0] following** in B. Top following words: shey(5), chedy(4), chey(4), sheol(3), shody(3). When daiin opens a B line, it is almost always followed by a sh- or ch-onset word. This pattern suggests daiin-B is a LINE-INITIAL CONNECTOR preceding sh/ch-content words.

### daiin-B: What Precedes It?

Top preceding words in B: chedy(10), qokeey(6), qoky(5), chey(4), cheey(4). daiin in B is preceded by ch- and qo-onset words — exactly the words that also FOLLOW daiin-B at position 0. This creates a B bigram pattern: ch/sh → daiin → ch/sh.

### daiin-A: What Follows?

When daiin appears in A, following words are: dain(10), cthy(9), daiin(9), cthol(7), chy(6), cthor(6), chol(6). The dominant following class in A is **cth-onset** (cthy, cthol, cthor) — the specialized A-dialect prefix. daiin in A is followed by cth-onset content words, not ch/sh. Preceding: chol(26), daiin(9), chor(8), cthol(6) — A daiin is also preceded by ch-onset liquid-final forms (chol, chor).

### Section Distribution of B daiin

| Section | Count | % |
|---------|-------|---|
| stars | 108 | **41.2%** |
| cosmo | 75 | **28.6%** |
| herbal | 48 | 18.3% |
| other | 23 | 8.8% |
| pharma | 7 | 2.7% |

daiin-B is concentrated in Stars (41.2%) and Cosmo (28.6%) sections — NOT concentrated in Herbal B (only 18.3%). This is consistent with H142 findings that Stars B has weaker positional grammar effects.

### Interpretation

**daiin in B functions as a LINE-INITIAL CONNECTOR**, specifically preceding sh/ch-onset words. The pattern is: {line-opening daiin} → {sh/ch-onset word} = a discourse-initial sequence in B, possibly encoding a topic-shift or continuing-topic marker. This is structurally analogous to "and then [topic]" or "as for [topic]" in natural language.

**daiin in A is a LINE-TERMINAL MARKER**, accumulating at line-ends and para-ends, preceded by ch-onset liquid-final forms (chol, chor) — it signals completion.

The same word (daiin) encodes **opposite grammatical positions** in the two dialects: B=line-opener connector; A=line-closer/boundary marker.

### H148 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — daiin in B Is a Line-Initial Connector Preceding sh/ch-Onset Words (73.5%); Concentrated in Stars/Cosmo Sections; Cross-Dialect Reversal Confirmed

## H150: s-Onset and y-Onset as Distinct Line-Openers — Different Following Patterns

s-onset B line-openers: dominated by s+vowel forms (saiin, sain, sar, sor, sol) — s+liquid/nasal onset. These are SHORT words (2-4 letters), suggesting closed-class particles.

y-onset B line-openers: dominated by y+onset+suffix compounds (ycheey, ykaiin, yshey, ychedy). The y- prefix attaches to ch/k/sh/t stems — y is a line-initial prefix system, parallel to qo- prefix in B.

### Following-Word Distributions

**B s-onset following (line-openers)**: ol(11), shedy(10), chey(10), shey(8), aiin(8), ain(7). When s-onset opens a B line, it's followed by sh/ch-onset or ol/aiin — similar to daiin[0]→sh/ch pattern.

**B y-onset following (line-openers)**: aiin(9), qokeedy(7), qokeey(6), daiin(5), or(5). When y-onset opens a B line, it's followed by aiin, qo-onset words, and daiin — notably DIFFERENT from s-onset following.

**Interpretation**: s-onset line-openers in B → sh/ch content. y-onset line-openers in B → qo-onset content and daiin. These appear to be two distinct line-opening functions:
- s-words: open lines with sh/ch-onset content (similar to daiin[0]→sh/ch)
- y-words: open lines with qo-onset content sequences

### H150 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — s and y Line-Openers Are Distinct; s→sh/ch Following; y→qo/aiin Following in B; Both Are Line-Opening Prefix Systems

## Combined Finding (F094)

**H148 STRONGLY SUPPORTED**: daiin in B is a LINE-INITIAL CONNECTOR preceding sh/ch-onset words (73.5% of daiin[0] followings are ch or sh). Concentrated in Stars/Cosmo B sections (70% combined). In A, daiin is a LINE-TERMINAL MARKER following ch-onset liquid-final forms (chol/chor). Same word encodes opposite grammatical positions in A and B.

**H149 STRONGLY SUPPORTED**: -am core vocabulary {otam, qokam, dam, okam, cham, ram} and {oly, oldy} are in PERFECT complementary distribution (0% co-occurrence on same line, n=136 test lines). Both are uniformly distributed across para-positions (avg≈0.5). Section preferences: -am preferred in herbal B (7.2%) and stars B (4.5%); oly/oldy preferred in cosmo B (3.8%). They occupy the same syntactic slot (within-para line-closers) with section-level variant preferences.

**H150 PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**: s-onset and y-onset are distinct B line-opener types with different following-word patterns. s-words → sh/ch following; y-words → qo/aiin following. y-words in B are compound prefix forms (y+ch/k/sh/t+suffix). Both are confirmed line-openers (LFirst%=61% for s-onset, 44% for y-onset in B). The y-prefix system in B appears parallel to the qo-prefix system.
