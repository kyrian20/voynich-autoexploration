## H266: -am AS PARAGRAPH-FINAL MARKER — NOT CONFIRMED (REVERSED)

**-am is specifically a WITHIN-PARAGRAPH line-final marker; it is SUPPRESSED at paragraph-final positions (0.00× enrichment).**

### Paragraph Detection
- 260 paragraphs detected (255 multi-line) across 3806 body-text lines
- Paragraph starts identified by `@`/`*` prefix characters in EVA ZL3b locus codes

### -am at Para-Final Positions
| Position | -am count | -am rate | Enrichment vs all-line-final |
|----------|-----------|----------|------------------------------|
| All line-final | 379/3806 | 10.0% | 1.00× (baseline) |
| Para-final line-final | **0/255** | **0.0%** | **0.00×** |
| Non-boundary line-final | 365/3296 | 11.1% | 1.11× |

**-am = 0 occurrences at para-final positions across ALL 260 paragraphs.** This holds across all sections.

### Para-Final Suffix Profile
| Suffix | Para-Final | All Line-Final | Enrichment |
|--------|-----------|---------------|------------|
| -other | 97.6% | 34.1% | **2.87×** |
| -edy | 0.8% | 3.0% | 0.26× |
| -ol | 0.8% | 3.8% | 0.20× |
| -y | 0.4% | 15.9% | 0.02× |
| -am | 0.0% | 10.0% | **0.00×** |
| -dy | 0.0% | 8.6% | 0.00× |
| -aiin | 0.0% | 7.2% | 0.00× |

Para-final lines almost exclusively end with "other" suffix (97.6%) — these are paragraph tail lines (often `=Pt` type in ZL3b, marked with `=` prefix) that end with short words or special transcription markers (`<$>`) not matching the 16-suffix taxonomy.

### -am Internal to Para-Final Lines
-am words appearing ANYWHERE in para-final lines: 7/1177 words = 0.59% (vs 1.79% corpus-wide = **0.33× DEPLETED**). -am is actively suppressed even within para-final lines.

### Interpretation
H266 is reversed: -am is not a sentence-final marker, it is specifically a WITHIN-PARAGRAPH line-final marker. This is consistent with the old finding from F086/F094 that "-am is suppressed at para-final in B (2.0% vs 12.6%) AND A (1.9% vs 6.4%)." The 7.87× and 7.48× line-final enrichments for cosmo and stars (F142) operate at the within-paragraph line level, not the paragraph-final level. **-am marks LINE BOUNDARIES within paragraphs, not PARAGRAPH BOUNDARIES.**

The "other=97.6%" at para-final reflects that paragraph-tail lines (`=Pt`) end with transcription markers or short bare words (no recognized suffix), not with -am or -y closers.

## H267: UNIVERSAL CORE POSITIONAL DISTRIBUTION — CONFIRMED

The 71 universal words are significantly more medially distributed than section-exclusive words, consistent with "function word" behavior.

### Positional Distribution
| Word class | LF% | MID% | LL% | n tokens |
|-----------|-----|------|-----|---------|
| Universal core (71 types) | 11.6% | **78.9%** | 9.5% | 6,708 |
| Section-exclusive | 16.1% | 60.2% | **23.8%** | 7,686 |

**Universal core: 78.9% medial vs section-exclusive 60.2%.** Section-exclusive words are much more likely to be line-closers (23.8% vs 9.5%). The universal core words are medially concentrated and avoid line edges.

### Within-Universal Variation
The universal core is not homogeneous — it contains positional sub-classes:

**Strong LINE-OPENERS (>40% LF)**:
- saiin: 53.5% LF — the s-OPENER from F139
- dain: 29.9% LF — d-function opener

**Strong LINE-CLOSERS (>25% LL)**:
- dy: 41.9% LL — d-closer
- dal: 26.9% LL
- oty: 25.6% LL

**Strongly MEDIAL (>90% MID)**:
- chey: 95.7%, cheey: 96.3%, chol: 93.1%, aiin: 92.8%, sheey: 92.6%, shey: 89.7%, okeey: 89.4%

**Interpretation**: The universal core encompasses the entire positional grammar — it includes opener-class words (saiin, dain), medial content words (chol, chey, okaiin), and closer-class words (dy, dal). The 71 universal words represent the core of the manuscript's grammatical and lexical system — they are the words that the whole manuscript is built around. Section-exclusive words by contrast are predominantly line-closers (section-specific closing formulas).

### Onset Profile of Universal Core
Compared to section-exclusive vocabulary:
| Onset | Universal% | Exclusive% |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| s | **9.9%** | 4.8% (2.1× overrep) |
| ot | **8.5%** | 4.9% (1.7× overrep) |
| sh | 9.9% | 6.7% (1.5× overrep) |
| ok | 7.0% | 4.6% (1.5× overrep) |
| d | 11.3% | 9.3% (1.2× overrep) |
| l | 0.0% | 5.2% (0.0× — ABSENT) |
| p | 0.0% | 1.2% (0.0× — ABSENT) |
| cth | 0.0% | 1.5% (0.0× — ABSENT) |

**l, p, cth are ABSENT from the 71 universal words.** l-prefix is B-specific stars/cosmo vocabulary (F131); p-prefix is B-specific opener (F135); cth is A-specific herbal/pharma nominal class (F133). The universal core contains NEITHER section-specific nominal class (cth/l), consistent with being grammatical/functional vocabulary.

The s, ot, d, ok, sh onsets dominate the universal core — these are the cross-dialect function word onsets (d=daiin/dar/dal, s=saiin/s particle, ot=otaiin/oty, ok=okaiin/okal).

### Bigram Context
After universal core words vs section-exclusive words: essentially identical onset-class distribution of following words. Universal words do NOT selectively precede specific onset classes. They are positionally flexible and context-neutral — consistent with being function words that can appear in any onset-class neighborhood.

## H268: COSMO-ABSENT WORDS — A-DIALECT DISTRIBUTION — CONFIRMED

**55% of high-frequency cosmo-absent words are A-dialect dominated.** Cosmo absence is primarily driven by A-dialect vocabulary exclusion.

### Onset Profile: Cosmo-Absent vs Cosmo

**ol/s/l are in cosmo but ABSENT from the cosmo-absent set.** These three onset classes are cosmo-specific features (ol=cosmo 2.06× from F141; s=cosmo 1.52× from F139; l=B-stars/cosmo from F131). The cosmo-absent set lacks these entirely.

**cth = 10.90× enrichment** in cosmo-absent: cth-onset words (cthy, cthol) are 96-97% A-dialect and concentrated in A-herbal. Their absence from cosmo is purely A-dialect driven — cosmo is B-dialect (B depletes cth universally per F139).

### Key Cosmo-Absent Word Profiles
| Word | Total | A% | Sections | Interpretation |

### Summary
**H268 CONFIRMED**: Cosmo absence is primarily A-dialect driven. The 31 cosmo-absent-from-all-4 words are dominated by A-dialect vocabulary (cth-onset words 96-97% A; ch-onset words 73-87% A). The cosmo section's B-dialect composition eliminates virtually all A-dialect cth/ch nominal vocabulary. The ol/s/l onset classes — which ARE in cosmo — are systematically absent from the cosmo-absent set, confirming that cosmo's exclusive vocabulary consists precisely of B-dialect onset classes (ol, s, l, r, p) that other sections lack.

**Core mechanism**: Cosmo is B-dialect → lacks cth (universal B depletion per F139) → 10.90× depletion of cth in cosmo vocabulary. The "cosmo-absent" set is essentially the A-dialect nominal vocabulary of cth/ch/k onset classes.

## Summary of New Findings (F143)

1. **-am is WITHIN-PARAGRAPH, not paragraph-final**: 0 occurrences of -am at para-final position (0.00×); actively suppressed even in para-final lines (0.33× internal). Para-finals end with "other" (97.6% — short words/transcription markers).
2. **Universal core (71 words) is medially concentrated (78.9%)** vs section-exclusive words (60.2%); both positional sub-classes (openers: saiin, dain; closers: dy, dal) AND medial words (chol, chey) are in the universal core.
3. **l/p/cth ABSENT from universal core** — the three most section/dialect-specific onset classes never appear in the universal vocabulary.
4. **Cosmo-absent words are 55% A-dialect dominated** (40/73 words); cosmo absence is driven by B-dialect suppression of A-nominal vocabulary (cth=10.90× in cosmo-absent set).
5. **ol/s/l onsets = cosmo-positive features** (0.00× in cosmo-absent set) — these are cosmo's own exclusive vocabulary.
