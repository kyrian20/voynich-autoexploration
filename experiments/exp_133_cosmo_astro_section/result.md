### Per-Folio Homogeneity

**Cosmo (f75-f84)**: qok ranges 9.7%-22.1% across folios; ol ranges 5.1%-14.3%. F75/f77/f82 are qok-peak (>18%). Some cosmo folios are more d-heavy (f78=11.0%, f84=11.4%).

**Stars (f103-f116)**: extremely variable. f105 has other=25.8% (most heterogeneous star folio). f111 has ch=19.6%. f108 has ok=11.3%. The within-stars variability in onset profiles explains why stars (though high-cohesion=0.954) is slightly less cohesive than cosmo (0.965).

### H261 Verdict: CONFIRMED — Cosmo and stars are linguistically distinct B-dialect sections. COSMO: ol-dominant (2.06× corpus baseline), qok-dominant (1.75×), s as TOP line-opener (17.7%), line-closer=-y (25.5%), -edy suffix dominant (19.23%), qol-prefix exclusive vocabulary (qolchedy/qoly). STARS: ch-dominant (near baseline), ot-elevated (1.43×), l-elevated (1.72×), line-closer=-am (17.0%) and other, -eey suffix elevated (0.68×), ch+eo exclusive vocabulary (cheo/cheody/cheeo). Key differentiators: ol (2.61× cosmo/stars ratio), -edy (2.01× cosmo/stars), s at line-first (cosmo 17.7% vs stars 11.5%), -am at line-last (stars 17.0% vs cosmo 5.6%). The cosmo-stars Jaccard of 0.178 confirms they are distinct sections with largely exclusive vocabularies despite both being B-dialect.

### What Makes f67 (True Astro) Distinctive?

f67 onset profile vs corpus:
- **y: 8.92% (2.36× corpus)** — extremely y-elevated; y-words dominate as line-openers
- **s: 5.60% (2.15× corpus)** — s-elevated
- **other: 21.99% (1.87× corpus)** — heavily "other"-onset (particles: aiin, ar, al, am)
- **d: 12.86% (1.36× corpus)** — moderately d-elevated
- **qok: 3.11% (0.33× corpus)** — heavily qok-DEPLETED
- **qot: 0.62% (0.19× corpus)** — heavily qot-DEPLETED
- **ol: 1.66% (0.38× corpus)** — ol-DEPLETED

f67 is an A-dialect folio (no -edy words) that has y/s line-openers and "other"-onset particles dominating, with very few B-paradigm onset classes (qok/qot/ol). The top astro words include ar(13), daiin(11), shey(9), aiin(9), dy(6) — function words and particles.

**INTERPRETATION**: f67 appears to contain dense A-dialect grammatical text (particles, function words, y/s-openers) with very little B-content vocabulary. It may be a unique formulaic section (astrological calendar? diagram labels?).

### Why Is Astro the Least Cohesive Section?

Because "astro" (f67-f73) is not a linguistically uniform section:
1. **f67**: True "astro" folio — unique onset profile (y/s-elevated, qok/qot-depleted, particle-heavy)
2. **f68, f70**: A-herbal-like (sim=0.960/0.968 to herbal centroid, nearly identical to each other)
3. **f69**: Pharma-like (too sparse to characterize reliably)

The section boundaries f67-f73 group heterogeneous content. The mean self-similarity (0.886) is the lowest because f67 is so unlike f68/f70.

### Why Does f87 (Pharma) Best-Match Astro?

f87 deviations from pharma centroid:
- **s↑+0.087** (f87=12.35% vs pharma=3.69%) — f87 has extreme s-onset elevation
- **cth↑+0.069** (f87=9.41% vs pharma=2.48%) — f87 is cth-elevated
- **qok↓-0.063** (f87=1.18% vs pharma=7.50%) — heavily qok-depleted
- **ok↓-0.052** (f87=2.35% vs pharma=7.56%) — ok-depleted
- **ch↓-0.051** (f87=13.53% vs pharma=18.58%)

f87 is a HIGHLY UNUSUAL pharma folio: s=12.35% (3.3× pharma baseline!), cth=9.41% (3.8× pharma baseline!), but qok/ok/ch severely depleted. This unusual profile makes f87 match astro better than pharma because astro ALSO has: s-elevated (2.15× corpus), y-elevated (2.36× corpus), and qok/qot-depleted (0.33×/0.19×).

**f87 similarity to astro**: Both share s-elevation and B-paradigm depletion. The astro centroid is pulled toward f67's y/s/other profile, which partially overlaps with f87's s/cth elevation and qok/ok depletion. Note: the cosimo similarity (astro=0.8838 vs pharma=0.8705) is a small margin — f87 is genuinely an atypical pharma folio that barely matches astro.

### H260 Verdict: CONFIRMED — Astro section (f67-f73) is NOT a cohesive linguistic section. It groups 4 heterogeneous folios: f67 (true astro: y=8.92% 2.36×, s=5.60% 2.15×, other=21.99% 1.87×, qok=0.33× depleted — particle-heavy A-dialect text), f68/f70 (A-herbal-like, sim=0.960/0.968 to herbal centroid, pairwise sim=0.953), f69 (pharma-like, too sparse). The low cohesion (0.886) is entirely explained by f67 being a structural outlier from f68/f70 (pairwise sim=0.558). f87 (pharma) best-matches astro because f87 is an atypical pharma folio with extreme s-elevation (s=12.35% vs pharma=3.69%) and qok/ok depletion — both of which characterize f67's astro profile. The "astro section" label may be a manuscript-structure label rather than a linguistic-register label.

## Combined Finding (F140)

**H261 CONFIRMED (F140)**: Cosmo and stars are linguistically distinct B-dialect sections separated by onset-class profiles, suffix distributions, and exclusive vocabulary. Key differentiators: COSMO = ol-dominant (2.06× corpus), qok-dominant (1.75×), s as TOP line-opener (17.7%), -y line-closer (25.5%), -edy dominant suffix (19.23%), qol-prefix exclusive vocabulary. Cosmo-stars Jaccard=0.178 (distinct vocabularies). Stars has 77.5% exclusive types; cosmo 54.4%. The cosmo-stars linguistic distinction is as large as either section's distance from A-dialect sections.

**H260 CONFIRMED (F140)**: Astro section is a heterogeneous grouping, not a unified register. f67 is the only "true astro" folio (y=2.36×, s=2.15×, other=1.87×, qok=0.33× depleted, particle-heavy A-dialect text). f68 and f70 are A-herbal-like (sim=0.960/0.968 to herbal centroid; pairwise sim=0.953). f69 is pharma-like (too sparse). Low cohesion (0.886) = f67 outlier effect: f67 vs f68 pairwise sim=0.558. f87 (pharma) best-matches astro because both share s-elevation and qok/ok depletion. The "astro section" may be a codicological label (these folios contain astronomical diagrams) rather than a linguistic register label — the folios have diagram text mixed with body-text-like content.
