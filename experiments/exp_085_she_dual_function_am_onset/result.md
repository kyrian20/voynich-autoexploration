## H140: she-Prefix Para-Start and qo-Predictor Functions Are INDEPENDENT — Not the Same Phenomenon

### she-→qo Transition Rates by Line Type (Currier A)

| Context | she-→qo rate | sh-other→qo rate |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Para-start lines | 27.5% (14/51) | 6.5% (5/77) |
| Non-para-start lines | 24.1% (52/216) | 14.3% (62/435) |
| Base qo rate in A | 11.4% | — |

**she-→qo is nearly IDENTICAL on para-start vs non-para-start lines (27.5% vs 24.1%).**

This means the qo-prediction is an INTRINSIC PROPERTY of she-prefix words, not a function of the para-start context. The two roles (para-start marker and qo-predictor) are co-present in she-words but are INDEPENDENT functions.

### Para-Start Line Coverage by Opener Type

| Opener type | Para-start lines opened |
|------------|------------------------|
| she-opener | 9.9% of para-starts (16/162) |
| sh-other-opener | 14.2% of para-starts (23/162) |
| she-opener in non-para-start | 1.3% of normal lines (19/1519) |

she-prefix is 7.6× more likely to open a para-start line than a normal line (9.9%/162 vs 1.3%/1519 proportionally). But sh-other opens MORE para-start lines in absolute count (23 vs 16).

### Pharma A Comparison

- Pharma A she-→qo: 30.7% (35/114 transitions) — slightly HIGHER than Herbal A (24-27%)
- Pharma A para-start she-openers: 8.5% (5/59), sh-other: 8.5% (5/59)
- Pharma A has equal she-/sh-other para-start opening (vs Herbal A she=4.74× enriched)

### Interpretation

The she-→qo link is a LEXICAL property: she-words are followed by qo regardless of position. The para-start enrichment of she-words (4.74×) is a DISTRIBUTIONAL property: she-words happen to congregate at para-starts. These two effects are independent — she-words predict qo anywhere they appear, not just at para-starts.

Hypothesis that she-→qo and she-para-start are "two aspects of the same grammatical function" is REFUTED. The correct model: she-prefix encodes a specific semantic/grammatical content that (a) tends to appear at discourse-initial positions AND (b) tends to be followed by qo-onset continuers as a separate lexical-level co-occurrence.

### H140 Verdict: REFUTED — she- Dual Function Is Two Independent Properties, Not One Phenomenon

The para-start function (positional distribution) and qo-predictor function (transition rate) are independent. she-→qo rates are 27.5% on para-start lines and 24.1% on non-para-start lines — essentially the same. The qo-prediction is intrinsic to she-lexemes, not context-dependent.

## H141: B ol-Prefix Stem Complexity Gradient — Complex Stems Shift Toward Para-Start Role

### B ol-Prefix by Stem Complexity Class

| Stem class | n_tokens | LLast% | PSFirst% | Interpretation |
|-----------|----------|--------|---------|----------------|
| Simple (len≤1, e.g. oly=∅+y) | 327 | 27.5% | 0.9% | Primarily line-closing |
| Medium (len 2-3, e.g. olchedy=ch+edy) | 175 | 12.0% | 2.3% | Mixed |
| Complex (len 4+, e.g. olkair=kair+∅) | 13 | 7.7% | 7.7% | Para-start enriched |
| Gallows-containing stem (any len) | 253 | 12.3% | 2.4% | Mildly para-start |

**Gradient exists**: as stem complexity increases, line-closing role decreases (27.5%→7.7%) and para-starting role increases (0.9%→7.7%). However, complex-stem sample is very small (n=13 tokens, 4 types).

### Top ol-Prefix Words by Position (n≥3)

Notable para-starters: olfchedy (PSFirst=50%, stem=fch/complex), olkair (25%, stem=kair), olkeeey (9.1%, stem=ke/medium), olar (12.5%, stem=ar/medium), olkey (33.3%, stem=k/simple)

Notable line-closers: oly (89% line-last, stem=∅/empty), oldy (79%), olam (100%), ols (44%), olol (50%)

The dominant line-closer oly has an EMPTY stem (ol+y directly). oldy has empty stem (ol+dy). The stem-complexity gradient is real but the strongest predictor is simply WHICH SPECIFIC WORDS — oly/oldy/olam are the pure line-closers regardless of formal stem length.

### Gallows Hypothesis

Gallows-containing stems (k,t,p,f present): line-last=12.3%, PSFirst=2.4%. Non-gallows medium/complex: variable. Gallows presence does NOT cleanly predict either role — gallows stems appear across line-closers (olkedy=18% line-last) and para-starters (olkey=33% PSFirst).

### H141 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — Stem Complexity Gradient Exists But Is Weak; Specific Word Identity Is Stronger Predictor

A modest gradient exists (simple→line-closer, complex→para-starter) but it is not robust: sample size for complex stems is small, and specific lexical identity matters more than stem structure. The two functional sub-classes (oly/oldy=pure line-closers; olfchedy/olkey/olar=para-starters) remain the operationally useful classification from F091.

### -am Stem Structure (internal morphology)

Top stems before -am in B: k (43×), t (39×), d (15×), r (15×), ar (11×)
Top stems before -am in A: t (20×), k (10×), ar (6×)

The -am suffix predominantly attaches to t, k, d, r stems — short single-consonant or consonant-pair stems. The productive structure is {onset}{short-stem}am: o+t+am=otam, d+∅+am=dam, qo+k+am=qokam.

### Cross-Dialect -am Words

25 word types appear in both A and B dialects: dam, otam, qokam, okam, cham, ram, qotam, ytam, olkam, daram, oram, aram, dalam, alam, sham...

**dam and otam are the core cross-dialect -am forms.** dam is A-dominant (A=38, B=22); otam is B-dominant (B=27, A=13). qokam is B-dominant (B=22, A=3).

### Onset Suppression Pattern for -am

Both dialects: **sh-onset suppresses -am** (B=0.37×, A=0.56×). Both dialects: ch-onset mildly suppresses -am (B=0.77×, A=0.63×). A-only: p-onset completely absent. B-only: qo-onset suppressed (0.59×).

This matches the overall sh/ch pattern: sh-onset (which already has strong -am suppression) avoids certain suffixes. The -am suppression by sh/ch may be related to the sh-onset para-start enrichment (para-start lines disfavor -am within-para markers).

### H143 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — -am Is Onset-Structured; d-Onset Dominant (A), d/t/o in B; sh-Onset Suppressed in Both Dialects

-am suffix rate is highly onset-dependent. d-onset is the strongest -am affinitor (A=2.01×, B=1.67×). sh-onset is suppressed in both dialects (A=0.56×, B=0.37×). p-onset is completely absent in A. Cross-dialect core -am lexicon: {dam, otam, qokam, okam}. The -am morpheme is predominantly attached to {d,t,k,r} stems with varied onsets.

## Combined Finding (F092)

**H140 REFUTED**: she-prefix para-start and qo-predictor functions are INDEPENDENT. she-→qo rates are nearly identical on para-start (27.5%) and non-para-start (24.1%) lines — qo-prediction is an intrinsic lexical property of she-words, not context-dependent. Pharma A shows slightly higher she-→qo (30.7%). The two functions co-occur in she-lexemes but are not causally linked.

**H141 PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**: B ol-prefix stem complexity shows a gradient (simple→line-closer, complex→para-starter) but the effect is weak and lexical identity is the stronger predictor. olfchedy (PSFirst=50%) and olkair (25%) are complex para-starters; oly/oldy/olam are empty-stem line-closers. The stem-complexity hypothesis is directionally correct but not mechanistically clean.

**H143 STRONGLY SUPPORTED**: -am is onset-structured. d-onset dominant in A (2.01×), d/t/o enriched in B. sh-onset suppressed in both dialects (A=0.56×, B=0.37×); p-onset absent in A. Core cross-dialect -am vocabulary: dam, otam, qokam, okam. The -am morpheme attaches to {t,k,d,r} short stems. The cross-dialect -am core is a conserved closed-class morphological form set.
