## Summary of New Findings (F154)

1. **B-COMPOUND-ch words (chdy/chckhy/chcthy/chody) are the best B "medial nominal" parallel to A-cth**: MID-enriched (1.10-1.28×) and LF-depleted (0.52-0.68×). These gallows-embedded compound ch-forms dominate the B ch-vocabulary. chol is a B line-opener (LF=1.42×), not a medial nominal.

2. **d is DEPLETED before B-ch (0.79×), opposite to A-cth (d=1.30×)**: The d-before-cth grammatical marker does NOT transfer to B-ch. Before B-ch: ch-self-succession (1.54×) and k-class words (1.40×) dominate. The grammatical contexts are structurally different — B-ch is embedded in a ch-chain context; A-cth is embedded in a d-chain context.

3. **ch self-succession in B (1.57×) is real and the strongest positional parallel to A-cth self-succession (1.84×)**: Both have ~1.5-1.8× self-succession; both are the only onset classes in their respective dialects showing clear self-succession. This is the strongest parallel: A-cth and B-ch are "self-coherent" onset classes that tend to follow themselves.

4. **exp_145 daiin→sh=2.33× REVISED to 1.19× in P-type body text**: The B-dialect daiin→sh enrichment was inflated by non-body-text locus lines. In P-type only, daiin→sh is near-baseline. daiin→ch_e retains moderate enrichment (1.77×), but the strongest B-daiin signal is daiin→d (2.70×), reflecting daiin-daiin self-succession and daiin leading into d-onset words.

Compute: daiin→daiin (self) vs daiin→dam vs daiin→dar separately. If daiin→daiin self-succession is the driver, daiin acts as a "repeating boundary marker" in B.
