### H136 Verdict: SUPPORTED — ol-Prefix Splits into Line-Closer Sub-Class (oly/oldy) and Para-Starter Sub-Class (olkey/olaiin)

The B ol-prefix is NOT a unified class. The split is between oly/oldy (pure line-closers, common words) and olkey/olaiin (para-starters, rare words). The ol-prefix appears to form a productive B morphological class where ol+ prefix encodes specific positional roles depending on the following stem.

## H137: she- Prefix Is 3× MORE Enriched at A Para-Starts Than Plain sh-Forms — e-Infix Marks Paragraph Openings in A

**she-prefix words are 4.74× enriched at A para-starts** (5.4% PSFirst vs 11.4% overall para-start rate = 162/1681 lines × proportional). This is **3× greater than plain sh-other forms (1.62×)**. The e-infix morphological marker is a specific para-start signal in A.

### Ordering of Para-Start Enrichment

e-infix > plain-form: she-=4.74× > sh-other=1.62×; che-=2.37× > ch-other=1.92× (weaker contrast for ch)

**daiin is ANTI-para-start (0.13×)**: 0.2% PSFirst vs 19.4% line-first overall. daiin almost never opens a paragraph.

### Top she-/ch- Words at A Para-Starts

Top she- para-starters: shey (5), sheol (4), sheo (1), sheey (1)
Top ch- para-starters: chor (5), chydaiin (1), choly (1), chtoiin (1)

**shey and sheol are the dominant A para-start words from the she-class.** chor (ch+or) is the top ch-onset para-starter.

### Dual Role of she-Prefix in A: Para-Start Marker AND qo-Predictor

she-prefix is 4.74× enriched at A para-starts (this experiment) AND predicts qo-following at 2.23× in y-dom Herbal A (F086/F088). These may be two aspects of the same grammatical function: she-words typically open lines that then develop sh→qo transitions. Or: she-words mark STATEMENT-OPENING positions, which happen to be followed by qo-onset continuers.

### H137 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — she-Prefix Is the Primary A Para-Start Marker (4.74× vs sh-other 1.62×); daiin Is Anti-Para-Start

The e-infix morphological class marks paragraph openings in A (she-=4.74×), while daiin marks paragraph closings (PSFirst=0.13×, PFLast=3.4×). These are complementary grammatical functions: she- = OPENING MARKER; daiin = CLOSING MARKER. The A positional grammar is now: [**she-prefix PARA-START (4.74×)**] → [-am line-ends 6.4%] → [**daiin PARA-FINAL (8.6%)**].

## H138: daiin Rate Rises 2.2× from First Line to Last Line; Binary Jump (First Line Suppressed), Not Smooth Gradient

### daiin Rate by Paragraph Position

| Position bucket | Lines | daiin% | daiin-as-last-word% |
|----------------|-------|--------|-------------------|
| 0.0-0.2 (first) | 370 | **3.9%** | 6.5% |
| 0.2-0.4 | 304 | 5.3% | 6.9% |
| 0.4-0.6 | 302 | 4.8% | 4.3% |
| 0.6-0.8 | 304 | 4.7% | 5.9% |
| 0.8-1.0 (last) | 401 | 5.4% | **8.0%** |

### daiin Rate: First vs Middle vs Last Lines (n≥3-line paragraphs)

| Line position | daiin% | words |
|--------------|--------|-------|
| **First line** | **2.93%** | 818 |
| Middle lines | 4.83% | 7411 |
| **Last line** | **6.50%** | 508 |

**Gradient confirmed: First line = 2.93%, Middle = 4.83%, Last = 6.50% — a 2.2× increase from start to end.** The shape is primarily a BINARY STEP UP from first line to all subsequent lines, then a further increase at para-final.

### daiin as Last Word by Line Ordinal

| Line ordinal | daiin-as-last% | n lines |
|-------------|---------------|---------|
| 1 | 3.70% | 162 |
| 2 | 8.02% | 162 |
| 3 | 7.89% | 152 |
| 4 | 6.34% | 142 |
| 5 | 4.72% | 127 |
| 6 | 8.55% | 117 |
| 7 | 3.70% | 108 |
| 8 | **11.00%** | 100 |

The daiin-as-last-word pattern shows HIGH VARIABILITY (3.7-11%) by ordinal — it doesn't increase monotonically but shows elevated rates at lines 2, 3, 6, 8. The **FIRST LINE has the lowest daiin-last rate (3.70%)** and the general trend increases for later lines (11.00% at line 8), but with noise.

### Interpretation

daiin's positional gradient is NOT a smooth monotonic increase through all paragraph positions. Instead:
1. **First line STRONGLY SUPPRESSED** (2.93% vs 4.83% middle — 39% reduction): daiin rarely appears in para-opening lines
2. **Last line ELEVATED** (6.50% vs middle 4.83% — 35% increase): daiin accumulates at para-endings
3. Middle lines are fairly uniform (~4.8%), not a gradient

This is **binary-plus-terminal**: {SUPPRESSED at first line} → {BASELINE in middle} → {ELEVATED at last line}. Not a "sentence-final particle across hierarchy" but rather a para-boundary marker with a specific anti-opening / pro-closing distribution.

### H138 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — daiin Has Binary-Step Pattern (First Line Suppressed, Last Line Elevated), Not Smooth Hierarchy

daiin rate: first line=2.93%, middle=4.83%, last=6.50% (2.2× gradient first-to-last). The first line suppression is the stronger effect (first line = 61% of middle rate). daiin-as-last-word shows high variability by ordinal (3.7-11%), not monotonic. **Conclusion: daiin is a PARA-BOUNDARY marker with two distinct properties**: (1) avoidance of paragraph-opening positions; (2) accumulation at paragraph-closing positions. This is consistent with a "closing/terminal particle" interpretation rather than a hierarchical boundary marker at all scales.
