## H199: CONFIRMED — Voynichese Follows Zipf's Law; α=1.050 (Lower End of NL Range); Heaps-Zipf Deviation Suggests Flatter Top Distribution Than Typical NL (No Single Dominant "the"-Equivalent Word)

**Heaps-Zipf consistency check**: From β≈0.744 (A herbal) → theoretical α≈1/β≈1.34. Observed α=0.876 (A herbal). This deviation (observed < predicted) indicates the vocabulary distribution is FLATTER than a pure power law predicts — the top-ranked words are LESS dominant than in typical NL.

**The top-ranked words are dramatically UNDER-represented vs Zipf prediction**. The Zipf fit (based on ranks 10-70%) projects rank-1 frequency at ~4785, but the actual rank-1 word (daiin) has only 707 tokens. This is the OPPOSITE of typical natural language (where "the" typically exceeds Zipf predictions for lower ranks).

**Voynichese lacks a single dominant grammatical word equivalent to English "the" (~7% of tokens)**. The top word (daiin) is only 707/28300 = 2.5% of tokens. This is consistent with daiin being a SECTION-SPECIFIC function word (concentrated in A; only 12-22‰ in B sections) rather than a universal article. The flatter top distribution reflects the section-specific nature of high-frequency words.

### H199 Verdict: CONFIRMED — Voynichese Follows Zipf's Law at α=1.050 (Lower End of NL Range); However, the Top Words Are Under-Represented vs Zipf Prediction (Zipf-Mandelbrot Pattern); No Single "the"-Equivalent Dominates; The Flatness of the Top Distribution Reflects Section-Specific Function Words (daiin in A, chedy in B) That Dilute Each Other's Corpus-Wide Rank

## Combined Finding (F112)

**H200 CONFIRMED (F112)**: The "OTHER" tier (43.8% of all tokens) has clear sub-structure: (1) o-prefix content words (ok/ot/ol+anything = 37% of OTHER) — the body-text object-naming vocabulary related to the label system; (2) d-onset line-boundary words (~19%): dal/dam/dy/dar = line-closers; dain/dol/dor = line-openers; (3) aiin/ain short particles (~9%); (4) s-onset line-openers (saiin/sain, 6%). The OTHER tier is NOT residual — it is a coherent second word class complementary to MID (ch/sh). The d-onset sub-class within OTHER is a LINE-BOUNDARY MARKER system; the o-prefix sub-class is an OBJECT-NAMING system matching the label corpus.

**H199 CONFIRMED (F112)**: Voynichese follows Zipf's law at α=1.050 (lower end of NL range 1.0-1.5). By section: B cosmo=1.105 (most Zipfian, most repetitive), A herbal=0.876 (flatter). Heaps β≈0.74 predicts α≈1.34, but observed α≈1.05 — the DEVIATION indicates a Zipf-Mandelbrot pattern where top words are under-represented vs power law (no single "the"-equivalent; daiin is only 2.5% of all tokens). This is explained by section-specific function words: daiin (A-dominant, 55.6‰ in A herbal) and chedy (B-dominant) dilute each other's corpus-wide rank.

**H201 CONFIRMED (F112)**: 44 universal words span all sections at ≥3 tokens. Universal words = 21-28% of each section's tokens. These are OTHER-tier (daiin, ol, aiin, or, ar, dar, dal, okaiin) and MID-tier (chol, qokeey, chey, shey, shol) words — the grammatical and base-vocabulary core. 72-79% of each section's vocabulary is section-specific. B_stars has 215 section-exclusive words (most diverse); B_herbal has only 12 (most shared with B sections).

**KEY FINDING (F112)**: Voynichese vocabulary has THREE distinct layers:
1. **Universal core** (~21-28% of tokens): 44 words universal across all sections — OTHER-tier particles (daiin, ol, aiin) and MID-tier base words (chol, chey, shey). These are the "function words" of the manuscript.
2. **Section-specific content** (~72-79% of tokens): Thousands of words appearing in 1-2 sections. These carry the section-specific information.
3. **The OTHER tier** (43.8% of ALL tokens): Comprises mostly o-prefix body-words + d-onset line-boundary markers + short particles. The "OTHER" label is misleading — this is a coherent word class with three distinct sub-functions.
