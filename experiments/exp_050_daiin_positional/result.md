# Experiment exp_050: daiin Positional Analysis in Currier A
Date: 2026-04-09

## Question (H060)
Is daiin (Currier A's most frequent word, 4.31%) concentrated at line-final position, like B's -ain suffix? Does this suggest A's daiin is a functional parallel of B's line-final -ain words?

## Results

### daiin Positional Profile in Currier A

- Total occurrences: 423 (4.31% of Currier A tokens)
- Line-first: 15.1% (vs baseline ~15.9% — **neutral**)
- Line-final: **22.5%** (vs baseline ~15.9% — **elevated 1.41×**)
- Medial: 62.4%

**daiin IS concentrated at line-final position in Currier A.** It appears at line endings 1.41× more than chance.

### -aiin Suffix in Currier A (Parallel Test)

- Total -aiin tokens in A: 1,214
- First: 17.1%, Last: **20.2%**, Medial: 62.8%

The entire -aiin suffix class is line-final elevated (~1.27× above baseline). daiin and the broader -aiin class both lean line-final in Currier A.

### Comparison: -ain in Currier B

- Total -ain tokens in B: 1,349
- First: 14.2%, Last: **8.5%**, Medial: 77.2%

**-ain in Currier B is NOT line-final elevated — it is below baseline.** This contradicts the expected A/B parallel. In B, -ain words are predominantly medial.

## Finding (F057)
In Currier A, 'd'-initial words concentrate strongly at line-final positions: dam=62.5% final, dy=49.2%, dal=35.5%, daiin=22.5% — all 1.4–3.9× above the baseline rate of 15.9%. The entire -aiin suffix class in A is also line-final elevated (20.2%). This contrasts sharply with -ol/-or words (chol=0.9% final, shol=1.0% final, chor=4.1% final) which are almost excluded from line endings — a strong positional complementary distribution. The 'd' stem is a LINE-FINAL MARKER in Currier A: multiple suffixed forms of 'd' (dy, dal, dam, dar, daiin, dain) collectively function as line-terminating elements. This parallels the suffix-specific positional biases found in Currier B (exp_038), where -aiin is line-initial dominant and -edy is medial dominant. The A/B parallel is: 'd'-initial words (A) ↔ specific suffix classes (B) for positional encoding. **The chol/shol vs. daiin positional split is the strongest positional signal yet found in Currier A.**

## H060 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED
daiin IS line-final elevated in Currier A (22.5% vs 15.9% baseline, 1.41× ratio) — supporting the idea that daiin has a line-boundary function. However, B's -ain suffix is NOT a strong line-final marker (8.5% final, below baseline) — so daiin and -ain are NOT functional parallels as originally hypothesized. The real finding is that the entire 'd' stem paradigm marks line endings in Currier A, while -ol/-or words strictly avoid them. This is the Currier A positional structure parallel to the Currier B suffix-specific positional biases from exp_038.
