## Combined Finding (F114)

**H205 CONFIRMED (F114)**: The A/B suffix paradigm split is a UNIVERSAL GRAMMATICAL PROPERTY. All 16 tested onset classes show the same direction: A-type suffixes (-ol/-or/-y) more common in A-dialect; B-type suffixes (-edy/-eedy/-eey) more common in B-dialect. The magnitude varies: bare_t=12.6× B-ratio (strongest); d+gallows=9.3×; d/s-onset=5.2-5.7× (weakest). d-onset and s-onset in A-dialect produce ZERO -edy/-eedy tokens (absolute suppression), while in B-dialect they produce modest amounts (3-11%). The paradigm is not a property of specific onset classes — it is the grammatical signature of the dialect. This means a single underlying phonological or morphological rule drives the entire A/B distinction.

**H206 CONFIRMED (F114)**: s-onset (11.8% of tokens) is a LINE-OPENER class complementary to d-onset (8.2%):
- Both show VOWEL/CONSONANT split: s/d + vowel = opener; s/d + consonant = mixed
- Both are followed by qo-onset at 2.1-2.5× enrichment (same grammatical function)
- But they are SUFFIX OPPOSITES: d-onset enriches -aiin (3.08×, A-paradigm); s-onset enriches -edy (1.40×, B-paradigm)
- s-onset is UNIDIRECTIONAL (opener only; LLast=9.9%); d-onset is BIDIRECTIONAL (both opener and closer)
- **Combined grammar**: A line-interior sequence often uses either s-onset OR d-onset as line openers (depending on dialect context), both leading to qo-mid-line. In B, s-onset opener (→ sh-words → qo-sequence). In A, d-onset opener (→ ch-words → aiin-closing).

**KEY FINDING (F114)**: The Voynichese positional grammar has TWO LINE-OPENER CLASSES (s-onset and d-onset) that are morphologically COMPLEMENTARY:
- s-onset: B-paradigm aligned (allows -edy suffix), unidirectional (opener only)
- d-onset: A-paradigm aligned (suppresses -edy), bidirectional (opener and closer)
The universal paradigm split (H205) means these are not ad hoc word-class properties — they reflect a single deep grammatical rule that pervades the entire manuscript.
