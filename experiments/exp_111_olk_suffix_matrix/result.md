### Key Morphological Asymmetry Finding

**A-paradigm suffixes have ABUNDANT free-word forms**:
- ol (523 free tokens), or (356), aiin (458), ar (331), al (213), ain (105)
- These A-paradigm suffixes exist as major standalone vocabulary items

**B-paradigm suffixes are NEAR-ZERO as free words**:
- edy (2 tokens), eedy (5 tokens), eey (8 tokens)
- These B-paradigm suffixes essentially NEVER appear standalone

**'y' = ZERO standalone** despite being the 6th most common suffix — 'y' appears only as a suffix and has no free-word existence at all.

### Implications for Morphological History

The free-word vs suffix dichotomy suggests:
1. **A-paradigm suffixes** (-ol/-or/-ar/-al/-aiin) are GRAMMATICALIZED FREE WORDS — they still exist as standalone function words (ol=523, or=356) while ALSO being used as suffixes. This is a classic grammaticalization pathway (free word → clitic → suffix).
2. **B-paradigm suffixes** (-edy/-eedy/-eey) are NOT grammaticalized from free words — they have no free-word existence. This is consistent with B-suffixes being NEWER formations (from F117) or derived from a different source than grammaticalization of existing free morphemes.
3. **standalone 'dy'** = A-dialect closing particle (42.9% LLast) — the free-word form that gives rise to the '-dy' suffix in some A-dialect words.
4. **'y' standalone = zero** — the most common Voynichese suffix (-y appears in 12% of all words) has NO free-word form, suggesting it fully grammaticalized before the attested period.

### H219 Verdict: CONFIRMED — Standalone 'dy' Is A-Dialect LINE-CLOSER (LLast=42.9%, A=9.5‰); 'y' Has ZERO Standalone Form (Fully Grammaticalized); A-Paradigm Suffixes (-ol/-or/-ar/-aiin) = Abundant Free Words (100-500+ tokens); B-Paradigm Suffixes (-edy/-eedy/-eey) = Near-Zero Free Words (2-8 tokens); The Asymmetry Supports: A-Paradigm = Grammaticalized Free Morphemes; B-Paradigm = Non-Free-Morpheme Suffix Innovation
