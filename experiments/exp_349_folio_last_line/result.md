# exp_349: Folio-Last Line Grammar — The Anti-Gallows Closing Formula (H551)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H551 — Do folio-last lines have different LF/LL grammar? Predicted: folio-last LF may be anti-gallows (inverse of folio-first LF gallows enrichment, F345).
**Result:** H551 CONFIRMED AND EXTENDED. Folio-last lines are SHORTER (B: 4.81 words vs continuation 7.86; A: 3.94 vs 5.78) and have a cross-dialectally conserved **anti-gallows LF** (B: gallows E=0.60×; A: E=0.44×). A-dialect: p-onset completely absent from folio-last LF (0/83 = 0.0%) despite p=37.3% at folio-first LF — the most extreme inversion in the manuscript. B-dialect folio-last LF: l/ol/ot-onset enriched (3.38-3.94×). Canonical am/l-onset LL-terminal formula is NOT applied at folio-last LL (l-onset E=0.22×, am-suffix E=0.61×). The VMS has a three-register line grammar: folio-opener (gallows LF, long line, no LL formula), continuation (normal d/qok/s/y LF, am/l LL formula), and folio-closer (anti-gallows LF, SHORT line, no LL formula).

## Findings

### Finding 1: Folio-Last Lines Are SHORT (4-5 Words vs 7-8 Words)

B-dialect folio-last lines average 4.81 words (vs continuation 7.86 — nearly half). A-dialect: 3.94 vs 5.78. Folio-last lines are structurally short — they are closing tags, not full content lines. This is independent of the LF/LL grammar but co-occurs with it.

### Finding 2: Cross-Dialectal Anti-Gallows LF at Folio-Last Lines

B: folio-last gallows LF=12.5% (E=0.60×); A: 6.0% (E=0.44×). Both dialects show gallows depletion at folio-last LF. This is the inverse of folio-first LF (74% gallows both dialects). The VMS uses gallows-onset to OPEN folios and non-gallows-onset to CLOSE folios.

### Finding 3: A-Dialect p-Onset Completely Absent from Folio-Last LF (0/83 = 0.0%)

A-dialect p-onset at folio-first LF = 37.3% (the dominant onset class). At folio-last LF = 0/83 (0.0%). This is the most extreme positional inversion found in any analysis: p-onset is simultaneously the modal folio-opener and is completely excluded from folio-closers. In A-dialect, p-onset = opening signal only.

### Finding 4: B-Dialect Folio-Last LF Uses l/ol/ot-Onset (Terminal-Class Words)

B-dialect folio-last LF: l-onset 10.4% (E=3.87×), ol-onset 10.4% (E=3.38×), ot-onset 8.3% (E=3.94×). These are the terminal/content-onset classes that are normally LL-enriched or MID-terminal. Using them at LF means folio-last lines OPEN with the word class that normally CLOSES lines. This is a grammatical inversion.

### Finding 5: Canonical am/l-Onset LL-Terminal Formula NOT Applied at Folio-Last LL

At folio-last LL: B l-onset=2.1% (E=0.22× vs continuation LL 9.5%); am-suffix=8.3% (E=0.61× vs 13.7%). The l-onset terminal formula that dominates continuation LL is absent from folio-last LL. Folio-last lines are TOO SHORT to apply the canonical LL formula — the LL word is typically the 4th-5th word of a short closing line, which uses content-class words.

### Finding 6: B-Dialect Folio-Last LL Has sh-Onset Enrichment (E=3.45×)

B-dialect folio-last LL: sh=14.6% (E=3.45×). This is one of the strongest LL-enrichments observed in any line type. The folio-last line closes with sh-onset words in B-dialect. This may be a specific folio-closing formula involving sh-terminal words.

## Summary: Three-Register Line Grammar

The VMS line grammar has (at minimum) three position-sensitive registers:

| Register | Line type | LF onset | LL formula | Mean length |
|----------|-----------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Folio-opener | Folio-first | Gallows (74%) | None applied | ~7-8 words |
| Continuation | Middle lines | d/qok/s/y (normal) | am/l formula | ~7-8 words |
| Folio-closer | Folio-last | Anti-gallows (12-6%) | None applied | ~4-5 words |

The folio-opener and folio-closer are both exempt from the LL-terminal formula. The folio-closer is additionally SHORT and uses anti-gallows LF.
