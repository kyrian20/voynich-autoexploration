# exp_365: A-Dialect {aiin→ol} Shift — Word-Level Vocabulary (H578)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H578 — Characterize which specific words drive the A-dialect {aiin→ol} body shift in pharma vs herbal sections. Prediction: qokaiin→qokol and chaiin→chol are the principal lexical pairs; the shift is largest for qok and ok onsets; d-onset does not shift.
**Result:** H578 ANSWERED. qok-onset drives the largest shift (herbal ol/aiin=0.85 → pharma ol/aiin=6.60); qokaiin accounts for 8.04% of qok tokens in herbal but only 0.98% in pharma, while qokol rises (6.64%→11.76%). ok-onset also shifts (herbal=0.65→pharma=2.38), with okaiin dropping from 31 to 5 tokens. ch and sh are already ol-biased in herbal (ol/aiin≈2.9–3.0) and shift only marginally in pharma (≈3.2–3.3). ot and qot show no consistent shift. d-onset control: stays strongly aiin-biased in both sections (0.16→0.32), confirming F340.

## Findings

### Finding 1: qok-Onset Is the Primary Driver of the A-Dialect {aiin→ol} Shift in Pharma

qok-onset shows the largest herbal→pharma ol/aiin ratio change (+5.75): from aiin-neutral (0.85) in herbal to strongly ol-biased (6.60) in pharma. The mechanism is qokaiin disappearance: qokaiin is 8.04% of herbal qok tokens (N=23) but only 0.98% in pharma (N=2). qokol is stable or increases. The qok {aiin→ol} shift is primarily a lexical disappearance — qokaiin has near-zero pharma representation.

### Finding 2: ch and sh Were Already ol-Biased in Herbal; Their Pharma Shift Is Marginal

ch-onset: ol/aiin=2.94 in herbal, 3.30 in pharma — negligible change. sh-onset: 2.96→3.22 — similarly small. Both onsets are already ol-dominated in herbal. The pharma shift for these onsets is not a body-class inversion (they were never aiin-biased) but rather a further aiin-word dropout: chaiin, chain, shaiin, sheaiin, shain all absent in pharma. The "shift" is an aiin-zero floor effect in pharma, not a true herbal→pharma transition.

### Finding 3: ok-Onset Shifts from aiin-Biased to ol-Biased (0.65→2.38) via okaiin Dropout

ok-onset is aiin-biased in herbal (okaiin=31, okol=17, ol/aiin=0.65) and becomes ol-biased in pharma (okaiin=5, okol=18, ol/aiin=2.38). okaiin drops by 84% (31→5) while okol is stable. This mirrors the qok pattern: the shift is okaiin disappearance, not ol-word emergence.

### Finding 4: d-Onset Does Not Shift — Confirms F340

d-onset is strongly aiin-biased in herbal (ol/aiin=0.16: 443 aiin-body vs 72 ol-body) and remains aiin-biased in pharma (0.32: 128 vs 41). daiin is the dominant d-body word in both sections (herbal N=326, pharma N=101). This confirms F340: d-onset is exempt from the {aiin→ol} shift. The shift is selective across onset classes.

### Finding 5: ot and qot Do Not Show Consistent Pharma-Specific aiin→ol Shift

ot-onset: herbal ol/aiin=1.50, pharma=1.25 (slight reverse). qot-onset: herbal=1.71, pharma=1.00. Neither shows a significant pharma amplification. The {aiin→ol} shift is concentrated in qok and ok onset classes; ot and qot are approximately neutral across sections.

### Finding 6: The Shift Mechanism Is Primarily aiin-Word Dropout, Not ol-Word Emergence

In all shifted onset classes (qok, ok, ch, sh), the pharma aiin-body words are absent or sparse: qokaiin, okaiin, chaiin, chain, shaiin, sheaiin, shain — all at near-zero in pharma. The ol-body words (qokol, chol, chor, shol, okol) do not systematically increase in pharma. The shift is driven by lexical restriction (specific aiin-compound words are excluded from the pharma register), not by a positive attraction to ol-body words.

## Summary

H578 ANSWERED. The A-dialect {aiin→ol} shift is primarily driven by qok-onset (herbal=0.85 → pharma=6.60) via near-complete qokaiin dropout, and secondarily by ok-onset (0.65→2.38) via okaiin dropout. ch and sh are already ol-dominated in herbal; their pharma change is small (aiin-floor effect). ot and qot show no shift. d-onset (control) stays strongly aiin-biased in both sections, confirming F340. The shift mechanism is selective lexical exclusion: specific aiin-body compound words (qokaiin, okaiin, chaiin, chain, shaiin, sheaiin) are essentially absent from the pharma section in A-dialect. This is not a positive affinity to ol-body words but a negative constraint on aiin-body compounds in pharma.
