## Major Findings

### Finding 1: ch↔sh MUTUAL AVOIDANCE in B-dialect (Bidirectional)

B-dialect: sh→ch = 0.555× AND ch→sh = 0.567×. Both are strongly depleted.

The two most frequent onset classes in B-dialect (ch=17.7%, sh=10.5%) AVOID each other in BOTH SUCCESSION DIRECTIONS. This is not a unidirectional rule — it's a mutual non-succession constraint.

In A-dialect: sh→ch = 0.565× (consistent with B) BUT ch→sh = 0.731× (borderline — less strongly depleted). The sh→ch depletion is cross-dialect; ch→sh avoidance is weaker in A.

The ch↔sh avoidance in B is the strongest systematic depletion in the corpus (both directions ≈ 0.56×) despite ch and sh being two of the three most frequent onset classes. If they were independent, they would follow each other ≈(0.177×0.105×11511) = 214 times; actual: ch→sh = 119 (44% below expected).

### Finding 2: Five Cross-Dialect Invariant Elevated Pairs

**(a) y→y: 2.49× (B), 2.85× (A)** — y self-succession is the strongest invariant. Known from prior work (battery metric y_lf includes y→y). The new quantification at 2.5-2.9× clarifies the magnitude.

**(b) sh→qok: 1.97× (B), 1.74× (A)** — already analyzed in exp_323 (Q004 closed). Confirmed as a cross-dialect structural invariant.

**(c) ok→ok: 1.32× (B), 1.96× (A)** — ok SELF-SUCCESSION is elevated in both dialects. NEW finding. ok-onset words tend to follow other ok-onset words. This is analogous to the y→y self-succession rule but at 1.3-2.0× rather than 2.5-2.9×.

**(d) y→d: 1.37× (B), 1.38× (A)** — y-onset words are followed by d-onset words more than expected in BOTH dialects (17+37=54 observations). NEW finding. The y-onset/d-onset co-succession is cross-dialect invariant with consistent magnitude. This may be a content/formula coupling: y+gallows formula words (exp_322) tend to be followed by daiin-dominated d-onset words. Alternatively, y-onset and d-onset share a similar grammatical function (formula + content word sequence).

**(e) qok→s: 1.36× (B), 1.39× (A)** — qok-onset words tend to be followed by s-onset words. NEW finding. Both dialects show consistent ~1.37× enrichment with N=22 (B) and N=6 (A) observations. Small sample in A but consistent direction.

### Finding 3: d self-succession elevated in B (1.54×) but not A (1.28×)

B-dialect d→d = 1.54× (elevated); A-dialect d→d = 1.28 (near-neutral). This is a B-specific property: d-onset words in B-dialect tend to repeat within lines more than expected. Given that d-onset is dominated by daiin (21.7% of B-d tokens), this means daiin→daiin sequences are enriched in B-dialect lines.

### Finding 4: A-dialect qok↔ok bidirectional coupling absent in B

A-dialect: qok→ok = 2.03× AND ok→qok = 1.68×. Both are strongly elevated.
B-dialect: qok→ok = 1.16× (neutral) AND ok→qok = 0.96× (neutral).

A-dialect has a strong bidirectional coupling between qok and ok onset classes that is completely absent in B. This is the most striking discordant pair: A qok↔ok functions like B's qok↔ch coupling (which is 1.15-1.23× in B but present).

In A-dialect, qok and ok form a coupled word pair (qok-words tend to be followed by ok-words and vice versa), while in B-dialect, ch plays the coupling role for qok.

### Finding 5: A-dialect qok self-succession (1.75×) stronger than B (1.17×)

qok→qok: A=1.75× vs B=1.17×. A-dialect qok-onset words have elevated self-succession (though not as extreme as y→y). B-dialect qok self-succession is near-neutral. This contributes to A-dialect's stronger closed paradigm sizes (A-qok CP90=57.3% vs B-qok=18.2%): A-qok words repeatedly follow other A-qok words, creating longer sequences of qok-onset words within lines.

The complete bigram matrix reveals a STRUCTURED ONSET-CLASS GRAMMAR that goes well beyond the battery metrics:

**Cross-dialect invariants (both B and A):**
- sh→qok: enriched 1.7-2.0× (coupling rule)
- sh→ch: depleted 0.55× (avoidance rule)
- y→y: enriched 2.5-2.9× (self-succession rule)
- ok→ok: enriched 1.3-2.0× (self-succession rule, NEW)
- y→d: enriched 1.37× (coupling rule, NEW)
- qok→s: enriched 1.36× (coupling rule, NEW)

**B-dialect specific:**
- ch→sh: depleted 0.57× (bidirectional ch↔sh mutual avoidance, NEW)
- d→d: enriched 1.54× (d self-succession)
- qok→y: depleted 0.64× (qok avoids y)
- d→qok: depleted 0.59× (d avoids qok)

**A-dialect specific:**
- qok→ok: enriched 2.03× (A-specific coupling, NEW)
- ok→qok: enriched 1.68× (bidirectional, NEW)
- sh→sh: enriched 1.31× (sh self-succession in A only)
- qok→qok: enriched 1.75× (qok self-succession in A, neutral in B)

The grammar has a clear STRUCTURE: template classes (ch/sh/ok/qok) show both coupling rules (qok↔ch in B; sh→qok in both; qok↔ok in A) and avoidance rules (ch↔sh in B; qok→y depleted). Grammar-word classes (y/d) show self-succession (y→y) and specific couplings (y→d).

## Limitations

- Enrichment values for pairs with small N (e.g., qok→s: N=22 in B, N=6 in A; y→d: N=17 B, N=37 A) have higher variance. The consistency of direction in both dialects strengthens confidence despite small N.
- 'other' onset class not included — words with unclassified onsets contribute to total word counts but are not analyzed as transition targets/sources
- The analysis uses MID words only (1:-1 positions). LF and LL onset-class bigrams may show different patterns
- Independence null model P(onset1)×P(onset2) does not correct for section-level clustering (if sh and qok words cluster in the same sections, some enrichment reflects spatial clustering not sequential grammar)
