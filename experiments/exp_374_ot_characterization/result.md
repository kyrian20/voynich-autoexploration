## Findings

### Finding 1: A ot Is LL-Enriched (E=1.24×)/LF-Near-Baseline (0.91×), Mirror-Image of A qot (LF-Enriched/LL-Depleted)

A-dialect ot positional: LF=0.91×, pos1=0.69×, MID=1.03×, pre_LL=1.15×, LL=1.24×. A ot is slightly LL-enriched, not strongly LF-enriched. A qot (LF=1.65×, LL=0.72×) is the complement: high LF, low LL. Together, qot and ot form a COMPLEMENTARY pair in A-dialect: qot opens lines, ot closes them. B-dialect: both ot (E=0.33×) and qot (E=0.51×) are strongly LF-depleted — the LF-enrichment of qot is an A-dialect phenomenon only.

### Finding 2: A ot Self-Succession Peaks at pre_LL→LL (E=2.65×) — Mirror-Image of A qot (pre_LL=0.00×)

A ot→ot: LF→pos1=0.38× (depleted), MID_inner=2.19×, pre_LL→LL=2.65× (N=7/60). The self-succession INTENSIFIES toward line end for ot (peak at pre_LL→LL). For qot: self-succession ABSENT at pre_LL→LL (0/36). This mirror-image structure suggests A-dialect lines have a spatial grammar:
- LF region: qot self-succession chains (E=3.34× at LF→pos1)  
- MID/pre_LL/LL region: ot self-succession chains (E=2.19-2.65×)

### Finding 3: B ot Successor Distribution Is DEPLETED Against Major Template Classes — ot/qot Form Closed Clusters

B-dialect ot successors: ch=0.83× (depleted), sh=0.73× (depleted), qok=0.81× (depleted), d=0.82× (depleted), ok=0.98× (near baseline). ONLY ot (E=1.93×) and qot (E=1.29×) are elevated. This means: after B ot, the next word is almost always another ot or qot. B ot forms **closed ot/qot clusters** in the line body — ot-class words appear in runs of 2-4 words (ot→ot→ot or ot→qot→ot) rather than transitioning to other classes.

Compare A-dialect: ot→d=1.18×, ot→sh=1.11×, ot→ok=1.21×, ot→y=1.19× (near baseline, not depleted). A-dialect ot is less isolated — it transitions to other classes at ~baseline rates.

### Finding 4: B ot pre_LL→LL = ol at LL (E=1.68×, N=18/192) and A ot pre_LL→LL = ol at LL (E=2.40×, N=4/60) — Cross-Dialect ot→ol Line-Terminal Rule

B ot→ol pre_LL→LL E=1.68× (18/192). A ot→ol pre_LL→LL E=2.40× (4/60, directional but consistent). Top pairs: otedy→ol(2), otaiin→ol(2), otain→ol(2), otar→ol(2). The bare word "ol" is the top successor. This is a cross-dialect rule: ot at the penultimate position → bare "ol" at line-end. Architecture: ot pre_LL → ol LL line-terminal formula (cross-dialect, connects to B ol LL enrichment from F368).

### Finding 5: A ot→y Is MID-Concentrated (E=1.74×, N=14/176) — Parallel to A qot→y (MID E=2.86×)

A ot→y: MID_inner=1.74× (14/176), LF→pos1=0.00× (absent), pre_LL→LL=0.73× (near baseline). A ot→y is a MID succession rule, parallel to A qot→y (MID=2.86×) from F377. Both qot and ot in MID position → y-onset successor. This may reflect A-dialect grammar: {qot,ot} class words in MID position tend to transition to y-class words. Top ot→y pairs: otaiin→ykaiin(2) (body-parallel: aiin body preserved, onset changed ot→y).

### Finding 6: A ot Self-Succession Same-Word Rate Is 28% (Higher Than Other Classes); Body-Parallel Templating

A-dialect ot→ot same-word rate = 28% (7/25), higher than y (7.3%), ch (3.4%), ok/qok (class-level). This suggests A-dialect ot has some specific word-type self-repeating: otaiin→otaiin(2) (the top pair). B-dialect ot→ot same-word rate = 7.2% (class-level, like other B classes). B ot shows body-parallel templating: otedy→qotedy(3), oty→qoty(2) — same body, onset changes ot→qot.

### Finding 7: ot and qot Body Structures Are Parallel Across Dialects

A-dialect ot bodies: otol(8.6%), otaiin(7.8%), oty(7.8%), otal(6.2%), otchol(5.9%), otchy(5.7%). No single dominant; ch-compound bodies ~17%.
A-dialect qot bodies: qotchy(15.7%), qoty(8.9%), qotol(7.3%), qotor(5.6%), qotaiin(4.4%).
The shared body forms (y, ol, aiin, al, chol, chy) between ot and qot confirm these two are STRUCTURALLY PARALLEL onset classes with shared body vocabulary — the onset (ot vs qot) is interchangeable while bodies are drawn from the same template vocabulary. This supports body-parallel templating as the mechanism for ot↔qot succession (same body, onset substitution).

H588 ANSWERED. ot is a MID/LL-enriched template class in both dialects (B LF=0.33× strongly depleted). A ot self-succession peaks at pre_LL→LL (E=2.65×) — MIRROR-IMAGE of A qot (pre_LL=0.00×). A ot and A qot form a COMPLEMENTARY spatial pair: qot opens lines (LF cluster), ot closes lines (LL cluster). B ot successor distribution depleted against major classes (ch/sh/qok/d all 0.73-0.83×) — B ot forms closed ot/qot clusters. Cross-dialect rule: ot at pre_LL → ol at LL (A=2.40×, B=1.68×). A ot→y MID (E=1.74×) parallels A qot→y (E=2.86×). Body structure: ot and qot share body vocabulary (y/ol/aiin/al/chol/chy) — structurally parallel classes supporting body-parallel templating. Architecture: ot requires class-memory rule (MID+pre_LL self-succession) and a pre_LL→ol LL terminal rule; A-dialect needs SEPARATE positional scoping for qot (LF cluster) vs ot (LL cluster).
