## H345: B-cthedy/cthey LF FORMULAS — FRONT-ENRICHED, NOT JUST LF-ENRICHED

**REVISION**: Both cthedy and cthey are enriched at BOTH LF and second position (not just LF):

| word   | n  | LF rate | LF enrich | second rate | second enrich | MID   | LL    |
|--------|----|---------|-----------|-------------|---------------|-------|-------|
| cthedy | 11 | 18.2%   | **1.53×** | 18.2%       | **1.55×**     | 54.5% | 9.1%  |
| cthey  | 10 | 20.0%   | **1.69×** | 20.0%       | **1.70×**     | 50.0% | 10.0% |

Both words are enriched at the **front of lines** (LF+second combined) and depleted at MID/LL. This is a "line-front" enrichment, not specifically "line-opening."

**cthedy@LF cases (n=2) — second words:**
- f76 [bio]: `cthedy oteol chdar chckhy...` → second = `oteol` (onset=ot)
- f107 [stars]: `cthedy lshedy cheolchear or,alam...` → second = `lshedy` (onset=l)

**cthey@LF cases (n=2) — second words:**
- f106 [stars]: `cthey chey okal chey keeyrain...` → second = `chey` (onset=ch_e)
- f108 [stars]: `cthey chedain chedy okedy...` → second = `chedain` (onset=ch_e)

**PATTERN**: cthey@LF is always followed by a ch_e-onset second word (2/2 cases). cthedy@LF has diverse successors (ot, l). But n=2 for each is too small to confirm a formula.

**No systematic line-opening formula detected** comparable to A-dialect's [ychor@LF → cthor@second]. cthedy/cthey are front-enriched but do not show a fixed two-word opening sequence. This may reflect that B-dialect line-structure is less formulaic than A at this level.

**cthedy@second cases (n=2):**
- f31 [herbal]: `dair cthedy qokedy okeody...` — dair precedes (d-onset)
- f114 [stars]: `cheo cthedy qotchedy qotaiin...` — cheo precedes (ch_e-onset)

**cthey@second cases (n=2):**
- f33 [herbal]: `daiin<->ydain cthey dols<->sheky ar,aiin` — daiin compound precedes
- f104 [stars]: `y,cheoraiin cthey chol sheody...` — y-word precedes

No consistent precursor pattern at second either.

## H346: [d][cth][d] TRIGRAMS — NOT PREDOMINANTLY [daiin][cth][daiin]

**Total [d][cth][d] in A-dialect: 16**

| | Count | Fraction |
|---|---|---|
| daiin before cth | 8 | 8/16 = 50% |
| daiin after cth | 5 | 5/16 = 31% |
| daiin BOTH sides | 2 | 2/16 = 12.5% |
| other d-word combinations | 9 | 9/16 = 56% |

**The [d][cth][d] pattern is NOT predominantly [daiin][cth][daiin]**. Only 2/16 have daiin on both sides. Daiin is the most common single d-word (8/16 before; 5/16 after), but d-class diversity is high — other d-words: dol (3×), dam (2×), dain, dchain, dchol, dockhy, dalchey, dar, dair.

**cth-word in middle (n=16):**
- cthol: 5 (dominant — cthol is specifically the cth-word that gets flanked by d-words)
- cthy: 2
- cthar, cthal, cthoiin, cthain, cthody, cthor, ctheody, ctheey: 1 each
- cth-compounds: 2 (cth<->dain, cthey<->ykol)

**cthol (5/16) is the dominant middle word in [d][cth][d]** — more than any other cth-word. The 5 cthol cases:
- f6: `daiin cthol dol`
- f30: `daiin cthol doiin`
- f49: `dchol cthol daiin` (dchol left, daiin right)
- f53: `dockhy cthol dam`
- f89: `daiin cthol daiin` (one of the 2 [daiin][cth][daiin] cases)

**Pattern**: [daiin][cthol][non-daiin-d] is the dominant sub-pattern — daiin introduces cthol, which is then followed by a different d-word (dol, doiin, dam, dchol). The [daiin][cthol] bigram is established (6 cases total from exp_167); the following d-word is diverse.

**[ch][d][cth]=17 (highest onset-trigram) — specific words:**
- D-word between ch and cth: daiin=12/17 (71%) — highly daiin-dominated
- [ch][daiin][cth] is the dominant form — the highest-frequency cth onset-trigram is [ch-word][daiin][cth-word]
- cth at end: cthy=4, cthol=3, diverse others

## H347: B-cthdy LL-ENRICHMENT CONFIRMED — PARALLELS A-cthy

**B-dialect cthdy positional profile (n=6 exact matches):**

| slot   | count | rate  | baseline | enrichment |
|--------|-------|-------|----------|------------|
| LF     | 0     | 0.0%  | 11.9%    | **0.00×**  |
| second | 0     | 0.0%  | 11.7%    | **0.00×**  |
| MID    | 4     | 66.7% | 64.5%    | 1.03×      |
| LL     | 2     | 33.3% | 11.9%    | **2.81×**  |

**cthdy NEVER appears at LF or second (0.00×)** and is strongly LL-enriched (2.81×). This DIRECTLY PARALLELS A-dialect cthy:

| word   | dialect | LF     | second | MID   | LL       |
|--------|---------|--------|--------|-------|----------|
| cthdy  | B       | 0.00×  | 0.00×  | 1.03× | **2.81×** |
| cthy   | A       | 0.15×  | 0.93×  | 1.09× | **1.63×** |

Both are LL-enriched line-closers, both avoid LF. B-cthdy has even stronger LL-enrichment (2.81× vs 1.63×) but smaller n.

**B-cthdy LL cases (n=2):**
- f78 [bio]: `solkeedy sheckhy qokedy otal,shedy otar olchedy cthdy` — cthdy is final word; pred=olchedy
- f108 [stars]: `qokeeo,dar chedam chlal okaldain sheed lchedy l,keedy ched,kel cthdy` — cthdy is final word; pred=ched,kel

Both LL cases are in genuinely B-dialect sections (bio, stars). In both cases, cthdy is the absolute last word of a long B-dialect line, preceded by ch-type compounds.

**cthdy is UNIQUE among B -dy forms as a LL-CLOSER:**

| B-suffix word | LL/total | LL rate | LL enrich |
|---------------|----------|---------|-----------|
| **cthdy**     | 2/6      | 33.3%   | **2.81×** |
| chedy         | 23/415   | 5.5%    | 0.47×     |

All standard B-suffix -dy words are LL-DEPLETED (0.14-0.47×). cthdy alone is LL-enriched (2.81×). This confirms that cthdy's role is grammatically different from chedy/shedy/qokedy — it functions as a terminal element, not a medial content word.
