# exp_396: A Dialect sh-pos1 LF×pos2 Matrix (H616)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis tested:** H616 — Does A dialect have the same five B sh-pos1 grammar types?
**Outcome:** ANSWERED — A sh-pos1 grammar is structurally different from B. A sh-pos1 baseline: sh dominant (17.1%) vs B qok dominant (27.1%). A y→sh→ch=0.62×(DEPLETED) — OPPOSITE of B y→sh→ch=2.94×. A y→sh→ok=2.47×(N=3). A d→sh→sh depleted (0.59×). A sh→sh→sh=1.72× (A sh self-chain extends 3 positions). Most A effects weaker than B counterparts.

## 4. A-Specific sh-pos1 Features

**A sh→sh self-chain:** sh→sh→sh=1.72×(N=5/17) — sh at LF + sh at pos1 routes back to sh at pos2. This extends the A sh self-succession chain to 3 consecutive sh positions. Not previously documented.

**A d→sh→sh=0.59×/ch=0.75× (both depleted):** In A, d→sh lines avoid both sh self-succession and ch at pos2. The pos2 of A d→sh lines is distributed (qo=1.67×, other=1.63×). By contrast, B d→sh is near-Markovian (all E values 0.59–1.39×).

**A k→sh→qok=2.71×(N=4/16):** k at LF routes to qok at pos2 via sh. This parallels B qok→sh→k=4.08× (F398) — in B, qok at LF routes to k; in A, k at LF routes to qok — a quasi-reciprocal relationship. Both are template class transitions at pos2 after sh.

**A o→sh→cth=4.13×(N=4/15):** o onset (rare) at LF routes to cth at pos2 after sh. cth is an A-dominant class (F388: A cth=4.0% vs B=0.3%). This is A-specific.

The B five-type sh-pos1 taxonomy does NOT map 1:1 to A. The content-formula type inverts (A y→sh→ch depleted), the template-class type is absent in A (qok too rare at A LF), and A-specific features emerge (sh self-chain, k→sh→qok, o→sh→cth).
