## H129: In Aiin-Dom, she- Words Have NO Sequential Grammar Bias (Neutral Except qo-Suppression); the e-Infix Is Genuinely Reversed

### she-→X Following Distribution by Register

| Following | Aiin-dom she- | Enr | Y-dom she- | Enr | B she- | Enr |
|-----------|-------------|-----|------------|-----|--------|-----|
| **qo** | 2.9% (n=1) | **0.41×** | **25.2%** | **2.07×** | **42.1%** | **2.02×** |
| ch | 23.5% (n=8) | 1.02× | 13.4% | 0.73× | 9.2% | 0.59× |
| o | 17.6% | 1.22× | 13.4% | 0.76× | 19.7% | 0.84× |
| sh | 8.8% | 0.68× | 10.1% | 1.08× | 8.7% | 0.92× |
| other | 14.7% | 1.29× | 8.4% | 0.78× | 8.4% | 0.63× |

**In aiin-dom (n=34), she- prefix words are NEUTRAL for all onset classes except qo (0.41× suppression).** There is no enriched following class — she- follows base rates for ch (1.02×), o (1.22×), sh (0.68×). In y-dom and B, she- strongly predicts qo (2.07×/2.02×) and suppresses ch (0.73×/0.59×). **The e-infix morphological marker has ZERO sequential grammar signal in aiin-dom** — it neither predicts qo nor predicts any alternative onset class.

### Top she- Words in Aiin-Dom

Aiin-dom she-words: shey (12), sheey (5), sheol (3), sheo (3), sheky (2), shees (2). **Aiin-dom she-words are predominantly MINIMAL FORMS** (shey=she+y, sheey=she+ey, sheol=she+ol) — they are the simplest possible e-infix words with no intervening stem. These minimal forms follow base rates for subsequent words because they are not grammatically specialized qo-predictors.

In contrast, y-dom's longer she-words (sheol, sheey appear at lower rates but in contexts where she- specifically precedes qo) have the qo-coupling property.

### Interpretation

The e-infix (she-) does NOT encode qo-coupling in aiin-dom. In y-dom and B, she- is a qo-predictor (2.07-2.02×). In aiin-dom, she- is neutral. The aiin-dom she-words appear to be SHORT SUFFIX FORMS (shey, sheey, sheol) that don't carry the qo-anticipatory function — they're using the e-infix notation for a different morphological role (likely suffix formation, given shey/sheey/sheol = she+y/ey/ol).

### H129 Verdict: SUPPORTED — she- Is Grammatically Neutral in Aiin-Dom (NOT ch-Predictor); The e-Infix Encodes Different Information by Register

In aiin-dom, she-words have no sequential grammar bias except qo-suppression (0.41×). They are NOT ch-predictors (1.02× = at base rate). The aiin-dom she-words are predominantly minimal suffix forms (shey, sheey, sheol) rather than the longer qo-anticipatory she-forms of y-dom and B. The e-infix morphological marker is SEMANTICALLY INERT for sequential grammar in aiin-dom — the grammar-morphology dissociation is complete: aiin-dom uses d→qo (1.90×) as its primary qo-predictor while the she-infix carries no qo information.

## H128: Aiin-Dom's -ol/-or Dominance Is Global (All Onset Classes) and Uses the SAME WORDS — It's a Rate Difference, Not a Different Paradigm

| Onset | Aiin-dom -ol% | Aiin-dom -or% | Combined | Y-dom -ol% | Y-dom -or% | Combined |
|-------|-------------|-------------|---------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **ch** | **19.2%** | **18.0%** | **37.2%** | 14.0% | 11.4% | 25.4% |
| **sh** | **18.8%** | 11.6% | **30.4%** | 12.0% | 9.9% | 22.0% |
| **qo** | 10.8% | 14.7% | **25.5%** | 7.6% | 9.2% | 16.8% |
| **k** | 13.9% | 12.5% | **26.4%** | 16.7% | 9.5% | 26.2% |
| o | 13.7% | 7.8% | 21.6% | 7.0% | 6.7% | 13.8% |

**The -ol/-or elevation in aiin-dom affects ALL onset classes**, not just specific ones. ch-onset: 37.2% vs y-dom 25.4% (+11.8%); sh-onset: 30.4% vs 22.0% (+8.4%); qo-onset: 25.5% vs 16.8% (+8.7%). This is a GLOBAL increase in liquid-final suffix usage.

### Same Words, Different Rates

**The SAME words (chol, shol, cthol) dominate -ol in both registers.** This is NOT a different paradigm — it's the same words appearing at HIGHER FREQUENCY in aiin-dom. Similarly for -or: chor (47/51), shor (17/25), cthor (10/11) top both lists.

### ch-Onset Suffix Paradigm Comparison

| Suffix | Aiin-dom ch | Y-dom ch | Difference |
|--------|------------|---------|-----------|

**Aiin-dom ch-words use -ol/-or 7-12% more than y-dom; y-dom uses -ey/-eey 4-8% more than aiin-dom.** This is COMPLEMENTARY SUBSTITUTION at the suffix level: aiin-dom -ol/-or replaces y-dom -ey/-eey for ch-onset words. The underlying ch-stems (chol vs chey; chor vs cheey) use DIFFERENT SUFFIXES in the two registers.

### H128 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (BUT REVISED) — Aiin-Dom -ol/-or Is the Same Vocabulary at Higher Rates Plus Complementary Suffix Substitution for -ey/-eey

Aiin-dom -ol/-or dominance is NOT a completely different paradigm — the top -ol/-or words (chol, shol, cthol, chor, shor) appear in BOTH registers. The difference is: (1) higher rate across all onset classes in aiin-dom; (2) complementary substitution where -ey/-eey suffixes in y-dom correspond to -ol/-or suffixes in aiin-dom. The same ch/sh/cth stems appear with -ol/-or in aiin-dom but with -ey/-eey in y-dom. This IS a different suffix paradigm: the liquid-final paradigm (-ol/-or) dominates aiin-dom while the glide-final paradigm (-ey/-eey) dominates y-dom.

## H130: B Paragraph Structure Is NOT Parseable from Word Features — Markers Are Probabilistic, Not Deterministic; p-Onset Is NOT B Para-Start Marker

### Critical Correction: p-Onset Does NOT Mark B Para-Starts

**p-onset first-word at B para-starts = 0.0%** (40 p-onset first-words out of 2416 lines, NONE at para-starts). The p-onset para-start claim from F087 was INCORRECT for Currier B. B para-start first words are dominated by: **o-onset (36.6%), qo-onset (18.8%), sh-onset (14.9%), d-onset (9.9%), ch-onset (8.9%)**.

**B paragraph structure: para-starts are o-onset dominated** (36.6% first-word o-onset), not p-onset marked. This corrects the F087 tripartite model.

### Para-Final Prediction Accuracy

| Feature | Para-final rate | Within-para rate | Enrichment | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|-----------|---------|--------|---|
| last-word o-onset | 32.7% | 28.0% | 1.17× | 4.8% | 32.7% | 8.4% |
| last-word d-onset | 10.9% | 9.3% | 1.17× | 4.8% | 10.9% | 6.7% |
| last-word -am | 2.0% | 12.6% | 0.16× | — | — | — |

**Para-boundary prediction from word features is near-useless** (F1 < 10% for all tested features). The o-onset last-word enrichment at para-finals (1.17×) is real but far too weak for parsing — 28.2% of ALL B lines end with o-onset, making precision only 4.8%.

### -am Suppression at Para-Final Confirmed

**-am at within-para line-ends = 12.6% (292/2315 lines); -am at para-final = 2.0% (2/101 lines)**. This is the strongest categorical difference found: -am is 6.3× more common at within-para line-ends than para-final line-ends. The -am line-ending marker is definitively WITHIN-para only.

### Para-Final Last-Word Onset Distribution

B para-final last word onset: o=32.7%, qo=12.9%, ch=12.9%, other=10.9%, d=10.9%, sh=8.9%, s=5.0%. The o-onset dominance at para-final is real (32.7% vs base ~23.3%) but not overwhelming — 2/3 of para-final lines have non-o-onset last words.

### H130 Verdict: FAILED — B Paragraph Structure Is NOT Parseable from Word Features; p-Onset Correction Required

B paragraph structure is NOT parseable from word features alone (F1 < 10%). All tested features (o-onset, d-onset, -am, -edy) fail to distinguish para-final from within-para lines with useful precision/recall. The markers are PROBABILISTIC CLASS-LEVEL EFFECTS, not deterministic signals. **CRITICAL CORRECTION to F087**: p-onset does NOT mark B para-starts (0.0% rate). B para-starts use o-onset (36.6%) and qo-onset (18.8%) first words. The tripartite model should be revised: [o/qo/sh-onset PARA-START] → [-am LINE-ENDs, 12.6%] → [o-onset PARA-END, 32.7%].
