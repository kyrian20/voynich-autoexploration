# exp_143: cthor→ot Bigram & cth-Runs in A-Herbal

## H283: cthor→ot BIGRAM — PARTIALLY CONFIRMED

cthor is followed by ot-onset at 21.9% of cases (7/32), with a recurrent "daiin cthor ot*" triple construction.

ot-onset is the second most common onset after cthor (23.3%), far above the baseline ot-rate in A-herbal (~7%). The 6.18× enrichment (exp_142) is confirmed: cthor→ot is a real bigram preference.

Folio distribution: f9, f10, f14, f19, f24, f42, f52 — spanning early to late A-herbal (not concentrated in early folios). cthor→ot is not specifically an early-A-herbal feature.

**Recurrent construction**: "daiin cthor [ot-word]" appears 3 times (f10, f14, f19): `daiin cthor otydy sain`, `daiin cthor oty`, `daiin cthor otam`. This is a specific nominal phrase formula: **[daiin-particle][cthor-nominal][ot-modifier]**. The most natural reading is a template: [determiner/particle][shape-nominal (cthor)][quantity/type-modifier (ot-word)].

### Interpretation
cthor has two positional behaviors:
1. **After daiin (A-particle)**: daiin→cthor→ot-word — a three-element nominal phrase
2. **In general mid-line position**: followed by ch (26.7%) or other content words

The cthor→ot construction is semantically coherent: cthor may be a "round/container" nominal form and ot-words (otol/oty/otam/otal) may be modifiers or quantities. The construction appears across A-herbal but doesn't explain the cthor-specific decline in late A-herbal — since f42 and f52 (late A-herbal) also show cthor→ot.

**Revised explanation of cthor decline**: The faster decline of cthor in late A-herbal (T3/T1=0.24) is NOT primarily due to the cthor→ot construction disappearing. More likely, cthor as a whole (all its usages) simply declines uniformly, possibly reflecting reduced focus on "round/container" botanical forms in later herbal folios.

## H284: cth-RUNS IN A-HERBAL — NOT CONFIRMED (RARE)

cth-runs of 2+ consecutive words are present but rare (6.8% of cth-occurrences are in runs; 19 total 2+ runs out of 280 cth-word occurrences).

No dominant cth-bigram exists. The self-succession enrichment observed in exp_142 (cth before cthol: 1.55×; cth after cthol: 2.25×) reflects relative enrichment from a low base — adjacent cth-word pairs are enriched vs baseline but remain rare in absolute terms.

### Folios With Most cth-Runs
f1, f15, f18, f22, f28, f44 each have 2 runs of 2+ cth-words. These are spread across early to mid A-herbal (f44 is relatively late). No strong early-folio concentration.

### Near-Consecutive cth-Pairs (Separated by 1 Word)
Total: 9 near-pairs (all unique). Examples:
- `cthor+[ot]+cthol` — cthor then cthol with ot-word between (related to H283 cthor→ot finding)
- `cthol+[d]+cthom` — cth-near-pair with d-onset separator
- `cthy+[ch]+cthaiin` — alternating cth and ch-class words

### Interpretation
cth-runs are rare (6.8% of cth-occurrences). The cth self-succession signal in exp_142 is a relative enrichment from a small absolute base. There is no dominant "cth-cluster formula" — consecutive cth-pairs are diverse and each appears only once. This rules out a fixed cth-nominal phrase template (like a recurring "cthol+cthy" construction). cth-words appear predominantly isolated (93.2%) in the text, with occasional accidental adjacency.

## Summary of New Findings (F150)

1. **cthor→ot bigram is REAL (21.9% of cthor usages) but NOT specifically early-A-herbal**: the "daiin cthor ot*" triple construction (3/7 cases) is a recurrent formula but appears across f9-f52. Varied ot-words (otol/oty/otydy/otam/otal) — no dominant specific pair. cthor's faster decline in late A-herbal is not due to cthor→ot disappearing (appears in f42, f52 which are late).
2. **cth-RUNS ARE RARE (6.8% in 2+ runs; all bigrams are hapax)**: 93.2% of cth-words are isolated. No dominant consecutive-cth formula. The cth self-succession enrichment (1.55×-2.25×) from exp_142 is real but reflects small absolute counts. cth-words are predominantly isolated content words, not clustered nominals.
3. **REVISED interpretation of cthor decline**: cthor's T3/T1=0.24 decline is not explained by the cthor→ot construction disappearing (late A-herbal still uses it). More likely: cthor as a whole class of "round/container"-associated botanical content words is less frequent in later herbal entries, while cthol (more abstract medial particle) maintains usage.
