# exp_346: Why Is A-Dialect Cosmo (f86v5) Balanced in {ol,or}/{aiin,ain}? (H545)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H545 — A-dialect cosmo section has a lower {ol,or}/{aiin,ain} ratio (1.25×) compared to herbal (3.98×) and pharma (17.88×). Why?
**Result:** H545 ANSWERED. A-dialect "cosmo" = single folio f86v5 (cosmological rosette, N=344). The near-balanced ratio is NOT a preserved aiin vocabulary but rather a structural consequence: f86v5 has a radically different onset-class distribution (y=17.7%, ot=11.0% dominant; ch=7.3%, d=4.7% — both strongly depleted vs herbal); many bare aiin/ar/al words without recognized onset prefix (10.5% "other" = onset-less); and template-onset words have low N (ch N=25, qok/ok N=10-13) making {ol,or}/{aiin,ain} ratios noisy. f86v5 is label/diagram text, not running prose.

## Findings

### Finding 1: A-Dialect "Cosmo" = Single Folio f86v5 (Cosmological Rosette)

The A-dialect "cosmo" section in the F342 analysis is entirely contained in one folio: f86v5, the large 9-panel cosmological diagram page (N=344, edy_rate=3.8%). This is NOT a text section — it is label/caption text surrounding cosmological diagrams. Its word grammar is fundamentally different from herbal prose text.

### Finding 2: f86v5 Has a Radically Different Onset Distribution

Compared to A-dialect herbal (N=6306):
- y-onset: **17.7% vs 6.0% (2.97× enriched)** — diagram labels are y-onset heavy
- ot-onset: **11.0% vs 4.8% (2.30× enriched)** — circular/term labels
- "other" onset: **10.5% vs ~1-2%** — bare words like "aiin", "ar", "al" appear without onset prefix
- ch-onset: **7.3% vs 20.7% (0.35× depleted)** — the dominant A-dialect template onset is absent
- d-onset: **4.7% vs 16.2% (0.29× depleted)**

### Finding 3: The "Balanced" Ratio Is a Low-N Artifact for Template Onsets

With ch=25, qok=13, ok=10, qot=13 words, each onset has 1-3 aiin/ain and 0-2 ol/or tokens. These are statistically meaningless ratios. The "cosmo={aiin,ain}:{ol,or}=1.25×" finding in F342 was computed over all onset classes combined, and was dominated by: (a) bare "aiin" words (onset=other, contributing to the aiin count), and (b) ot-onset words (aiin=9, ol=2, ratio=0.22×) which pull down the ol/or side.

### Finding 4: f86v5 Is a Structural Outlier — Diagram Labels, Not Prose

The top word types in f86v5 (otal=7, otam=6, otaiin=6, ytaiin=6, ykaiin=6) suggest short labels attached to diagram elements. The y+k-compounds (ykaiin, ykar) and ot-compound words (otaiin, otal, otam) are characteristic of VMS label conventions. The high "other" onset fraction (aiin, ar, al as bare words) also fits a label register where onset-less words function as standalone markers.

## Summary

The A-dialect cosmo section is not comparable to herbal/pharma as a text section. It is a single folio (f86v5) with diagram-label grammar: y/ot-onset dominant, ch/d depleted, many bare words. The "balanced {aiin,ain}:{ol,or}" finding is an artifact of this different grammar + low N per onset class. H545 is answered: cosmo is structurally unlike A-dialect prose sections; its onset distribution matches diagram-label register rather than body text.
