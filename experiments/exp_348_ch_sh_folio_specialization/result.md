# exp_348: Folio ch/sh Specialization — What Determines ch vs sh Dominance? (H530)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H530 — 15 B-dialect folios are ch-dominant (>70% ch). What drives this? (A) Section-specific formula; (B) scribe/hand change; (C) ch taking on content-word roles.
**Result:** H530 ANSWERED. The folio-level ch/sh ratio is primarily section-specific with two distinct mechanisms: (1) Bio section is strictly balanced (ratio=0.437-0.571, all 10 bio folios) because bio uses ch+edy/ey and sh+edy/ey symmetrically; (2) Ch-dominant folios (text/cosmo-middle/some herbal) have ch expanding into ol/aiin-body content-word roles (cheody, chol, chedaiin) while sh remains edy-class only. The ch-dominance is driven by the extra ch-onset vocabulary not accessible to sh-onset.

## Findings

### Finding 1: Bio Section Has a Specific ch/sh Symmetry Constraint

Bio section is uniquely constrained: all 10 bio folios have ratio 0.437–0.571. The folio-level variance is near-zero. This cannot be sampling noise — it is a section-specific grammatical constraint that enforces approximate ch/sh parity.

The mechanism: in bio, both ch and sh use the edy/ey template class symmetrically. The top bio ch-words are chedy(24.6%), chey(10.5%), chckhy(5.2%); the top bio sh-words are shedy(31.8%), shey(11.7%), shckhy(4.1%), sheckhy(2.9%). Every major ch-word type in bio has a structural sh-parallel.

### Finding 2: Bio Section Has sh+ckhy Compounds (shckhy, sheckhy) Essentially Absent Elsewhere

Bio sh words include shckhy(4.1%) and sheckhy(2.9%) which are nearly absent from ch-dominant folios (0.6% and 0.3%). These bio-specific sh+ckhy compounds are part of the bio formula. Ch-dominant sections lack these sh+ckhy words — they are bio-exclusive.

### Finding 3: Ch-Dominant Folios Have ch Expanding Into ol/aiin Body Vocabulary

In ch-dominant folios, ch-words include cheody(2.8%), chody(1.6%), chol(3.5%), chedaiin(1.9%) — these are ch+ol/or/aiin body words (content-class body vocabulary). In bio, chol=1.7% only (E=2.03× enriched in ch-dominant). Cheody and chody are ABSENT from bio (0/706 ch-tokens in bio). 

The ch-dominance is partly explained by ch gaining access to content-word body vocabulary (ol/aiin body = "content-class ch words") while sh in the same section remains edy/ey class-only. More ch vocabulary slots → more ch tokens → higher ch/sh ratio.

### Finding 4: Ch-Dominant Sections Show LOWER chedy Fraction (E=0.38× vs bio)

Paradoxically, ch-dominant folios have LESS chedy (9.4%) than bio (24.6%, E=0.38×). The ch-dominance is not from more chedy — it's from more "non-edy ch" words (cheody, chody, chol, chedaiin). This confirms that ch-dominant sections have expanded ch vocabulary beyond the template class.

### Finding 5: Cosmo Section Is Bimodal — f103/f116 Are Balanced, f104-115 Are ch-Dominant

Among cosmo folios: f103=0.448 (balanced), f116=0.531 (balanced), but f104-115 all show 0.690-0.834 (ch-dominant). This suggests cosmo has internal structure — the boundary folios (f103, f116) have different grammar from the main cosmo pages (f104-115). The main cosmo pages are among the most ch-dominant in the entire manuscript.

## Summary

H530 ANSWERED. Folio-level ch/sh specialization has two mechanisms:
1. **Bio formula**: strict ch/sh symmetry enforced by the section's specific word types (ch+edy ↔ sh+edy parallel, bio-specific sh+ckhy compounds). The bio section behaves as if ch and sh are interchangeable template markers.
2. **Content expansion**: ch-dominant sections have ch taking on content-word roles (ol/aiin body class) that are absent in bio. This is consistent with F347 showing B-dialect ch is edy-dominant overall, but cosmo/text sections add ol/aiin body vocabulary to ch specifically.

The text section (ratio=0.766) is the most ch-dominant, and cosmo middle pages are second (0.690-0.834). Bio is strictly balanced. Herbal is in between, with later herbal folios (f65/f66) more balanced.
