**Critical contrast with A-dialect daiin→cth**:
- daiin→cth in A: single word (daiin), 3.19× enrichment, ALL other particles = 0.00×

**d is strongly DEPLETED before l in B (0.42×)**: A-dialect had d=1.30× before cth. B reverses this — d avoids preceding l-words. This confirms that the B-dialect grammatical structure around l-onset is fundamentally different from A's cth structure.

**Bonus: A-dialect before l-onset words** (n=51):
- qot=3.99×, qok=2.40×, y=2.02×, cth=1.87×
- In A-dialect, l-onset (rare, 59 tokens) is preceded by qo-stem words at 2.4-4.0× — the opposite of B where qok=1.14× before l. A l-onset words may be a specialized sub-vocabulary following the qo-morphological environment.

### H290 Conclusion: NOT CONFIRMED

No single B-particle parallels daiin's cth-specific determiner role. Instead:
- l-onset words in B are preceded by **-eey suffix words** (chey/okeey/qokey) at 2-3× — a morphological (suffix-class) association, not a determiner
- d is DEPLETED before l in B (0.42×), opposite of d→cth in A
- The B-dialect grammatical environment preceding l-onset is structurally different from A's daiin→cth pattern

## Summary of New Findings (F153)

1. **B-dialect sh is a LINE-OPENER (LF=1.90×), NOT a medial nominal**: sh contrasts with A-cth (near-baseline LF=0.94×). daiin→sh in B (2.33×, F152) does NOT mean "daiin is sh's determiner" — it reflects both daiin and sh being LF-enriched in B, and B's sequential grammar (sh→qo hinge). The structural analogy of "daiin→cth in A = daiin→sh in B" is DISPROVEN.

2. **No self-succession for sh or ch_e in B** (0.87–0.93×): A-cth has 1.84× self-succession; B-sh/ch_e have near-random succession. B-ch (control) shows 1.57× self-succession — closer to A-cth. The positional grammar of sh in B is fundamentally different from cth in A.

3. **No B-dialect "daiin-equivalent" for l-onset**: The highest before-l onset enrichments in B are ch_e=1.42× and ch=1.27×; individual -eey words reach 2.66-3.37× but at small n. No categorical 3×+ single-particle determiner for l-onset exists. d is strongly depleted before l (0.42×). The grammatical environment preceding l-words in B is -eey morphological class association, not a single determiner particle.

4. **A-dialect l-onset (rare, n=59) follows qo-environment** (qot=3.99×, qok=2.40×): A's l-words appear after qo-stem words at high enrichment — potentially a functional class distinct from B's l-words (which follow -eey words). The A and B l-onset classes may be different vocabularies with different grammatical environments.

## Hypotheses Spawned

If B-ch is the B-dialect "medial nominal" parallel to A-cth (both LF-depleted, both self-succeeding), then the daiin→ch_e/ch enrichment (2.11×) should be examined more carefully at the sub-word level (specific ch-words following daiin in B).
- **H292**: The -eey suffix class (chey, okeey, qokey, oteey) precedes B l-onset words at 2.66-3.37×. Is this a specific construction: [-eey word][l-word], like a phrase-internal morphological sequence? Or is it an artifact of collocations in specific B sections (stars/cosmo where l-words concentrate)?
