## Findings

### Finding 1: A-Dialect Three-Register Grammar Is Section-Invariant (Parallel to B-Dialect F353)

A-dialect herbal (N_first=88) and pharma (N_first=23) independently show the three-register grammar:
- Herbal: folio-first=75% gallows (E=6.05×); folio-last=2.0% (E=0.16×); len_last=3.55 vs 5.27
- Pharma: folio-first=69.6% (E=3.83×); folio-last=9.1% (E=0.50×); len_last=4.23 vs 7.33

The three-register structure (gallows folio-opener, anti-gallows short folio-closer) is now confirmed as a cross-dialect, cross-section universal constraint. Both A and B dialects, in all major sections, follow this scribal layout convention.

### Finding 2: A-Dialect Folio-Closer Formula Uses y-Onset; B-Dialect Uses ol/l/ot Onset

Cross-dialect contrast at folio-last LF:
- **A-dialect**: y=22.9% (E=1.60×), s=13.3% (E=2.14×), ch=13.3% (E=1.79×)
- **B-dialect**: ot=8.5% (E=4.09×), l=10.6% (E=3.94×), ol=10.6% (E=3.43×)

Both dialects have the same anti-gallows constraint at folio-last LF (p=0% in A, t/k depleted in B), but the POSITIVE formula content differs. A-dialect uses y-onset words as the folio-closer signal; B-dialect uses line-terminal onset classes (l/ol/ot). This is a vocabulary-level dialect-specific difference within the shared structural constraint.

### Finding 3: A-Dialect p-Onset Absolute Exclusion from Folio-Last LF Confirmed (0/83)

A-dialect folio-last LF: p=0/83=0.0% (confirmed absolute exclusion across all A-dialect sections). Contrast: A folio-first LF = p=35.2% (herbal) / p=43.5% (pharma). The p-onset inversion — complete switch from folio-opener dominant to folio-closer excluded — is confirmed as a pan-A-dialect constraint.

### Finding 4: A-Dialect Folio-Closer Formula Word is ycheor

A-herbal folio-last LF top words: ycheor(3), daiin(2). A-pharma folio-last LF: yeoy(1), ykodar(1) — y-onset words present. The A-dialect folio-closer y-formula word appears to be ycheor (y+ch compound = yc- class, consistent with F354: continuation-LF uses yc- compounds at 29-34%). A-dialect folio-closer = ycheor class (y+ch compound), whereas B-dialect folio-closer y-words = yk- compounds (83%, F354).

### Finding 5: A-Dialect Astro Section Anomalous (N=9, Likely Atypical Format)

A-dialect astro: folio-last NOT depleted (E=2.37×, N=9). This is likely an artifact of the astro section's non-prose format (circular zodiac wheels, label text). Small N and non-linear text arrangement make the folio-first/folio-last classification unreliable for astro. The positive finding (folio-first = 66.7% gallows, E=7.11×) is consistent with the grammar but the non-depleted folio-last is anomalous.

### Finding 6: A-Dialect sh Strongly Depleted at Folio-Last LF (E=0.16×)

A-dialect folio-last LF: sh=1.2% (E=0.16×). Continuation sh=7.4%. sh is one of the most depleted onset classes at A-dialect folio-last LF. In B-dialect, sh is also somewhat depleted at folio-last LF (E=0.38×) but B-dialect folio-last LL had sh ENRICHED (E=3.45×). This confirms: sh-depletion at LF position is a shared cross-dialect folio-closer constraint.

## Summary

H557 CONFIRMED. A-dialect three-register grammar is section-invariant:
- **Folio-opener**: 66-75% gallows (herbal=75%, pharma=69.6%, astro=67%)
- **Continuation**: 9-18% gallows (section-specific baseline)
- **Folio-closer**: 0-9% gallows, short lines (3.55-4.23 words)

Cross-dialect comparison reveals:
- **Shared constraints**: anti-gallows folio-closer, gallows folio-opener
- **Dialect-specific vocabulary**: A folio-closer = y/ch/s/d onset; B folio-closer = ol/l/ot onset
- **A-dialect formula word**: ycheor (y+ch compound) at folio-last LF
