## Summary of New Findings (F156)

1. **No A-particle introduces sh or ch_e at 3×+**: sh self-succession (1.68×) and ch_e self-succession (1.98×) are the dominant signals. The strongest sh-introducer is sheor (2.75×) — a sh-word itself. No dedicated particle-gatekeeper exists for sh or ch_e.

2. **cth-enriched particles (daiin/chol/chor/cthol) tend to avoid sh/ch_e**: The before-cth vs before-sh profiles are anti-correlated. Particles that precede cth at elevated rates (daiin 3.08×, chol 2.18×, cthol 2.21×) also tend to avoid sh and ch_e. This suggests a systematic complement: A-grammar organizes content words into a cth-class (introduced by daiin/chol/chor) and non-cth classes (sh/ch_e/ol, introduced by aiin/ol/s/or).

3. **aiin/ol/ar precede cth at 0.00× while enriching ch_e (1.43-1.97×)**: The "null-cth" particles (aiin, ol, ar) specifically introduce ch_e at moderate enrichment. This is a weaker but systematic signal: while daiin uniquely introduces cth, the "other particles" collectively lean toward ch_e.

4. **dy→sh=1.96×** (32 transitions): an unexpected moderately strong sh-predecessor. dy (the short suffix word) precedes sh-words at nearly 2×. This may reflect a line-internal construction [dy][sh-word] in A-herbal.
