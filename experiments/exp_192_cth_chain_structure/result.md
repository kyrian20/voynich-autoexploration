## 1. H339: qok→cth DEPLETION IS GENUINE (NOT RARITY) — 0.54× IN A, 0.17× IN B

A-dialect: qok→cth = 10 observed vs 18.5 expected → **0.54× enrichment** (depletion). A has 529 qok-words — sufficient sample. qok prefers: ok (1.77×), qok-self (1.62×), ch (1.21×). These are the qo-class and ok-class — structurally parallel to qok itself. cth is systematically avoided.

B-dialect: qok→cth = 1/2317 qok-bigrams → **0.17×** (even stronger depletion). B has abundant qok-words.

**H339 SUPPORTED**: qok-words systematically avoid cth-onset successors in both dialects. This is not explained by qok rarity in A. The avoidance is structural: qok (qo+k onset class) and cth share no morphological affinity — qo-words select ok, qok, ch as successors; cth is incompatible. This is the complement of the [ch→cth] and [k→cth] enrichments (F188/F189): the cth-introducing grammar is constrained to specific onset classes (ch, d, k, y) and specifically excludes qok.

**Most striking finding: 3 CONSECUTIVE cth-WORDS appear in two lines:**

**H340 SUPPORTED (with surprise)**: cth self-succession is class-level (all 23 pairs distinct). cthy is the preferred cth-successor (7/23). The 3-consecutive-cth phenomenon (f6.16, f7.2) reveals that cth-chains of length ≥3 exist, implying a cth-phrase grammar rather than just occasional cth-cth bigrams.
