# exp_335: ch/sh Body-Class MID Position Split — Do ch+'a'-Body Words Appear Later Than ch+'e'-Body Words? (H538)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H538 — ch+'a'-body words (chol/char/chor) appear at LATER MID positions than ch+'e'-body words (chedy/cheedy), parallel to the ot+'a'>ot+'e' finding (z=2.67σ from exp_329).
**Result:** H538 PARTIALLY CONFIRMED with REVERSAL SURPRISE. ch+'a'-body is LATER than ch+'e'-body in A-dialect (z=+2.04σ, SIGNIFICANT) but NOT in B-dialect (z=-0.54σ, NS). sh+'a'-body is significantly EARLIER than sh+'e'-body in B-dialect (z=-2.19σ, OPPOSITE DIRECTION). The 'a'-body vs 'e'-body position split is NOT a universal "a=content=late" rule — it is gradient-position-dependent: for late-MID classes (ot), 'a'-body is later; for early-MID classes (sh), 'a'-body is earlier. ch behavior is dialect-split: A-dialect ch+'a' words (particularly chaiin=0.578, chain=0.573, chal=0.721) are late; B-dialect ch+'a' words have mixed positions (char=0.498, chal=0.407, chaiin=0.243).

## Findings

### Finding 1: 'a'-body vs 'e'-body position split is gradient-position-dependent, not universal

The expected pattern ("'a'-body is content words = later in MID span") holds for LATE-MID onset classes (ot: z=+2.67 B, +1.40 A) and A-dialect ch (z=+2.04). But for EARLY-MID onset classes, the pattern can REVERSE. B-dialect sh shows 'a'-body EARLIER than 'e'-body (z=−2.19). This suggests the relationship depends on where the onset class sits in the gradient:
- Late-MID classes (ot, d): 'a'-body content words appear at the END of the MID span (already near line-terminal positions)
- Early-MID classes (sh): 'a'-body words appear at the BEGINNING of the MID span (near line-opening)
- Middle classes (ch, ok, qok): mixed or dialect-specific split

The underlying mechanism: sh as early-MID class typically produces template words (shedy/sheey) in the middle of MID lines. When sh produces 'a'-body words (shar), those words appear EARLY — first or second MID position. This may reflect shar being a "line-opening content" formula (short AR-body word near start of line). The ot 'a'-body words (otaiin, otain) are line-closing content words.

### Finding 2: B-dialect shar is a very-early-MID formula word (mean position 0.153)

shar (B: N=12, mean=0.153) appears in the first quarter of the MID span, on average the 2nd MID word in a 10-word line. This is extremely early — earlier than any other sh-onset word type. shar has a specific early-line grammatical function in B-dialect that is distinct from template sh-words (shedy/shey) which appear throughout the MID span. shaiin (B: N=3, mean=0.217) also appears very early.

By contrast, A-dialect shar (N=6, mean=0.331) is not quite as extreme but still earlier than A-dialect sh class mean (0.410).

### Finding 3: A-dialect ch 'a'-body words concentrate in late MID positions (chal=0.721, cham=0.833)

A-dialect ch+'a'-body words (except char which is early at 0.396) appear in the LATE half of the MID span. chal(N=7, 0.721) and cham(N=5, 0.833) are among the latest-positioned ch word types in A-dialect. This is consistent with A-dialect ch developing a late-MID content function: in A-dialect, ch participates more heavily in 'a'-body vocabulary (28% vs B 6.2%), and those content words appear late in lines.

### Finding 4: B-dialect ch 'a'-body shows no significant positional split (z=−0.54)

In B-dialect, ch 'a'-body N=74 (small). The mean positions are: char(28, 0.498), chal(21, 0.407), chaiin(7, 0.243). These are scattered throughout the MID span — no consistent pattern. The B-dialect ch 'a'-body is so rare (6.2% of all ch words) that individual word patterns dominate. The NS result is consistent with B-dialect ch+'a' being rare exceptional occurrences without a specific positional grammar.

## Interpretation

The hypothesis that "template-onset 'a'-body = content function = later MID position" requires qualification. The position of body-class words is jointly determined by:
1. The onset class's baseline MID gradient position
2. The specific word's grammatical function (some 'a'-body words like shar are early-formula; others like cham are late-content)
3. Dialect (A-dialect ch develops strong late-MID 'a'-body content function; B-dialect ch does not)

The significant results form a cross-pattern:
- B sh: 'a'-body EARLIER (shar as early-formula)
- B ot: 'a'-body LATER (otaiin as late-content)
- A ch: 'a'-body LATER (chaiin/chain/chal as late-content)

These are not contradictory — they reflect different word functions within the same body vocabulary. The 'a'-body participation by sh (in B) represents an early-formula role; by ch (in A) represents a late-content role; by ot (in B) represents an end-of-span terminal role.

## Limitations

- B-dialect sh 'a'-body N=26 (very small; shar dominates with N=12). The z=−2.19 significance relies heavily on shar's mean=0.153.
- B-dialect ch 'a'-body N=74 (borderline). The z=−0.54 NS could be power-limited.
- A-dialect ch 'a'-body N=96 with z=+2.04 is the most reliable result in this experiment.
- s-onset has N<5 for e-body in both dialects; excluded from analysis.
