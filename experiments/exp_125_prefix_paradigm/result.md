## H249: CONFIRMED — Four-Prefix Paradigm Generalizes to All k/t Stem Classes; ok-/ot-ONSET = o-PREFIX ON k/t-STEMS; qok-/qot-ONSET = qo-PREFIX ON k/t-STEMS; ch/sh Stems Take ONLY l-Prefix (NOT qo/o); r-Stems Are B-Dominant Bare Forms; FIVE-PREFIX System: o-/qo-/l-/y-/p- (New)

### The Full Stem Prefix Paradigm

The k-stem paradigm (from F131) generalizes fully to t-stems:

**k-stem paradigm (kaiin example):**

| Prefix form | Word | n | A% | Top section |
|-------------|------|---|-----|------------|
| bare-k | kaiin | 77 | 23% | stars(47%) |
| **o+k = ok-onset** | okaiin | 201 | 17% | stars(48%) |
| **qo+k = qok-onset** | qokaiin | 265 | 8% | stars(45%) |
| **l+k** | lkaiin | 48 | 0% | stars(81%) |

**CRITICAL**: **ok-onset (okaiin) = o-prefix on k-stem**! And **qok-onset (qokaiin) = qo-prefix on k-stem**! The "ok" and "qok" onset classes in our taxonomy are NOT independent stem classes — they are **o-prefixed and qo-prefixed forms of bare k-stems**.

**t-stem paradigm (taiin example):**

| Prefix form | Word | n | A% | Top section |
|-------------|------|---|-----|------------|
| bare-t | taiin | 44 | 34% | stars(39%) |
| **o+t = ot-onset** | otaiin | 137 | 21% | stars(56%) |
| **qo+t = qot-onset** | qotaiin | 75 | 12% | stars(53%) |
| l+t | lteedy | 6 | 0% | stars(67%) |

**ot-onset (otaiin) = o-prefix on t-stem. qot-onset = qo-prefix on t-stem.** The same four-prefix structure.

### ch/sh Stems: DIFFERENT Pattern — Only l-Prefix, NOT qo/o

**ch-stem paradigm (chedy example):**

| Prefix form | Word | n | A% | Top section |
|-------------|------|---|-----|------------|
| bare-ch | chedy | 489 | 0% | cosmo(42%) |
| qo+ch | qochedy | 1 | 0% | stars — essentially ABSENT |
| **l+ch** | lchedy | 116 | 1% | cosmo(51%) |
| o+ch | ochedy | 8 | 0% | — rare |

ch-stems take ONLY l-prefix (lchedy=116; qochedy=1). **ch/sh stems are NOT prefixed with qo- or o- in body text** — the qo-prefix selectively applies to k/t-initial stems.

This explains why the original qo-stripping experiments found qo-stripped stems starting with k/t (not ch/sh)!

### The Full Prefix-Stem Compatibility Matrix

| Prefix | k-stems | t-stems | ch-stems | sh-stems | r-stems |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| bare | ✓ (herbal) | ✓ (mixed) | ✓ (B-body) | ✓ (B-body) | ✓ (B-stars) |
| o- | ✓ ok-onset | ✓ ot-onset | ✗ (rare) | ✗ (rare) | ✓ (oraiin) |
| qo- | ✓ qok-onset | ✓ qot-onset | ✗ (absent) | ✗ (absent) | ✗ (absent) |
| l- | ✓ lk (stars) | ✓ lt (rare) | ✓ lch (cosmo) | ✓ lsh (cosmo) | ✗ |

**Key finding**: qo-prefix is EXCLUSIVE to k/t-stems. ch/sh-stems can be l-prefixed but not qo-prefixed. This suggests the qo-prefix and l-prefix have different stem-class selectivity.

### Additional Prefix Classes: y-, p-, r-

The prefix recovery analysis revealed additional prefix classes:

| Prefix | n | Stem recovery | A/B ratio | Top section |
|--------|---|--------------|-----------|------------|
| **r-** | 346 | **87.0% tokens** | 0.12 (8.4× B) | stars(51%) |
| **y-** | 1374 | 85.6% | 0.76 (balanced) | herbal(39%) |
| **p-** | 528 | 76.1% | 0.30 (3.4× B) | stars(43%) |

- **r-prefix** (raiin, rain, raram): 87.0% stem recovery, 8.4× B-dominant, stars/cosmo concentrated — another B-dialect prefix! r+aiin=raiin, r+am=ram, r+ar=rar.
- **y-prefix** (ykeey, ykaiin, yteedy): 85.6% recovery, more balanced A/B (0.76). y-prefix is less dialect-specific.
- **p-prefix** (polaiin, pcheor, pchodain): 76.1% recovery, 3.4× B-dominant, stars concentrated — another B-dialect prefix for stars entries.

The prefix-section matrix confirms:
- **qo-**: strongly B-cosmo (31%) and B-stars (36%)
- **l-**: strongly B-stars (58%), B-cosmo (28%)
- **r-**: strongly B-cosmo (33%) and B-stars (51%)
- **k-** (bare): A-herbal (30%), B-stars (31%)
- **t-** (bare): A-herbal (25%), B-stars (26%)

### Revised Prefix Taxonomy

| Prefix | Stem selectivity | Register/Context | Dialect |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|---------|
| o- | k/t-stems (ok/ot onset) | Body text | Both |
| qo- | k/t-stems ONLY | B body text | B-dominant |
| cth- | o/y/e-stems | A body text | A-dominant |
| l- | k/ch/sh/d-stems | B stars/cosmo | B-dominant |
| r- | aiin/am/ar-stems | B stars/cosmo | B-dominant |
| p- | ch/sh-stems | B stars/cosmo | B-dominant |
| y- | k/ch/t-stems | Mixed | Balanced |

**The onset taxonomy needs revision**: ok-onset and ot-onset are NOT independent classes — they are o-prefixed k/t-stems. Similarly, qok-onset and qot-onset are qo-prefixed k/t-stems.

### H249 Verdict: CONFIRMED — The four-prefix paradigm (o/qo/l/bare) generalizes to all k/t stem classes; ok-onset = o+k, ot-onset = o+t, qok-onset = qo+k, qot-onset = qo+t; ch/sh stems take l-prefix (lchedy, lshedy) but NOT qo/o-prefix; qo-prefix is exclusively k/t-stem selective; NEW prefix classes discovered: r- (87% recovery, B-stars), p- (76%, B-stars), y- (86%, balanced); full revised prefix taxonomy: o-/qo-/l-/r-/p-/y-/cth-; onset classes ok/ot/qok/qot are derived forms (prefixed), not primitive stem classes

## H250: CONFIRMED — B-Dialect cth = HYBRID WORDS: cthedy (B_frac=0.917) and cthdy (B_frac=1.000) Are B-Paradigm Suffixes on A-Onset cth; B-cth Concentrated in B-Herbal Folios (f26/31/46/48) and f113/116; Pre-B-cth: ch=2.03× (B-content words); cth→cth Self-Cluster in B = Only n=1 (Earlier 8.79× Was Based on Single Token)

### B-Dialect cth: 55 Tokens in 26 Folios

B-dialect cth is not uniformly distributed — it is concentrated in:
- **B-herbal folios**: f26 (19.35‰), f48 (20.73‰), f46 (15.21‰), f31 (9.95‰) — the highest-cth B-folios
- **Stars f113 (7.03‰) and f116 (7.68‰)** — specific stars folios

### cth-Word Profile: Hybrid B-Paradigm Forms

| Word | B | A | B_frac | Interpretation |
|------|---|---|--------|----------------|
| **cthedy** | 11 | 1 | **0.917** | cth + -edy (B-suffix!) — B-specific hybrid |
| **cthdy** | 6 | 0 | **1.000** | cth + -dy (B-suffix) — B-ONLY |
| cthey | 11 | 32 | 0.256 | cth + -ey (shared) |
| cthy | 5 | 91 | 0.052 | common A-word, rare in B |
| cthor | 2 | 38 | 0.050 | common A-word, rare in B |
| cthol | 2 | 52 | 0.037 | common A-word, rare in B |

**cthedy and cthdy are B-dialect-specific hybrid words**: they apply B-paradigm suffixes (-edy, -dy) to the A-dialect onset (cth-). These are structurally novel — they appear almost exclusively in B-dialect text and represent B-scribes applying their suffix paradigm to the cth- onset prefix.

The long compound forms in B-text (cthedyqokeedy, cthdalyotchedy, cthdyokain, cthororaiin) suggest B-dialect scribes combined cth-onset with B-suffix strings — potentially representing different content than A-dialect cth-nouns.

### Folio-Level Distribution: Mostly B-Herbal

The 17 B-herbal cth tokens are distributed across B-herbal folios (f26, f31, f33, f34, f43, f46, f48, f57). These are the B-dialect folios WITHIN the herbal section (the "bifolio insertions" identified in F123). Their cth usage may reflect the B-scribe encountering A-dialect plant names (cth-nouns) and adapting them with B-paradigm suffixes.

### Context: B-Dialect cth Is Preceded by B-Content Words

Pre-B-cth onset: ch=2.03× enriched (B-content words precede B-dialect cth). Post-B-cth: ot=1.91× (B-dialect ot-onset follows). This is the OPPOSITE of A-dialect context (where A-cth is followed by d-function words, not ot-words). B-dialect cth functions differently than A-dialect cth.

### cth→cth Self-Clustering: n=1 ONLY

The B-dialect cth→cth transition occurs exactly ONCE (ctheey → ctharad in f113). The earlier finding from exp_123 of B-cth→cth=8.79× was mathematically correct (1/47 = 2.1% vs baseline ~0.25% = 8.5×) but based on n=1. The "mandatory" cth→cth cluster pattern (4.78× overall) is an A-dialect phenomenon; B-dialect does NOT show cth self-clustering.

### H250 Verdict: CONFIRMED — B-dialect cth-tokens (n=55) are: (1) hybrid words using B-paradigm suffixes on cth-onset (cthedy=B_frac=0.917, cthdy=1.000); (2) common A-cth words appearing in B-herbal folios (cthy, cthor — low B_frac); (3) concentrated in B-herbal folios (f26/f48 highest, ~20‰) and stars f113/f116; pre-B-cth: ch-onset=2.03× (B-content); cth→cth in B: only n=1 (exp_123's 8.79× was a small-n artifact); B-dialect scribes adapted cth-onset with B-paradigm suffixes (-edy, -dy) creating hybrid forms specific to B-dialect
