# exp_188: cth-Word Chain Analysis — [chol][daiin] Bigram, cthedy Successors, cthol→cthy
**Date**: 2026-04-11
**Hypotheses**: H351 ([chol][daiin] bigram stability), H352 (cthedy successor specificity), H353 (cthol→cthy)
**Source**: F176 (exp_169): [ch][daiin]→cth=5.89×; cthedy successor l=5.79×, ot=5.07×; cthol successor cth=2.41×.
**Method**: Full ZL3b word inventory; dialect assignment by edy rate (≥5% → B). A-dialect: 10,258 tokens; B-dialect: 20,490 tokens.

## 1. H351: [chol][daiin] BIGRAM — DAIIN AMPLIFIES THE chol→cth CONNECTION (5.30×)

### Findings

**[chol/chor][daiin]→cth enrichment: 5.30× (5/25 cases = 20%).**
**[chol/chor]→cth direct (no daiin): 2.30× (32/369 cases = 8.7%).**

daiin acts as a **bridge amplifier**: when daiin separates chol/chor from the following word, the probability of that word being cth-class increases by 2.3× relative to the direct (no-daiin) case (8.7% → 20%, ratio = 2.3).

**Full [ch-word][daiin][cth-word] trigram inventory (A-dialect, n=12)**:
| ch-word | daiin | cth-word | Count |
|---------|-------|----------|-------|
| chol | daiin | cthy | 2 |
| chor | daiin | cthy | 1 |
| chor | daiin | cthoiin | 1 |
| chy | daiin | ctholdy | 1 |
| chol | daiin | cthol (+ folio-break artifact) | 1 |
| chotchol | daiin | cthol | 1 |
| [others] | daiin | [various cth] | 5 |

**cthy is the dominant cth target**: 4/12 = 33% of [ch][daiin]→cth cases lead to cthy specifically. chol (3×) and chor (2×) are the dominant ch-words (confirmed from F176).

**[chol/chor][daiin] successor breakdown (n=25)**:
- → cth-word: 5 (20%) — enriched 5.30×
- → ch-word: ~6 (24%) — [chol][daiin][chol] = 2× (unexpected: self-referential chain)
- → daiin: 1 (4%) — [chol][daiin][daiin] = 1×
- → d-words, other: ~13 (52%)

**New finding**: [chol][daiin][chol] appears 2× — a self-referential chain where chol reintroduces itself. This suggests [chol] is not just a precursor to cth but also a recurrent nominal element in the chain: [chol][daiin][chol][daiin][cth?].

### Assessment: H351 SUPPORTED

daiin significantly amplifies the chol→cth connection (5.30× vs 2.30×, fold-amplification = 2.3). daiin is NOT simply a random insertion — it specifically selects contexts where cth-words are likely to follow. However, [chol][daiin]→cth is not a fixed formula: 80% of [chol][daiin] bigrams lead to non-cth successors. The chain is probabilistic, not deterministic.

## 2. H352: cthedy SUCCESSOR SPECIFICITY — ONSET CLASS ENRICHED, WORDS DIVERSE

All 10 cthedy occurrences have successors. All 10 successors are DIFFERENT WORDS.

| qokedy | qok | ~0.8× (baseline) |

**Onset-class summary**:
- l-onset: 3/10 = 30% (base 5.8%) → **5.2× enrichment** (confirmed F176's 5.79×)
- ot-onset: 3/10 = 30% (base 6.6%) → **4.5× enrichment** (confirmed F176's 5.07×)
- qo-class (qo+qot+qok): 3/10 = 30% (base ~19.8%) → 1.5× (slight enrichment, unexpected)

### Assessment: H352 PARTIALLY SUPPORTED

The l-onset and ot-onset enrichments from F176 are fully confirmed (5.2× and 4.5×). However, **there is no stable word-pair formula**: all 10 successors are distinct words. cthedy selects the ONSET CLASS of its successor (preferring l and ot), not a specific word. The pattern is a selectional constraint at the class level, not a lexicalized bigram formula. With n=10 (small), the individual word identities are unreliable; the onset-class enrichment is stable.

**cthol → cth-word (any): 4/44 = 9.1% → enrichment 9.1%/3.77% = 2.41×** (exactly matches F176)

**cthol → specifically cthy: 1/44 (2.3%)**. Not enriched relative to base cthy rate — cthy is not the preferred cth-word after cthol. The four cth successors are: cthy (1), ctholo (1), ctheol (1), cthol (1) — all different.

**[cthol][daiin]→cth: 2/5 (40%)**:
- [cthol][daiin][ctholdar] × 1
- [cthol][daiin][cthom] × 1

The predicted [cthol][daiin][cthy] pattern does NOT appear. When daiin mediates after cthol, the cth-words that follow are ctholdar and cthom — not cthy.

**cthol's most common successors are daiin (6×) and chol (3×)**, not cth-words. The [chol][daiin] bidirectional connection pattern appears here: [cthol][...][chol] appears, suggesting cthol and chol are related nominal elements.

### Assessment: H353 NOT SUPPORTED

cthol→cthy is NOT specifically preferred. The cth enrichment (2.41×) from cthol operates at the CLASS level — any cth-word may follow, not specifically cthy. The hypothesized [cthol][daiin][cthy] trigram is absent. cthol's most frequent successors are daiin (non-enriched) and chol (depleted for ch-class but specifically chol appears 3×).

## 4. SYNTHESIS: cth-WORD CHAIN STRUCTURE

The three experiments clarify the structure of the [ch][daiin][cth] chain:

1. **chol and chor** are the primary ch-words that participate in [ch][daiin][cth] trigrams (confirmed)
2. **daiin amplifies the chol→cth connection** (5.30× vs 2.30× without daiin — fold: 2.3×)
3. **cthy is the dominant cth-word at the END of the chain** (4/12 = 33% of all [ch][daiin][cth] trigrams)
4. **cthol is NOT the terminal cth-word in these chains** — it appears as a successor to chol in some [chol][daiin][cthol] cases (2×), but cthol's own successor pattern is daiin or chol, not cthy. cthol may be a medial element.
5. **cthedy is a B-dialect element** (10 cases in B, 1 in A) with onset-class selectivity (l and ot onset enriched) but no specific word-pair formula.
6. **The [chol][daiin][chol] chain** (2 cases) suggests chol can be reduplicated — possibly a nominal compound with internal bracket structure.

### cth-word functional differentiation (revised)

| cth-word | Position preference | Key chain pattern | Assessment |
|---------|--------------------|--------------------|------------|
| cthy | terminal (LL) strongly | [ch][daiin]→cthy; [cth-MID][cthy@LL] (from F176) | Terminal nominal element |
| cthol | medial or independent | [cthol]→daiin (6×), [cthol]→chol (3×), [chol][daiin]→cthol (2×) | Medial nominal element; chol-related |
| cthedy | B-dialect terminal? | → l-onset (5.2×), ot-onset (4.5×); diverse words | B-dialect class selector |
| ctheol | appears in [chol][daiin][ctheol] (1×) and [cthol]→ctheol (1×) | — | Rare; similar function to cthol? |

## Self-Audit

**Corpus artifacts**: ZL3b contains line-break markers (`<->`) and paragraph markers (`<$>`) that appear in some token strings. Words like `cthey<->choaiin` (line-break continuation) and `chckhey<$>` are artifact tokens. These affect word-identity counts but not onset-class analysis (the onset of `cthey<->choaiin` is correctly identified as `cth`). Approximately 5-10 tokens in the full chain analysis are affected. Core findings are onset-class-level and not affected by word-identity artifacts.

**Target-awareness**: None. This is a descriptive corpus analysis with no model calibration.

**Magnitude context**: [chol/chor][daiin]→cth at 5.30× is consistent with the overall [ch][daiin]→cth=5.89× finding from F176 (the slightly lower value here uses only chol/chor, not all ch-words). The l-onset enrichment for cthedy (5.2×) is consistent with F176's 5.79× (small n=10 variance).

**Negative result scope**: cthol→cthy specifically not supported. [cthol][daiin][cthy] absent in this corpus. Applies to the full ZL3b A-dialect corpus.

## CONCLUSION

H351 SUPPORTED: daiin amplifies the chol→cth connection (5.30× with daiin, 2.30× without). daiin is a bridge amplifier, not just a random filler. New: [chol][daiin][chol] = 2× (self-referential chain — chol may be reduplicated as a nominal compound unit).

H352 PARTIALLY SUPPORTED: cthedy onset-class enrichment (l: 5.2×, ot: 4.5×) confirmed. No stable word-pair formula — successor words are completely diverse. The constraint operates at onset-class level.

H353 NOT SUPPORTED: cthol→cthy not specifically preferred (1/44 cases). cth enrichment from cthol (2.41×) is at the class level. [cthol][daiin][cthy] absent. cthol's primary successors are daiin (6×) and chol (3×).
