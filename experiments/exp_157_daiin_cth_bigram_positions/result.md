## H314: daiin POSITIONAL DISTRIBUTION AND daiin→cth BIGRAM POSITIONS

### daiin Positional Distribution in A (n=414 total):

| Slot   | n   | rate  | baseline | enrichment |
|--------|-----|-------|----------|------------|
| LF     | 71  | 17.1% | 16.0%    | 1.07×      |
| second | 49  | 11.8% | 15.4%    | **0.77×**  |
| MID    | 225 | 54.3% | 52.2%    | 1.04×      |
| LL     | 69  | 16.7% | 16.0%    | 1.04×      |

**daiin is near-baseline across ALL positional slots.** It is NOT specifically MID-enriched. F157's claim "daiin at MID=61%" was based on a different (and possibly wrong) baseline or slot-swapping error; the actual MID rate is 54.3% (≈baseline 52.2%, enrichment=1.04×). daiin appears in all positions essentially at the baseline rate.

### daiin→cth Bigrams Positional Context (n=43 total):

| daiin position | n  | % |
|---------------|-----|---|
| MID           | 30  | 69.8% |
| LF            | 8   | 18.6% |
| second        | 5   | 11.6% |
| LL            | 0   | 0.0%  |

When daiin precedes a cth-word, it is predominantly at MID (69.8%) — significantly above daiin's baseline MID rate (54.3%). But 18.6% of daiin→cth bigrams have daiin at LF.

**daiin@MID → cth**: cth at MID (17 cases) or LL (13 cases). Classic medial-determiner construction.

**daiin@LF → cth@second**: 6 cases (+ 2 daiin@LF → cth@LL for very short lines). This is a LINE-OPENING construction where daiin opens the line and cth-word follows at second position. Lines include:
- `daiin cthor chol chor` → daiin@LF, cthor@second
- `daiin cthor chol ykchor chordy` → daiin@LF, cthor@second
- `daiin cthor daiin dal dys` → daiin@LF, cthor@second
- `daiin cthain o,tolosheey qocthey tolchory` → daiin@LF, cthain@second
- `daiin ctheey ckheckhy cthody chor s,al` → daiin@LF, ctheey@second
- `daiin ctheol cheol okor or aiin` → daiin@LF, ctheol@second

**NEW CONSTRUCTION: [daiin@LF][cth@second]**: daiin as line-opener with cth-word immediately following at position 2. This is the complement to [daiin@MID][cth@subsequent] — daiin can also open lines with an immediate cth-word.

## H313: LF WORD WHEN chol IS AT SECOND (CONFIRMED)

**chol@second lines (n≥3): n=66**

LF onset distribution when chol@second (A-dialect):

| Onset | n  | rate  | baseline | enrichment |
|-------|----|-------|----------|------------|
| ot    | 7  | 10.6% | 3.6%     | **2.95×**  |
| qok   | 6  | 9.1%  | 5.8%     | **1.57×**  |
| ch    | 6  | 9.1%  | 6.9%     | 1.32×      |
| ok    | 4  | 6.1%  | 4.3%     | 1.41×      |
| y     | 10 | 15.2% | 15.4%    | 0.98×      |
| d     | 9  | 13.6% | 16.0%    | 0.85×      |
| sh    | 6  | 9.1%  | 10.5%    | 0.86×      |

**ot-onset is the most enriched LF class before chol@second (2.95×, n=7)**: ot-words open lines that have chol at second. Top ot-words: otol (3), otchol (2). Top LF words overall: shol (4), otol (3), dor (3), or (3).

**H313 CONFIRMED**: Lines with chol@second are modestly biased toward ot-onset LF words (2.95×), but no single specific word dominates. The [ot@LF][chol@second] construction is the strongest specific pattern — ot-nominal words (otol, otchol) open lines that have chol at second position.

## Summary of New Findings (F164)

1. **daiin is near-baseline positionally (LF=1.07×, MID=1.04×, LL=1.04×, second=0.77×)**: NOT specifically MID-enriched as F157 claimed. daiin appears at all positions roughly proportionally.

2. **daiin→cth bigrams: 70% from daiin@MID, 18.6% from daiin@LF**: When daiin precedes cth, it is predominantly at MID (70%) — a medial-determiner role — but a significant minority (8/43=18.6%) are from daiin at LF.

3. **NEW CONSTRUCTION [daiin@LF][cth@second]**: daiin as LF word → cth-word at second (6 cases): specifically [daiin@LF][cthor@second] (3 cases), [daiin@LF][cthain@second] (1), [daiin@LF][ctheey@second] (1), [daiin@LF][ctheol@second] (1). This is a line-opening construction distinct from the medial daiin.

4. **daiin→cthol always places cthol at MID (6/6)**: daiin introduces cthol only as a medial word. Consistent with cthol=MID-enriched (F161).

5. **ot-onset at LF before chol@second (2.95×, n=7/66)**: ot-nominal words (otol, otchol) open lines with chol at second. Not a strong single-word effect — diverse LF words precede chol@second.
