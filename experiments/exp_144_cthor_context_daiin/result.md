**cthor→chol** (3×) parallels cthor→ot* (7×) — both create nominal phrase patterns. Combined:
- cthor→ot*: 21.9% of cthor usages

- f15: `daiin cthor chol chor<$>`

So "daiin cthor chol" is a three-element formula (appearing 2×), alongside "daiin cthor ot*" (3×). The daiin+cthor phrase can be completed by either chol or an ot-word.

## H285: "daiin cthor ot*" CONSTRUCTION CONTEXT (CONFIRMED)

The three "daiin cthor ot*" occurrences are all preceded by qo/ok-type words:

| Folio | Full context |
|-------|-------------|
| f10 | `**qoty** daiin cthor otydy sain` |
| f14 | `**qotchy** daiin cthor oty` |
| f19 | `**okchor** daiin cthor otam` |

In all three cases, the word before "daiin" is a qo-stem (qoty, qotchy) or ok-stem (okchor). This creates a recurring 4+ element construction:
**[qo/ok-word][daiin][cthor][ot-word]**

This may be a specific botanical nominal phrase: [qo/ok-type determiner/classifier][article/particle: daiin][shape-nominal: cthor][size/type modifier: ot-word].

The qo/ok words before daiin are: qoty (qo+ty), qotchy (qot+chy), okchor (ok+chor). All have A-paradigm suffixes (-y, -chy, -chor). The qo-prefix in A-dialect is less common than in B but does appear.

## Summary of New Findings (F151)

1. **cthor→chol dominant (37.5% of cthor→ch cases, 3/8)**: "daiin cthor chol" is a three-element formula appearing twice (f15, f19). cthor can be followed by either chol (ch-nominal) or ot-word — both completion types give a [cthor][content-word] nominal phrase.
2. **"daiin cthor ot*" is preceded by qo/ok-words (all 3 cases)**: Extended construction = [qo/ok-word][daiin][cthor][ot-word]. The qo/ok-prefix before daiin may be a higher-level classifier or quantifier.
3. **daiin is a UNIVERSAL cth-preceder (43 cases) across all cth-words**: daiin→cthy(9), daiin→cthol(6), daiin→cthor(6). Not specific to cthor. daiin is a grammatical particle that introduces cth-nominal phrases.
4. **ot-onset follows the A/B suffix paradigm split**: B ot-words dominated by -edy/-eedy/-ain/-oteey; A ot-words by -ol/-y/-aiin/-otchy. ot is moderately B-biased (1.67× by rate), similar to other content-word onsets.
