## Findings

### Finding 1: A-Dialect d-Class LL Enrichment Is CLASS-WIDE (E=2.08×); d-not-daiin Is MORE Enriched (E=2.35×) Than daiin (E=1.55×)

A-dialect LL = 24.7% d-onset, MID = 11.9% → E=2.08×. Decomposition: d-not-daiin accounts for 18.4% of LL words (E=2.35×); daiin is 6.3% (E=1.55×). The LL d-dominance is a **d-class property**, not a daiin formula. dy, dam, dal, dar are the primary d-LL words beyond daiin. The architectural implication: the simulation requires a d-class LL affinity rule (not just a daiin-word formula) for A-dialect.

### Finding 2: H541 ANSWERED — A-Dialect daiin LL Enrichment Is Real but Secondary to d-Class Effect (E=1.55× for daiin; E=2.35× for d-other)

daiin is LL-enriched in A-dialect (E=1.55×: 6.3% vs 4.1% MID). But the d-class LL enrichment is driven MORE by non-daiin d-words: dy/dam/dal/dar all elevated. daiin's LF enrichment in A (E=1.27×: 5.2% vs 4.1%) is modest — A-dialect daiin is weakly LF-enriched AND LL-enriched (it is a content word that appears across positions but concentrates at LL). Contrast with B-dialect where daiin is LL-NEUTRAL (E=0.90×) and LF-enriched (F341: E=2.57×). This confirms the A-dialect daiin LL assignment is real but embedded within a broader d-class LL grammar rule.

### Finding 3: B-Dialect d-Not-daiin Is ALSO LL-Enriched (E=2.10×) — Cross-Dialect d-Class LL Property

B-dialect d-other LL = 8.1%, MID = 3.8% → E=2.10×. B-dialect top LL d-words: dy(22,1.0%), dal(19,0.8%), dam(18,0.8%), dar(17,0.7%) — identical word list to A-dialect (dy/dam/dal/dar). The d-class LL affinity is CROSS-DIALECT. Both dialects prefer d-not-daiin words (dy/dam/dal/dar) at LL. The dialect difference is only for daiin: A-dialect daiin E=1.55× at LL, B-dialect daiin E=0.90× (neutral/depleted, because daiin is the B LF-formula). The d-class LL rule is: {dy, dam, dal, dar, dain} → elevated LL probability in BOTH dialects.

### Finding 4: A-Dialect Positional Grammar — Full LF and LL Profiles

**LF**: p=6.40×, f=4.57×, s=4.20×, t=3.63×, y=2.63×, qot=2.09× — gallows + formula classes strongly enriched. ch depleted (0.34×), qok depleted (0.83×). 
**LL**: d=2.08×, s=2.91×, ol=1.37×, ot=1.14× — content/terminal classes enriched. sh depleted (0.49×), qok depleted (0.39×), ch depleted (0.75×).
Architectural role: LF = formula/gallows register; LL = d-class + s + ol register. ch occupies pos1 (1.30×) and mid (baseline) but is depleted at both LF and LL.

### Finding 5: B-Dialect Positional Grammar — Strong LF Formula and LL ol-Enrichment

**LF**: p=17.12×, s=11.36×, y=5.46×, t=6.30×, d=3.09× — B-dialect LF formula is even more concentrated than A's. daiin LF (3.7% = E=3.09× vs MID 4.9%... wait, d total LF E=3.09×). 
**LL**: d=1.84×, ol=1.69×, s=1.75× — d-class, ol-class, and s-class enriched at LL. ol-onset LL enrichment in B-dialect (E=1.69×) vs A-dialect (E=1.37×): B-dialect has stronger ol-LL coupling. This is consistent with F366's finding that B-dialect top LL words include ol(1.5%) and oly(1.4%).

### Finding 6: d→d Succession Is a Cross-Dialect Class-Level Sequential Rule (A: E=1.61×, B: E=1.54×)

d-onset succession: A 19.2% vs MID baseline 11.9% = E=1.61×; B 7.6% vs 4.9% = E=1.54×. This is weaker than ok→ok (A: 1.93×) but comparable to ch→daiin (E=1.30×/1.45×). Top A d→d pairs: (daiin,daiin)=9, (daiin,dal)=6, (daiin,dain)=5 — daiin is the most common d-word, so it anchors most d→d pairs. The succession is class-level (many different d-word pairs), not dominated by any single pair.
