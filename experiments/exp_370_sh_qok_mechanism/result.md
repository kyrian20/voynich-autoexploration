# exp_370: sh→qok Mechanism — Word-Level Analysis (H522/Q004)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H522 — Is sh→qok a (a) class-level rule (all sh words equally predict qok), (b) sh-sub-class rule (specific sh words drive coupling), or (c) word-pair specific (specific sh×qok pairs dominate)?
**Result:** H522 ANSWERED; Q004 CLOSED. B-dialect sh→qok is driven by SPECIFIC sh-WORD SUBCLASS: shedy/shey/sheedy/sheol account for 57.6% of sh→qok pairs while being only 44.7% of sh tokens — they are over-represented. shedy alone: 36.4% qok-succession rate (E=1.42× vs sh-class average). HOWEVER, the qok word distribution after sh is BASELINE-PROPORTIONAL (qokeedy=12.2% after sh vs 12.2% baseline) — sh selects INTO qok-class uniformly, no specific qok word is preferentially chosen. A-dialect sh→qok is similar: 'she-' prefix sh words drive coupling (sheody E=3.52×, sheol E=2.63×), while high-frequency sh words (shol/sho/shor) have LOW qok rates. Mechanism: **specific sh-word subclasses (B: shedy/shey/sheedy; A: sheol/sheey/sheody) select uniformly into the qok onset-class.** This is NOT a uniform sh-class rule and NOT a word-pair coupling.

## Findings

### Finding 1: B-Dialect sh→qok Is Driven by a Specific sh-WORD Subclass (shedy/shey/sheedy), Not Uniform sh-Class

In B-dialect, shedy (335 tokens) has 36.4% qok-succession rate (E=1.42× above sh-class average); shey has 32.9% (E=1.28×); sheedy 36.6% (E=1.42×); sheckhy/shecthy have ~43% (E=1.68×/1.70×). Together, shedy/shey/sheedy/sheol account for 57.6% of sh→qok pairs while being only 44.7% of sh tokens. Conversely, shody=5.6% (E=0.22×), shy=9.5% (E=0.37×), shol=13.0% (E=0.51×) are below average. The sh→qok rule is **NOT a uniform sh-class rule** but applies primarily to 'she-' body sh words and the edy-suffix sh compound (shedy). The common short sh words (shol/shor/shody) do NOT drive the coupling.

### Finding 2: B-Dialect qok Word Distribution After sh Is BASELINE-PROPORTIONAL

The top qok words following sh in B-dialect (qokeedy=12.2%, qokedy=12.2%, qokain=11.7%, qokaiin=11.4%) are nearly identical to their baseline frequencies. E values for individual qok words after sh range 0.88-1.18× — all near-baseline. This means: the specific sh words (shedy/shey) select INTO the qok class uniformly, choosing any available qok word approximately in proportion to qok word frequencies. The rule is "shedy-class → qok-onset" where the qok-word is then drawn from the qok vocabulary proportionally. No specific shX→qokY pair dominates (top pair shedy→qokedy=18 is proportional: 18/122=14.8% vs qokedy baseline 11.0%).

### Finding 3: A-Dialect sh→qok Is Also Sub-Class Driven ('she-' Prefix sh Words), But Less Concentrated

A-dialect: 'she-' prefix sh words show high qok rates: sheody=31.2%(E=3.52×), sheol=23.3%(E=2.63×), sheey=16.7%(E=1.88×). High-frequency sh words shol(7.1%), sho(1.3%), shor(6.5%) are below sh-class average. However, the top-5 sh words in A-dialect account for only 27.0% of sh→qok pairs while being 40.1% of all sh (under-represented). A-dialect sh→qok is more diffuse and not as concentrated in top words as B-dialect. Both dialects show 'she-' prefix sh words as the primary carriers of sh→qok coupling.

### Finding 4: Cross-Dialect Mechanism Is 'she-' Body sh Subclass → qok-Class

Both dialects share the pattern: sh words with 'she-' body (sheol, sheey, sheody in A; shedy, sheedy, shey, sheckhy in B) have elevated qok-succession rates. The 'sho-' and 'sh-aiin' body words (shol, shor, shody, shaiin in A) have below-average qok rates. This suggests the sh→qok coupling is a BODY-LEVEL rule: sh words with 'e' second character (EVA 'she-') tend to precede qok-onset words. The rule is not onset-level (all sh) and not word-pair specific (many different qok successors).

### Finding 5: Q004 CLOSED — sh→qok Mechanism Is 'she-' Body sh Sub-Class → qok-Class Uniform Successor

Q004 asked: is sh→qok class-level (a), sh-word-type selection (b), or specific pairs (c)? Answer: primarily (b) with uniform qok word selection. shedy/shey/sheol/sheedy are the primary sh words driving the coupling; the qok word chosen is approximately proportional to qok frequency. Architecture: model as a sub-class rule within sh-onset: sh words with 'e' second character (sheXXX forms) have elevated P(next=qok-onset). Alternatively: implement as a body-conditional rule where sh+e-body → elevated qok successor probability.

## Summary

H522 ANSWERED; Q004 CLOSED. sh→qok mechanism is a SPECIFIC sh-WORD SUBCLASS ('she-' body sh words: shedy/shey/sheol/sheedy/sheody/sheckhy) selecting uniformly into the qok-onset class. In B-dialect: top-5 sh words account for 57.6% of sh→qok (over-represented); shedy alone drives 30.9% of all sh→qok. The qok-word distribution after sh is baseline-proportional — no preferred qok word. In A-dialect: same 'she-' prefix pattern (sheody=3.52×, sheol=2.63×) but more diffuse. Common short sh words (shol/shor/shody) have LOW qok rates (0.2-0.8×). Cross-dialect: the 'she-' body sh subclass is the universal carrier of sh→qok coupling. Architecture: implement as sh-body sub-class rule (not uniform onset-class), with qok successor drawn from qok vocabulary proportionally.
