## Findings

### Finding 1: d Is LF-ENRICHED, LL-ENRICHED, pos1-DEPLETED — OPPOSITE Profile to ch/sh

A d: LF=1.15×, pos1=0.58×, LL=1.68×. B d: LF=2.29×, pos1=0.46×, LL=1.37×. The d onset class is OPPOSITE to ch and sh in positional structure: while ch and sh are pos1-enriched "second-position response classes," d is LF-enriched and LL-enriched with strong pos1 depletion. B d LF=2.29× is the strongest LF-enrichment of any onset class characterized so far. Architecture: d is a "line-opening class" that preferentially occupies the first word of the line (LF); it explicitly avoids being the second word (pos1). The LF-formula rules (ch/sh following LF-formula words) are a separate mechanism: when those LF words are d-class, the pos1 response is NOT d but ch/sh (since d avoids pos1). d also enriches at LL (A=1.68×, B=1.37×), giving d a dual LF+LL enrichment pattern. Cross-dialect: B d is substantially MORE LF-enriched than A d (2.29× vs 1.15×), but both share pos1-depletion and LL-enrichment.

### Finding 2: d Self-Succession Is PRE_LL-Concentrated (A=2.40×, B=3.63×) — d→d Chain Drives LL Enrichment

A d→d: LF→pos1=0.67×(depleted), MID=1.20×, pre_LL→LL=2.40×. B d→d: LF→pos1=0.43×(depleted), MID=1.50×, pre_LL→LL=3.63×(very strong). B d at pre_LL → d at LL E=2.10× from the pre_LL predecessor analysis. The d→d chaining mechanism is specifically a pre_LL→LL phenomenon: when a d-word appears at pre_LL, it is followed by another d-word at LL with E=2.40×(A)/3.63×(B). This is the PRIMARY mechanism driving d's LL enrichment. Top A pairs: daiin→daiin(9), daiin→dal(6), daiin→dain(5). Top B pairs: daiin→daiin(3), dal→daiin(3), dy→daiin(3). Architecture: d→d self-succession is specifically a "LL terminal coupling" — d at pre_LL positions the line to close with another d word. Notably, d→d chaining is actively DEPLETED at LF→pos1 (0.67×/0.43×), emphasizing its pre_LL-concentration.

### Finding 3: A d→cth Pan-Positional E=1.61× — Confirms F373, Now Position-Resolved as Pan-Positional

A d→cth: LF→pos1=1.75×(N=21/255), MID_inner=1.44×(N=32/472), pre_LL→LL=1.84×(N=17/196). Overall E=1.61×. This confirms F373 (A d→cth E=1.7×). F373 is now resolved as pan-positional: A d→cth coupling is not position-specific but elevated at all positions (1.44-1.84×). This is distinct from the position-specific rules of other onset classes. B d→cth: only 3 tokens (E=1.19×, very small N, unreliable). The d→cth coupling is A-specific and pan-positional. Top A pairs: daiin→cthy(9), daiin→cthol(7), daiin→cthor(5). The "daiin→cth..." bigram (daiin at MID → cth-onset at MID+1) is the core of this rule; in A, d-body words regularly precede cth-onset words across all line positions.

### Finding 4: B d→sh LF=2.44× — Strongest B d Outgoing Rule; d Is the Primary B sh Predecessor at LF

B d→sh: LF→pos1=2.44×(N=81/344), MID_inner=1.37×(N=67/506), pre_LL→LL=0.49×(depleted). Overall E=1.63×. The strongest B d outgoing coupling is B d→sh at LF→pos1 (E=2.44×), followed by B d→sh at MID (E=1.37×). Top pairs: daiin→shey(7), daiin→shedy(5), dal→shedy(5), dar→shedy(5). Architecture: B d words at LF are preferentially followed by sh words at pos1 (E=2.44×). This is the "B d-opens, sh-responds" mechanism — a specific B-dialect version of the general LF-formula → ch/sh-pos1 pattern. This confirms the connection between d as LF-formula word and sh as second-position responder in B. Notably B d→ch at LF=1.37× is also elevated but weaker than B d→sh at LF (2.44× vs 1.37×): in B, d at LF prefers sh over ch as pos1 partner (unlike ch→sh avoidance).

### Finding 5: B d at pre_LL → d at LL E=2.10× — New B LL Routing Entry for d

B d at pre_LL → LL onset distribution: d=2.10×(N=25/128, STRONG), ch=0.72×(depleted), sh=1.27×, qok=0.62×(depleted), ol=1.12×. A d at pre_LL → d at LL E=1.38×. The strongest B d pre_LL routing is to d at LL (E=2.10×). Updated B LL routing table: ch→d(2.01×, F383); s/qok/ok/ot→ol(F376/F379/F380); qot/qok→r(F377/F380); sh→qok(2.73×, F384); **d→d(2.10×, NEW)**; r→d/ol(F382). B d actively routes to d-LL terminal when preceding the LL slot; qok and qot are depleted after d at pre_LL (B d→qok=0.62×, d→qot=0.44× at pre_LL). A d LL routing is less differentiated (d=1.38× mild, all others near-baseline).

### Finding 6: A d Vocabulary Is daiin-Dominant (31.1%); B d Is More Distributed — Cross-Dialect Divergence

A d: daiin=31.1% (403/1297 tokens), then ar(5.6%), ain(5.4%), al(4.9%), y(4.4%). B d: daiin=21.5% (255/1187), ar=11.5%(NEW!), ain=7.8%, al=7.4%, y=4.7%. A d is strongly daiin-dominated; B d is daiin-first but with much broader distribution (ar is 11.5% in B vs 5.6% in A). B d also includes dshedy(2.4%) and dchedy(1.8%) — compound-body forms not present in A top vocabulary. A d body: aiin-body (daiin + variants) = ~35%+; B d body: aiin-body =~25%, followed by ar-body. The "daiin hub" role (F362) is more concentrated in A; B d has more vocabulary spread to ar/ain/al bodies. Cross-dialect: both dialects share the aiin/ar/ain/al body repertoire as top forms, but the proportions differ substantially.

### Finding 7: A d→r=0.38× (Globally Depleted) — d Does Not Produce r Successors

A d→r: overall E=0.38× (N=1/923, near-absent). B d→r: overall E=0.65× (N=10/978, depleted). A d→r overall is categorical: only 1 bigram total (pre_LL: 1 case, E=1.80× from tiny N). A d avoids producing r successors across all positions. B d→r is also depleted (0.65×) but not categorical. This contrasts with ch at pre_LL → r (A=6.39×, F383): r is produced after ch not after d. Architecture: in A-dialect, r-onset words are produced by ch at pre_LL (F383) and by the B qot/qok pre_LL routing (F377/F380); d is EXCLUDED from r production. The A grammar separates r production (ch-mediated) from d-terminal production (d→d chaining).
