## Findings

### Finding 1: y→y Is CLASS-LEVEL Onset Memory (92.7% Different Words in A, 86.7% in B)

y→y succession is driven by class-level onset memory: in A-dialect, 38/41 y→y pairs involve different y-onset words; in B-dialect, 26/30 pairs are different words. No dominant word pairs — top y→y pairs each have count=1 (except ytaiin→ytaiin=2 in B). This is parallel to ok→ok (F363: 84-86% different words) and confirms self-succession universality (F369) has the same CLASS-LEVEL mechanism across onset classes. Word clustering is not the mechanism.

### Finding 2: y→d Is Confirmed Near-Baseline in Continuation Lines (A=0.97×, B=1.08×) — F361 REPLICATED

y-onset successor profile: y→y is the ONLY elevated successor (A=1.92×, B=2.71×). All other onsets are near-baseline or depleted: y→d=0.97×/1.08×, y→ch=0.86×/1.09×, y→qok=0.74×/0.83×. This replicates F361's refutation of y→d coupling in continuation lines. The F326 y→d enrichment was a cross-register artifact. In continuation lines, y-onset words have no preferred successor class except themselves.

### Finding 3: y→y Is Not LL-Biased (Unlike ok→ok in A-Dialect) — LF→pos1 and MID_inner Dominant

y→y position distribution: A-dialect = LF→pos1(11), MID_inner(23), pre_LL→LL(2). B-dialect = LF→pos1(12), pos1→pos2(5), MID_inner(8), pre_LL→LL(5). The bulk of y→y pairs occur at LF→pos1 and MID_inner, not near line end. Contrast with ok→ok (F363: A-dialect LL-biased at 15.5%). y-onset's LF enrichment (A=14.3%, B=11.5%) means many y→y successions occur at the line beginning (y at LF, y at pos1).

### Finding 4: B-Dialect y-Onset Vocabulary Is edy-Suffix Heavy (B-Dialect Marker)

B-dialect top y-words include yteedy(25), ykeedy(22), ykedy(14), ytedy(13) — all ending in edy. A-dialect top words: ykaiin, ykeey, ytaiin, ykar — edy-suffix rare. B-dialect's y-class includes B-specific compound forms (y+gallows+edy), consistent with the edy/eedy = B-dialect rate ≥5% criterion. y-onset compounds carry the B-dialect character: the B y-class is a gallery of y+gallows+edy compounds.

### Finding 5: No Strong Cross-Onset Rules from y-Onset; y Is a Self-Contained Class

y-onset successor profile shows no cross-onset elevations ≥1.3× in either dialect. y-onset words lead only to more y-onset words (self-succession) or to baseline-rate onset classes. Predecessors of y-onset words: all near-baseline except y→y itself. The y-onset class is grammatically self-contained — it participates in succession chains but does not couple to other onset classes. The only exception is B y←ot E=1.36× (weak, N=28) — ot-onset words slightly more often precede y-onset words in B-dialect.

## Summary

H581 CONFIRMED. y→y self-succession is CLASS-LEVEL (A=92.7% different words, B=86.7% different words). Mechanism is identical to ok→ok (F363). y-onset's grammar is self-contained: the only elevated succession rule from y is y→y itself; all other onset successors are near-baseline. B-dialect y-vocabulary is edy-suffix heavy (B-specific compound forms). y→y concentrated at LF→pos1 and MID_inner (not LL-biased). F361 y→d refutation replicated (A=0.97×, B=1.08× in continuation lines). Architecture: y-onset needs class-level onset memory (alongside ok/ot/qot), with no cross-class coupling rules needed.
