## Findings

### Finding 1: ol Is LL-Enriched, LF-Depleted in Both Dialects — Cross-Dialect LL-Terminal Grammar

A ol: LL=1.43×, LF=0.64×, MID/pre_LL near-baseline. B ol: LL=1.59×, LF=0.58×, MID/pre_LL near-baseline. B ol accounts for 8.4% of all LL positions (181/2161 pre_LL→LL pairs) — the second largest LL onset class after d-onset (B d-class LL E=2.10× per F368). Cross-dialect consistency: both dialects show LL-enrichment (1.43-1.59×) and LF-depletion (0.58-0.64×) for ol. Architecture: ol is a LL-terminal class in both dialects, but it is more LF-depleted (0.58×) than ot (B LF=0.33× per F378 — ot has stronger LF depletion). ol's LL profile: confirmed LL-terminal but with softer LL enrichment than d-class.

### Finding 2: Bare 'ol' Dominates ol Vocabulary (A=32.6%, B=27.7%) — The Single Most Frequent Word in its Class

A bare 'ol': 72/221=32.6% of all A ol tokens. B bare 'ol': 261/942=27.7% of all B ol tokens. This is an extraordinary concentration — no other onset class has a single word type accounting for 27-33% of all class tokens (compare: B qok top word qokeedy=12.5%; A qot top word qotchy=15.7%; A ot top word otol=8.6%). Bare 'ol' is a degenerate onset-only word with no body. Its dominance reflects that the ol onset can occur as a complete word form, unlike template classes (qot/ot/qok/ok) where body forms are necessary. Architecture: 'ol' functions as a minimal grammatical particle (analogous to how 'daiin' functions as a minimal d-class LL terminal). The bare form's dominance at LL (top B LL word in F366: oly(3×), and bare ol appears in top LL word pairs qokain→ol(3)) is consistent with ol being a closed-paradigm LL terminal.

### Finding 3: A ol Self-Succession Is Pan-Positional (LF=2.88×, MID=1.59×, pre_LL=2.32×) — Unique Among Characterized Onset Classes

A ol→ol: LF→pos1=2.88×, MID_inner=1.59×, pre_LL→LL=2.32× — elevated at ALL three position categories. Overall E=1.92×. No other characterized onset class shows uniform pan-positional self-succession elevation: qot peaks at LF/MID but is absent at pre_LL (F377); ot peaks at MID/pre_LL but is depleted at LF (F378); qok peaks at MID but shows 0.85× at pre_LL (F380). A ol is the only class with elevated self-succession at every position category. Architecture: A-dialect ol clusters throughout lines at any position, not restricted to line-initial or line-final zones. This suggests A ol functions as a continuation-class filler (can appear anywhere) that tends to generate local ol-onset chains.

### Finding 4: B ol Self-Succession Concentrated at pre_LL→LL (2.55×) While LF-Flat (1.02×) — B ol Chains Specifically at Line Endings

B ol→ol: LF→pos1=1.02× (flat), MID_inner=1.61×, pre_LL→LL=2.55×. Overall E=1.68×. B ol self-succession concentrates at line-end: when ol appears at pre_LL in B-dialect, it tends to chain to another ol at LL (2.55×). But when ol appears at LF, it does not cluster with ol at pos1 (1.02×=baseline). Contrast with A ol (pan-positional): B ol's clustering rule is LL-specific. Top B ol→ol pairs: ol→olaiin(4), olkain→ol(3), olkaiin→ol(3), ol→ol(3) — confirming the bare 'ol' LL terminal role (olkain→ol: a full-body ol word at pre_LL → bare 'ol' at LL).

### Finding 5: B ol→ch LF→pos1=1.98× — New B LF Formula: ol-Onset LF → ch-Onset pos1; B ol→d LF→pos1=0.00× (Complete Absence)

B ol→ch: LF→pos1=1.98× (24/70) — when ol occupies the LF position in B-dialect, it transitions to ch-onset at pos1 at nearly double the baseline rate. B ol→d: LF→pos1=0.00× (0/70) — d-onset is completely absent at pos1 after ol in B. Top pairs: ol→chedy(16), ol→cheey(8), ol→chey(5). This establishes a new B-dialect LF formula: ol-LF → ch-pos1. Compare to known B LF formulas: s→sh (F376: LF→pos1=2.47×), d→sh (F373: pos1→pos2 concentrated). The B-dialect line-opening formula space now includes: gallows-LF → diverse pos1; d-LF → sh-pos1; s-LF → sh-pos1; ol-LF → ch-pos1. B LF-onset classes have specific pos1 couplings.

### Finding 6: A ol→ok Bi-Positional Coupling (LF→pos1=2.49×, pre_LL→LL=2.51×) — ol and ok Are Coupled at BOTH Line Extremes in A

A ol→ok: LF→pos1=2.49× (N=4/25), pre_LL→LL=2.51× (N=5/31). Both positions elevated. Overall E=1.47×. This bi-positional coupling means A ol and ok co-occur at both line-opening (ol-LF → ok-pos1) and line-closing (ol-pre_LL → ok-LL) transitions. This is a distinct coupling pattern from A qok↔ok MID bidirectional (F380: concentrated at MID_inner). The A-dialect ok-onset appears to have dual couplings: with qok in MID (F375/F380) and with ol at line extremes. Architecture: A-dialect ok operates as a boundary-transition class (receives from ol at LF and pre_LL, pairs with qok at MID).

### Finding 7: B ol→r pre_LL→LL=2.41× — Extends the B pre_LL→r Rule to ol-Onset Predecessors

B ol→r: pre_LL→LL=2.41× (N=4/105). Compare: B qot→r pre_LL=5.18× (F377), B qok→r pre_LL=3.01× (F380). Three onset classes (qot, qok, ol) now show elevated B pre_LL→r transitions. N=4 is directional; corroborated by the consistent B pre_LL→r pattern across qot/qok. Top pairs: olkedy→roroteedy(1), olkeey→raiin(1) — 'r'-onset body forms at LL. GENERALIZED B pre_LL→r rule: now covers {qot,qok,ol} at pre_LL (E=2.41-5.18×). Architecture: B-dialect r-onset LL words are selectively preceded by a growing set of onset classes at pre_LL.

### Finding 8: B Predecessors of ol at LL — s-Onset Is the Strongest Predictor (E=2.98×); ch-Onset Is Depleted (E=0.63×)

B pre_LL onset → ol at LL: s=2.98× (6/181), qok=1.25× (31/181), ok=1.33× (18/181), ot=1.12× (18/181), ol=1.71× (15/181). Depleted: ch=0.63× (19/181), y=0.63× (2/181). The strongest B pre_LL predecessor of ol is s-onset (E=2.98×), consistent with F376's B s→ol pre_LL=4.46× (measured from s's perspective; 2.98× here is from ol's perspective). ch-onset at pre_LL DEPLETED before ol at LL (E=0.63×) — when ch occupies pre_LL in B, it does not tend to produce ol at LL. This is the inverse of ch→daiin (F371: B ch→daiin MID=1.54×, pre_LL=1.68×): ch → daiin (not ol) in B. Architecture: B LL grammar has complementary routing: ch at pre_LL → daiin at LL; s/qok/ok at pre_LL → ol at LL.

### Finding 9: A ol→r Completely Absent (0/169) — A-Dialect ol and r Are Mutually Exclusive

A ol→r: E=0.00× (N=0/169 outgoing ol bigrams). r-onset does not appear after ol in A-dialect continuation lines. This is a categorical exclusion, not a depletion. Also A ol→qot strongly depleted (E=0.24×, N=1/169). In A-dialect, the r-onset appears at LL rarely (it is a B-dialect phenomenon per F368: B LL is enriched for r; A LL is d-class-dominated). A ol→r=0.00× is consistent with r being essentially absent from A-dialect in general, not a specific ol→r exclusion.
