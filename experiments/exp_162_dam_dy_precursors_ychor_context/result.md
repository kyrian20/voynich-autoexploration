## Summary of New Findings (F169)

1. **[dam@LL] and [dy@LL] have NO dominant grammatical precursor** (H321 CONFIRMED as NEGATIVE): The penultimate slot before dam/dy is diverse (d-onset 2.05-1.80×, y-onset 2.77×, but no single word enriched >3.23×). Unlike [daiin][cthy@LL] (which is a specific fixed phrase at 3.57×), dam and dy are freely placed terminal particles. They are categorical terminal words, not part of a specific bigram construction.

2. **[ychor cthy] lines both in A-herbal (f17 and f45), third word = ch-onset both times** (H323 CONFIRMED): The [ychor cthy [ch-word] ...] 3-gram construction is a specific A-herbal formula. The f17 line continues with ot-words at positions 4-5 (`otor oteol`), creating a [ychor cthy ch-word ot-word ot-word] 5-gram with two consecutive ot-words at LL.

3. **ychor appears in B-dialect contexts (f86/cosmo, f106/stars)** — 2/15 ychor lines are B-dialect. ychor is predominantly A-herbal but not A-exclusive.

4. **d@LF→cth@second decomposition (H324 CONFIRMED)**: daiin@LF→cth@second=2.69× (8/71=11.3%); d-ch-prefix words (dchor, dchol, dshor, dchey)→cth@second=3.98× (16.7% each); LL-enriched d-words (dar, dor, dair, dol) → cth@second = 0×. The d@LF→cth@second construction is driven by: (1) daiin as the dominant contributor by count (38% of cases), (2) d-ch-prefix opener words as higher per-capita rate contributors. LL-enriched d-words never introduce cth at second — confirming the LF/LL paradigm split within d-onset.
