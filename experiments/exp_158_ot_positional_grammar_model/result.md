## H315: [daiin@LF][cthor@second] LINES — FULL CONTEXT

**3 lines** (all short, 3-5 words):
1. `daiin cthor chol chor` → third word: chol (ch-onset)
2. `daiin cthor chol ykchor chordy` → third word: chol (ch-onset)  
3. `daiin cthor daiin dal dys` → third word: daiin (d-onset)

**Pattern**: 2/3 have [daiin][cthor][chol] as the opening triple. These are the shortest lines with cthor. The [daiin cthor chol chor] pattern (2 occurrences) appears to be a recurring formula.

**For comparison, daiin@MID → cthor@MID lines** (also n=3):
- `sheo pcheey qoty daiin cthor otydy sain` (7 words, cthor at MID)
- `okshy daiin okchor,chky qotchy daiin cthor oty` (8 words, cthor at MID)
- `qotol d[o:a]r okchor daiin cthor otam` (6 words, cthor at MID)

All longer; all have ot-words after cthor (otydy, oty, otam). The daiin@MID→cthor@MID construction tends to have [daiin cthor ot-word] continuation (consistent with F151's finding of daiin→cthor→ot triple).

## CORRECTED cth-INTRODUCTION GRAMMAR MODEL (H316)

Five constructions for cth-word introduction in A P-type body text:

**Construction 1: [daiin@LF][cth@second]** (n=6 daiin@LF→cth cases)
- Form: Line opens with daiin, cth-word at position 2
- cth-words: cthor (3), cthain (1), ctheey (1), ctheol (1)
- Sub-pattern: [daiin cthor chol...] appears 2× as a 3-word formula

**Construction 2: [daiin@MID][cthol@MID]** (n=5 cases)
- Form: daiin at medial position directly precedes cthol (also at MID)
- All 5 cases: cthol ends up at MID (never LL)

**Construction 3: [daiin@MID][cthy@LL]** (n=5+5 cases from F164 and F160)
- Form: daiin at medial position, line closes with cthy
- cthy goes to LL in 55.6% of daiin→cthy bigrams

**Construction 4: [chol@second/MID][cth@subsequent]** (n=20 cases)
- Form: chol at non-LF position introduces a cth-word later in the line
- cth-words: cthol (6), cthy (5), cthor (2), others (7)
- chol is always introduced by some other LF word (ot-onset=2.95× before chol@second)

**Construction 5: [chor@second/MID][cth@subsequent]** (n=12 cases)
- Form: chor at non-LF position introduces a cth-word later in the line
- cth-words: cthom (3), cthol (2), cthy (2), cthor (2), others (3)

## Summary of New Findings (F165)

1. **ot-words have a 4-way internal positional paradigm**: otchor/otchol=LF-openers (2.17-3.64×); oty/otal=LL-closers (1.39-2.08×); otol/otaiin/otchy=MID-medials (1.25-1.35×). ot-class is not uniform — different ot-words specialize in different line positions.

2. **y-onset is the strongest line-opener in A-dialect (LF=2.46×)**; d-onset is the strongest line-closer (LL=1.68×); cth never opens lines (LF=0.15×); ch/ch_e strongly avoid LF (0.23-0.47×) and are second-position words (1.45-1.46×).

3. **[daiin cthor chol chor] appears to be a recurring formula** (2/3 daiin@LF→cthor@second lines share this opening). Distinct from daiin@MID→cthor@MID lines (which tend toward [daiin cthor ot-word] continuation).

4. **Corrected 5-construction grammar model for cth-introduction in A**: (1) [daiin@LF][cth@second]; (2) [daiin@MID][cthol@MID]; (3) [daiin@MID][cthy@LL]; (4) [chol@second/MID][cth]; (5) [chor@second/MID][cth].
