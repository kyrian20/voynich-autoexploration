## H277: cth VOCABULARY SHIFT IN A-HERBAL — PARTIALLY CONFIRMED

The cth decline in A-herbal is **not purely proportional**: cthor declines 2.5× faster than expected while cthaiin is completely stable. This is partial evidence for a content shift.

### Proportionality Test
**Overall cth rate T3/T1 = 0.598×** (if proportional decline, each word should decline by this factor).

Key deviations from proportional:
- **cthor: 0.24 (expected 0.60, dev=0.41×)** — declines at 2.5× the expected rate. cthor goes from the 2nd most common cth-word (6.942‰) to minor status (1.688‰).
- **cthaiin: 1.03 (expected 0.60, dev=1.73×)** — completely stable, unchanged across all three periods.
- **cthol: 0.78 (expected 0.60, dev=1.30×)** — relatively stable (declines slower than average).
- cthy/cthey/cthar/cthal: all decline at similar rate (~0.46×, roughly proportional to the 0.60 overall).

### "Stable" cth-Words (T3/T1 ratio 0.5-2.0, T1 > 0.1‰)

The 4 stable words are: **cthol** (A-herbal nominal, most common), **cthaiin** (particle-like), **cthy<$>** (transcription artifact), **ctheol**.

### "Complete Disappearance" from T3
35 cth-types present in T1 are absent from T3. However, **all are hapax legomena in T1** (n=1 each, except cth[a:o]r=2). The "disappearances" are consistent with **expected sampling behavior** in a smaller corpus (T3=1777 words vs T1=2449 words) — rare words won't be resampled. These are NOT evidence of systematic vocabulary disappearance.

The one partial exception: **ctholy** (T1=0.408‰, T2=0.970‰, T3=0) — appears in both T1 and T2 but not T3. This is a specific form (cth-ol-y, triply suffixed) that might be botanically specific.

### Interpretation
**Mixed evidence**: The cth decline is PARTIALLY content-driven and PARTIALLY proportional scribal drift:
1. **cthor disproportionate decline** (T3/T1=0.24 vs overall 0.60): cthor is the A-herbal "-or suffix" cth nominal (parallel to cthol/-ol suffix). The specific decline of cthor (but not cthol) suggests that later herbal entries describe plants with fewer "cthor"-type objects, while "cthol"-type objects remain common. This is weakly consistent with content change.
2. **cthaiin stability** (T3/T1=1.03): cthaiin is a fixed particle-like form that doesn't vary with botanical content — consistent with it being a grammatical/positional word rather than a content word. Its stability supports the view that the cth CONTENT words (cthor, cthy) are declining while the PARTICLE forms (cthaiin) are stable.
3. **Most other cth-words decline proportionally (~0.46×)**: cthy, cthey, cthar, cthal all decline at similar rates. This is consistent with a GENERAL reduction in cth vocabulary use in late A-herbal, not just specific words.

**Most likely interpretation**: The A-herbal cth decline is primarily a **general vocabulary density reduction** (all cth-words used less often in later folios), with cthor additionally declining faster due to a content shift in botanical subject matter.

### Correction to exp_139 — loldaiin is A-EXCLUSIVE
exp_139 classified `loldaiin` as a "clean B-form" because it ends in -aiin (in B_SUFFIXES list). **This was wrong.** Corpus-wide: loldaiin A=1, B=0 — it is **A-exclusive**, not B-dialect. The -aiin suffix is common in B but also appears in A vocabulary. loldaiin is an A-form (l+ol+daiin compound). Corrected late A-herbal l-forms from exp_139:
- **lkeedy** (A=1, B=32, 97%B): genuine B-dialect marker appearing in late A-herbal
- **lkeey** (A=1, B=33, 97%B): genuine B-dialect marker appearing in late A-herbal
- **loldaiin**: A-exclusive — NOT a B-vocabulary intrusion

### B-Exclusive vs A-Exclusive l-Words
- **B-exclusive l-types (absent from any A-folio): 376 types** — the vast majority of B l-vocabulary is inaccessible to A-dialect.
- **A-exclusive l-types: 28 types** (mostly hapax legomena with transcription artifacts).

### Interpretation
B-dialect l-vocabulary is structurally rich: lch-stem (lchedy, lchey, lshedy — l+ch-paradigm stems), lk-stem (lkaiin, lkeey, lkedy — l+k-paradigm stems), plus standalone `l` as a morpheme. The l-onset is the B-dialect parallel to A-dialect's cth-onset: a morphological prefix that marks B-paradigm content words. lchedy (l+ch+edy) parallels cthol (cth+ol) as a prefix+stem+suffix combination. The 19× ratio (B:A l-tokens) confirms l is a B structural onset, not a shared particle.

## Summary of New Findings (F147)

1. **cth vocabulary decline in A-herbal is MIXED (not purely proportional)**: cthor disproportionately declines (T3/T1=0.24, 2.5× faster than overall 0.60). cthaiin is stable (1.03×). cthol relatively stable (0.78×). Most other cth-words decline proportionally (~0.46×). Pattern: general cth density reduction PLUS cthor-specific additional decline. Consistent with scribal drift + botanical content shift (fewer "cthor"-type plants in later herbal section).
2. **B-dialect l-vocabulary: 1120 tokens (vs A 59 tokens), dominated by lch- and lk-prefixed forms**: lchedy(102, 100%B), lkaiin(37, 100%B), lkeey(33, 97%B), lkeedy(32, 97%B) are the core B l-words. Suffix paradigm: B l-words = 42.3% B-suffix (vs A l-words = 11.9%). Stars has the most B l-words (725 tokens, 65% of all B l-tokens).
3. **loldaiin CORRECTED from exp_139**: loldaiin was misclassified as "B-form" in exp_139. Corpus-wide: A=1, B=0 — it is A-exclusive. The genuine B-dialect l-forms in late A-herbal are only lkeedy(1) and lkeey(1).
4. **lk-prefix is the B l-morphological marker**: lkaiin, lkain, lkedy, lkar, lkeedy, lkeey are all essentially 100% B-dialect. These parallel cth-prefix in A-dialect (both are productive prefix morphemes on stem classes).
