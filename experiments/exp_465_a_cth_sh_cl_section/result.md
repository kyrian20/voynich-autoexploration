## Interpretation

**H_new_AC CONFIRMED: cth→sh CL is herbal_a-concentrated (9/12 cases, E=1.5×) with cosmo directional (N=3, E=4.0×).** This parallels the B section grammar finding (F444): herbal-section cross-line couplings involving sh (B d→sh, sh→d) and A cth→sh CL are both herbal-concentrated. cth→sh CL is driven by o-body (ctho* words, E=2.31×/N=6) and e-body (cthe* words, directional).

**NEW: cth→qot CL E=2.07×(N=7).** The cth onset class has TWO elevated cross-line targets: →sh (1.93×/N=12) and →qot (2.07×/N=7). cth at LL predicts BOTH sh and qot at the next line's first word. cth also predicts →ok CL (1.74×/N=6). cth routes AWAY from y and d cross-line. This makes cth a "routing onset" similar to ch (which routes to qot in B, to ch in A): cth at LL is a cross-line signal for the sh/qot/ok content-class onset cluster.

**H_new_AD ANSWERED: A stars zeros are NOT due to paragraph length.** Stars paragraphs average 13.0 lines vs herbal_a 11.0 — longer on average. The zero cross-line couplings in exp_464 are CAUSED BY A STARS LF GRAMMAR THAT USES DIFFERENT ONSET CLASSES. Specifically:
- The tested couplings (ch→ch, ok→y, s→qot, s→s) involve onset classes that are rare or absent at LF in stars: ch=4% (depleted 2.6×), qot=0.8% (depleted 9×), ok=4.8%
- Stars uses t (12%), d (20.8%), y (20%), s (10.4%) as dominant LF onset classes
- A cth→sh CL shows 0 cases in stars — consistent: stars doesn't use sh at LF (5.6% vs herbal 8.7%)

**Key finding: A "stars" section (f57-72, zodiac/calendar) has a DISTINCT LF grammar from A herbal_a:**
- t-onset at LF: 3.3× elevated vs herbal_a — the most extreme A section-grammar difference found so far
- qot depleted 9× at LF in stars
- This creates a stars-specific LF profile: {t, d, y, s} dominant; {qot, ot, ch} absent

**Cross-section comparison:**
- B astro: qok-LF elevated 1.9× (F431 — qok at 19.6% vs 5.6% elsewhere)
- A stars: t-LF elevated 3.3× (12.0% vs 3.7% in herbal_a); t is NOT qok's analog

B astro (qok dominant LF) and A stars (t dominant LF) use different onset classes as their "astronomical grammar marker" — another A/B inversion at section level.
