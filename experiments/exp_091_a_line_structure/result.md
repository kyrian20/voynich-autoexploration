### Revised A Line Grammar Model

**A line structure (modular):**
- **OPENER** (optional, line-pos0): d+ch or y+ch compound (59-86% LFirst); alternatively s-onset, daiin, o-onset
- **MID-LINE** (pervasive): bare ch-onset words (27.3% at pos1, often self-continuing ch→ch)
- **CLOSER** (optional, line-final): d-onset bare words (dal/dar/dam/daiin/dy at 23.0% last)

The mid-line ch-onset and line-final d-onset are the GRAMMAR BACKBONE of A lines. Openers are optional positional markers that appear when a line begins a new discourse segment.

## Combined Finding (F098)

**H158 STRONGLY SUPPORTED**: A has two line-initial prefix systems for ch-stems: y+ch (LFirst=85.9%, 8.86× vs bare) and d+ch (LFirst=59.3%, 6.17× vs bare). Both are strongly line-initial; bare ch is the mid-line form. Top line-initial forms: dchor(66.7%), dchol(72.7%), ychor(100%), ychol(85.7%).

**H159 STRONGLY SUPPORTED**: A ch-stem paradigm shows: bare (9.7%) << d+ch (59.6%, 6.17× contrast) ≤ y+ch (85.9%, 8.86×). Three-stem comparison confirmed on 12 ch-stems appearing in all three forms. Unlike B's 3-tier system (bare < qo-mid < y-initial), A has a 2-tier system (bare < {d,y}-initial) with NO clear mid-line prefix.

**H160 CONFIRMED**: A mid-line is dominated by bare ch-onset words (chol/chor/chey/cheol at pos1). ch self-continues (ch→ch at pos2 = 28.4%). A line grammar is modular: [d+ch or y+ch OPENER] → [bare ch MID-LINE × N] → [d-onset CLOSER]. The A mid-line ch = A's pervasive grammatical content backbone, analogous to B's qo-prefix mid-line presence.

**CROSS-DIALECT STRUCTURAL INSIGHT (F098)**: Both dialects implement a positional grammar:
- B: LINE-OPENER (y+k/t) → MID-LINE (qo+k/t) → LINE-CLOSER (-am/oly)
- A: LINE-OPENER (y+ch or d+ch) → MID-LINE (bare ch) → LINE-CLOSER (d+suffix)

The SAME logical positions (opener, mid, closer) are encoded by different morphological strategies: B uses prefix alternation (y vs qo vs bare); A uses prefix alternation (y vs d) for openers and bare ch for mid-line. Both are PREFIX-ENCODED POSITIONAL GRAMMAR.
