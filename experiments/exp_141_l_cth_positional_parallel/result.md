### Interpretation of Positional Profiles
- **cth-words**: Strongly LF-depleted (0.14× — almost never appear at line-start), significantly LL-enriched (1.38×). Profile: "non-opener, line-closer skew." Only 2.3% of cth-tokens appear at line-first.
- **lch-words**: Moderately LF-depleted (0.81× — somewhat less LF than baseline), moderately LL-enriched (1.27×). More flexible at LF than cth-words.
- **lk-words**: Strongly LF-depleted (0.33×) AND strongly medial (74.5%, 1.15× baseline). LL rate is BELOW baseline (0.87×). lk-words are the "most purely medial" l-subgroup.
- **Bottom line**: cth and lch-words both avoid LF and tend toward LL/MID. But lk-words are more medial than either, and lch-words are more LF-flexible than cth-words.

## Summary of New Findings (F148)

1. **lch-words and cth-words share broadly similar positional profiles (both LF-depleted, MID-primary, LL-enriched) — PARTIAL structural parallel CONFIRMED**: cth-words: LF=2.3% (0.14×), MID=58.0% (1.13×), LL=22.5% (1.38×). lch-words: LF=9.6% (0.81×), MID=59.9% (0.93×), LL=15.0% (1.27×). Both avoid line-first position and show LL enrichment. Key finding: **lchedy and cthol have nearly identical positional profiles** (MID=66.7%/69.4%, LF=21.6%/20.4%, LL=11.8%/10.2%) — the canonical B l-form and canonical A cth-form are positionally equivalent.
2. **lk-words are MORE MEDIAL than cth-words** (lk: MID=74.5%, 1.15× baseline; cth: MID=58.0%, 1.13× baseline). lk-words are also LF-depleted (0.33×) and LL-near-baseline (0.87×) — a different positional profile from cth (which is LL-enriched).
3. **Bigram contexts are COMPLETELY DIFFERENT between cth and lch**: cth-words preceded/followed by d/ch/sh (A-dialect onsets); lch-words preceded/followed by qok/l/ch_e (B-dialect onsets). Both word types are embedded in their respective dialect's grammatical environment — the structural parallel does NOT extend to lexical context.
4. **cthor anomaly: LF=39.4%** — cthor is unusually LF-present for a cth-word. This may explain its disproportionate decline in late A-herbal (F147): if cthor marks line-opening content (a different grammatical role than medial cthol), and if late A-herbal has fewer such constructions, cthor's specific decline reflects a grammatical/content shift.
