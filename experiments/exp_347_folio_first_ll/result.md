# exp_347: Folio-First LL Gallows Depletion — Length or Grammar? (H549)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H549 — Folio-first LL is gallows-depleted (B: E=0.68×, A: E=0.29× vs continuation LL, F346). Two explanations: (1) length-driven — folio-first lines are shorter so LL falls on MID-class words; (2) grammar-driven — folio-first lines don't apply the canonical LL-terminal formula.
**Result:** H549 ANSWERED: GRAMMAR-DRIVEN, not length-driven. B-dialect folio-first lines have the SAME mean length as continuation lines (7.67 vs 7.80 words). A-dialect folio-first lines are LONGER (6.47 vs 5.68). The folio-first LL lacks canonical LL-terminal onset classes (l-onset depleted: B E=0.15×) and instead has content-class onsets (s/sh/y enriched: 2.08-3.17×). The folio-opener grammar constrains position 1 (LF) and does not apply the standard LL-terminal formula to the last position.

## Findings

### Finding 1: H549 ANSWERED — Folio-First LL Depletion Is Grammar-Driven

B-dialect folio-first lines have identical mean length to continuation lines (7.67 vs 7.80 words). A-dialect folio-first lines are longer (6.47 vs 5.68). The gallows depletion at folio-first LL cannot be explained by shorter lines — if anything, longer A-dialect folio-first lines would produce a richer LL vocabulary, not a depleted one.

The explanation is grammatical: the folio-opener formula constrains only position 1 (LF = gallows onset, F346). The LL position of folio-first lines does NOT apply the canonical LL-terminal grammar that generates am/l-onset terminal words. Instead, folio-first LL is populated by content-class words (s/sh/y/o) that happen to end the line.

### Finding 2: The Canonical LL-Terminal l-Onset (dal, sal) Is Strongly Depleted from Folio-First LL

B-dialect folio-first LL l-onset = E=0.15× vs continuation LL. The l-onset words (dal=2, and others) are strongly depleted. In continuation lines, l-onset words (dal, sal = am/al-terminal class) are enriched at LL (F339 confirmed am-suffix LL enrichment). The folio-first line does not trigger the LL-terminal selection rule that generates am/al words.

### Finding 3: s/sh/y Content Onsets Are Enriched at Folio-First LL

B-dialect folio-first LL: s(3.17×), y(3.17×), sh(2.77×). A-dialect: s(2.17×), sh(2.08×), o(3.12×). These are onset classes with MID-dominant distribution (s/sh/y are primarily MID or LF onset classes). Their enrichment at folio-first LL suggests the last word of folio-first lines is effectively a MID-class word that happens to fall at the end of the line — i.e., no terminal formula is applied.

### Finding 4: A-Dialect d-Onset Is Depleted at Folio-First LL (E=0.63×) Despite Being LL-Enriched in Continuation Lines

A-dialect daiin is LL-enriched in continuation lines (F341, E=1.37×). But at folio-first LL, d-onset is E=0.63× (depleted). This is symmetric with the LF finding (daiin avoids folio-first LF, F344). Folio-first lines in A-dialect are free of the daiin formula at BOTH LF (F344) and LL (here). Folio-first lines are a "formula-free" zone for daiin in A-dialect.

## Summary

The folio-opener formula has a two-part structure:
1. **Position 1 (LF): gallows-onset one-word constraint** (p/k/t/f-onset, F345/F346)
2. **Position N (LL): NO canonical terminal formula** — the last word is whatever content word ends the line

This means folio-first lines have ONLY the LF constraint and otherwise fill positions with normal content. The LL gallows depletion (F346) follows from the absence of the l-onset terminal formula (dal, sal), not from shorter lines.
