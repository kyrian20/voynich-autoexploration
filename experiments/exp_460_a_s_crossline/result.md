## Interpretation

**H_new_Y CONFIRMED:** A s→qot CL = 2.82×(N=6), confirmed from exp_459's 2.77×. 6 distinct folios, 5 unique pairs = class-level rule.

**A/B s cross-line divergence — new finding:**
- A s at LL: →qot (2.82×) AND →s (2.58×) — two elevated targets; y depleted
- B s at LL: →s (2.15×) AND →qok (1.48×) — two elevated targets
- The {s, qot} vs {s, qok} cross-line routing is a dialect-specific rule difference

**Body-class driver (A s→qot):** y-body appears to drive s→qot (3 of 6 cases from s+y-body), but N is too small to confirm. aiin-body clearly drives s→s (F441 pattern confirmed: s+aiin→s at 3.64×/N=5). The body-class specificity for s→qot needs larger N.

**Mechanism context:** A s at LL → qot at next LF is consistent with paragraph grammar. If A-dialect paragraphs have an s-modal phase (lines ending in s-onset words) that transitions to a qot-modal phase (lines opening with qot-onset words), this would generate the s→qot cross-line coupling. The B analog uses qok instead of qot as the transition target. Both dialects have s-continuation across lines (the s-phase can persist) plus a transition target (A: qot, B: qok).

**F442 complement:** B ch-LL → qot-LF (F442). If A s-LL → qot-LF is a real rule, then qot-LF in both dialects is preceded by different onset classes: in B, ch-LL lines precede qot-LF lines; in A, s-LL lines precede qot-LF lines. Different paragraph-entry paths to qot.
