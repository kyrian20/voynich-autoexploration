# exp_357: Gallows-Onset LF → pos1 sh Elevation (H566)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H566 — Is the elevated sh at folio-opener pos1 (A: 25.6%) explained by a general gallows-LF → sh onset-class bigram rule, or is it register-specific to folio-opener lines?
**Result:** H566 CONFIRMED: the folio-opener pos1 sh elevation is largely explained by a cross-dialect **gallows→sh onset-class bigram rule**. A-dialect continuation lines where LF is gallows-onset have sh=23.4% at pos1 (vs non-gallows-LF lines: sh=13.6%, E=1.72×). B-dialect: gallows-LF→sh=19.6% vs non-gallows 16.1% (E=1.22×). NEW FINDING: gallows→anti-ch onset-class bigram also confirmed (A: ch after gallows-LF=17.4% vs 29.4%, E=0.59×; B: 13.2% vs 20.9%, E=0.63×). Cross-dialect invariant: after a gallows-onset word, sh is elevated and ch is depleted at the next position. This directly explains why the folio-opener pos1 (where 74% of LF words are gallows-onset) has elevated sh and balanced ch/sh.

## Findings

### Finding 1: Gallows→sh Onset-Class Bigram Is Cross-Dialect (A: E=1.72×, B: E=1.22×)

In continuation lines, gallows-LF words (p/k/t/f) produce elevated sh at pos1 compared to non-gallows-LF words. This is a cross-dialectal onset-class bigram rule: A-dialect E=1.72× (23.4% vs 13.6%); B-dialect E=1.22× (19.6% vs 16.1%). The rule is stronger in A-dialect than B-dialect. This gallows→sh rule is the primary explanation for the balanced ch/sh at A folio-opener pos1 (since opener LF is ~74% gallows, the opener inherits the gallows→sh elevation).

### Finding 2: Gallows→anti-ch Onset-Class Bigram Is Also Cross-Dialect (A: E=0.59×, B: E=0.63×)

After gallows-LF words, ch is DEPLETED at pos1 (A: 17.4% vs 29.4% non-gallows = E=0.59×; B: 13.2% vs 20.9% = E=0.63×). This is the mirror image of the gallows→sh rule: gallows words push pos1 toward sh and away from ch. Together: gallows→{sh+, ch-} is a cross-dialect onset-class bigram pair.

### Finding 3: The Folio-Opener pos1 ch/sh Balance Is Largely Explained by the Gallows→sh Rule

The folio-opener pos1 balance (A: ch=sh=25.6%) is predicted by the gallows→sh bigram:
- Folio-opener LF is ~74% gallows (which produce sh=23-29% at pos1) and ~26% non-gallows (sh=14-16%)
- Weighted prediction: 0.74×25% + 0.26×14% ≈ 22% sh at folio-opener pos1
- Observed: 25.6%
- Close match — the gallows→sh bigram largely explains the opener's elevated sh
- A opener gallows-LF pos1 sh=29.2% (vs cont gallows-LF 23.4%): slight register-specific elevation (5.8pp above cont baseline), but most of the pattern is explained by the onset-class rule

### Finding 4: A-Dialect k-Onset Has Strongest Gallows→sh Rule (38.1% sh at pos1)

Among specific gallows onset characters, A-dialect k produces sh at pos1 in 38.1% of continuation-line cases (N=42). This is substantially above the gallows-class average (23.4%) and non-gallows baseline (13.6%). B-dialect p-onset has the strongest sh induction (23.0%). The gallows→sh strength varies by specific gallows character and by dialect: A has k > p > f > t; B has p > t > k > f.

### Finding 5: The Gallows→sh/anti-ch Bigram Is Distinct from sh↔ch Avoidance (F328)

The ch↔sh avoidance (F328) documented that ch→sh and sh→ch onset-class bigrams are DEPLETED. The gallows→sh rule found here is a DIFFERENT mechanism: p/k/t/f-onset (not ch/sh) produces sh at pos1. This is gallows→sh, not ch→sh (which is depleted) or sh→ch (which is also depleted). The two rules are compatible: both reduce the probability of ch-sh adjacency (gallows→sh means ch is less likely after gallows, and ch↔sh avoidance directly depletes ch→sh).

## Summary

H566 CONFIRMED. The folio-opener pos1 sh elevation is explained by a cross-dialect **gallows→sh onset-class bigram** (A: E=1.72×, B: E=1.22×) plus a paired **gallows→anti-ch rule** (A: E=0.59×, B: E=0.63×). These are new bigram rules not previously identified in the 7×7 onset-class matrix (which used class-level coding). The rules operate at the individual onset-character level (p/k/t/f → sh elevation) and are detectable at the "gallows class vs non-gallows class" level in continuation lines.
