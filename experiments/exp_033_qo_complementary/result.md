# Experiment exp_033: qo-Word Complementary Distribution Test
Date: 2026-04-09

## Hypothesis Tested
H032: qo-forms and their bare stem equivalents (qokeedy/keedy, qokain/kain, etc.) appear in complementary distribution by position-in-line, Currier dialect, or section — testing whether they are true grammatical alternants.

## Results

### 1. Line Position: NO Complementarity

| Position | qo-form % | bare-form % | Difference |
|----------|-----------|-------------|------------|
| initial | 9.1% | 6.4% | +2.7% |
| medial | 85.5% | 87.0% | -1.5% |
| final | 5.4% | 6.6% | -1.2% |

Differences are minimal (<3%). Both forms appear predominantly in MEDIAL line position (~86%). **No positional complementarity.** Exception: qokeedy vs keedy shows a significant final-position difference (1% vs 10%, chi²=13.5) but this is an outlier among the top pairs.

### 2. Currier Dialect: STRONG BIAS (not complementarity)

| Dialect | qo-form % | bare-form % | Difference |
|---------|-----------|-------------|------------|
| A | 11.6% | 26.6% | **-15.0%** |
| B | 88.0% | 72.7% | **+15.3%** |

qo-forms are overwhelmingly Currier B (88%). Bare stems are more balanced but still mostly B (73%). This is NOT complementarity — both forms appear in both dialects — but a strong DIALECTAL BIAS.

Individual pair examples:
- qoky (76% B) vs ky (45% B): bare form more balanced
- qokey (83% B) vs key (43% B): strong B bias for qo-form
- qokol (50% B) vs kol (24% B): notable difference

### 3. Section: LARGE Distribution Difference

| Section | qo-form % | bare-form % | Difference |
|---------|-----------|-------------|------------|
| bio | 39.3% | 18.2% | **+21.1%** |
| herbal | 14.1% | 37.2% | **-23.1%** |
| S (special) | 37.1% | 34.7% | +2.4% |
| pharma | 2.7% | 2.0% | +0.7% |

qo-words concentrate in **bio section (39%)** and are scarce in herbal (14%). Bare k-initial words are the reverse — dominant in herbal (37%) and less common in bio (18%). This is consistent with the Currier A/B split: herbal=A, bio=B.

## Verdict

**H032: NOT SUPPORTED as grammatical complementarity.**

The distribution is NOT complementary — qo-forms and bare forms coexist in the same sections and dialects (no exclusive territory for either). Instead, the pattern is:

1. **qo is a DIALECTAL MARKER** for Currier B vocabulary: bare k-initial stems exist in both dialects, but qo-forms are overwhelmingly B (88%).

2. **The qo-prefix marks Currier B's version of k-initial stems**: same stem, more qo-prefix in B, less in A. This is consistent with a dialect feature where B uses qo-prefixed forms while A uses bare forms — similar to dialect variation (e.g., "a house" vs "the house" as article usage in different registers).

3. **Within Currier B**, both forms coexist (not complementary), suggesting qo-prefix may mark something subtler than a simple dialect toggle — perhaps a grammatical subtype within B vocabulary.

## New Finding (F040)
qo-forms are 2.3× less common in Currier A than bare k-initial stems (11.6% vs 26.6%) and 1.2× more common in Currier B (88.0% vs 72.7%). This creates a systematic A/B vocabulary correspondence: bare k-forms dominate A, qo-k-forms dominate B. Section-level: qo-forms 39% bio vs 14% herbal; bare stems 18% bio vs 37% herbal. The qo-prefix is a Currier B/biological section marker for k-initial vocabulary, not a grammatical alternant.

## Implications for A/B Structure
The qo-dialectal-marker finding strengthens exp_023's conclusion that A and B are NOT dialects of the same system. qo-words are a B-centric feature that exists in both corpora but belongs primarily to B. The A corpus uses bare k-initial forms where B uses qo-forms — suggesting a systematic vocabulary substitution rule rather than phonological conditioning.
