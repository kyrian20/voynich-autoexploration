## H297: POSITIONAL SLOTS OF cth-INTRODUCERS

### daiin→cth (n=36)

| Position of particle | LF | second | MID | LL |
|---------------------|-----|--------|-----|-----|
| daiin (before cth)  | 13.9% | 25.0% | **61.1%** | 0.0% |
| cth-word (after daiin) | 0.0% | 11.1% | **52.8%** | **36.1%** |

**daiin introduces cth from MEDIAL position** (MID=61.1%, second=25.0%). The resulting cth-word is at MID (52.8%) or LL (36.1%). This is a **within-line medial-to-closing nominal phrase**: [daiin at MID] → [cth at MID or LL].

**Notable**: 36.1% of daiin→cth bigrams end with the cth-word at LL (line-final). daiin introduces a cth-word that closes the line. This explains why cth-words show mild LL enrichment (1.20× overall) — the daiin→cth construction places cth-words at line ends.

### chol→cth (n=20) and chor→cth (n=12)

| Position of particle | LF | second | MID | LL |
|---------------------|-----|--------|-----|-----|
| chol (before cth)   | **50.0%** | 35.0% | 15.0% | 0.0% |
| cth-word (after chol)| 0.0% | **45.0%** | 40.0% | 15.0% |
| chor (before cth)   | **50.0%** | 16.7% | 33.3% | 0.0% |
| cth-word (after chor)| 0.0% | **50.0%** | 33.3% | 16.7% |

**chol/chor introduce cth from LINE-FRONT (LF=50%)**. When chol or chor is the first word of the line, it is followed by a cth-word at second (45%/50%) or MID (40%/33%). This is a **line-opening nominal phrase**: [chol/chor at LF] → [cth-word at second/MID].

### Two Distinct cth-Introduction Constructions

| Construction Type | Particle | Particle slot | cth-word slot | n |
|-------------------|---------|--------------|--------------|---|
| **Type 1: Medial-Closing** | daiin | MID (61%) | MID+LL (89%) | 36 |
| **Type 2: Line-Opening** | chol/chor | LF (50%) | second/MID (85-95%) | 32 |

**F157 KEY FINDING**: "cth-introduction" is not a single construction — it has two structurally distinct forms:
- **Type 1** (daiin): mid-line nominal phrase where daiin functions as an in-line determiner, followed by a cth-nominal that may close the line
- **Type 2** (chol/chor): line-opening nominal phrase where the line begins with chol/chor (typical A line-openers), followed immediately by the cth-word as the second element

This explains why chol and chor "introduce cth" (F156, enrichment 2.1-2.7×) even though they are not grammatical particles — they are line-opener words that happen to be followed by cth-nominals in a systematic construction.

### cthol→cth (n=4)

| Position | cthol at second/MID (100%) → cth-word at MID (100%) |

cthol precedes cth from second/MID position — fully medial, like daiin.

### Non-cth Particles (aiin, ol, ar, s) → cth

**All zero**: aiin, ol, ar, s have 0 cases of preceding cth-onset words in A P-type body text. The 0.00× enrichment is categorical.

## H298: dy→sh — NOT CROSS-LINE

dy positional distribution overall (n=62):
- LF: 6 (9.7%), second: 4 (6.5%), MID: 22 (35.5%), **LL: 30 (48.4%)**

dy→sh bigrams (n=6), positions of dy:
| dy slot | cth-word | Note |
|---------|----------|------|
| second  | shey     | within-line |
| second  | shey     | within-line |
| MID     | shory    | within-line |
| LF      | shol     | within-line |
| MID     | shedy    | within-line |
| MID     | sheetey  | within-line |

**None of the dy→sh bigrams have dy at LL**. All are within-line (dy at LF/second/MID). The dy→sh enrichment is NOT a cross-line artifact.

**Refined interpretation**: dy is predominantly LL (48.4%) and cannot precede anything from that slot. Among the 32 non-LL dy tokens (LF+second+MID), 6 are followed by sh — a rate of 18.75%, vs baseline sh rate ~9.6%, giving enrichment ~2.0×. The dy→sh construction is real and within-line: when dy appears at non-LL positions, it preferentially precedes sh-words.

This may reflect an A-grammar construction [dy][sh-word] that appears mid-line: dy acting as a short medial word before an sh-onset nominal.

## Summary of New Findings (F157)

1. **TWO DISTINCT cth-INTRODUCTION CONSTRUCTIONS**: Type 1 = [daiin at MID] → [cth at MID/LL] (medial determiner phrase, n=36); Type 2 = [chol/chor at LF] → [cth at second/MID] (line-opening nominal phrase, n=32). chol/chor's "cth enrichment" (F156) reflects their frequent line-opener position followed by cth as the second word — they are line-starters, not grammatical determiners.

2. **daiin→cth places 36% of cth-words at line-final (LL)**: The daiin→cth construction often results in the cth-word closing the line. This explains cth's mild LL enrichment (1.20× overall) — the daiin→cth-at-LL construction is responsible.

3. **H298 CONFIRMED: dy→sh is a REAL within-line construction** (dy at second/MID/LF, never LL in the bigrams). Among non-LL dy occurrences, ~19% are followed by sh (vs baseline ~10%). The dy→sh enrichment is genuine, not a cross-line artifact.

4. **aiin/ol/ar/s precede cth at 0.00× (confirmed)**: All confirmed zero in P-type A-dialect body text. The "non-cth particles" are categorically excluded from cth-introduction.

5. **Cluster (daiin/chol/chor/cthol) = 22.9% of before-cth bigrams at 2.77×**: Together, these four words account for ~23% of all before-cth bigrams, with 2.77× collective enrichment. The remaining ~77% comes from diverse words including shaiin (8.31×, n=4), chcthy (4.36×, n=3), okaiin (3.88×, n=4) — all high enrichment, low n.
