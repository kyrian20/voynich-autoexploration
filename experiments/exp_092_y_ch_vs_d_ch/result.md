## H161: y+ch and d+ch Are NOT Interchangeable — Both Have 0% Para-Start Rate; Differ in Following-Word Distribution

| Opener type | n_lines | PS_rate | Enrichment vs background |

**BOTH y+ch and d+ch have ZERO para-start rate.** These are WITHIN-PARA LINE OPENERS, not paragraph-initial markers. This revises the earlier model: y+ch and d+ch mark the start of individual LINES within paragraphs, not paragraph boundaries.

### H161 Verdict: CONFIRMED WITH EXTENSION — y+ch and d+ch Are NOT Interchangeable: (1) Both Have 0% Para-Start Rate (Within-Para Line Openers Only); (2) Different Following-Word Environments: y+ch→o-onset (8.15× over d+ch), d+ch→qo-onset (6.25× over y+ch); (3) Share Same Stems, So Prefix Encodes Syntactic Context Not Lexical Content; (4) Can Co-Occur in Same Paragraph (Not Complementary)

## H163: Inter-Line Context — d+ch Follows d-Ending Lines (35.7%); y+ch Follows o-Ending Lines (23.0%); A Para-Starts Dominated by p/t/k-Onset

### Preceding Line's Last-Word Onset (A Dialect)

| Prev onset | y+ch % | d+ch % | bare ch % | Ratio y/d |
|------------|--------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **o** | **23.0%** | 12.9% | 10.2% | **1.79×** |
| other | 17.6% | 11.4% | 11.8% | 1.54× |
| **d** | 16.2% | **35.7%** | 22.8% | **0.45×** |
| ch | 10.8% | 17.1% | 22.8% | 0.63× |
| sh | 8.1% | 4.3% | 5.5% | 1.89× |
| qo | 6.8% | 2.9% | 10.2% | 2.36× |

**Key asymmetry:**
- When previous line ends with a **d-onset word** (d-closer!), the NEXT line's opener is d+ch (35.7%) at 2.76× the rate of y+ch (16.2%)
- When previous line ends with an **o-onset word**, y+ch opener is preferred (23.0% vs d+ch 12.9%, ratio 1.79×)
- **d-chain pattern**: [d-closer] → [d+ch opener] → [ch mid] → [d-closer] — the d+ch opener is TRIGGERED by d-closing on the preceding line!

## Combined Finding (F099)

**H161 CONFIRMED WITH EXTENSION**: y+ch and d+ch are NOT interchangeable. Critical revision to working model: BOTH have 0% para-start rate — they are WITHIN-PARA line openers only. They differ contextually: y+ch follows o-ending lines (23%), d+ch follows d-ending lines (35.7%). Following-word distributions also differ: y+ch→o-onset(24.3%), d+ch→qo-onset(16.4%). Both share the same ch-stems (10 shared stems); the prefix encodes syntactic position, not lexical content.

**H162 STRONGLY SUPPORTED**: The A 3-tier positional paradigm (bare < d+stem < y+stem) is UNIVERSAL — it applies identically to sh-stems: bare sh(13.2%) << d+sh(68.8%, 5.21×) ≤ y+sh(85.7%, 6.49×). Nearly identical ratios to ch-stems (6.17× / 8.86×). This confirms the prefix-encoded positional grammar is a general A-dialect feature, not ch-specific.

**H163 NEW FINDING**: A para-starts are NOT opened by y+ch or d+ch (0% PS rate confirmed). Para-starts in A are dominated by p-onset words (12.41× enrichment, 28.8% of PS lines!), k-onset (4.32×), and t-onset (2.72×). This is a NEW MAJOR signal — p-onset A words are PARAGRAPH STARTERS. The A grammar has THREE tiers: para-start markers (p/t/k-onset) > within-para line openers (y+ch, d+ch, y+sh, d+sh) > mid-line content (bare ch, bare sh).

**REVISED A GRAMMAR MODEL (F099)**:
- PARAGRAPH OPENER: p-onset words (12.41× enrichment) / t-onset (2.72×) / k-onset (4.32×)
- LINE OPENER (within-para): y+ch (85.9% LFirst) / d+ch (59.6%) / y+sh (85.7%) / d+sh (68.8%)
- MID-LINE: bare ch / bare sh (background rate ~13%)
- LINE CLOSER: d-onset words (dam/dy/dal/dar, LLast=33-63%)
- d-CHAIN: d-closer on prev line → triggers d+ch opener on next line
