# exp_339: daiin and d-Onset Positional Grammar — A vs B Dialect Divergence (H541)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H541 — A-dialect daiin is LL-enriched (explaining the aiin LL_enr=1.225× in F339), while B-dialect daiin is NOT LL-enriched.
**Result:** H541 CONFIRMED with INVERSION SURPRISE. B-dialect daiin is LF-enriched (33.0%, E=2.57×) and LL-depleted (9.5%, E=0.74×). A-dialect daiin is LF-neutral (18.3%, E=1.05×) and LL-enriched (23.9%, E=1.37×). The positional grammar of daiin is INVERTED between dialects. Additionally, A-dialect d-onset words broadly show LL enrichment: dar(1.67×), dal(2.30×), dain(1.38×), dam(3.68×). B-dialect d-onset shows LF enrichment for short-body forms (daiin, dain) but LL enrichment for terminal forms (dal=1.76×, dam=6.17×). The B-dialect daiin LF enrichment (2.57×) was not previously characterized at the word level.

## Findings

### Finding 1: B-dialect daiin is LF-enriched (E=2.57×); A-dialect daiin is LL-enriched (E=1.37×) — positional INVERSION

The positional function of daiin is fundamentally different between dialects. B-dialect: daiin appears most often at the START of lines (LF: 33%, E=2.57×). A-dialect: daiin appears most often at END of lines (LL: 24%, E=1.37×). This is a direct inversion of the primary positional grammar rule for the most frequent d-onset word type.

B-dialect daiin LF enrichment (2.57×) was not previously characterized at the word level. The prior finding (F294) noted d-onset is "LF-enriched" at the onset-class level — this is now confirmed as driven primarily by daiin (the dominant d-onset word). A-dialect daiin LF enrichment is near-baseline (1.05×) — the A-dialect d-onset class is less LF-concentrated overall.

### Finding 2: A-dialect d-onset words broadly show LL enrichment; B-dialect d-onset is LL-neutral or LF-enriched

In A-dialect, ALL measured d-onset words (except dol) show LL enrichment above baseline:
- dam: 3.68× LL; dal: 2.30× LL; dar: 1.67× LL; daiin: 1.37× LL; dain: 1.38× LL

In B-dialect, only the terminal forms (dal: 1.76×, dam: 6.17×) are LL-enriched. dam is the extreme LL form in both dialects (B: 6.17×, A: 3.68×) — the -am suffix is canonical LL in both. But the difference is: B-dialect LL-enrichment is specific to the -am suffix; A-dialect LL-enrichment extends to the entire d-onset word class.

### Finding 3: d-onset positional grammar diverges fundamentally between A and B

The d-onset positional structure in B-dialect:
- daiin/dain: LF function (formula-word at line start)  
- dal/dam: LL function (terminal markers)
- dar/dol: MID function (content words)

The d-onset positional structure in A-dialect:
- daiin/dain/dar/dal: broadly distributed, weakly LL-enriched (content words appearing throughout but especially at line end)
- dam: LL function (terminal, but weaker than B)
- dol: MID function

A-dialect d-onset has WEAKER positional grammar — words are less position-constrained. This is consistent with the general observation that A-dialect has larger paradigms (more word types) and more distributed usage.

### Finding 4: A-dialect daiin explaining the F339 aiin LL enrichment

F339 found A-dialect 'aiin' suffix LL_enr=1.225×. This experiment confirms: A-dialect daiin (N=443) has LL_enr=1.37×. Since daiin is the dominant 'aiin'-suffix word in A-dialect, daiin's LL enrichment directly explains F339's finding. B-dialect 'aiin' suffix LL_enr=0.634× is similarly explained by B-dialect daiin's LL depletion (0.74×).

## Interpretation

The d-onset positional inversion between dialects (B-dialect: daiin = LF word; A-dialect: daiin = weakly LL word) suggests that the grammatical role of daiin differs fundamentally between dialects:
- B-dialect: daiin is a "formula word" appearing at the start of lines (similar to a chapter/paragraph opener in natural language)
- A-dialect: daiin is a "content word" that tends to appear near the END of lines (more semantic, less positional)

This inversion is consistent with the broader A-B divergence pattern: B-dialect has tighter positional grammar (specific words in specific positions); A-dialect has looser positional grammar (same words distributed across positions, with weaker enrichment signals).

## Limitations

- Total counts include LF+MID+LL (not MID-only). B-dialect daiin N=264; A-dialect daiin N=443. Rates are reliable (N≥30 for significance). 
- The LF/MID/LL classification uses first/last word per line; short 2-word lines have no MID words.
- "Positional inversion" is relative: both dialects have daiin at all positions, just different concentrations.
