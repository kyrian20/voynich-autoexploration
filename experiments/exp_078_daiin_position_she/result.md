## H113: daiin Rate Rises From Para-First to Para-Last — daiin Encodes Paragraph Boundary

### daiin Rate by Para-Line Position

| Position | daiin count | Total tokens | Rate |
|---------|-----------|-------------|------|
| **Para-first line** | 24 | 985 | **2.44%** |
| Para-middle lines | 358 | 8,225 | 4.35% |
| **Para-last line** | 41 | 597 | **6.87%** |

**daiin rate rises 2.8× from para-first (2.44%) to para-last (6.87%).** This is consistent with daiin being a paragraph-boundary marker: suppressed at the start, enriched toward the end.

### daiin Rate by Token Position Within Line (All A)

| Token position | Rate | Enrichment vs corpus (4.3%) |
|--------------|------|--------------------------|
| First token | 4.09% | ~0.95× |
| Middle tokens | 3.95% | ~0.92× |
| **Last token** | **6.11%** | **~1.42×** |

**daiin is enriched specifically at the LAST token position within lines (6.11%)**, consistent with the line-final marker role found in F057.

**Combined effect**: daiin appears most often as the last word of the last line of a paragraph — the paragraph-boundary signal compounds the line-final signal.

### H113 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — daiin Encodes Paragraph Position

The daiin gradient (2.44% para-first → 6.87% para-last) directly shows that daiin encodes paragraph position. It is suppressed at paragraph openings and enriched at paragraph closings. Combined with token-position enrichment (last token: 6.11%), daiin is systematically more frequent in "closing" positions at both the line level and the paragraph level. **daiin is a hierarchical boundary marker** — marking both line-endings (1.42×) and paragraph-endings (2.08× — from F084).

## H114: she- Prefix Predicts Higher qo-Following Rate Across ALL Dialects and Sections

### she- vs sh-other Sequential Behavior

| Context | she-→qo | Enrichment | sh-other→qo | Enrichment |
|---------|---------|-----------|------------|-----------|
| **All A** | **26.2%** | **2.41×** | 12.9% | 1.18× |
| Herbal A | 15.4% | 1.52× | 12.1% | 1.19× |
| **Pharma A** | **38.5%** | **2.91×** | 20.4% | 1.54× |
| **Currier B** | **40.8%** | **2.19×** | 26.9% | 1.45× |

**she- forms precede qo at 2.19-2.91× enrichment in ALL contexts; sh-other forms precede qo at only 1.18-1.54×.** The she-/sh-other distinction is cross-dialect and cross-section.

**Crucially, even sh-other forms in Pharma A precede qo at 20.4% (1.54×)** — higher than both she- AND sh-other forms in Herbal A. This means Pharma A has a grammatical context that elevates qo-following for ALL sh-words, not just she- forms.

**In Currier B**: she-→qo=40.8% (2.19×) vs sh-other→qo=26.9% (1.45×) — the same structural distinction holds in B. she- forms preferentially precede qo in B as well.

### she- Word Examples

| Section | Top she- forms | she-→qo | sh-other examples | sh-other→qo |
|---------|--------------|---------|-----------------|------------|
| Herbal A | shey, sheey, sheol, sheor | 15.4% (1.52×) | shol, sho, shor, shy | 12.1% (1.19×) |
| Pharma A | shey, sheol, sheey, sheor | **38.5% (2.91×)** | shol, sho, shor | 20.4% (1.54×) |
| Currier B | (shedy=B dominant) | **40.8% (2.19×)** | sholdy, shoy, shal | 26.9% (1.45×) |

### H114 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — she- Prefix Is a Cross-Dialect qo-Predictor

she- prefix sh-words predict qo-following at 2.19-2.91× across A and B. The she-/sh-other distinction is a genuine morphological feature of sh-onset words that predicts sequential behavior. However, the distinction is not categorical — sh-other forms also precede qo at elevated rates (1.18-1.54×), and ALL sh-forms in Pharma A precede qo more than in Herbal A. **Two levels of qo-following:** (1) she- vs sh-other (consistent within context); (2) Pharma A > Herbal A for both classes (context effect on top of morphological effect).

## H115: oly/otam/okam Are Near-Pure Line-Final Words That Follow o-Onset Chains

### Positional Profile

| Word | n | Last% | Mid% | First% | Preceding onset |
|------|---|-------|------|--------|----------------|
| **oly** | 39 | **84.6%** | 12.8% | 2.6% | o(14), other(5), qo(5), ch(4) |
| **oldy** | 15 | 80.0% | 20.0% | 0% | o(4), ch(3), d(2), qo(2) |
| **olkam** | 10 | 80.0% | 20.0% | 0% | o(3), qo(3), other(2), ch(2) |
| **okam** | 20 | 75.0% | 25.0% | 0% | o(6), ch(6), other(4), qo(2) |
| **otam** | 35 | 65.7% | 34.3% | 0% | o(11), ch(7), qo(5), d(3) |

**oly/okam/oldy/olkam almost never appear at line-start (0-1/15-39 first tokens).** They are near-exclusively line-final or line-medial. **o-onset is the most common preceding word** (oly: o=14, otam: o=11, okam: o=6) — these words close sequences of o-onset words.

**o → o → oly/otam/okam** appears to be a common B line-closing pattern: an o-onset chain terminates with one of these specific o-onset line-closers.

Following onset: predominantly ch (oly: ch=3, okam: ch=4, olkam: ch=1) — when these words are not line-final, they are followed by ch-onset words.

### H115 Verdict: SUPPORTED — oly/otam/okam Are Near-Pure B Within-Para Line-Closers

These o-words are 65-85% line-final, essentially never line-initial, and are preceded by o-onset or ch-onset words. They form a specific sub-class of o-onset words with a line-closing function. The pattern o → o → oly (or otam/okam) creates a recognizable 3-word closing sequence in B within-para lines. This suggests these words function as grammatical line-boundary markers (like function words), not content words.

## H116: A Registers Differ in she-→qo Coupling, But Y-Dom Has Stronger she- Effect

### Register sh-Vocabulary Comparison

| Register | Total sh | she- % | she-→qo | sh-other→qo |
|---------|---------|--------|---------|-----------|
| Aiin-dominant | 136 | 25.0% | 9.4% (1.18×) | 8.5% (1.07×) |
| Y-dominant | 507 | 22.7% | **17.3% (1.59×)** | 13.1% (1.20×) |

**Paradox**: y-dominant Herbal A has STRONGER she-→qo coupling (1.59×) than aiin-dominant (1.18×) — despite both having similar she-/sh-other fractions (~23-25%). Both she- and sh-other forms precede qo MORE in y-dominant than in aiin-dominant Herbal A.

This is the OPPOSITE of what might be expected given that aiin-dom has higher overall bigram PPL reduction (12.7%) than y-dom (10.2%). The aiin-dom's stronger sequential grammar comes from OTHER pathways (qo→ch: 1.72×, s→ch: 1.76×), not from the sh→qo coupling.

**Two distinct sequential grammar strategies in Herbal A registers:**
- **Y-dominant A**: uses sh→qo as the primary sequential coupling (she-→qo 1.59×, sh-other→qo 1.20×)
- **Aiin-dominant A**: uses qo→ch (1.72×), s→ch (1.76×), and daiin→qo (2.38×) pathways; sh→qo is weak (1.07-1.18×)

### H116 Verdict: SUPPORTED (OPPOSITE DIRECTION) — Aiin-Dom Does NOT Have Stronger she- Effect; Registers Use Different Grammar Pathways

The aiin-dominant register's stronger sequential grammar (12.7% bigram PPL) is NOT driven by sh→qo coupling — it uses qo→ch and s→ch pathways instead. Y-dominant Herbal A actually has stronger she-→qo (1.59×) than aiin-dom (1.18×). The two A registers represent fundamentally different sequential grammar strategies: y-dom uses sh-qo coupling; aiin-dom uses qo-ch and daiin-qo couplings.
