The mixed-corpus bigrams (exp_119) obscured the underlying dialect structure. The pre-cth pattern is **NOT a unified phenomenon** but TWO separate patterns:

**A-dialect pre-cth words**: daiin (2.64×), chol (1.62×), shol (1.45×) — A-paradigm words (ch/sh-ol; d-aiin) appearing before cth ONLY in A-dialect text

**The same-onset pattern is NOT universal.** Only otaiin shows a strong same-onset preference. qokaiin actually AVOIDS qok-onset words after it (0.27×). 

### Individual Profiles

**otaiin → ot = 4.27×**: strongest same-onset pattern. After otaiin, the next word is ot-onset at 4.27× baseline. Words after otaiin: otal (5), otaiin (4), otedy (3) — all ot-onset. otaiin is a genuine ot-class determiner. Stars-concentrated (7.30‰ vs 2.93‰ herbal), B-dominant (4.69‰ B vs 2.69‰ A). Nearly entirely mid-line (88.5%).

**daiin → cth = 4.38× > daiin → d = 1.56×**: daiin preferentially precedes cth, not its own d-onset class. So daiin is NOT a d-class demonstrative — it's a cth-class determiner (at least in A-dialect).

**qokaiin → ch = 1.84×, qok = 0.27×**: qokaiin AVOIDS qok-onset words after it. It precedes ch-onset words slightly. qokaiin (qok + -aiin) breaks the pattern — it does not demonstrate its own class.

**shaiin → cth = 12.16×** (n=20, very small). shaiin appears to strongly precede cth. But n=20 makes this fragile.

### aiin Words Preceding Each Other: Chains

Words BEFORE otaiin: okaiin (6), aiin (5), otaiin (4), qokaiin (4), daiin (3) — predominantly -aiin words! This suggests **-aiin words cluster together in sequences**: okaiin → otaiin → [ot-noun] chains.

## Combined Finding (F127)

**H236 CONFIRMED REVISED (F127)**: The pre-cth bigram pattern from F126 is dialect-split. A-dialect uses daiin (2.64×), chol (1.62×), shol (1.45×) — A-paradigm suffixed words — to precede cth nouns. B-dialect shows chor (19.04×!), dain (8.00×) in the very rare cases where cth appears in B-text. cth is almost exclusively A-paradigm (39.29‰ A vs 2.57‰ B = 15× ratio). Pre-qok pattern: shedy→qok (3.75×) and qokeedy→qok (2.94×) are B-ONLY. chedy→qok=23.31× in A-dialect text (B-words in A-context follow qok-precursor rule). In A-dialect, daiin is the top precursor of BOTH cth (2.64×) AND qok (5.2% of cases) — daiin may be a general A-dialect determiner.

**H237 CONFIRMED PARTIAL (F127)**: The [onset-aiin] → [same-onset] hypothesis is confirmed for otaiin (4.27×) but fails for qokaiin (0.27×). Individual characterizations: otaiin = ot-class determiner (4.27×, stars-concentrated, B-dominant, mid-line); daiin = cth-class determiner (4.38×, A-dominant); okaiin = multi-class (ot=2.2×, cth=2.4×); aiin = ok-class (2.5×); qokaiin = ch/sh precursor (1.8×, NOT qok). -aiin words form sequential chains (okaiin→otaiin→[ot-noun]). The suffix -aiin marks a class of "cross-referential" words but their target onset class varies by stem, not mechanically by onset of the aiin-word.
