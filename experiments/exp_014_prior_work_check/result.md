## What Prior Work Covers

### Zandbergen (2024) — zandbergen_writing.md
The most directly relevant KB entry. Key quotes:
- Lists f49v among folios with "marginal notations" (alongside f17r, f66r)
- Open question raised explicitly: **"What do the 'f49v' numbered character sequences (1–5 in left margin) represent — a cipher key, a counting system?"**
- Notes "rare characters" concentrate on specific pages (consistent with @SPECIAL cluster finding)

This is the only KB source that acknowledges the f49v margin sequences as anomalous. It raises the question but does not analyze them.

### Brumbaugh (1977) — brumbaugh_mysterious_1977.md
Identified a "glyph-to-number cipher key" for the Voynich manuscript. Not related to f49v/f76r specifically; his key was applied to plant labels. Not independently verified.

### Davis multispectral (2024) — davis_multispectral_2024.md
Mentions "substitution cipher key" in context of Marci letters analysis. Not related to the line marker sequences.

### Greshko / Naibbe (2025) — greshko_naibbe_2025.md
Homophonic substitution cipher using a 78-card tarot deck as key, reproducing Voynich statistics. Relevant context: a card-deck key (physical artifact used as cipher key) is conceptually similar to a "key-like sequence" encoded in a manuscript folio. If f49v/f76r/f57v encode cipher key material, a tarot-style structured key (ordered card deck) would be a natural analogy.

## What Prior Work Does NOT Cover
The following findings from this investigation have NO corresponding coverage in the KB:

1. **f76r key-like designation** — ZL3b transcribers explicitly wrote "has 'key-like' sequence, here transcribed as L0 labels" in the f76r header. No KB summary discusses this.

2. **f57v ↔ f66r @SPECIAL glyph connection** — f57v circular text (Cc locus) repeats a sequence containing @169; and @172; — the same special glyphs that appear in f66r column 2. ZL3b transcribers cross-referenced them with "cf f57v?" comment on f66r L38. No KB entry discusses this connection.

3. **f57v "key-like" designation** — ZL3b header for f57v reads "has 'key-like' sequences." The KB folio entry correctly identifies f57v as astronomical/cosmological but does not mention key-like designation or the repeating circular sequence with @SPECIAL glyphs.

4. **Structural analysis of line marker sequences** — The marker sequences on f49v and f76r have not been analyzed for periodicity, character frequency bias (gallows overrepresentation), or body text correspondence. Zandbergen raises the question; no analysis in KB.

5. **f66r column 2 alphabet coverage** — The question of whether f66r column 2 is an alphabet inventory has not been systematically computed in any KB source. Exp_012 is the first such coverage calculation.

6. **Three-way "key cluster"** — The connection between f49v (Scribe 1, Currier A, line markers with @SPECIAL), f57v (Scribe 1, Currier A, circular "key-like" text with @SPECIAL), and f66r (Scribe 5, Currier B, two-column list with @SPECIAL) has not been identified in any KB source.

## Critical Observation: The @SPECIAL Cluster
Three folios share non-standard EVA glyphs (@NNN; notation):
- **f57v** (Scribe 1, astronomical): @169, @170, @171, @172 in repeating circular sequence
- **f66r** (Scribe 5, pharmaceutical): @169, @172, @195 in column 2 list
- **f49v** (Scribe 1, botanical): 6 @SPECIAL glyphs among 26 line markers

The @169 and @172 glyphs appear on BOTH f57v AND f66r — across two different scribes (Hand 1 and Hand 5). Either:
(a) These are the same manuscript glyphs transcribed identically because they look alike
(b) These glyphs had a conventional meaning shared between scribes

ZL3b cross-references f66r ↔ f57v explicitly. The circular text on f57v (Cc locus, "key-like") repeats the @NNN sequence 3–4 times.

## Summary: Prior Work Gap
Zandbergen (2024) acknowledges f49v margin sequences as anomalous. No published analysis (in KB) addresses:
- The structural relationship between f49v, f57v, and f66r via shared @SPECIAL glyphs
- Whether the sequences encode cipher key material
- f76r's key-like designation at all

These are genuinely open research questions with no KB coverage.
