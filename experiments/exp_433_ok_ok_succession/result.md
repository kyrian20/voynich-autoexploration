# exp_433: ok→ok Self-Succession — Class-Level vs Word-Level Mechanism

**Hypothesis (H570):** Is ok→ok self-succession (B: 1.32-1.36×, A: 1.86-1.93×) driven by specific word types, or is it class-level memory?

**Method:** B and A continuation lines. Collect all ok→ok adjacent pairs. Classify as same-word (w1=w2) or different-word. Measure type diversity (unique w1/w2 types). Check if any word drives dominant self-succession.

---

## Interpretation

**H570 ANSWERED: CLASS-LEVEL MEMORY, with mild word-level clustering.**

ok→ok self-succession is primarily class-level: 84-87% of pairs involve different ok-onset words; 26-30 unique word types appear in w1 positions (no concentrated vocabulary). No word pair occurs more than 5 times (okaiin→okaiin, N=5 in B).

The same-word rate (13-16%) is elevated above chance (~0.5% per word if random), confirming mild word-level clustering on top of the class-level effect. Specifically: okaiin (the most frequent B ok word at 156 occurrences) has a 38% self-succession rate when it appears as a leading word in an ok→ok pair. But the numerator (N=5) is too small to treat as a reliable estimate.

**Consistent with F363**: ok→ok is class-level memory (F363 found 84-86% different words). This experiment confirms that finding with extended vocabulary analysis.

A-dialect ok→ok enrichment (1.86×) is stronger than B (1.36×), consistent with A-dialect having stronger onset-class clustering generally.

---

## Limitations

- B same-word rate denominator N=14 is small; okaiin 38% rate based on N=5.
- Mild word-level clustering could be an artifact of specific folio/section concentrations (okaiin is concentrated in certain bio-section folios).
