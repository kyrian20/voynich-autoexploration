## Findings

### Finding 1: A-Dialect {p,f}→sh vs {k,t}→ch Split Confirmed at MID_inner

In A-dialect MID_inner positions, {p,f} are sh-biased (aggregate ch/sh=0.50; p→sh=30.8%, k→sh=10.9%). {k,t} are ch-biased (aggregate ch/sh=2.35; k→ch=26.1%, t→ch=26.2%). The split is large: p has ch/sh=0.50 while k has ch/sh=2.38 — a 4.8× difference in the ch/sh ratio within the "gallows class." This confirms that the internal gallows split is a persistent structural property of A-dialect across line positions.

### Finding 2: B-Dialect Split COLLAPSES at MID_inner — p/f Become ch-Biased

In B-dialect MID_inner, p and f are BOTH ch-biased: p→sh=3.9% (ch/sh=4.00, N=51), f→sh=0% (N=15). k (ch/sh=2.78) and t (ch/sh=1.29) are also ch-biased. All gallows onsets are ch-biased at B MID_inner — no sh-biased subgroup. The B-dialect all-positions p→sh-bias (F360: B p overall ch/sh=0.49) was entirely driven by the LF→pos1 effect. At MID positions, p-onset in B is strongly ch-biased. This means the {p,f}→sh label is POSITION-SPECIFIC in B (LF→pos1 only) but POSITION-GENERAL in A (persists at MID).

### Finding 3: A-Dialect LF→pos1 Reversal — k Is sh-Biased at LF→pos1 (k ch/sh=0.44)

At A-dialect LF→pos1, k→sh=38.1% (highest of any gallows), giving k ch/sh=0.44 (most sh-biased). This is the opposite of k's MID behavior (ch/sh=2.38). The LF→pos1 gallows→sh effect (E=1.72×) is so strong that it overrides the k→ch tendency, making k appear sh-biased in LF context. This is why F359 reported k→sh=38.1% as the strongest gallows→sh rule — it's specifically the LF-onset effect, not the all-positions property of k.

### Finding 4: A-Dialect pre-LL→LL — k Is Strongly ch-Biased (k→ch=20.0%, ch/sh=10.00)

At A-dialect pre-LL→LL, k→ch=20.0% (N=50) while k→sh=2.0% (ch/sh=10.00). This is the most extreme ch-dominance of any gallows onset at any position in A-dialect. Pre-LL k-onset words are followed by ch-onset LL words — suggesting a k→ch word-level rule at line end.

### Finding 5: Structural Interpretation — A-Dialect Has Two Gallows Sub-Classes; B-Dialect Does Not

A-dialect data support two genuine gallows sub-classes:
- **p (and f)**: sh-biased successor (MID ch/sh=0.50; LF→pos1 ch/sh=0.67) — sh-inducing gallows
- **k (and t)**: ch-biased successor (MID ch/sh=2.35; pre-LL ch/sh=6.50) — ch-inducing gallows

B-dialect data: both {p,f} and {k,t} are ch-biased at MID_inner. No sh-biased gallows sub-class exists in B MID. The {p,f}→sh signal in B (all-positions F360) is entirely a LF→pos1 positional effect, not a character-class property.

## Summary

H568 CONFIRMED WITH DIALECT SPLIT. A-dialect: {p,f}→sh vs {k,t}→ch split persists at MID_inner (pf ch/sh=0.50, kt ch/sh=2.35). B-dialect: split collapses at MID_inner (all gallows ch-biased). The A-dialect gallows class should be modeled as two sub-classes in A (p/f as sh-type, k/t as ch-type). B-dialect: the LF→pos1 p/f→sh signal is a line-opening positional rule, not a character sub-class property.
