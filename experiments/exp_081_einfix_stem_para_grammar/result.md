## H123: e-Infix Is a VOCABULARY PARTITION, Not a Productive Morphological Operation — The e-Suffix Co-Occurrence Is Driven by Minimal Words

### Stem Overlap Between e-Infix and Non-e Forms

| Onset | Dialect | Shared stems | Only e-stems | Only non-e stems | Shared % |
|-------|---------|-------------|-------------|-----------------|----------|
| sh | B | 15/82 | 35 | 32 | 18% |
| ch | B | 30/103 | 30 | 43 | 29% |
| k | B | 3/41 | 13 | 25 | 7% |
| o | B | 5/228 | 5 | 213 | 2% |
| sh | A | 23/98 | 17 | 58 | 23% |
| ch | A | 35/160 | 30 | 95 | 22% |

**70-98% of stems are EXCLUSIVE to either e-infix or non-e contexts** — the vast majority of stems appear only with e-infix (she+stem) or only without (sh+stem), not both. This is a vocabulary partition, not a productive morphological alternation.

### Critical Finding: When Same Stem Appears in Both Contexts, Suffixes Are NOT Complementary

In shared stems, the e-suffix rate (rate of -edy/-eedy/-ey/-eey suffixes) does NOT differ significantly:

| Onset | Dialect | e-context e-suffix% | non-e-context e-suffix% |
|-------|---------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| sh | B | 16.0% | 14.6% |
| ch | B | 17.8% | 21.3% |
| sh | A | 8.6% | 11.2% |
| ch | A | 10.4% | 12.5% |

**In shared stems, the e-infix has NO predictive power for e-suffix selection.** The same stem (e.g., 'ckh' appearing as she+ckh+y AND sh+ckh+y) takes similar suffix distributions in both contexts.

### Overall e-Suffix Rate by e-Infix Status (Currier B)

| Onset | e-infix e-suffix% | non-e e-suffix% |
|-------|------------------|----------------|
| sh | 17.8% (219 tok) | 16.3% (172 tok) |
| ch | 17.2% (361 tok) | 22.3% (449 tok) |
| k | 18.2% (33 tok) | 34.7% (72 tok) |
| o | 18.2% (11 tok) | 48.6% (2162 tok) |

**The e-infix does NOT predict higher e-suffix rates at the token level.** Non-e forms frequently have HIGHER e-suffix rates (especially o-onset and k-onset). This contradicts a simple "e-infix → e-suffix" rule.

### Resolution of the H120/H123 Contradiction

The H120 finding (B she-→-edy 29%) is DRIVEN BY MINIMAL WORDS: **shedy, chedy, keedy** — words with EMPTY stems between onset+e and suffix. These words are the form onset+e+suffix directly, with no intervening stem. The stem analysis (H123) excluded these (stem_core='') because they have no stem content. These minimal words (she+edy, che+edy) create the appearance of e-infix → e-suffix co-occurrence, but they are the minimal e-infix form itself: the e IS the "stem" in these cases.

**Conclusion: The e-infix syllable template is NOT {onset}+e+{stem}+{e-suffix}. Instead:**
- Some e-infix words are minimal: {onset}+e+{e-suffix} (shedy = sh+e+dy or she+dy is ambiguous)
- Most e-infix words are vocabulary items with unique stems that don't overlap with non-e vocabularies
- The e-infix does not CHANGE the suffix selection when added to a stem

### H123 Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED AS STATED — e-Infix Is a Vocabulary Partition, Not a Productive Suffix-Conditioning Morpheme

The e-infix defines TWO LARGELY DISJOINT STEM VOCABULARIES: e-onset-words and non-e-onset-words (70-98% non-overlapping stems). When stems appear in both contexts (18-29% of sh/ch stems), the suffix distribution doesn't change. The H120 finding that she-forms preferentially take -edy reflects the MINIMAL WORD class (shedy, chedy) where e-infix IS the entire word between onset and suffix, not a general productive rule. The e-infix marks a distinct lexical class, not a morphological operation on shared stems.

## H124: B Para-Final Words Use -edy/-eedy More Than Within-Para Line-Finals; -am Is Roughly Equal

### Suffix Distribution at B Para-Final vs Within-Para Line-Final

| Suffix | Para-final (n=68) | Within-para final (n=1586) | All B words |
|--------|------------------|--------------------------|------------|
| **-edy** | **16.2%** | 12.0% | 18.9% |
| **-eedy** | **7.4%** | 4.7% | 8.1% |
| -y | 8.8% | 17.6% | 9.0% |
| -am | 8.8% | **11.3%** | 1.7% |
| -aiin | 11.8% | 5.4% | 8.1% |
| -dy | 5.9% | 7.4% | 5.8% |

**Key contrast**: -am is higher at WITHIN-para line-finals (11.3%) vs para-finals (8.8%), confirming the H121 finding. Conversely, **-edy and -eedy are ENRICHED at para-finals** (16.2% + 7.4% = 23.6%) vs within-para (12.0% + 4.7% = 16.7%) vs all words (18.9% + 8.1% = 27.0%).

### Top B Para-Final Words and Onset+Suffix Combinations

Top para-final last words: chedy (5), okeedy (2), qokeedy (2), otaiin (2), qokaiin (2), or (2)

Top para-final onset+suffix: ch+edy (5), o+eedy (3), o+aiin (3), o+am (2), qo+eedy (2), qo+aiin (2)

**B para-final morphological profile**: ch-onset with -edy suffix AND o/qo onset with -eedy/-aiin suffixes. The para-final uses BOTH the dominant B suffix (-edy) and the aiin-suffix. The -am contrast (8.8% para-final vs 11.3% within-para line-final) confirms -am marks within-para line-boundaries specifically.

### -am Counts Clarified

- B para-final last words with -am ending: 7/68 = 10.3%
- B within-para line-final last words with -am ending: 198/1586 = 12.5%

The para-final -am suppression is 17% relative (12.5% → 10.3%), confirming the exp_080 finding directionally but the magnitude is smaller when measured at the word level vs line level.

### H124 Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — B Para-Final Uses -edy/-eedy More; -am Suppression Confirmed But Modest at Word Level

B para-final last words use -edy/-eedy at higher rates than within-para line-finals (23.6% vs 16.7%), while -am is modestly suppressed (10.3% vs 12.5%). The dominant B para-final word is **chedy** (ch-onset, -edy suffix), not an o-onset word — the o-onset para-closing effect is at the class level, not concentrated in a single word. Within-para line-finals favor -y (17.6%) and -am (11.3%) most. The para-final and within-para line-final have different suffix fingerprints: para-final = {-edy, -aiin, -eedy}; line-final = {-y, -am, -edy, -dy}.

## H125: Aiin-Dom vs Y-Dom Grammar-Morphology Dissociation Confirmed — OPPOSITE she-→qo Coupling; DIFFERENT Suffix Paradigm

### Key Transition Enrichments

| Transition | Aiin-dom | Y-dom | Ratio A/Y |
|-----------|---------|-------|-----------|
| **sh→qo** | 1.38× | **1.75×** | 0.79 |
| sh→ch | 0.68× | 0.74× | ~equal |
| ch→ch | 1.14× | 0.98× | slightly A |
| **d→qo** | **1.90×** | 0.74× | 2.57 |
| s→ch | **1.48×** | 1.33× | slightly A |
| o→qo | 1.07× | 0.88× | slightly A |

**Y-dom has STRONGER sh→qo (1.75×) than aiin-dom (1.38×)**. Aiin-dom's compensating strength is d→qo (1.90× vs 0.74× — 2.57× stronger in aiin-dom). The two registers use OPPOSITE sequential grammar pathways: **Y-dom = sh→qo coupling; Aiin-dom = d→qo coupling**.

### she-→qo: SUPPRESSED in Aiin-Dom, Strongly Enhanced in Y-Dom

| Register | she-→qo rate | enrichment | sh-other→qo | enrichment |
|---------|-------------|-----------|------------|-----------|
| Aiin-dom | 2.9% (n=34) | **0.41×** | 9.5% (n=126) | 1.31× |
| Y-dom | 30.1% (n=299) | **2.23×** | 18.7% (n=358) | 1.39× |

**In aiin-dom, she-prefix words SUPPRESS qo-following (0.41×) — the morphological e-infix is ANTI-correlated with the qo-following behavior.** In y-dom, she-prefix words strongly predict qo (2.23×). The e-infix morphological marker operates OPPOSITELY in the two A registers — it encodes different sequential behavior depending on register.

### Suffix Paradigm Comparison

| Suffix | Aiin-dom | Y-dom |
|--------|---------|-------|
| **-ol** | **13.59%** | 6.45% |
| **-or** | **12.17%** | 5.88% |
| **-aiin** | 12.38% | 12.79% |
| **-y** | **21.42%** | 14.71% |
| -ey | 4.70% | 6.65% |
| -eey | 2.21% | **5.99%** |
| -dy | 4.06% | **7.07%** |
| -am | 1.71% | 2.10% |

**The two registers have DIFFERENT suffix paradigms**: Aiin-dom is dominated by -y (21.4%), -aiin (12.4%), **-ol (13.6%), -or (12.2%)**. Y-dom uses more -dy (7.1%), -ey (6.7%), -eey (6.0%). Aiin-dom heavily favors -ol/-or suffixes (25.8% combined vs y-dom's 12.3%).

**Bigram PPL reduction**: Aiin-dom=5.8%, Y-dom=4.0%. Aiin-dom has STRONGER sequential grammar despite suppressing the she-→qo effect.

**e-infix word rate**: Aiin-dom=7.54%, Y-dom=12.59%. Y-dom has MORE e-infix words, consistent with its she-→qo coupling pattern.

### H125 Verdict: STRONGLY SUPPORTED — FULL DISSOCIATION in Aiin-Dom: Stronger Sequential Grammar but Weaker e-Infix Usage and OPPOSITE she-→qo Coupling

Aiin-dominant Herbal A has: (1) STRONGER bigram PPL reduction (5.8% vs 4.0%); (2) STRONGER d→qo coupling (1.90× vs 0.74×); (3) FEWER e-infix words (7.54% vs 12.59%); (4) SUPPRESSED she-→qo (0.41× — below base rate!); (5) DIFFERENT suffix paradigm (heavy -ol/-or). Y-dom has: stronger sh→qo (1.75× vs 1.38×), stronger she-→qo (2.23×), more e-infix words. **The e-infix morphological marker and the qo-coupling sequential grammar are FULLY DISSOCIATED in aiin-dom**: aiin-dom has stronger grammar but the grammar uses d→qo NOT sh/she→qo. The grammar-morphology dissociation found in Pharma A (F087) is also present within Herbal A, confirming it is a general property of Voynichese, not a Pharma A anomaly.
