## H252: CONFIRMED — aiin→am=4.62× Is NOT a Fixed [daiin][am] 2-Word Phrase; It Reflects -am as Universal Line-Closer Attracted to Determiner-Containing Lines; [daiin][dam] Appears in Herbal but Only n=6; Stars=36% of aiin→am Bigrams

### Top aiin→am Word Pairs

Total [aiin-word][am-word] bigrams: 67 across 57 unique pairs.

| Pair | n |
|------|---|
| daiin → dam | 6 |
| daiin → otam | 4 |
| taiin → dam | 2 |
| qokaiin → otam | 2 |
| (all others) | 1 each |

The bigram is NOT dominated by a single fixed phrase — 57 unique pairs for 67 occurrences means there is no repetitive [daiin][am] formula. The aiin→am=4.62× from exp_126 reflects a DISTRIBUTIONAL pattern: -aiin words appear mid-line and -am words cluster at line-end, so their proximity enrichment is a consequence of both being present in the same line, not a fixed phrase.

### daiin Follow-Up Distribution

From 632 post-daiin words:
- Top suffix: -y (16.9%), other (15.3%), -ol (10.6%), -aiin (9.2%), -dy (7.0%)
- Top words: daiin(11), chey(11), dal(10), shey(10), **cthy(9)**, dain(9), chedy(9), okaiin(8)

cthy=9 (rank 5) is consistent with F125's daiin→cth=4.89× enrichment — cth-onset words hide in the -y suffix category (cthy has -y suffix). The aiin→am=19 occurrences from daiin (3.0% of post-daiin words) = modest, not a dominant pattern.

### Section Distribution of aiin→am

Stars = 35.8% of aiin→am bigrams, herbal = 29.9%. This is surprising — stars-heavy despite the NP template being herbal-associated. Suggests that stars lines also frequently end with -am closers (consistent with F128's "stars line-end = am-particles" finding).

### H252 Verdict: CONFIRMED — aiin→am=4.62× is NOT a fixed 2-word phrase; it reflects -am as a universal line-closer that is attracted to lines containing determiner (-aiin) words; the 67 [aiin][am] bigrams span 57 unique pairs; [daiin][dam] appears only n=6 (herbal folios); stars contribute 36% of aiin→am bigrams; the aiin→am enrichment is a statistical artifact of the within-line suffix grammar (F133) where -aiin appears mid-line and -am appears last

Top 3-gram instances:

- chor daiin cthoinn (n=1)

### 4-gram Template

4-gram matches [-ol/-or][aiin-word][cth-onset][-am/-dy/-al]: **3** occurrences in 6,049 A-dialect 4-grams.

| 4-gram | Folio | Context |
|--------|-------|---------|

**Enrichment = 4.92×** (observed 3 vs expected 0.6 by independence; P(-ol/-or)=0.229, P(-aiin)=0.111, P(cth-onset)=0.036, P(-am/-dy/-al)=0.110).

### Interpretation

The 4-gram template IS significantly enriched (4.92×) but extremely rare (only 3 occurrences). The 3-gram without the closer has 23 matches — meaning in 20/23 cases, the cth-noun is NOT immediately followed by a -am/-dy/-al closer. The line may end with the cth-noun itself, or there may be an additional word between the cth-noun and the closer.

The 3 matching 4-grams are ALL in A-dialect herbal section, consistent with the NP template being an A-herbal phenomenon.

### H253 Verdict: CONFIRMED (WEAK) — The 4-gram template [-ol/-or][aiin][cth][-am/-dy/-al] is enriched 4.92× vs independence baseline; observed 3 vs expected 0.6 in 6,049 A-dialect 4-grams; all 3 instances are in A-dialect herbal; the 3-gram (23 matches) is more common because the cth-noun often ends the line or precedes unlisted closing elements; the template is real but lines have variable length and the closer may be separated from the noun phrase; the canonical A-dialect clause is approximately [chol/chor][daiin][cthy] with optional [-dy/-al/-am] closer

### TWO y-Prefix Sub-Types Identified

**A-y sub-type** (B% ≤ 25%):
- Words: ykchy (12%), ychor (12%), ytchy (20%), ydaiin (25%), ytor (21%), yky (23%)
- Pattern: y + k/ch/t-stem + A-paradigm suffix (-chy/-or/-ol/-aiin)
- Section: predominantly herbal (60-88%)
- Function: A-dialect line-continuation particle (matches F057's y-initial=continuation particle)

**B-y sub-type** (B% ≥ 80%):
- Words: yteedy (100%), ykeedy (100%), ytedy (100%), ykedy (100%), ychedy (100%), yshedy (likely ~100%)
- Pattern: y + k/t/ch-stem + B-paradigm suffix (-edy/-eedy/-dy)
- Section: herbal + stars balanced
- Function: B-dialect line formula opener (matches F130's y-prefix=B-stars formula opener)

**A/B vocabulary split**:
- A-exclusive types (top): ycheor, ykeor, ykchor, ysho, ycho — all A-paradigm suffixes (-or/-o)
- B-exclusive types (top): ykeedy, yteedy, ytedy, ykedy, ychedy — all B-paradigm -edy suffix

A/B Jaccard = **0.174** (nearly as low as A vs B overall Jaccard of 0.148). y-prefix has almost separate A and B vocabularies despite being the same morpheme.

**k/t stems = 65.4% of y-stripped stems**. This is the SAME selectivity profile as qo-prefix (qo+k=62.6%, qo+t=19.9% from F075). y-prefix and qo-prefix both preferentially attach to k/t-stems!

**Critical finding**: y-prefix selects k/t-stems (65.4%), the same stem class as qo-/o-. But y- is dialect-balanced (A/B=0.81) while qo- is B-exclusive (88% B). This implies y- may be a DIALECT-NEUTRAL version of the k/t-stem prefix — usable in both A and B dialect contexts, contrasting with qo- (B-exclusive) and o- (label-register).

This extends the prefix paradigm on k/t-stems:
- **bare** k/t: A-herbal (kaiin=23% A; taiin=34% A)
- **o-** prefix: body text labels
- **qo-** prefix: B-dialect body text
- **l-** prefix: B-stars/cosmo
- **y-** prefix: BOTH dialects, predominantly line-initial (continuation opener function)

y- may be a GRAMMATICAL prefix (marking a specific syntactic position = line-start/continuation) rather than a lexical class prefix.

### H251 Verdict: CONFIRMED — y-prefix splits into A-y (y+A-paradigm: ycheor/ydaiin/ytchy, B%≤25%, herbal-concentrated) and B-y (y+B-paradigm: yteedy/ykeedy/ykedy, B%=100%, herbal+stars); BOTH sub-types select k/t-stems (65.4% = same selectivity as qo-/o-prefix); A/B Jaccard=0.174 (distinct vocabularies despite shared prefix); line-start=39.6% (y-words are line-continuation openers); y-prefix is DIALECT-NEUTRAL version of k/t-stem prefixing: y-[both dialects, line-start] vs qo-[B-body] vs o-[labels] vs l-[B-stars] — y marks a GRAMMATICAL position (continuation opener) rather than a register/dialect; B-exclusive y-words have -edy suffix; A-exclusive y-words have -or/-ol/-chy suffix; these are structurally the y-prefix applied to the existing A and B paradigm forms

## Combined Finding (F134)

**H252 CONFIRMED (F134)**: aiin→am=4.62× is a distributional artifact of -am as universal line-closer and -aiin words appearing mid-line; NOT a fixed 2-word phrase. [daiin][dam] appears only 6 times in herbal. The 67 aiin→am bigrams span 57 unique pairs. Stars contribute 36% of instances (consistent with stars -am line-endings). The enrichment reflects the within-line suffix grammar (F133) rather than a lexicalized phrase.

**H253 CONFIRMED WEAK (F134)**: 4-gram template [-ol/-or][aiin][cth][-am/-dy/-al] enriched 4.92× vs independence baseline (3 observed vs 0.6 expected). All 3 instances are in A-herbal. The 3-gram (23 matches) is the more common form because the closer is often non-adjacent or absent. The canonical A-clause is [chol/chor][daiin][cthy] ± closer, not a rigid 4-element sequence.

**H251 CONFIRMED (F134)**: y-prefix has two distinct sub-types: A-y (y+A-paradigm stems, B%≤25%, herbal, continuation particle) and B-y (y+B-paradigm stems, B%=100%, herbal+stars, formula opener). Both select k/t-stems (65.4%) — same selectivity as qo-/o-. A/B Jaccard=0.174 (distinct vocabularies). y-prefix is dialect-neutral but grammatically specialized: marks line-continuation/start position. Extended k/t-stem prefix taxonomy (bare/y/o/qo/l) now covers: bare=A-herbal, y=both+line-initial, o=labels, qo=B-body, l=B-stars/cosmo. y- is a grammatical (positional) prefix, the others are register/dialect prefixes.
