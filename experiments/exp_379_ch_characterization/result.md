## Findings

### Finding 1: ch Is LF-DEPLETED, pos1-ENRICHED in Both Dialects (A: LF=0.39×, pos1=1.43×; B: LF=0.25×, pos1=1.23×) — ch Is a Second-Position Class

A ch: LF=0.39×, pos1=1.43×; B ch: LF=0.25×, pos1=1.23×. Both dialects: ch is the most strongly LF-depleted major onset class characterized (B=0.25× is the strongest LF depletion seen, stronger than r=0.30× and ot=0.33×). ch is enriched at pos1 — it follows LF-onset words. This is structurally consistent with all known B LF formulas pointing to ch or sh at pos1: d-LF → sh-pos1 (F373), s-LF → sh-pos1 (F376), ol-LF → ch-pos1 (F381), qot/ok-LF → ch-pos1 (F377). ch occupies the second slot in the B-dialect LF formula grammar. Architecture: ch-onset words are "responders" to LF-formula onset words, not initiators. The three-register grammar (F351-F355) has LF = gallows/d/s/ol, and the second word (pos1) is dominantly ch/sh.

### Finding 2: B ch Self-Succession Is Near-Baseline at ALL Positions (E=0.94× Flat) — B ch↔sh Avoidance Eliminates ch Clustering in B

B ch→ch: LF→pos1=0.92×, MID=0.94×, pre_LL=0.94×. Every position category is near-baseline — B ch has essentially no self-succession tendency. This confirms and extends F328 (B ch↔sh bidirectional avoidance): the avoidance mechanism not only suppresses cross-class coupling (ch↔sh) but also suppresses B ch's own intra-class succession. B ch is the only major onset class with self-succession completely flat across all positions. Contrast: A ch self-succession LF→pos1=1.61×/MID=1.29× (A has ch clustering). The B ch↔sh avoidance (F328) explains this: in B, ch at one position predicts BOTH lower sh AND lower ch at the next position — the avoidance is directional and self-applies.

### Finding 3: A ch Self-Succession LF→pos1=1.61×, MID=1.29× — A ch Chains at Line-Opening and Mid-Line; Depleted at pre_LL (0.86×)

A ch→ch: LF→pos1=1.61×(N=39/110), MID=1.29×(N=293/1036), pre_LL→LL=0.86×(N=52/276). Same-word rate=7.8%. Top A pairs: chol→chol(21=most frequent same-class pair), chor→chol(8). A-dialect ch chains at pos1/MID: when ch appears in LF or MID positions, it tends to produce another ch-class word. At pre_LL, ch chains below baseline. This is the opposite of ot (which peaks at pre_LL) — ch is a mid-line self-chaining class in A, ot is a line-ending self-chaining class.

### Finding 4: A ch→d at pre_LL E=1.95× and A ch→r at pre_LL E=6.39× — A-Dialect ch Pre_LL Has Two Routing Targets: d-Class (Primary) and r (Secondary)

A ch→d at pre_LL→LL: E=1.95× (N=79/276). Top pair: chol→daiin(25). This is the primary mechanism for A-dialect LL d-class dominance after ch words: when ch appears at pre_LL, 28.6% of the time a d-class word (primarily daiin) follows at LL. A ch→r at pre_LL→LL: E=6.39× (N=5/276, directional). Five confirmed A ch-pre_LL → r-LL transitions: cha→rchealcham(1), chotain→ry(1), cho→rol(1), chor→ry(1) (from section 4) — all ch-body words at pre_LL preceding r-onset at LL. CROSS-VALIDATED: exp_378 Section 7 found A r-at-LL has ch=2.18× as predecessor (5/10 events). Architecture: A-dialect ch at pre_LL primarily routes to d-class LL (E=1.95×, large N), secondarily routes to r LL (E=6.39×, small N but consistent with cross-validation from r-side).

### Finding 5: B ch→d at pre_LL E=2.01× — B-Dialect ch Also Routes to d at LL (Not Just via daiin Formula)

B ch→d at pre_LL→LL: E=2.01× (N=39/360). Top pairs: chedy→daiin(10), chey→daiin(4), chckhy→daiin(3). This extends F371 (B ch→daiin pre_LL=1.68×) to the full d-class: ch at pre_LL in B routes to d-class LL at 2.01×. B ch→qok MID=1.21× (N=328/2016): ch→qok coupling is MID-concentrated in B (depleted at LF=0.84× and pre_LL=0.64×). B ch→r at pre_LL=1.76× (N=10/360): mild routing to r at LL after B ch (compare: B qot→r=5.18×, qok→r=3.01× — ch is a weaker B pre_LL→r predictor than qot/qok).

### Finding 6: B ch→sh Overall=0.60× (Depletion) — B ch↔sh Avoidance Fully Characterized

B ch→sh: overall=0.60×; LF→pos1=1.30× (mild elevation — but the LF effect is small), MID_inner=0.60×, pre_LL→LL=0.43×. The B ch↔sh avoidance (F328: ch→sh=0.567×) is now position-resolved: the MID avoidance (0.60×) and pre_LL avoidance (0.43×) are the primary locations. B ch→sh is ELEVATED at LF→pos1 (1.30×) — when ch occupies LF (rare: E=0.25× but it happens), it sometimes precedes sh at pos1. The position-specific decomposition is: B ch→sh is avoided everywhere EXCEPT LF→pos1, where it is mildly elevated. Architecture: the B ch↔sh avoidance is a MID and pre_LL phenomenon, not a universal rule.

### Finding 7: A ch Vocabulary = ol/or-Body Dominant (chol=13.1%, chor=8.0%); B ch = edy-Body Dominant (chedy=14.9%, chey=7.1%)

A ch top body forms: ol(13.1%), or(8.0%), ey(4.3%), y(4.1%), eol(3.1%), eor(2.6%). Total A=1668 tokens, 417 types, 66% hapax. B ch top body forms: edy(14.9%), ey(7.1%), eey(3.8%), ckhy(3.7%), dy(3.5%). Total B=2791 tokens, 551 types, 66% hapax. Cross-dialect vocabulary divergence confirms F347 (A ch ol/or-dominant vs B ch edy-dominant). B chedy is the single most frequent ch word (416/2791=14.9%); A chol is the single most frequent A ch word (219/1668=13.1%). Architecture: the ch body vocabulary is a primary A/B diagnostic — ol-body → A-dialect; edy-body → B-dialect. ch is the onset class with the clearest cross-dialect body vocabulary split.

### Finding 8: B ch pre_LL → ol at LL E=0.63× (Depleted) — ch at pre_LL Does NOT Route to ol Terminal; A ch pre_LL → ol E=1.00×

B ch at pre_LL: ol at LL = 5.3% (19/360), baseline=8.4%, E=0.63×. B ch-pre_LL avoids ol-LL. Compare: B s/qok/ok/ot at pre_LL → ol LL (E=1.12-2.98×, F376/F379/F380/F381). ch is the major pre_LL class that does NOT route to ol at LL — it routes to d-class/ch/qok instead. A ch pre_LL: ol=4.0%, baseline=4.0%, E=1.00× (neutral). Architecture: B LL grammar has complementary routing by pre_LL onset class: ch → d-class/ch; s/qok/ok/ot → ol; qot/qok → r. ch is excluded from the ol-LL pathway.
