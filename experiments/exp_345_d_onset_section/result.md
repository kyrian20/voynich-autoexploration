## Findings

### Finding 1: A-Dialect d-Onset Is Consistently aiin-Dominant Across All Sections

A-dialect d-onset: herbal=37% aiin+ain, text=37%, pharma=31% (small N). The fraction of ol+or body vocabulary in d-onset is 0-9% — always far below the template-onset ch counterpart (14-23% ol+or). This confirms that d-onset's resistance to the universal A-dialect {aiin→ol} shift (F340) is section-invariant, not driven by any specific section.

### Finding 2: A-Dialect ch-Onset ol/or-Dominance Is Section-Graded

A-dialect ch-onset: herbal=23% ol+or, text=14%, pharma=8%. The herbal section shows the strongest ol/or shift, consistent with F342 (herbal A-dialect {aiin→ol} enrichment = 3.98×). Pharma ch-onset shows weaker ol/or (8%) despite pharma having the most extreme overall {aiin→ol} ratio (F342=17.88×). This discrepancy may reflect that pharma is dominated by qok/ok-onset words (not ch) in A-dialect, and ch-onset in pharma has small N=25.

### Finding 3: B-Dialect d-Onset Is Also aiin-Dominant Cross-Section, With Section Variation

B-dialect d-onset aiin+ain: cosmo=37%, bio=26%, herbal=22%, pharma=19%. ol+or is always 1-6%. B-dialect d-onset is aiin-dominant (like A-dialect) but the fraction varies more across sections (19-37%). In B-dialect, pharma d-onset is ar-dominant (ar=18, aiin=16 — tied), suggesting B pharma d-onset has more diverse body vocabulary.

### Finding 4: A/B Contrast in ch-Onset Is the Sharpest Structural Discriminant

B-dialect ch-onset: herbal=0% aiin+ain, 2% ol+or, 12% edy/eey — B-dialect ch is edy-dominant.
A-dialect ch-onset: herbal=3% aiin+ain, 23% ol+or, 1% edy/eey — A-dialect ch is ol/or-dominant.

The body vocabulary of ch-onset words is the sharpest A/B discriminant: B ch = {edy,dy,ckhy,ey} class; A ch = {ol,or,y,ey} class. d-onset is the least discriminating body class across dialects — both A and B are aiin-dominant.

---

## Summary: The d/Template Distinction Is Section-Invariant

The d-onset vs template-onset body vocabulary divergence (F340) holds across all sections:
- d-onset: ~37% aiin+ain in all A-dialect sections, ~19-37% in B-dialect sections
- ch-onset: 0-4% aiin+ain in A-dialect; 0% in B-dialect
- The divergence is the onset class, not the section

This confirms the functional distinctiveness of d-onset: it maintains its body vocabulary regardless of which section of the manuscript it appears in. Template onsets adapt their body vocabulary by section; d-onset does not.
