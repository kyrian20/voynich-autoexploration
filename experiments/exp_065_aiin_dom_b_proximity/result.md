# Experiment exp_065: Aiin-Dominant Sub-Dialect B Proximity Test
Date: 2026-04-09

## Question (H077)
Is the aiin-dominant herbal A sub-dialect closer to Currier B than to standard (y-dominant) Currier A?

## Results: Aiin-Dominant A Is NOT B-Like — It Is a Genuine A Variant

### Vocabulary Jaccard Comparisons

| Comparison | Jaccard | Spearman rho |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| aiin-dom A vs **y-dom A** | **0.138** | 0.501 |
| aiin-dom A vs **herbal B** | 0.087 | -0.199 |
| aiin-dom A vs **all B** | 0.046 | 0.211 |
| y-dom A vs herbal B | 0.101 | -0.126 |
| y-dom A vs all B | 0.060 | 0.127 |
| all A vs all B (baseline) | 0.120 | -0.280 |

**Aiin-dominant A has higher Jaccard with y-dominant A (0.138) than with any B corpus (0.046-0.087).** H077 is directly refuted — the aiin-dominant sub-dialect is closer to standard A than to B.

### Suffix Distribution: Both A Registers Are Firmly A-Type

| Suffix | aiin-dom A% | y-dom A% | all B% |
|--------|------------|---------|-------|
| -aiin | 17.3% | 10.4% | 9.6% |
| **-edy** | **0.1%** | **0.1%** | **12.8%** |
| **-ol** | **14.2%** | **14.2%** | **5.2%** |
| **-or** | **9.2%** | **12.6%** | **2.8%** |
| -y | 13.2% | 12.4% | 8.9% |

The hallmark B suffix (-edy=12.8%) is nearly absent from BOTH A registers (0.1%). The hallmark A suffixes (-ol, -or) are 2-3× more common in both A registers than in B. Both A sub-groups are firmly in the Currier A morphological system.

### Exclusive Vocabulary Analysis

- Words in aiin-dom A but NOT in any B text: **only 2 words** (cthaiin, qotchol — rare forms)
- Words in all B but NOT in aiin-dom A: **188 types** (major B words: chedy, shedy, qokeedy, qokain, qokedy, etc.)

The aiin-dom A vocabulary is essentially a subset of Currier A vocabulary that lacks B-specific bio/special-section words. Its low Jaccard with B reflects genuine dialect separation.

### What Explains the Low aiin-dom A vs y-dom A Jaccard (0.138)?

The low within-A Jaccard (0.138) is NOT because aiin-dom uses B vocabulary. It's because:
1. Different positional marker sets (y-initial words absent in aiin-dom; chaiin/chey/chy elevated)
2. Section-internal vocabulary differences (herbal A has botanical terms; the two groups appear to describe different plant categories or use different descriptive conventions)
3. The small corpus sizes (aiin-dom=1127 tokens, y-dom=1766) amplify hapax-driven divergence

## H077 Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED

The aiin-dominant herbal A sub-dialect is NOT closer to Currier B than to standard Currier A. Jaccard comparisons show aiin-dom A is significantly more similar to y-dom A (0.138) than to any B corpus (0.046-0.087). The shared suffix distribution (both A groups: -ol=14.2%, -or=9-12%, -edy≈0%) confirms both registers are genuine Currier A morphology. The two-register system is an internal A variation — not a B-scribal-tradition infiltration. The near-identical Jaccard between the two A registers (0.138) and between A and B overall (0.120) is coincidental: the within-A split reflects a different partitioning of the A vocabulary, not cross-dialect contamination.

## Finding (F072)

The herbal A two-register system (aiin-dominant vs y-dominant) is a genuine internal variation within Currier A, not a B-dialect intrusion: (1) Both registers show -ol (14.2%), -or (9-12%), minimal -edy (<0.2%) — classic A suffix profile. (2) aiin-dom A Jaccard with y-dom A (0.138) > aiin-dom A Jaccard with any B corpus (0.046-0.087). (3) Only 2 words in aiin-dom A are absent from B — the vocabulary is A-type but doesn't appear in B due to section/content differences. The two A registers represent different scribal conventions within the same Currier A morphological system, not different dialects. This rules out the hypothesis that the aiin-dominant folios were written by a B-tradition scribe. The scribal tradition difference (suggested by quire clustering in Quires D/F) is an intra-A tradition difference — two ways of encoding the same A grammar.
