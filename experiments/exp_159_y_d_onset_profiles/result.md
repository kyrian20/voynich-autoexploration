**KEY FINDINGS**:

1. **ychor: PURE LF-OPENER** (13/13 tokens at LF; LF=6.24×, LL=0.00×). ychor appears at the absolute start of a line 100% of the time. This is the strongest single-word positional signal found in the corpus. ychor is a categorical line-opening formula word.

3. **y-onset LF rate = 40% in both A and B** (A: 254/644=40.1%, B: 345/836=41.3%). About 40% of all lines across both dialects open with a y-word. This is the most frequent single-onset line-opening pattern.

4. **B-dialect y-onset is even more LF-enriched** (LF=3.48×) than A (LF=2.46×). In B, y-words even more strongly dominate line openings.

5. **Internal split**: Most y-words are LF-enriched. But ytaiin (LL=2.08×) and yty (mildly LL) are LL-enriched. y-words have a small LF/LL paradigm like other onset classes.

## Summary of New Findings (F166)

1. **ychor is a PURE LF-OPENER (6.24×, 13/13 tokens at LF)**: The strongest single-word positional signal in the corpus. ychor categorically opens lines and never appears in any other position. ycheor is nearly as strong (5.35×, 6/7 at LF). ychor and ycheor are dedicated line-opening formula words.

2. **y-words open ~40% of all lines in both A and B dialects** (A=40.1%, B=41.3%). B y-onset is even more LF-enriched (3.48×) than A (2.46×). y-words are the dominant line-opening onset class across the manuscript.

3. **d-onset has internal split: LL-closers (dam=3.98×, dy=3.02×, daiiin=2.77×, dal=1.95×) vs LF-openers (dchol=3.74×, dchor=3.56×, dor=1.73×)**: The d-class encompasses both dedicated line-openers and dedicated line-closers. daiin itself is near-baseline (not a line-opener or closer).

4. **Corpus parsing artifact**: "daiin " (trailing space) at LL=21 tokens should be merged with daiin — these are daiin tokens with a trailing space character at line-end in the IVTFF format. Correcting: daiin@LL total ≈ 90/414=21.7% (1.36× — daiin is mildly LL-enriched after correction).
