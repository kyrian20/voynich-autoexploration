## H294: B-daiin→d DECOMPOSITION

**B daiin total transitions (P-type)**: 160  
**B baseline d-rate**: 5.56%

**B daiin→d**: 24 total cases (15.0%, 2.70×)

Decomposition:
- daiin→daiin (self-succession): **3 cases** only (12.5% of daiin→d)
- daiin→other d-words: **21 cases** (dam=2, dar=2, dal=3, dg=1, dy=1, dol=1, dain=1, etc.)

After removing daiin self-succession:
- daiin→(non-self d-words): 21 cases, obs=13.1%, baseline (d excl. daiin)=4.6%, **enrichment=2.85×**

**H294 CONFIRMED: The daiin→d enrichment is NOT a self-succession artifact.** Only 3/24 cases (12.5%) are daiin→daiin. The remaining 21 cases are diverse d-words (dam, dar, dal, dol, dain, etc.), all at 2.85× above baseline. B-daiin genuinely precedes d-onset words at elevated rates.

This is consistent with B's d-onset being a more prominent connector/functional class (B d-onset words like dam/dar/dal may encode line-internal grammatical relations that daiin introduces).

**Revised B-daiin role**: In B P-type body text, daiin's strongest enrichment is daiin→d (2.85×), followed by daiin→ch_e (1.77×). The originally reported daiin→sh=2.33× (F152) was an artifact of non-body locus lines. The B-daiin pattern is: **[daiin] → [d-onset or ch_e-onset content word]**, not a sh-determiner.

## Summary of New Findings (F155)

1. **B-daiin→d=2.85× is TRUE (not self-succession)**: Only 3/24 daiin→d cases are daiin self-succession. Enrichment persists at 2.85× with diverse d-words (dam, dar, dal, dol). B-daiin genuinely precedes d-onset words at elevated rates in body text.

2. **A-daiin P-type matrix confirms cth=3.08×, depletes sh (0.64×) and ch_e (0.42×)**: The daiin→cth enrichment from exp_145 holds in P-type body text (3.08× vs 3.19× all-locus). Crucially, A-daiin AVOIDS sh and ch_e — which are the onsets B-daiin favors (B: ch_e=1.77×). This is a full A/B reversal: A-daiin→sh=0.64× (depleted), B-daiin→sh=1.19× (near-baseline); A-daiin→ch_e=0.42× (depleted), B-daiin→ch_e=1.77× (enriched).

3. **B-daiin→d > A-daiin→d**: B's d-enrichment after daiin (2.85×) is double A's (1.40×). In B-dialect, d-onset words (dam/dar/dal) fill a stronger "post-daiin" grammatical slot than in A. This may reflect that in B-dialect, d-onset words encode a content function that daiin introduces (paralleling how A-daiin introduces cth-nominal content words).

## Hypotheses Spawned
- **H295**: In A-dialect, daiin→sh=0.64× and daiin→ch_e=0.42× (depleted). Are these the complementary pair to daiin→cth=3.08× (enriched)? Specifically: in A, do sh and ch_e-onset words appear specifically AFTER other types of particles (not daiin), while cth-words appear specifically after daiin? This would establish daiin as a cth-specific gatekeeper: [daiin → cth]; [other → sh/ch_e].
- **H296**: B-daiin→d=2.85× with diverse d-words (dam/dar/dal/dol). Is there a parallel: in A, do these same d-words (dam/dar/dal) appear after daiin at 1.40×, while d-words unique to B appear at higher rates in B? Or are the specific d-words different between A and B daiin→d bigrams? This would reveal whether daiin→d is a cross-dialect construction or A/B-specific.
