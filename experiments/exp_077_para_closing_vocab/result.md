**Pharma A sh-words are dominated by she- prefix forms** (sheol, sheey, sheor, sheody, sheo — words beginning with "she"), while **Herbal A sh-words are dominated by bare sh- forms** (shol, sho, shor, shy — shorter words).

Pharma A sh-words prefer -ey (15.0%) and -eey (9.6%); Herbal A sh-words prefer -aiin (8.6%) and -ol more.

### Sequential Behavior

| Transition | Pharma A | Herbal A |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| sh→qo | **32.3%** | 12.9% |
| sh→ch | 7.0% | 18.7% |
| sh→sh | 10.1% | 14.8% |
| sh→o | 20.3% | 12.2% |

**Pharma A sh-words follow qo in 32.3% of transitions vs Herbal A 12.9%.** This explains why Pharma A has stronger sh→qo coupling (2.44×). Pharma A she- forms (sheol, sheey, sheor) specifically tend to precede qo-onset words.

### H110 Verdict: SUPPORTED — Pharma A sh-Words Are Different Stems With Higher qo-Following Rate

Pharma A uses she- prefix sh-words (sheol, sheey, sheor) while Herbal A uses short sh- forms (shol, sho, shor). The she- forms specifically tend to precede qo-onset words (32.3% vs 12.9%). The Pharma A sh→qo=2.44× coupling reflects this morphological difference in sh-vocabulary: Pharma A's she- forms are grammatically different from Herbal A's sh- forms, not just different words from the same class.

**The dissociation of sequential grammar from morphological dialect (F083) is partially explained by vocabulary**: Pharma A's sh-words are morphologically different from Herbal A's, and these specific she- forms have a stronger qo-following tendency. The mechanism is intermediate between "same grammar with different parameters" and "completely different word class."
