# exp_338: ch+'a'-Body A-Dialect Enrichment — Body-Form Specificity (H537)

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Hypothesis:** H537 — ch+'ol'/'or' body forms are specifically enriched in A-dialect beyond the overall ch 'a'-body increase, implying a class-specific {ol,or}-body grammar rule for ch in A-dialect.
**Result:** H537 CONFIRMED with EXTENSION. ch+'ol' (4.74×) and ch+'or' (8.98×) are highly enriched in A-dialect, BUT so are ch+'aiin' (6.18×) and sh+'ol'/'or'/'aiin' in parallel. MAJOR EXTENSION: The {ol, or} body enrichment in A-dialect is UNIVERSAL across ALL template onset classes {ch, sh, qok, ok}: qokol (4.72×), qokor (4.29×), okol (6.96×), okor (8.82×). Simultaneously, {aiin, ain} body forms are DEPLETED in A-dialect for template onsets: qokaiin (0.51×), qokain (0.09×), okaiin (0.57×), okain (0.15×). The finding is NOT ch-specific: A-dialect has a universal template-class body VOCABULARY SHIFT from {aiin, ain} to {ol, or}. d-onset does NOT show this shift (daiin dominates in both dialects).

## Findings

### Finding 1: A-dialect body vocabulary UNIVERSAL shift: template onsets shift from {aiin, ain} to {ol, or}

ALL four template onset classes (ch, sh, qok, ok) show the SAME pattern in A-dialect:
- **{ol, or} enriched**: chol(4.74×), chor(8.98×), shol(3.86×), shor(4.98×), qokol(4.72×), qokor(4.29×), okol(6.96×), okor(8.82×)
- **{aiin, ain} depleted or flat**: qokaiin(0.51×), qokain(0.09×), okaiin(0.57×), okain(0.15×); ch and sh aiin partially enriched (chaiin 6.18×, shaiin 5.20×)

The {aiin, ain} depletion is most extreme for qok and ok (0.09-0.57×), while ch and sh show the opposite pattern (chaiin enriched 6.18×). This suggests ch/sh have a different relationship to the aiin body in A-dialect vs B-dialect: ch/sh 'aiin' frequency is so LOW in B-dialect that ANY increase in A-dialect appears as enrichment, while qok/ok 'aiin' frequency is HIGH in B-dialect and explicitly decreases in A-dialect.

### Finding 2: chor is the single most A-dialect-enriched template word type (ratio=8.98×)

chor (ch+or): B=26, B_rate=0.90%; A=151, A_rate=8.09% — ratio=8.98×. chor is more enriched in A-dialect than any other onset+body combination among template onsets. okor (ok+or) follows at 8.82×. The 'or' body is systematically the most enriched body type in A-dialect across template onsets. In F335, chor A=126 was noted as elevated — but in the ALL-POSITIONS analysis it's 151 A vs 26 B (even more extreme).

### Finding 3: The {ol, or} shift is dialect-level body vocabulary reorganization

B-dialect top template-class words:
- qok: qokain(270), qokaiin(237), qokal(156) — aiin/ain/al dominant
- ok: okaiin(159), okain(121), okar(85), okal(79)

A-dialect top template-class words:
- qok: qokol(44), qokaiin(26), qokar(23) — ol rises to #1
- ok: okaiin(39), okol(39), okal(35) — ol ties aiin

The body vocabulary hierarchy changes between dialects: B-dialect: aiin>ain>al>ar>ol>or; A-dialect: ol≈aiin>al>ar>or. The 'ol' and 'or' forms rise dramatically in relative importance in A-dialect.

### Finding 4: 'd-onset' does NOT shift to ol/or in A-dialect — the shift is template-onset-specific

d-onset body vocabulary: B-dialect daiin dominant (F297, F335); A-dialect daiin=256 (most frequent d-word). d+ol=34 (B=28, ratio≈1.8×, minor enrichment). d-onset maintains its aiin-dominant structure in A-dialect. The {aiin→ol} shift is specific to the TEMPLATE onset classes (ch/sh/qok/ok), not the content onset class (d).

## Interpretation

The A-dialect vs B-dialect divergence extends to the body vocabulary level:
- B-dialect: template onsets (ch/sh/qok/ok) primarily use AIIN-body forms: qokain, qokaiin, okaiin
- A-dialect: template onsets primarily use OL/OR-body forms: qokol, chol, chor, okol, okor, shol, shor

This is a structural shift in which body vocabulary template onsets draw on. The 'aiin' body in B-dialect is shared between template onsets and the content onset d. In A-dialect, 'aiin' body is substantially more exclusive to d-onset (daiin becomes more exclusive to d). Template onsets differentiate by using 'ol'/'or' body instead.

The finding is relevant to the CURRENT ARCHITECTURAL GAP noted in current_state.md: "A-dialect parallel: same positional taxonomy but 3× larger closed paradigms." The paradigm difference is partly driven by this body vocabulary reorganization — A-dialect qok paradigm includes many qokol/qokor types that are rare in B-dialect.

## Limitations

- Total counts are ALL positions (LF+MID+LL) not MID-only; onset totals (B=2886 for ch) exceed MID-only counts (B=2447).
- qok A_total=517 is substantially smaller than B=2387 (A-dialect uses qok less frequently), making rate comparisons valid but small-N sensitive for rare forms.
- The 'a'-body first-table analysis excluded ol/or since those have B0='o'. The cross-table analysis includes both B0='o' and B0='a' forms, which is correct for testing H537.
