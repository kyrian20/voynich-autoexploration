# Q007 Investigation: The Three-Way Constraint on sh/ch/d

**Date:** 2026-04-12
**Experiments:** exp_577 (adj_rate onset decomposition), exp_578 (copy-buffer amplification diagnosis)
**Status:** CLOSED — Q007 root cause identified

---

## The Constraint (Q007)

The 21-mechanism copy-buffer architecture (exp_314) cannot simultaneously achieve:
1. **Restricted vocabulary** for sh/ch/d onset classes (VMS CP90 = 145/290/146 types)
2. **Low adj_rate** for sh/ch/d (VMS adj_rate ≈ 0.48–0.76%)

When vocabulary is clamped to VMS sizes, the architecture's copy-buffer amplifies identical adjacent word pairs, pushing adj_rate above VMS. This was a known failing of calibration runs (exp_304: adj_rate 126.3% of target under sh/ch/d vocabulary restriction).

---

## Investigation Summary

### exp_577: VMS adj_rate Onset Decomposition

Direct analysis of VMS B-dialect adj_rate by onset class. No simulation.

**Key findings:**

1. **qok dominates adj_rate:** 64 of 174 identical pairs (36.8%) from 12.8% of tokens. qok adj_rate = 2.681%, 0.86× random expectation from CP90=32.

2. **sh/d adj_rate ≈ random expectation from their CP90 pools:**
   - sh: actual 0.764%, random expectation 0.690% (ratio = 1.108×)
   - d: actual 0.480%, random expectation 0.685% (ratio = 0.701×)
   
   VMS does NOT have a special anti-repetition mechanism for sh or d.

3. **ch is above random expectation:** actual 0.659%, random expectation 0.345% (ratio = 1.910×). Explained by ch's 15% self-succession rate (ch→ch): high self-succession concentrates ch words, creating more identical pairs than a flat distribution would.

4. **s and t have ZERO identical adjacent pairs (ratio = 0.000).** These are line-opening classes with structural anti-repetition: they appear in specific positional slots where repetition is structurally abnormal.

**Conclusion from exp_577:** VMS sh/ch/d adj_rate is consistent with random draws from finite CP90 pools. VMS has no special anti-repetition mechanism for these classes. The Q007 constraint was hypothesized to be a copy-buffer simulation artifact.

---

### exp_578: Copy-Buffer Amplification Diagnosis

Four conditions tested:
- Baseline (unclamped simulation)
- Sparse_clamp (qok/ok/ot/qot only clamped)
- All7_clamp (all 7 classes clamped to VMS CP90 sizes)
- Rich_only_clamp (sh/ch/d only clamped)

**Key findings:**

1. **The Q007 failure exists in the baseline (before any clamping):**
   | Class | VMS adj_rate | Baseline sim | Sim/VMS ratio |
   |-------|-------------|-------------|---------------|
   | sh    | 0.764%      | 1.081%      | 1.41×         |
   | ch    | 0.659%      | 1.020%      | 1.55×         |
   | d     | 0.480%      | 0.861%      | 1.79×         |
   
   The baseline simulation vocabulary is 7–14× richer than VMS (sh CP90=1303 vs VMS 145), yet adj_rate is 41–79% ABOVE VMS. Random draws from a 1303-type pool would give adj_rate ≈ 0.077%, far below the observed 1.081%.

2. **Clamping does NOT amplify sh/ch adj_rate:**
   - sh: 1.081% → 1.041% under All7_clamp (decrease of 0.040%)
   - ch: 1.020% → 0.828% under All7_clamp (decrease of 0.192%)
   
3. **Clamping DOES amplify qok and d:**
   - qok: 0.996% → 1.460% (+0.464%) when CP90 goes from 1401 to 55
   - d: 0.861% → 1.281% (+0.420%) when CP90 goes from 1114 to 153

4. **The global adj_rate is nearly VMS-matched in all conditions:** Baseline=0.909%, All7_clamp=0.956%, vs VMS=0.933%. The Q007 failure is a per-onset class failure, not a global adj_rate failure.

## Root Cause Identified

**The Q007 constraint is caused by the copy-buffer architecture interacting with self-succession coupling, not by vocabulary restriction.**

The mechanism:
1. The copy-buffer copies the previous word (or a recent word) at probability MP=0.90
2. When the previous word has onset sh/ch/d, the copy-buffer copies that word → adjacent identical pair
3. sh, ch, d have self-succession rates of 9.2%, 15.0%, and 8.6% respectively
4. Self-succession + copy-buffer = systematic overproduction of identical pairs for sh/ch/d
5. This effect is present at any vocabulary size — even with 7–14× richer vocabulary than VMS

**Why VMS achieves lower adj_rate:** VMS does not use a copy-buffer. Its words are generated by some mechanism that draws from its vocabulary at approximately random rates (adj_rate ≈ 1/CP90 for most onset classes). Without a copy-buffer, VMS's self-succession does not amplify identical word repetition above the random-pool expectation.

---

## Revision of Prior Hypothesis

Exp_577 concluded: "The constraint triangle: vocabulary restriction → copy-buffer collision increase → adj_rate violation."

**This is partially correct for qok and d but NOT for sh/ch.** The revised mechanism:

- **For sh/ch:** Copy-buffer + self-succession → adj_rate 1.4–1.6× above VMS regardless of vocabulary size. Vocabulary clamping does not amplify this; the failure is architectural.
- **For d:** Copy-buffer + self-succession gives 1.8× above VMS baseline; vocabulary clamping adds another amplification (+0.420%), making Q007 worse for d.
- **For qok:** Vocabulary clamping is the major driver of qok's amplification (+0.464%). qok's CP90 ratio (baseline 1401, VMS 32) is the most extreme, and its adj_rate jumps significantly under clamping.

---

## What Q007 Tells Us About Voynichese

1. **VMS word selection for sh/ch/d follows near-random draws from finite pools.** There is no anti-repetition mechanism operating on these classes. The mechanism that generates VMS words does not "remember" recent sh/ch/d words and avoid repeating them.

2. **s/t ARE anti-repetitive** — zero identical adjacent pairs despite having vocabularies (CP90=91/169) that would easily allow repetition by chance. This structural anti-repetition is explained by their positional constraint: s and t are line-opening classes appearing at pos1, where they always follow a line break (not another s/t word), making consecutive identical s/t pairs impossible.

3. **The copy-buffer architecture is fundamentally incompatible with VMS adj_rate for self-successive onset classes.** Any copy-buffer model with ≥8% self-succession rates for sh/ch/d will overproduce identical pairs. This is a strong constraint: the VMS generation mechanism either:
   - Has no copy-buffer component, OR
   - Has a copy-buffer that is selectively deactivated for onset-successive words, OR
   - Produces self-succession through a different mechanism that does not amplify identical word pairs

4. **The most parsimonious reading:** VMS generates words from fixed vocabulary pools without position-sensitive word copying. The 21-mechanism copy-buffer architecture models some VMS features (onset-class succession rates, word-length distribution, positional enrichment) but overshoots word repetition for successive onset pairs.

## Open Questions Raised (Not Investigated)

- Why does clamping sh/ch vocabulary DECREASE their adj_rate rather than staying flat? (Routing redistribution under vocabulary restriction?)
- Would a copy-buffer with P_STUTTER → 0 and onset-crossing copy only (never same-onset copy) reproduce VMS adj_rate for sh/ch/d? This would test the "selective deactivation" hypothesis.
- Is the s/t zero-pair result a genuine structural constraint or an artifact of their positional distribution (if s/t always follow line breaks, they structurally cannot be adjacent to themselves)?

## Summary for State Files

**Finding (proposed):** The Q007 three-way constraint (CP90 restriction, Menzerath r, adj_rate) for sh/ch/d is caused by the copy-buffer architecture producing adj_rate 1.4–1.8× above VMS for self-successive onset classes (sh=9.2%, ch=15.0%, d=8.6% self-succession rates). VMS's near-random adj_rate for these classes is incompatible with any copy-buffer mechanism that copies same-onset words. Vocabulary clamping is not the primary driver for sh/ch (though it amplifies the failure for qok and d).

**Implication for architecture:** The 21-mechanism copy-buffer architecture cannot reduce sh/ch/d adj_rate to VMS levels without either (a) eliminating same-onset copying, or (b) adding an explicit anti-repetition suppressor for sh/ch/d self-succession. Neither mechanism is currently in the architecture.
