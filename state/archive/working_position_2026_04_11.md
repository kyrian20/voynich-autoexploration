## 1. THEORIES TESTED

Four theories have been operationally defined and tested:

**1. Position-blind cipher hypothesis.** Voynichese is a homophonic substitution cipher of Latin or Italian plaintext where each plaintext character maps to EVA glyph strings without regard to line position. Operationalized as the Naibbe tarot-deck cipher (exp_170) and the Timm/Schinner local-copy algorithm (exp_171/176). Both are position-blind: their output cannot have position-dependent word distributions by construction.

**2. Self-citation hypothesis.** Voynichese is generated by copying recent words with occasional character modification (Timm/Schinner algorithm). Produces onset self-succession as a mechanical side-effect. Operationalized with 75-parameter-combination sweep (exp_176).

**3. Template-generation hypothesis.** Voynichese is generated by filling fixed positional slots from a limited vocabulary without onset-class-specific grammar. Operationalized as the slot-grammar residual analysis (exp_174).

**4. Structured grammar hypothesis.** Voynichese is a purposefully designed writing system with morphological rules, onset-class grammar, and positional constraints intrinsic to the system. This is the positive hypothesis that has survived discrimination testing by elimination of the first three. It does not imply natural language, decipherability, or any specific origin.

## 2. WHAT WAS RULED OUT AND HOW

### 2.1 Position-blind ciphers: ruled out as the sole mechanism

**Key metric**: -am LL enrichment is 6.811× in VMS B-dialect. Across 1,900 position-blind simulations (400 Naibbe × 1, 1,500 Timm × 75 parameter combinations), the maximum any position-blind mechanism achieves is 0.572×. The gap (6.8× vs 0.6×) cannot be overcome by parameter tuning — it is structural: position-blind mechanisms cannot produce position-dependent distributions by definition (exp_170, exp_176).

Convergent failure: Naibbe (homophonic cipher) and Timm (local copy) are completely different mechanisms. Both fail on the same four positional metrics (-am LL, y-onset LF, d LL, qok LF depletion). This convergent failure at exactly the same four metrics points to position-blindness as the shared failure cause, not a mechanism-specific artifact.

Statistical confidence: all four core positional metrics survive dual Bonferroni correction at m=175 (correcting for the full 175-finding characterization phase), with p < 10⁻²⁶ for the weakest (d LL A-dialect) and p < 10⁻¹⁶¹ for the strongest (-am LL B-dialect). Cross-validated on 80/20 random splits and leave-one-section-out splits (exp_172, exp_173).

**Position-sensitive ciphers are NOT ruled out.** A target-calibrated position-sensitive cipher (exp_177, Latin Cicero, separate LF/MID/LL tables, 90% clause-final detection) achieves am_ll = 5.756× — substantially closer to VMS 6.811× than any position-blind mechanism. This establishes position-sensitivity as necessary but not sufficient: the cipher fails completely on self-succession (ss_mean ≈ 1.0× vs VMS 1.465×), and the calibration was target-aware (LL table explicitly maps Latin clause-final morphemes → EVA -am, knowing -am is the VMS target). A position-sensitive cipher remains a live hypothesis but must simultaneously explain positional grammar AND self-succession.

### 2.2 Pure self-citation: ruled out as the sole mechanism

Timm overproduces exact-word repetition (adj_rate = 0.037 vs VMS 0.009, 4×) at parameters that produce VMS-level self-succession. At parameters that lower adj_rate toward VMS, ss_mean drops far below VMS. The two constraints pull in opposite directions throughout the parameter space — no combination of COPY_PROB and MODIFY_PROB resolves the joint constraint failure (exp_176, 75 combinations). Additionally, VMS MI at lags 2–7 is BELOW Timm's 95% CI: any copy-window mechanism generates elevated MI within the window, but VMS lacks this signature (exp_175, robust lags 1 and 2–7; lags 10–12 retracted as sampling artifact).

### 2.3 Pure template-generation: ruled out as the sole mechanism

-am is uniquely 6.9× enriched at LL while edy (the most common B-suffix overall at 18%) is 0.35× depleted at LL. A generic positional slot would not produce this contrast — the slot must have onset-class grammar rules specifying which suffix classes belong at LL (exp_174). Self-succession also survives position control (residual 1.2–1.8× after removing positional co-occurrence contribution), meaning onset-class sequential rules operate independently of positional assignment.

## 3. THE DUAL CONSTRAINT

**Voynichese exhibits two statistically robust structural properties that no single tested generation mechanism can produce simultaneously.**

**Property (1): Position-sensitive onset-class grammar.** -am LL enrichment (6.811×) and y-onset LF enrichment (3.506×) are present in VMS, absent from position-blind mechanisms (~1.0× across 1,900 simulations), and partially reproduced only by a target-calibrated position-sensitive cipher (5.756× on am_ll). Both findings survive Bonferroni m=175 correction and 80/20 cross-validation.

**Property (2): Sequential word-class memory.** Onset self-succession (1.465× mean across qok, ok, ch in both dialects) and qok→ch enrichment (1.2–1.3×, surviving position control) require word-class decisions to be correlated across adjacent words. No tested cipher mechanism produces this at VMS levels: position-blind ciphers produce ~0.86–0.96× (below VMS); position-sensitive substitution produces ~1.0× (no enrichment); Timm overproduces (1.5–2.4×) and cannot satisfy adj_rate simultaneously (exp_171, 176, 177).

**Any viable generation mechanism for VMS must be simultaneously position-aware AND sequentially structured.** This combination rules out all position-blind mechanisms on Property (1) and all position-sensitive substitution ciphers on Property (2). The only mechanism class that passes both constraints is the position-aware copy-buffer hybrid described in Section 4.

## 4. THE 19/19 MECHANISM: WHAT IT MEANS AND DOESN'T MEAN

### 4.1 Architecture overview

The "Mode B" copy-buffer architecture (exp_217–257) is the first mechanism that jointly satisfies both dual-constraint properties within ±20% tolerance across 19 measured metrics. It is a position-aware hybrid: line-final (LL) and line-first (LF) words are generated by a position-sensitive cipher component; mid-line (MID) words are drawn from a copy buffer and modified (character-level Timm modification, onset preserved, edy-suffix preserved). This gating decouples -am LL enrichment (produced by the cipher component, unaffected by buffer copying) from self-succession (produced by buffer recycling of words with shared onset classes).

**Finalized B-dialect architecture (19/19 PASS, 21 mechanisms, exp_314):**
- p_blind=cond_mild (n-conditional: p_short=0.060/n≤5, p_long=0.180/n≥10, linear interpolation; 18th mech, exp_309)
- max_ll_len=mild_s30 (max(3,round(8−0.3×max(0,n−5))), MAX_RETRY=10; 19th mech, exp_311)
- beta_eff(n)=0.50×1.8×n/8.4 [for word_idx≥IDX_THRESH=2] (20th+21st mech, exp_312/314)
- vocab clamp (qok/ok/ot/qot sparse-midfix onsets: full VMS type inventory; 17th mech, exp_304/305)
- p_lf=1.0, cp=0.55, mp=0.90, p_qokch=0.12, p_chqok=0.12, p_stutter=0.006
- r_lf=0.12, r_mid=0.20, mu_len=8.4, sigma_len=3.0
- p_mf=0.20, p_lf_mf=0.50, p_ll_mf=0.00, gamma=0.30, alpha=0.20, beta_base=0.50, beta_scale=1.8
- **Total: 21 mechanisms as of exp_314**
- **Battery: 19/19 PASS (N=30). Menzerath r: −0.234 (104% of VMS −0.225)**
- **r_ll: −0.213 (97% of VMS). r_mid: −0.178 (100% of VMS — exact match). MID slope: −0.072 (123%)**
- **Stable: edy_rate=87.2% (7pp above 80% floor); all 19 metrics well within tolerance**

**Menzerath r progression (Phase 7, exp_302–314):**
- 16-mech (exp_287): −0.101 (45%) → 17th mech vocab clamp (exp_306): −0.109 (48%)
- 18th mech cond_mild p_blind (exp_309): −0.137 (61%)
- 19th mech LL word-len filter (exp_311): −0.163 (73%)
- 20th mech n-cond beta scale_1.0 (exp_312): −0.203 (90%)
- 21st mech beta_scale=1.8 (exp_314): −0.234 (104%) — within VMS 95% CI (−0.225 ± 0.019)

**Finalized A-dialect architecture (19/19 PASS, 13 mechanisms, exp_262/264):**
- p_blind=0.0505, d_boost=24.30%, r_lf=0.266, r_mid=0.87, p_stutter=0.002
- mu_len=5.64, cp=0.55, mp=0.90, p_qokch=0.12, p_lf=1.0
- p_y_y=0.10, p_o_o=0.10, p_sh_qok=0.10
- filter_other on all onset distributions

Both architectures are corpus-split stable: B-dialect 17/19 odd / 16/19 even (exp_273); A-dialect 18/19 odd / 17/19 even (exp_264/240). Split failures are VMS target instability from rare-feature Poisson noise, not simulation model failure.

**Onset-succession grammar taxonomy:**
- B-dialect: cross-class bidirectional coupling qok↔ch (p_qokch=p_chqok=0.12)
- A-dialect: within-class self-succession for low-frequency onsets (y→y at p=0.10; o→o at p=0.10)
- Threshold: explicit Markov rule needed only when onset MID rate < ~10%; buffer self-succession sufficient above ~15% MID

### 4.2 What 19/19 PASS means

The 19-metric battery tests onset-class positional enrichment (am_ll, y_lf, d_ll, s_lf, ch_ll, ok_ll, qok_ll, qok_lf, am_lf_enr, am_mid_enr, global_am), sequential structure (ss_mean, ss_d, ss_y, qok_ch), lexical properties (adj_rate, edy_rate, word_len_mean), and dialect-specific rate (qok_ch). Every metric falls within ±20% of its VMS target value. The architecture is corpus-split stable (not an overfitting artifact). A parameter-count control (exp_224) confirmed that each Mode B feature is individually necessary — removing any core component reduces the pass count.

### 4.3 What 19/19 PASS does NOT mean

**It does not prove the mechanism is correct.** The position-aware hybrid is a demonstration that a mechanism of this class CAN produce all 19 VMS structural signals simultaneously. It is not a proof that VMS was generated this way. All calibration parameters were set using knowledge of VMS target values (target-aware design). The architecture status is "precisely jointly achievable under target-calibrated parameters."

**It does not rule out intrinsic structured grammar.** Both (a) intrinsic grammar (the writing system has genuine onset-class positional and sequential rules) and (b) the position-aware hybrid are consistent with the evidence. These two hypotheses are functionally equivalent at the structural level: any mechanism that jointly satisfies both dual-constraint properties must implement the same constraints as a grammar. The mechanism relabels, rather than contradicts, the grammar hypothesis.

**It does not extend to the full bigram structure.** The 19-metric battery is a subset of VMS structural properties. The architecture is complete for global onset bigrams but incomplete for MID-specific bigram structure (see Section 6).

## 5. A-DIALECT GENERALIZATION

The Mode B architecture generalizes to A-dialect with dialect-specific parameter recalibration. Four structural parameters transfer unchanged (cp=0.55, mp=0.90, p_qokch=0.12, p_lf=1.0). Six calibration parameters require dialect-specific values (p_blind, d_boost, r_lf, r_mid, p_stutter, mu_len). Three additional dialect-specific Markov rules are needed (p_y_y=0.10, p_o_o=0.10, p_sh_qok=0.10) where B-dialect has p_qokch=p_chqok=0.12.

**A/B grammar differences:**
- B: qok↔ch bidirectional coupling (cross-class rules); A: y→y and o→o (within-class rules)
- A adj_rate = 0.00933 = B adj_rate to 4 decimal places — strongest cross-dialect structural invariant
- A am_ll = 3.499× (51% of B's 6.550×); different p_blind calibration required
- A has stronger d-LL enrichment (d_ll=1.655× vs B 1.358×) — d-onset grammatically distinct between dialects
- A-dialect erosion factor = 0.61 (vs B = 0.84); edy-rate-dependent (A has 0.45% edy words vs B 19.4%)

The 13/19 direct transfer (13 metrics pass with B parameters applied to A corpus without any recalibration) confirms the mechanism is not B-dialect specific. The remaining 6 calibration adjustments reflect corpus-level property differences, not architectural incompatibility.

## 6. PARAGRAPH GRAMMAR (PHASES 4–5)

The paragraph-level grammar is a 5-tier positional system operating above the line level. It was characterized in exp_194–217 using the ZL3b paragraph-aware parser (A=163 paragraphs, B=101 paragraphs, 28,300 words).

**Five positional tiers:** Para-Start LF (PS), Within-Para LF (WP-LF), MID, Within-Para LL (WP-LL), Para-Final LL (PF-LL).

**Confirmed grammar rules:**
1. **Para-start (PS) is 64–70% PARA tier** (p/k/t/f-onset gallows+ch compounds). Bare gallows NEVER appear at PS (0/264 paragraphs, both dialects). Only gallows+ch compounds function as para-starters.
2. **Gallows+ch PS hierarchy differs by dialect**: A: p+ch (12.41×) > f+ch (9.50×) > k+ch (4.32×) > t+ch (2.72×); B: k+ch (9.91×) > f+ch (7.62×) > p+ch (3.71×) > t+ch (2.05×). The k/p rank inversion is explained by p flooding B's WP-LF positions (9.61% vs A 2.32%), displacing k to PS (exp_199/F199).
3. **Para-final LL exclusion corrected**: ONLY p and f are perfectly excluded (0.00× both dialects); k is near-neutral (0.88–0.93×); t is slightly enriched in B (1.52×).
4. **t is universally excluded from pos=1** (second word of first line) in BOTH dialects: B 0/98, A 0/160, combined 0/258. Shared A/B grammar skeleton: [PS = gallows class] → [pos=1 = anti-gallows class].
5. **A 3-register paragraph architecture**: [PS: p/t/f/k formula] → [first-line body: sh/o/qo; cth depleted 0.17–0.38× ALL positions] → [middle: cth 1.13×, daiin hub 3.84×] → [last: d-onset/daiin 1.67–1.88×]. 
6. **B 3-register architecture**: [first: p/t/k/f + o/y/qo body; y+gallows compounds at pos=3+] → [middle: near-uniform, cth-absent] → [last: y/s/ot/ok; qot/qo excluded (0.16–0.45×); ok=2.48×/sh=2.66×].
7. **B edy-suffix** is a MID content-word class (pf_ll=0.77× — NOT para-terminal), not a line-ending word.
8. **ch-stem section classifier**: 88.7% leave-one-paragraph-out cross-validated accuracy in 4-class section prediction (herbal/pharma/astro/cosmo) using only ch/cth/sh word frequencies. Sections use demonstrably different ch-word inventories.
9. **dam cross-dialect LL invariance**: dam is 0% LF in both A and B (A: 64.1% LL; B: 79.2% LL), despite general d-onset positional reversal between dialects. First word with confirmed cross-dialect positional invariance.

## 7. BIGRAM STRUCTURE BEYOND THE BATTERY

Beyond the 19-metric battery, the architecture reproduces many but not all onset bigram signals (exp_241–266).

**Known Markov rules correctly captured:**
- qok→ch (B: 97.4% of VMS target), y→y (A: 101.4%)
- ch→qok bidirectional coupling introduced in exp_249: p_chqok=0.12 achieves 98.2% of VMS qok_ch

**Captured coverage** (B-dialect): 27 onset pairs captured, 71 overcaptured, 39 missed (137 statistically significant pairs total).

**Top missed pairs (architectural gaps, not in battery):**
- sh→qok (B: E_mid=1.967, N=339; A: E_mid=1.741, N=—): confirmed genuine within-MID grammar (exp_242); lag-1 only (exp_266); not lexical co-clustering (HHI/null=1.29×, exp_244). B-dialect: Markov mechanism insufficient (max E≈1.41 at p≈0.20, requires p≈0.42 for full closure — causes battery regression, exp_243/277). A-dialect: p_sh_qok=0.10 achieves 53% closure without regression (exp_262). Mechanism unknown for B.
- ch→ch, sh→sh depleted in B MID (E<1): simulation over-produces (+0.199 to +0.617, exp_275). A-dialect shows enrichment (opposite direction).
- ot→ot small gap in B MID: Δ=−0.161 after bug fix (exp_256); accepted as below battery tolerance.

**'other' onset bug**: Discovered in exp_254–256. The 'other' onset label was used as a literal string in make_word_sim, inflating simulated ot-onset rates by ~2×. Fixed with filter_other() applied to all onset distributions in all subsequent experiments. All architectures from exp_257 onward use the fix.

## 8. WHAT THE MODEL CANNOT REPRODUCE

### 8.1 ~~Menzerath's Law~~ — CLOSED in Phase 7 (exp_302–314)

~~VMS exhibits Menzerath's Law: r(line_length, mean_word_length) = −0.249 (A), −0.293 (B)~~ — this gap was closed across Phase 7 (exp_302–314) by five new mechanisms. The 21-mechanism architecture achieves Menzerath r=−0.234 (104% of VMS B-dialect −0.225), within the VMS measurement 95% CI (−0.225 ± 0.019). This is NOT in the 19-metric battery (Menzerath r is a meta-metric), but all three components are reproduced: r_ll=97%, r_mid=100%, r_lf≈0 (both VMS and sim).

**Phase 7 mechanism cascade (5 new mechanisms, exp_302–314):**
1. **17th mech — vocabulary clamping (exp_306):** Sparse-midfix onsets (qok/ok/ot/qot) draw from full VMS type inventory. CP90 ratios: 0.77-0.91× (near-VMS). BONUS: edy_rate fragility resolved (83%→100%), enabling beta to increase from 0.30→0.50.
2. **18th mech — n-conditional p_blind (exp_309):** p_blind scales with line length (p_short=0.060/p_long=0.180). Captures -am rate gradient with line length. Menzerath r: 48%→61%.
3. **19th mech — LL word-length filter (exp_311):** max_ll_len(n)=max(3,round(8−0.3×max(0,n−5))). Reproduces VMS LL word length gradient (shorter in longer lines). r_ll: 7%→94%. Menzerath r: 61%→73%.
4. **20th mech — n-conditional MID beta (exp_312):** beta_eff(n)=0.50×n/8.4. Reproduces VMS MID suffix length decrease with line length (2.696→2.599 chars). r_mid: 15%→87%. Menzerath r: 73%→90%.
5. **21st mech — beta_scale calibration (exp_314):** beta_scale 1.0→1.8. Closes r_mid from 87% to 100%. All scales 1.0-2.5 pass 19/19 at N=30 — exp_312 N=10 failures were seed artifacts. Menzerath r: 90%→104%.

**Key finding:** The Menzerath r gap is decomposable into three components (r_ll, r_mid, r_lf) addressable by separate mechanisms. Each mechanism is mechanistically motivated by VMS diagnostic data (VMS LL suffix length gradient, VMS MID suffix gradient, VMS LL word length gradient). The architecture reproduces the full Menzerath structure without target-fitting the correlation itself. (Note: scale_1.8 was selected because r_mid=100.1% — target-aware calibration at the scale parameter level, but the 19/19 battery pass is independent.)

### 8.2 sh→qok enrichment (B-dialect)

B-dialect sh→qok enrichment (E_mid=1.967, N=339) is a genuine within-MID succession rule (not cross-position artifact, exp_242; not lexical co-clustering, exp_244; lag-1 only, exp_266). The copy-buffer Markov approach cannot reproduce it: the required p_sh_qok ≈ 0.42 causes ss_d battery regression (exp_243/277). The enrichment is section-concentrated (stars=2.155, bio=1.832, exp_252) but grammatical (HHI/null=1.29×). Mechanistically identical to qok→ch but with ~4× larger magnitude. No viable architecture-consistent mechanism has been identified for B-dialect.

### 8.3 Word-internal structure and vocabulary richness

The simulation generates ~6,400 unique MID word types at optimal parameters vs VMS ~2,929 (2.2× over-production, exp_280/281). This is structural to Timm character mutation at MP=0.90 — reducing MP below 0.90 breaks the battery (exp_281). VMS vocabulary is richer in midfix structure (62–71% of MID words contain a midfix region, exp_269), and this midfix vocabulary is not captured by the onset×suffix combinatorial model. The vocabulary Zipf slope is −0.48 to −0.70 (flatter than natural language −1.0), consistent with glossographic generation, but the exact distribution is not reproduced.

Word repetition distances: simulation produces median MID repetition distance = 41 words (B-dialect) vs VMS median = 96 words (2.3× too short, exp_276). Root cause: vocabulary size mismatch (sim ~173 eligible types vs VMS ~397). Not yet closed.

## 9. WHAT REMAINS UNDETERMINED

**Whether the structured grammar encodes language.** The grammar is real. Whether it encodes natural language information or is a purposefully constructed artificial system cannot be determined from structural analysis alone.

**The plaintext language (if cipher).** If VMS is a position-sensitive cipher, the plaintext must produce clause-final morphemes at a density that generates 6.8× -am LL enrichment. Latin Cicero at 90% detection reaches only 5.8× (exp_177). An Italian source (Dante) reaches only 3.46× (exp_187). No tested source language is sufficient.

**Whether dialects A and B are scribal registers or different encodings.** The split is structurally real (vocabulary Jaccard 0.148, suffix paradigm inversion, d-onset positional reversal). The 13/19 direct transfer between dialects shows the architecture is shared; the 6 calibration differences reflect corpus-level divergence. Whether A and B represent scribal choice, section-based encoding change, or temporal drift is unresolved.

**Whether the sh→qok B-dialect mechanism has a structural explanation.** No mechanism within the current architectural vocabulary can reproduce it. Candidate explanations (grammatical slot rule, section-level clustering, lexical co-clustering) have been tested; grammatical slot rule is confirmed, but the Markov implementation is insufficient in magnitude.

**Quire-level stability.** Section-level LOSO cross-validation passed (exp_172). Quire-level LOSO has not been run. The anti-PS constraint (t excluded from pos=1 in 0/258 paragraphs) predicts stability but has not been tested at quire granularity.

**Long-range sequential structure beyond lag 1.** The lags 10–12 MI claim has been retracted (sampling artifact). No reliable evidence for sequential structure beyond lag 7 is available from this corpus.

## 11. EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS

1. **No decipherment.** This analysis identifies statistical regularities. It does not decode any VMS text, identify any word's meaning, or establish correspondence between EVA tokens and natural language words.

2. **No source language identification.** The analysis does not identify the source language, if any. The cipher hypothesis space is narrowed to position-sensitive mechanisms — but no specific natural language produces the VMS positional grammar when run through any tested cipher.

3. **No discrimination among structured grammar subtypes.** The "structured grammar" verdict does not distinguish between: a constructed/artificial language, a cipher of a language with unusual clause-boundary morphology, a mnemonic/memory system, or other structured symbol systems.

4. **All Mode B calibration parameters are target-aware.** The five-metric joint fit (exp_210/211: am_ll, d_ll, y_lf, ss_mean, adj_rate all within 3% of VMS) was achieved by setting cp/mp (from ss_mean/adj_rate), p_blind (from am_ll), d_am (from d_ll), and p_lf_blind (from y_lf) each using knowledge of the VMS target. The result demonstrates precise joint achievability, not independent prediction.

5. **The 19-metric battery is not exhaustive.** The battery tests global onset bigrams and positional enrichments. It misses MID-specific bigram structure (sh→qok, ch/sh anti-self-succession), Menzerath correlation, word repetition distances, vocabulary richness, and paragraph-grammar metrics. The architecture is complete relative to the 19 tested metrics; it has confirmed gaps in the broader structural picture.

6. **No validity claim for untested cipher mechanisms.** Many cipher mechanisms are untested: running-key ciphers, transposition ciphers, Polybius squares, route ciphers, null ciphers, and others. The verdict applies specifically to position-blind homophonic ciphers of Latin or Italian.

## 12. PHASE 6: WORD-INTERNAL STRUCTURE CHARACTERIZATION (exp_288–296)

Phase 6 characterized the morphological structure of VMS words at the onset/midfix/suffix level. Key findings consolidated here for reference.

### 12.1 Three-Tier Midfix Structure

Onset classes cluster into three structural tiers by ZeroMid rate (F287):
- **Sparse** (ZeroMid ≥60%): qok=78.4%, ok=68.2%, ot=66.7%, cth=83.3% (B-dialect). These are template-like onset+suffix combinations with little midfix.
- **Moderate** (ZeroMid 35-60%): ch=39.8%, sh=51.4%, d=59.8% (B-dialect).
- **Rich** (ZeroMid <35%): y=1.3%, p=9.4%, t=34.0%, o=9.2% (B-dialect). High midfix diversity.

A-dialect shows the same three-tier structure but with universally longer midfixes (all sparse classes have A/B midfix ratio > 1.5×). The largest A/B asymmetry is qok: A=1.34 chars vs B=0.52 chars (2.6×), driven by B-qok's concentrated edy/eedy/ain suffix paradigm and high ZeroMid rate (78.4% vs A=44.1%).

### 12.2 Two-Tier Vocabulary

The VMS vocabulary divides into two structurally distinct classes (F290/exp_289):
- **Template words** (frequent, ZeroMid=66-71%): onset + suffix, no midfix. Mean word length 4.6-4.9 chars.
- **Vocabulary-rich words** (hapax, ZeroMid=1%, MLen≈3.9 chars): onset + complex midfix + suffix. Mean word length 6.9-7.2 chars.

69.2% of VMS word types are hapax (appear once). Hapax words have 7× longer midfixes than frequent words. The two classes coexist: template words account for 66-71% of frequent-word tokens; vocabulary-rich words drive the hapax rate.

### 12.3 Position-Stratified Vocabulary

Hapax rates differ strongly by line position (F291/exp_290):
- MID: 11-16% hapax
- LF: 21-24% hapax
- LL: 22-29% hapax

Template words (ZeroMid) concentrate at MID: 79.1% of frequent-word tokens appear at MID. LF/LL are enriched for vocabulary-rich hapaxes but also contain template grammar words (y-onset LF, -am LL). Hapax midfix length is STABLE across positions (~4 chars at LF, MID, and LL alike) — hapax structure is position-invariant.

### 12.4 Character Zone Rules

Three morphological zones have distinct character inventories (F292/exp_291):
- **Onset zone**: q is onset-exclusive (11-12% of onset chars, 0.4% of midfixes, 0% of suffixes)
- **Suffix zone**: n and y are suffix-dominant; e/a/i are onset-absent (appear only in midfix and suffix)
- **Midfix zone**: highest character entropy (3.58 bits) and bigram entropy (5.4-5.7 bits); no character is exclusively midfix
- Gallows compounds (ch, ckh, pch, cth) appear as midfixes (~20% of all midfix instances) — midfixes are NOT vowel-dominated (33-39% consonant-like)

### 12.5 Onset-Position Mutual Information

Onset class explains 7.7% (A) and 13.0% (B) of positional uncertainty (I(onset;pos) = 0.096 and 0.136 bits, Z=149 and Z=412). All onset classes appear at all positions (C=0% in the position decomposition), but with strong enrichment ratios (F294/exp_293):

**LF-enriched (B-dialect)**: p=19.4×, s=11.6×, y/t=5.3×, d=3.1×
**MID-enriched**: ch=0.26× (LF depletion), sh=0.46×, ok=0.36×, ot=0.25×
**LL-enriched**: r=2.7×, ol=2.1×, o=1.8×

Positional grammar is PROBABILISTIC (enrichment, not exclusion). B-dialect has 1.4× stronger onset-position coupling than A. Cross-position word identity (F293/exp_292): 55-67% of MID tokens are words that ALSO appear at LF (identical word types); 33-45% are same onset class, different word; 0% from onset classes absent at LF.

### 12.6 Three-Way Onset Class Vocabulary Characterization

Phase 6 establishes a three-category onset class taxonomy (F296/exp_295):

1. **Template-copying classes** (qok, ok, ot, ch, sh): MID-enriched; hapax rate 4-12%; closed paradigm: 18-43% of vocabulary types cover 90% of tokens. B-dialect concentrates around ~32 word types for 90% of qok-onset tokens.

2. **Grammar-word LF classes** (p, y, s, t): LF-enriched; hapax rate 27-62%; larger paradigm (75-87% of types for 90% coverage). The cipher component output classes.

3. **Line-terminal content classes** (r, ol, o, d, l): LL or edge-enriched; moderate hapax (15-44%). d-onset is unusual: despite 59.8% ZeroMid it is LF-enriched (3.1×), showing the onset class identity determines positional preference more than ZeroMid rate.

### 12.7 daiin Structural Dominance

daiin accounts for 27.4% of all d-onset tokens — the highest concentration of any word in a large onset class (F297/exp_296). daiin is position-NEUTRAL (enrichments: LF=0.96×, MID=1.09×, LL=0.83×). Its dominance is mechanistic: the d→-aiin constraint (F289: 34.6% A, 22.5% B) × ZeroMid mode × d-onset high frequency all converge on the same word form.

Within d-onset, a three-way sub-structure mirrors the general grammar: (1) plain onset+suffix templates (daiin/dar/dain) at MID, (2) gallows-compound d-words (dshedy LF/MID=9.67×) at LF, (3) -am-suffix words (dam, LL/MID=3.12×) at LL.

### 12.8 Closed Paradigm Section Stability

B-dialect closed paradigms have a universal core but section-specific periphery (F298/exp_297). Bio section achieves 90% qok-onset token coverage with just 15 word types (100% overlap with global 90%-set); text section requires 39 types (74% overlap). Pairwise Jaccard between sections: 0.13–0.52. Two-tier paradigm structure:

1. **Universal core** (~15-30 types): identical across all B-dialect sections. Visible as the bio section's complete paradigm.
2. **Section-specific periphery**: text section adds 50–160% additional types on top of the universal core.

The bio section is the reference "closed paradigm" in the strictest sense — its ~15 qok types for 90% coverage represents the maximum concentration found anywhere in B-dialect.

### 12.9 A-Dialect Onset Class Taxonomy (Phase 6 Cross-Dialect Parallel)

A-dialect shares the same directional positional grammar as B (F302/exp_300):
- **Grammar-word LF**: A={p,f,t,y,s} ≈ B={p,s,t,y,d} — core classes {p,t,y,s} invariant; d is LF-enriched only in B
- **LL-terminal**: A={r,d,ol} vs B={r,ol,o,l,cth} — r and ol are universal LL-markers; l/cth become LL-terminal only in B
- **d-onset divergence**: LF-enriched in B (2.29×) vs LL-enriched in A (1.72×) — the single largest cross-dialect onset grammar difference

**Closed paradigm sizes — critical cross-dialect result:** A has 3× larger closed paradigm percentages for template-onset classes:
- A-qok: 71 types for 90% coverage (57.3%) vs B-qok: 32 types (18.2%)
- A-ok: 83 types (58.9%) vs B-ok: 67 types (33.8%)
- A-ot: 92 types (67.6%) vs B-ot: 83 types (39.0%)

A-dialect's template-onset classes (ch/sh/ok/qok) are MID-enriched like B's but with much higher hapax rates (60-80%) and no onset class achieves <60% hapax. B-dialect's type-concentration (template paradigm) is an additional layer not present in A. A top suffix = -ol; B top suffix = -edy (the fundamental A/B suffix paradigm shift).

### 12.10 Text-Section Within-Folio Lexical Diversity

Text section per-folio TTR is 1.6-1.7× higher than bio for qok/sh onset classes (text=0.350, bio=0.218; text=0.588, bio=0.346), confirming genuine within-folio diversity — not cross-folio register mixing. ok/ot onset classes show equivalent per-folio TTR (text≈bio at 0.49-0.51), meaning their larger text-section paradigm sets reflect Zipfian accumulation from larger section size rather than genuine diversity. Implication: the bio section is formulaic (small closed paradigm, low within-folio TTR); text sections engage a broader vocabulary per folio.

### 12.11 Architectural Implications

Phase 6 findings bear on the Mode B simulation in three ways:

1. **Copy-buffer onset composition**: The simulation's copy-buffer preferentially generates ch/sh/ok/ot/qok onset words (MID-enriched in VMS). Phase 6 confirms this is correct — these are the "template-copying" classes. The buffer vocabulary should be dominated by these onset classes.

2. **Vocabulary richness gap remains**: The simulation over-produces unique MID types (6,458 vs VMS 2,929). Phase 6 shows the VMS MID vocabulary is dominated by the closed paradigm of ch/sh/qok/ok/ot words (32-284 types cover 90% of tokens per onset class). The simulation needs to match these closed paradigm sizes, not just total vocabulary.

3. **B-dialect standardization is at word-type level, not suffix level**: B has HIGHER suffix entropy than A in 18/20 onset classes but smaller closed paradigm. The standardization is the concentration of token mass in a small number of ZeroMid template types. The simulation should produce similar word-type concentration in MID.

4. **Suffix length gradient (exp_287)**: beta=0.30 position-conditional suffix bias achieves MID slope 87% of VMS target and Menzerath r 45% of VMS target. This is the 16th mechanism. Fragile boundary: edy_rate=83.5% constrains further beta increase.

## 13. PHASE 8: LINE GRAMMAR CHARACTERIZATION (exp_388–413, F388–F417)

Phase 8 characterized the grammar structure of individual lines in both A and B dialects, from the line-first word (LF) to the line-last word (LL), through the trigram level (LF→pos1→pos2→pos3). This phase established a detailed taxonomy of line-opening formulas and revealed systematic A/B grammar inversions.

### 13.1 B Dialect Line Grammar Structure

**Grammar model**: B continuation lines follow a "near-Markovian with formula overlays" structure:
- **90.7% near-Markovian** at LF-conditional pos2 level (E_cond<2.0 for the (LF,pos1,pos2) trigram)
- **2.6% formula-typed at N≥5** (E_cond≥2.0; 59/2265 lines) — the correct characterization; at N≥3, the fraction is 8.6% but this is inflated by small-N noise (shuffled control=10.9% > real VMS at N≥3) (F415/F416)
- Formula effects operate primarily at positions 1-2 (LF-conditional); decay by pos3 (F407)
- Global bigram chain (pos1→pos2 unconditional) dominates from pos3 onward

**Most frequent B trigrams** (all near-Markovian): d→sh→qok(N=24, E=1.12×); s→sh→qok(N=21, E=1.28×); d→ch→ch(N=15, E=1.04×). Formula-typed lines first appear at rank 10.

**B formula types** (E_cond≥2.0, N≥5):
1. y→sh→ch=2.88×(N=8) — content-formula (y LF, ch at pos2 after sh)
2. p→sh→qot=2.04×(N=8) — gallows-formula (p LF, qot at pos2 after sh)
3. qok→sh→k=4.16×(N=5) — template-class (qok LF, k at pos2 after sh)
4. s→sh→l=2.06×(N=6) — formula-terminal (s LF, l at pos2 after sh)
5. p→ch→ot=5.33×(N=4) — ch-variant (p LF, ot at pos2 after ch)
6. qok→ch→qo=2.62×(N=8) — qok-ch continuation
7. d→ot→ot=4.13×(N=5) — d-LF with ot self-routing
8. l→ch→qok=2.96×(N=5) — l-LF ch→qok

**LF as pos2-router**: LF class primarily conditions pos2 (not pos1 choice). p→sh at LF→pos1 is only 1.37× enriched; the formula effect is at pos2 (given p→sh, qot=2.04×). This means LF class "routes" pos2 routing, not pos1 selection (F405/F408).

**p-formula complete structure** (F409): p-LF lines are LONGER than average (8.6 vs 7.8 words). Global ch-avoidance: pos1=0.42×/MID=0.69×/LL=0.47×. o-class enrichment at pos1(2.24×), MID(1.73×), LL(1.73×). LL routing: o enriched(1.73×), qot(1.53×), r(1.41×), ch depleted(0.47×). p-formula = p[LF]→sh/o[pos1]→[o/qo/qot-rich MID]→[o/qot/r LL].

**Global bigram chain** (pos2→pos3): sh→qo=2.16×(N=17); qot→ot=2.25×(N=15); l→l=2.16×(N=13); y→y=3.41×(N=5). The onset gradient (sh: pos1=17%→pos3=10%; qok: 12.5%→16.1%) reflects "grammar relaxation" timeline from LF-sensitive opening to Markovian MID by pos3.

**LF→LL associations** (F403): LF class has direct LF→LL associations beyond local bigram. B p→ch LL=0.47×(N=15/222) strongest depletion; B sh→qo LL=2.67×; B t→t LL=2.50×. Self-routing at LL: t/sh/ot→same class. The p-ch avoidance (pos2~0, MID=0.69×, LL=0.47×) is a global p-ch incompatibility across the entire line.

### 14.2 Hapax Internal Structure (F435)

Hapax words (f=1) are structurally different from frequent words (f≥5), but the difference is partly universal (exp_435, exp_436):
- **Word length gradient**: hapax 6.33-6.59 chars vs frequent 4.47-4.96 chars (1.33-1.42× ratio)
  - BUT natural language shows same/stronger gradient (Latin=1.79×, Italian=1.92×) — this is universal, not VMS-distinctive
- **Midfix length gradient** (VMS-specific): hapax midfix=2.73-2.79 chars vs frequent=0.36-0.43 chars (6-8× ratio). Frequent VMS words are nearly onset+suffix only; hapax words carry substantial mid-word material. No direct natural-language equivalent for this decomposition.
- **No-suffix hapaxes**: 17-22% of hapax words have no recognized suffix — equally distributed across recognized-onset and 'other'-onset hapaxes (not transcription noise)
- **Section distribution**: B astro has FEWEST hapaxes (E=0.70×, 9.4%) — astro is most repetitive (qok-formula dominance). Herbal/bio/pharma above baseline

### 14.3 Onset Class Suffix Typology (F436/F437)

Two findings from exp_437-438 establish onset-class suffix typology:

**edy-class suffix concentration (F436):** Template onset classes prefer edy-class suffixes; content onset classes do not:
- High edy: sh(45.8%)/qok(40.4%)/ch(36.1%)/ok(30.5%)/ot(28.5%)
- Low edy: d(4.3%)/s(4.9%) — d is most anti-edy; d words = aiin/ar/in endings
- This 10.7× range (d vs sh) quantifies the template/content onset split

**Onset structural taxonomy (F437, exploratory):** Onset classes differ dramatically in midfix requirement:
- **y-onset uniquely requires midfix**: 8.9%(B)/12.9%(A) zero-midfix — y is the most morphologically complex onset; y-words almost always carry mid-word material
- **B skeleton classes**: qok(83.4%)/qot(77.6%)/ok(75.3%)/ot(75.2%) — predominantly bare onset+suffix
- **d-onset**: skeleton in A (73.7%) and high in B (72.5%); d+aiin is the most suffix-concentrated combination (35%(A)/24%(B))
- Suffix entropy range: cth=3.10 (most restricted) → y=4.17-4.25 (most diverse)
- The B-qok structural shift (83% zero-midfix) vs A-qok (54%) directly explains F288/F434

### 14.4 Synthesis: Vocabulary Structure Constraints on Generation Models

The vocabulary phase establishes that VMS word-internal structure is NOT consistent with a simple onset × midfix × suffix productive system sampled uniformly:

1. **y-onset requires midfix** (morphological constraint, not statistical accident)
2. **Frequent words are skeleton-like** (near-zero midfix), hapax words are complex — but this gradient is present in natural languages too (Latin/Italian show same direction, stronger magnitude)
3. **Template/content onset split in suffix preferences** — same split as positional grammar (template class = LF-formula roles; content class = MID body words)
4. **A/B vocabulary divergence at qok** mirrors the dialect grammar divergence — the dialectal difference operates at both the grammar (positional routing) and vocabulary (word-internal structure) levels

These constraints are consistent with, but do not uniquely identify, either (a) a structured natural language being encoded, or (b) a purposefully designed writing system with different character classes having prescribed morphological templates.

## 15. PHASE 9 EXTENSION: CROSS-POSITION, WORD-LENGTH, AND CROSS-LINE GRAMMAR (exp_440–447, F438)

### 15.1 Cross-Position Word Identity (exp_440)

Decomposing cross-position token sharing reveals how the line grammar operates at the word level:
- **MID ← LF identity dominates**: 60-67% of MID tokens are words that ALSO appear at LF (identical word types). MID word selection is largely LF word reuse, not onset-class-constrained selection.
- **LL ← LF onset-class selection**: 57-58% of LL tokens are same-onset-as-LF but different word. LL grammar specifies an onset class (constrained by LF onset via bracket/formula rules) and then selects from the LL-specific sub-vocabulary within that class.
- **Different-onset words at LL ≈ 0%**: All onset classes appear at all positions, so cross-onset LL words are trivially absent.
- **daiin is the primary cross-position bridge word**: appears at LF, MID, and LL in both dialects; the sole word that is vocabulary-core at all three positions.

This shows a two-layer positional grammar: MID is governed by vocabulary reuse (draw from LF word pool); LL is governed by onset class selection (F413 bracket grammar) followed by LL-specific word-form selection.

### 15.2 Word-Length Component Structure (exp_443, H494)

Stolfi decomposition at the component level establishes the VMS word-length budget:
- **A-dialect**: onset=1.76 + midfix=1.12 + suffix=2.14 = **5.02 chars** mean
- **B-dialect**: onset=1.89 + midfix=0.88 + suffix=2.61 = **5.38 chars** mean
- **Suffix is the dominant component** (43% A, 48% B of total word length)
- **Midfix is sparse**: 50% (A) / 60% (B) of words have zero midfix (median=0)
- **A vs B length difference** (0.36 chars) is entirely suffix-driven (+0.47 chars in B) plus slight onset difference (+0.13 chars, driven by higher qok frequency in B); midfix is SHORTER in B (−0.24 chars)

**Diagnostic for 22% simulation overproduction**: If simulation generates words 22% too long (+1.1 chars), midfix cannot be the source (50-60% of VMS words already have zero midfix; more midfix in simulation would make words shorter). The 22% excess is most likely from suffix overselection — too many 4-char suffixes (aiin=4 chars, eedy=4 chars, chol=4 chars) which account for 20% of A words and 22% of B words. Calibrating the suffix-length distribution to match VMS (suf=2: 42% in A, suf=4: 20% in A) is the highest-leverage parameter fix.

### 15.3 Cross-Line Onset Grammar (F438, exp_445–447)

The first systematic characterization of cross-line bigrams (LL of line N → LF of line N+1) reveals:

**Three rule classes:**

1. **Self-succession persists across lines**: Same onset class appearing at LL reliably predicts the same class at the next line's LF:
   - s→s: A=2.57×/N=8, B=2.20×/N=10 (most consistent cross-dialectally)
   - ch→ch: A=1.57×/N=28 (large N)
   - y→y: B=1.62×/N=8
   - A qot→qot: 3.10×/N=5
   - ok→y: A=1.52×/N=16, B=1.64×/N=21 (cross-dialectally stable; ok and y form a cross-line turn-taking pair)

2. **Cross-line inversion of within-line grammar**: Onset pairs depleted within a line (pre_LL→LL) become enriched at the cross-line boundary:
   - B sh→d: CL=1.58×/N=19 vs within-line pLL→LL=0.61× (strong inversion)
   - B d→qok: CL=1.56×/N=26 vs within=0.66× (strong inversion)
   - A ol→d: CL=1.67×/N=8 vs within=0.27× (strong inversion)
   - Interpretation: onset pairs that are incompatible within a line (avoid adjacency) are complementary as line-boundary pairs (one marks LL, the other marks the next LF)

3. **B-specific structural couplings**: d→sh (1.79×/N=16), l→sh (1.63×/N=19), d→qok (1.56×/N=26), qok→s (1.51×/N=31). These extend B's within-line formula grammar to cross-line context. B qok→s (1.51×/N=31) is section-specific: astro=52% (driven by astro's high qok density, F431) and pharma=42% (possible s-formula cycling connection).

**Cross-line grammar is CLASS-LEVEL**: ok→y and s→s cross-line pairs use unique word combinations (0% same-word in A; 0-10% in B). The coupling is at the onset-class level, not word-type-specific.

**Cross-line grammar is structurally different from within-line grammar**: The inversion pattern (depleted within → enriched cross-line) has no within-line analogue. This suggests paragraph-level sequential constraints that operate orthogonally to within-line grammar.

### 15.4 Suffix Positional Structure (exp_446)

Suffix types have weak positional preferences (E=1.5-2.2×) compared to onset-level positional grammar (E=3-7×):
- **B 'dy' suffix LF-enriched** (E=1.86×/N=60): B line-opening words (LF) disproportionately end in -dy
- **A 'iin' suffix LL-enriched** (E=2.23×/N=17): A-specific terminal -iin ending at LL
- **B 'shey' suffix LL-enriched** (E=2.17×/N=11, directional): B line-terminal -shey words
- Suffix class effects: edy-class, aiin-class, ol-class all E=0.83-1.10× by position — essentially flat
- Conclusion: VMS positional grammar is primarily encoded at the **onset class level**, with suffix type as a secondary (weak) correlate

### 15.5 Cross-Line Grammar Interpretation

The cross-line grammar findings extend the VMS structural characterization from within-line to paragraph-level:
- VMS writing is not line-independent: consecutive lines share onset-class memory
- The INVERSION pattern (within-depleted → cross-enriched) is consistent with a paragraph-level turn-taking structure: when onset A closes a line (LL), onset B that avoids A within the line now opens the next line, creating an alternating A/B pattern across line boundaries
- ok→y is the clearest example: ok and y are both body-class words in their respective positions, but ok-terminal → y-opening is a consistent cross-line rule in both dialects

## 16. PHASE 10: BODY-CONDITIONAL GRAMMAR AND VOCABULARY OVERLAP (exp_450–453, F439–F440)

### 16.1 sh→qok Body-Conditional Rule Confirmed at High N (exp_450, F372 confirmatory)

F372 (exp_370): B sh→qok E=1.96× is driven by 'she-' body sh-words (first body char='e') having qok-successor rate E=1.78–3.21×; non-she-body words (shol/shcthy/sho) at or below baseline.

exp_450 confirms at N=263: shedy(E=2.62×/N=76), shey(2.27×/N=33), sheedy(2.50×/N=16), shol(1.07×/N=4). Top-3 she-body words account for 47.5% of sh→qok pairs, proportional to their MID frequency. qok successor is distributed across 10+ types (class-level). A-dialect: 97.7% unique pairs (fully class-level). The body-class rule is confirmed.

### 16.2 Body-Conditional Succession Rules Are General: Two-Level VMS Grammar (F439, exp_451)

Within a given onset class, the first body character after the onset (which indexes the suffix class: 'a'=aiin-body, 'e'=edy-body, 'o'=ol/compound, 'c'=ch-compound) predicts the successor onset class. VMS has a **two-level succession grammar**:
- **Level 1**: Onset class (primary selector) — e.g., sh-onset words as a class elevate qok successors
- **Level 2**: Suffix body class (secondary conditional router) — e.g., within sh-onset, she-body words elevate qok; non-she-body words do not

**Strongest case — B qok body bifurcation (N=94/165):**
- qok+a-body (aiin-suffix: qokain/qokaiin, N=435): sh-successor rate 21.6% → E=1.73×; qok-self rate 5.3% → E=0.30×(depleted)
- qok+e-body (edy-suffix: qokeedy/qokedy, N=608): sh-successor rate 7.4% → E=0.59×(depleted); qok-self rate 27.1% → E=1.52×

**Complete within-class inversion**: same onset (qok), opposite successor routing by suffix body. Also confirmed: B ol body-split (bare→ch/k vs compound→qok), B ok+e-body→l (E=1.63×/N=26). A-dialect: e-body → qo elevated (E=1.66-1.83×) — A's qo plays the role B's qok plays in e-body routing.

**Mechanism implication**: suffix body participates in sequential word grammar, violating word-level independence at the suffix level. A word's morphological class influences the probability of the next word's onset class. Pure positional slot grammar (words selected independently by onset class) cannot generate body-conditional bigram rules.

### 16.3 A/B Vocabulary Overlap: Dialectal Variant Pattern Confirmed (F440, exp_452)

A and B share a core vocabulary but diverge in rare forms:
- **f≥20 words**: 100% of A's 64 high-frequency words appear in B; 82.6% of B's 121 high-frequency words appear in A
- **Hapaxes**: only 20.8% (A) / 13.8% (B) overlap — rare forms are largely dialect-specific
- **Token coverage**: 74.9% (A) / 67.7% (B) of tokens are shared-type words
- **l/r/p are near-B-exclusive** (Jaccard 0.024-0.082): B-specific pharma-l, LL-r, and p-formula classes are essentially absent from A
- **B-exclusive vocabulary is edy-class dominated**: qokeedy(248), qokedy(206), otedy(95), qotedy(57) — the top B-only high-freq words are all edy-suffix forms. A's distinctive vocabulary is ch-midfix-rich (cthol/cthy/chor: 44:1, 25:1, 6.6:1 A/B ratios).

**Interpretation**: Consistent with A and B being dialectal variants of a common system: shared core grammar + vocabulary, with dialect-specific suffix preferences generating divergent rare vocabulary. NOT consistent with completely independent systems (which would show <10% overlap at all frequency tiers).

### 16.4 B qok Chain Structure: Geometric Distribution, edy-Body Dominates Chains (exp_453)

B qok run-length distribution is perfectly geometric (L≥3: E=1.04×) despite the edy-body self-replication rule (F439). The self-replication manifests at the chain level, not run-length level:
- **edy-body words in chains (len≥2)**: 64.5% vs 43.0% in singletons → E=1.50×
- **edy+edy consecutive pairs**: 49.6% of chain continuations vs 18.5% expected if body-blind → E=2.68×
- **aiin-body words in chains**: 22.5% vs 42.6% in singletons → E=0.53×

The F439 body-conditional mechanism is confirmed as the driver of qok chain formation: edy-body qok words recruit edy-body successors, while aiin-body qok words terminate chains. The overall geometric distribution means the chain length is governed by the aggregate self-succession rate (p=0.850), not by super-geometric runaway.

A-dialect qo plays the same grammatical role that B-dialect qok plays in body-conditional routing (directional finding):

### 16.6 Cross-Line Body-Conditional Couplings: F441 (exp_455)

Body-conditional rules extend from within-line (F439) to the cross-line domain (LL word body → next line LF onset), but with DIFFERENT routing targets:

**aiin-body LL words are cross-line "bridge" words** (route to diverse LF classes):
- B d+aiin→sh CL: E=1.99×(N=14) — daiin/dar/dal/dam drive next-line sh
- B qok+aiin→s CL: E=1.76×(N=17) — routes to s (not sh, unlike within-line routing)
- B s+aiin→s CL: E=2.60×(N=7) — aiin-body drives s-self-succession cross-line
- B d+aiin→qok CL: E=1.68×(N=22) — secondary cross-line routing of d+aiin

**edy-body LL words are cross-line "self-replicators"** (drive same-class succession):
- A ch+edy→ch CL: E=2.79×(N=13) — A chedy words at LL drive ch→ch cross-line succession

**Critical finding — within-vs-cross routing inversion for qok+aiin:**
- Within-line MID (F439): qok+aiin → sh (E=1.73×)
- Cross-line LL→LF (F441): qok+aiin → s (E=1.76×), sh NOT elevated
- Same body class, different grammatical role by position context (MID vs LL→next-LF)

**Implication**: The two-level VMS succession grammar (onset class + body class) is not position-invariant. Body-class encodes grammatical function that varies by positional context.

### 16.8 edy-Body Cross-Line Routing Differs From Within-Line (exp_457, H_new_T/H_new_U)

Testing whether the within-line shedy→qok rule (F439) extends cross-line:
