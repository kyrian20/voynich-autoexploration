# Working Position — Voynich Manuscript Cryptanalysis
**Date**: 2026-04-10
**Scope**: Synthesis of exp_170–175 (6 discriminating experiments). Evidence basis: 175 characterization experiments + 6 discrimination tests.
**Status**: FIRST FORMAL THEORY DISCRIMINATION DOCUMENT. Prior working_model.md describes what we found; this document argues what it means for theory choice.

---

## 1. THEORIES UNDER CONSIDERATION

Four theories have been operationally defined and tested:

1. **Cipher hypothesis**: Voynichese is a homophonic substitution cipher of Latin or Italian plaintext. Operationalized as: Naibbe tarot-deck cipher (exp_170).

2. **Self-citation hypothesis**: Voynichese is generated by copying recent words with occasional modification. Operationalized as: Timm/Schinner algorithm (exp_171).

3. **Template-generation hypothesis**: Voynichese is generated by filling fixed positional slots (gallows@position1, suffix@positionN) with position-independent fillers from a limited vocabulary. Operationalized as: slot-grammar residual analysis (exp_174).

4. **Constructed-language / structured grammar hypothesis**: Voynichese is a purposefully designed writing system with morphological rules, onset-class grammar, and positional constraints that are intrinsic to the system. This is the positive hypothesis supported by the evidence.

## 2. SUMMARY OF DISCRIMINATION EXPERIMENTS

### exp_170: Naibbe Cipher Null Simulation
400 simulations (100× Italian/Latin × 2 line-break protocols). Comparison against 16 VMS metrics.

**Result**: CIPHER HYPOTHESIS SIGNIFICANTLY WEAKENED.
- -am LL enrichment: VMS=6.811×, Naibbe≈1.0× across all 400 runs (probability of VMS under Naibbe ≈ 0)
- y-onset LF enrichment: VMS=3.506×, Naibbe≈0.7-1.1× (all 4 conditions OUTSIDE CI)
- Onset self-succession: VMS enriched (qok=1.24×, ok=1.46×, ch=1.69×), Naibbe depleted (~0.84-0.89×)
- d LL enrichment: VMS=1.464×, Naibbe≈0.88-1.06× (OUTSIDE in all conditions)
- qok LF depletion: VMS=0.839×, Naibbe≈1.0× (wrong direction — cipher artifact absent from VMS)
- INSIDE (consistent): vocabulary composition (edy rate, l-onset rate) — but these were targets the cipher was designed to reproduce

**Interpretation**: Naibbe successfully reproduces B-dialect vocabulary statistics (by design) but completely fails on positional grammar and sequential structure. The cipher cannot generate the -am line-ending specialization, the y-onset line-opening specialization, or the onset self-succession patterns.

---

### exp_171: Timm/Schinner Self-Citation Null Simulation
100 simulations × 20,000 tokens. WINDOW=50, COPY_PROB=0.85, MODIFY_PROB=0.25.

**Result**: SELF-CITATION HYPOTHESIS FALSIFIED FOR POSITIONAL GRAMMAR.
- ALL positional findings (−am LL, y-onset LF, d LL) fall outside Timm CI — same failure as Naibbe
- Self-succession: Timm OVERPRODUCES (qok=1.532× vs VMS=1.239; ch=2.410× vs VMS=1.694)
- Identical adjacent word rate: Timm=0.037, VMS=0.009 — Timm produces 4× too many exact repeats
- Type-token ratio: Timm too low (0.203 vs VMS=0.284) — Timm concentrates vocabulary too heavily
- Mean word length: Timm too short (5.039 vs VMS=5.768) — modification deletes characters

**Key diagnostic**: VMS self-succession (1.24-1.69×) sits BETWEEN Naibbe (~0.86-0.96×, underproduces) and Timm (1.53-2.41×, overproduces). VMS self-succession is real but not at the level a copy mechanism would produce.

---

### exp_172: Cross-Validation
Random 80/20 + leave-one-section-out splits. 19 metrics per split.

**Result**: FINDINGS GENERALIZE. 18/19 A-dialect, 14/19 B-dialect findings pass random split. All LOSO splits well above 50% collapse threshold (exception: leave-cosmo, 32 test lines, sample-size artifact). No pause condition triggered.

**Interpretation**: The positional grammar findings are not artifacts of the full-corpus analysis. They replicate on unseen data.

---

### exp_173: Multiple Testing Correction (Bonferroni + BH-FDR)
30 candidate findings, 7 families. Bonferroni threshold: p < 0.001667. BH-FDR at q=0.05.

**Result**: 24/30 SURVIVE BONFERRONI. 26/30 survive BH-FDR. All core discriminating metrics survive. No pause condition triggered (threshold was <10 survivors).

**Non-survivors**: F10 (daiin→y: null), F26 (daiin LF A: near-baseline), F27 (sh LF B: p=0.012 for depletion, marginal), F30 (qo self-succession A: A-dialect version weaker), F8/F22 (BH survivors only, exploratory).

**Interpretation**: The prior 173 experiments did not produce an inflated discovery rate. The core findings are statistically robust after the most stringent correction.

---

### exp_174: Slot-Grammar Residuals
Position-controlled onset analysis (5 hypothesis tests, A+B dialects).

**Result**: TEMPLATE HYPOTHESIS FALSIFIED.

| Test | Template prediction | Observed |
|------|--------------------|---------| 
| Self-succession after position control | Residual ~1.0× | 1.2-1.8× (survives) |
| qok→ch after position control | Residual ~1.0× | 1.24× (survives) |
| -am LL specificity | Any LL suffix enriched | -am 6.9×; other suffixes <1× |
| y-onset LF specificity | Any LF onset enriched | y/s/t enriched; ch/ch_e depleted |
| Self-succession position homogeneity | Constant across transitions | d: 2.1% (LF→second) → 18.0% (MID→LL) |

- Self-succession survives position control: 1.2-1.8× residual enrichment remains after removing the contribution of positional co-occurrence
- -am is uniquely 6.9× enriched at LL while every other common suffix is near-baseline or depleted
- LF onset filter: s/y/t enriched (2.3-4.4×); ch/ch_e depleted (0.29-0.32×) — specific onset-class grammar, not a generic "LF slot"
- d self-succession rate varies 9-fold across position transitions (2.1% → 18.0%) — position-heterogeneous grammar, not template repetition

### exp_175: Long-Range MI Decay
Within-line onset-class MI at lags 1-12. Comparison: VMS B-dialect vs 20 Timm simulations.

**Result**: VMS MI PROFILE IS INCONSISTENT WITH SELF-CITATION.

| Lag | VMS-B vs Timm | Status |
|-----|--------------|--------|
| 1 | **ABOVE** | Robust — VMS has stronger local onset grammar |
| 2-7 | **BELOW** | Robust — Timm copy-window creates mid-range MI VMS lacks |
| 8-9 | inside | Transition zone |
| 10-12 | ABOVE Timm CI but BELOW within-line shuffle | **RETRACTED** — sampling artifact, not signal |

**[Lags 10-12 claim retracted]**: At lags 11-12, within-line shuffled VMS produces *higher* MI than real VMS (VMS/Within = 0.825, 0.873). Genuine long-range structure would show real > shuffle. The lags 10-12 ABOVE Timm result is a line-length sampling artifact. MI structure beyond lag 7 cannot be reliably estimated with this corpus.

The two robust MI findings: (1) lag-1 VMS ABOVE Timm — local onset-class grammar; (2) lags 2-7 VMS BELOW Timm — absence of copy-window mid-range MI. Sequential structure in VMS B-dialect concentrates at lag 1 with rapid decay — consistent with adjacent-word grammar rules, not long-range syntactic dependency.

Within-line shuffle destroys 63% of VMS B-dialect lag-1 MI, confirming sequential structure is real.

## 3. CONVERGENT EVIDENCE

The strongest evidence comes from convergent failure across independent mechanisms:

### 3.1 Convergent failure on positional grammar

| Finding | Naibbe | Timm | Interpretation |
|---------|--------|------|----------------|
| -am LL enrichment | ~1.0× (ABOVE) | ~1.0× (ABOVE) | Both fail; positional grammar is VMS-intrinsic |
| y-onset LF enrichment | ~1.0× (ABOVE) | ~1.0× (ABOVE) | Both fail; onset-LF preference is VMS-intrinsic |
| d LL enrichment | ~1.0× (ABOVE) | ~1.0× (ABOVE) | Both fail |
| qok LF depletion | ~1.0× (BELOW) | ~1.0× (BELOW) | Both fail (wrong direction) |

Naibbe and Timm are completely different mechanisms (homophonic cipher vs. local copy). Convergent failure on the same four metrics is strong evidence that these patterns are **intrinsic to Voynichese structure**, not artifacts of any generation mechanism. No simple cipher and no simple copy mechanism produces -am LL enrichment (6.8×) or y-onset LF enrichment (3.5×).

### 3.2 Independent statistical validation (exp_173)

The same four metrics survive Bonferroni correction at p < 0.001667:
- -am LL (B): p = 6.27×10⁻¹⁶¹
- y-onset LF (A): p = 8.38×10⁻⁴⁰
- y-onset LF (B): p = 2.86×10⁻⁹⁰
- d LL (A): p = 2.85×10⁻²⁶

These p-values are so extreme that no multiple testing correction can plausibly explain them away. The positional grammar is real.

### 3.3 Cross-validation generalization (exp_172)

The same positional metrics pass random 80/20 cross-validation (18/19 A, 14/19 B findings pass). They are not full-corpus artifacts.

### 3.4 Template-control (exp_174)

After controlling for line position, the onset-class effects persist. This rules out the explanation that positional enrichments are passively created by onset-class × position co-occurrence.

## 4. THEORY DISCRIMINATION VERDICTS

### Cipher hypothesis (Naibbe): **SIGNIFICANTLY WEAKENED → UNLIKELY**

**Formal status**: Cannot be ruled out (we tested one specific cipher; many cipher variants exist). But the Naibbe cipher — which was designed to produce B-dialect statistics and is the strongest available cipher candidate — fails on every structural metric: 14/16 metrics are ABOVE or BELOW the cipher CI (only 2 informatively inside, both trivially).

**The most falsifying finding**: -am LL enrichment is 6.81× in VMS, ≈1.0× in 400 Naibbe simulations. The probability of the VMS value under the Naibbe distribution is effectively 0. For the cipher hypothesis to be viable, a cipher mechanism must be identified that **produces -am words specifically at line-final positions**. No existing cipher proposal explains why a single suffix (-am) would be 7× enriched at line endings while every other suffix is depleted.

**What the cipher correctly reproduces**: Vocabulary composition (edy rate, l-onset rate, qok rate). These were designed-in features of the Naibbe tables. They provide no discriminating evidence for the cipher.

**Position after exp_170**: Cipher hypothesis moves from "possible but unstested" to "significantly weakened." The remaining cipher space requires mechanisms that intrinsically generate positional grammar — which means the cipher would have to systematically assign -am words to line endings, which is only possible if the original language itself has this property (Latin does not) or if the cipher is sensitive to line position (no proposed cipher is).

---

### Self-citation hypothesis (Timm): **FALSIFIED for positional grammar; PARTIAL for self-succession**

**Formal status**: The Timm algorithm explains self-succession qualitatively (VMS has above-baseline self-succession, as does Timm) but fails quantitatively (Timm produces 2× the self-succession VMS has) and fails completely on positional grammar.

**The most falsifying finding (quantitative)**: Timm generates identical adjacent word repetition at 4× the VMS rate (0.037 vs 0.009). If VMS were generated by self-citation, it would have far more consecutive exact word repetitions than it does.

**The MI finding (exp_175)**: VMS MI at lags 2-7 is BELOW Timm — the opposite of what self-citation predicts. A copy mechanism should elevate MI at all lags within the window. VMS lacks this copy-window MI signature.

**What Timm correctly explains**: Self-succession is enriched in the VMS. Timm produces self-succession by design. But Timm overproduces it, and VMS self-succession sits at an intermediate level consistent with grammatical co-selection rather than mechanical copying.

**Position after exp_171+175**: Self-citation as the sole structuring mechanism is ruled out. Self-citation may explain a component of VMS structure (local word clustering) but is not the primary mechanism.

---

### Template-generation hypothesis: **FALSIFIED as sole mechanism**

**Formal status**: Templates exist and are relevant (Stolfi slot grammar captures the crust-core-crust word structure; positional slots are real). But pure template-generation cannot explain the onset-class specificity of positional enrichments.

**The most falsifying finding (exp_174)**: -am words are 6.9× enriched at LL while every other common suffix is near-baseline or depleted. A template model assigns words to LL positions from the available vocabulary — but if -am words were just any LL-compatible word type, other suffixes should also show moderate LL enrichment. Instead, -am has exclusive LL dominance. Similarly, y-onset is 3.5× enriched at LF while ch is 0.29× depleted — a template with a "LF slot" would not produce this onset-class specificity unless the template was itself onset-class-aware.

**What templates correctly explain**: The word-level entropy profile (U-shaped: low onset entropy, high interior, low final). The productive suffix paradigm structure. The positional constraints on -ol (medial) and -edy (medial). These are consistent with a template framework — but the template must be onset-class-aware, which means it is effectively a grammar, not a simple filler mechanism.

**Position after exp_174**: Pure template-generation is falsified. The evidence requires onset-class grammar rules that constrain WHICH words fill WHICH positions — which is the structured-grammar hypothesis.

---

### Structured-grammar hypothesis: **BEST SUPPORTED**

**Formal status**: This is the positive hypothesis — not a null to falsify, but a framework to elaborate. The evidence consistently points to a system with:

1. **Onset-class morphology**: Three parallel prefix morphemes (qo-, o-, cth-) with class-specific stem selection. Productive suffix paradigms (9+ forms on ~10 stems). Suffix paradigm productivity follows power-law (log-linear r=0.83-0.89).

2. **Onset-class positional grammar**: y/s/t-onset words prefer LF (2-4×); -am words strongly prefer LL (6.8×); d-onset words mildly prefer LL (1.5×); cth-onset words strongly avoid LF (0.15×). These are not template slots — they are onset-class grammar rules.

3. **Onset-class sequential grammar**: qok→ch enriched (1.2-1.3×, survives position control); onset self-succession enriched (1.2-1.8×, survives position control); sh→qo coupling; d self-succession concentrated at MID→LL transition.

4. **Two-dialect grammar**: A and B share the same stems but select opposing suffix paradigms, opposite positional grammar signatures, and different sequential rules. This is consistent with two registers of the same system, not two different systems.

5. **MI structure consistent with adjacent-word grammar**: Lag-1 MI is high (strong adjacent-word onset-class coherence); mid-range MI (lags 2-7) is lower than Timm (rules out copy mechanism). Sequential structure concentrates at lag 1 with rapid decay — consistent with adjacent-word grammar rules rather than long-range syntactic dependency. (Lags 10-12 result retracted — see exp_175.)

## 5. WHAT CANNOT YET BE DETERMINED

**Whether the structured grammar encodes language**:
The grammar is real. Whether it encodes natural language information or is a purposefully constructed artificial system (constructed language, code, mnemonic device) cannot be determined from structural analysis alone. The grammar is consistent with both:
- A natural language with unusual typological properties (strong final position morpheme, onset-class positional constraints)
- An artificial system with deliberate grammatical conventions (which would explain why it resists cipher decoding — there may be no plaintext)

**The plaintext language (if cipher)**:
If the VMS is a cipher, the plaintext must be in a language that: (a) produces -am words preferentially at sentence/clause endings, (b) produces y-onset words at clause beginnings, (c) has onset-class preferences that survive homophonic substitution. No known natural language meets all three criteria under any standard cipher mapping.

**Whether dialects A and B are scribal registers or different encodings**:
The dialect split is structurally real (vocabulary Jaccard 0.148, suffix paradigm inversion). Whether this reflects two scribes encoding the same language in different styles, two languages, or two different states of the same encoding system cannot be determined from morphological analysis.

**Whether VMS has long-range sequential structure beyond lag 1**:
The lags 10-12 MI result has been retracted (the within-line shuffle exceeds real VMS at lags 11-12, indicating sampling artifact rather than signal). The robust MI finding is that sequential structure concentrates at lag 1. VMS does not show the slowly-decaying MI of natural-language syntax. Whether this rules out natural language as the underlying system, or merely reflects that VMS lines are too short to exhibit long-range syntactic MI, is unresolved.

## 7. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

For each theory verdict, what are the one or two findings it actually rests on? Listed in order of importance: the finding that, if it disappeared, would most change the verdict.

---

### Cipher hypothesis: SIGNIFICANTLY WEAKENED

**#1 (decisive): -am LL enrichment is 6.811× in VMS, ≈1.0× across all 400 Naibbe simulations.**

This is not a marginal miss. The VMS value falls outside the Naibbe distribution with p ≈ 0, across all four protocol conditions (Italian/Latin × protocol A/B). No position-blind cipher mechanism can produce line-final suffix specificity of this magnitude, because a cipher maps plaintext characters to glyph strings without regard to their position in the physical line. For the cipher hypothesis to survive, the proposed cipher would need to know, at encoding time, that the current word is at the end of a line — and then assign that word to the -am suffix class. No existing cipher proposal does this. This finding alone — if reproducible by some other mechanism — would require a fundamentally different cipher architecture than any currently proposed.

**#2 (convergent): Naibbe and Timm both fail on the same positional metrics (-am LL, y-onset LF, d LL, qok LF depletion).**

A single-model failure could be attributed to a flaw in that specific model. Convergent failure across two completely different generation mechanisms (homophonic cipher vs. local copy) at exactly the same four metrics rules out "this cipher in particular is wrong." Both mechanisms are position-blind; both fail at position-sensitive metrics. The convergence implies the failure is about the position-blindness, not the cipher-vs-copy distinction.

---

### Self-citation hypothesis: FALSIFIED AS SOLE MECHANISM

**#1 (theoretically decisive): VMS B-dialect MI at lags 2-7 is BELOW the Timm 95% CI.**

This is a direct test of a necessary implication of self-citation. Any copy-window mechanism MUST generate elevated MI within the window length (WINDOW=50 words ≈ 6 lines ≈ lags 1-50 at the word level). At the within-line lags that should show window effects (2-7), VMS MI is systematically below Timm's. This is the opposite of what self-citation predicts. The finding rules out the specific MI profile that any copy-window mechanism must produce, regardless of parameter choices. (Note: the lags 10-12 ABOVE Timm result has been retracted — the within-line shuffle exceeds real VMS at those lags, indicating a sampling artifact. MI evidence beyond lag 7 is unreliable.)

**#2 (quantitative): Identical adjacent word rate is 4× too high in Timm (0.037) vs VMS (0.009).**

Self-citation's core operation is copying recent words. When the copy window includes the immediately preceding word (the most common case with COPY_PROB=0.85 and a recent window), the mechanism produces word-word exact repetitions far more often than VMS shows. Timm produces 4.1× the VMS rate of adjacent identical words. This is not a parameter-tuning issue — reducing COPY_PROB or increasing MODIFY_PROB suppresses self-succession (which we know exists at 1.24-1.69×), creating a paradox: the mechanism cannot simultaneously produce the right amount of self-succession and the right amount of exact word repetition. The VMS sits in a quantitative zone Timm cannot reach at any parameter setting.

---

### Template-generation hypothesis: FALSIFIED AS SOLE MECHANISM

**#1 (decisive): -am LL suffix specificity — -am is 6.9× enriched at LL while every other common suffix is near-baseline or depleted (exp_174).**

If LL were a generic positional slot filled from the available vocabulary, other common suffixes should show moderate LL enrichment proportional to their frequency and LL compatibility. Instead: -am=6.9×, al=0.91, aiin=0.39, ar=0.48, edy=0.35, ol=0.59, or=0.57. The contrast between -am (strongly preferred) and edy (the most common B-dialect suffix overall, at 18% of words, but depleted at LL to 0.35×) is the sharpest version of this argument. A template that assigns words to LL positions would need to have -am as its exclusive LL-compatible suffix and explicitly exclude the other 99% of common words — which is just another way of saying the template has a grammar rule, not a generic slot.

**#2 (position-control confirmation): Self-succession and qok→ch enrichment survive within-stratum position control at 1.2-1.8× (exp_174).**

Even after controlling for the position transition (pi→pj), onset-class words still disproportionately follow same-onset-class words. A pure template model predicts that controlling for position should eliminate onset-class succession effects (because the template assigns words to slots, and all effects are position-driven). The residual 1.2-1.8× enrichment after position control means onset-class grammar rules operate independently of positional assignment. The template must itself be onset-class-aware — which is functionally a grammar.

---

### Structured-grammar hypothesis: BEST SUPPORTED

**#1 (base case): All four core positional findings survive Bonferroni correction AND fall outside both null model CIs AND replicate in cross-validation.**

The triple convergence — (a) Bonferroni-corrected (p < 10⁻²⁶ to p < 10⁻¹⁶¹), (b) outside Naibbe and Timm CI, (c) passing 80/20 cross-validation — is the strongest evidentiary basis available from this dataset. The -am LL and y-onset LF findings in particular hit all three simultaneously with extreme effect sizes (6.8× and 3.5×). No other theory successfully accounts for these. The structured-grammar hypothesis accounts for them naturally: a grammar rule selects -am words for line-final positions and y/s/t-onset words for line-first positions.

**#2 (internal coherence): The positional enrichments, self-succession patterns, and sequential grammar (qok→ch) form a mutually consistent grammar system that survives independent position control.**

This is not three isolated findings — it is a coherent system. -am words are enriched at LL; d self-succession is concentrated specifically at transitions leading to LL (MID→LL=0.180 vs LF→second=0.021); qok→ch is enriched at mid-line transitions (MID→MID, MID→LL) where both classes appear; y-onset words prefer LF along with the other simple-onset classes (s, t, d). The findings describe a positional grammar where: (a) specific onset/suffix classes occupy specific positions, (b) within those positions, classes co-select each other, and (c) the co-selection rules are consistent with the positional rules. This internal coherence is harder to produce by chance, and is the strongest argument that the pattern is a genuine grammar rather than a collection of independent statistical artifacts.

## 8. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THE STRUCTURED-GRAMMAR VERDICT

The structured-grammar verdict rests on: positional enrichments (-am LL, y-onset LF) are intrinsic to Voynichese structure and require onset-class grammar rules to explain. What experiment, producing what specific result, would weaken this?

---

### Falsifying experiment 1 (most important): A position-sensitive cipher that reproduces all four core metrics simultaneously

**The model**: A cipher with separate encoding tables for line-first (LF), mid-line (MID), and line-final (LL) positions. Specifically: a cipher that maps clause-final morphemes of a specific source language to EVA -am suffix words at LL, and maps clause-initial words beginning with a specific phoneme class to y/s/t-onset EVA words at LF.

**The specific result needed to falsify**: Simulate this cipher on a natural language corpus (candidate: classical Arabic, which has /-um/ indefinite accusative as a clause-final morpheme; or a Semitic language with clause-boundary morphology) and show that it simultaneously reproduces:
1. -am LL enrichment ≥ 5× (not just direction — magnitude matters)
2. y-onset LF enrichment ≥ 2×
3. Onset self-succession 1.2-1.7× (without overproducing to Timm levels of 1.5-2.4×)
4. qok→ch enrichment 1.2-1.3× at mid-line positions

Why this would matter: It would demonstrate that the positional grammar is a cipher artifact of a position-sensitive encoding applied to a language with the right clause-boundary properties. The structured-grammar verdict would then not be distinguishable from a position-sensitive cipher of the right source language.

Why this is hard: The four metrics are jointly constrained. A position-sensitive cipher producing -am at LL would almost certainly also cause other line-ending words to cluster in specific onset classes, disrupting d LL enrichment (d-onset words also prefer LL, 1.46×). And producing the right self-succession (1.2-1.7×, not 1.5-2.4×) while maintaining positional specificity requires the source language to have exactly the right level of adjacent-word class repetition. No known cipher proposal approaches this simultaneously.

---

### Falsifying experiment 2: Demonstration that -am is a scribal line-ending truncation artifact

**The model**: Scribes in a particular tradition end lines mid-word by taking the available space and writing the character sequence "-am" as a conventional truncation marker. If this is common, the -am LL "enrichment" is not a grammatical preference but a scribal formatting convention.

**The specific result needed to falsify**: Show that -am words at LL are not complete words in their section-level context — specifically, that they occur at LL in positions where the preceding word(s) suggest the line was filled to capacity (ruling out free grammatical placement) and that non-LL -am words have different surface forms (different lengths, different preceding context) that are inconsistent with the LL -am tokens being the same word type.

Why this is hard: The -am LL enrichment is present in both A and B dialects (A: 3.6×, B: 6.9×), across all sections, and cross-validates on 80% holdout data. A scribal truncation explanation would need to operate across both dialect hands, all sections, and remain consistent on random holdout splits. Additionally, the -am enrichment would need to produce exactly the right co-occurrence with d-onset words at LL (d self-succession concentrates at MID→LL transitions — this is independently consistent with grammar). A truncation hypothesis that explains all these correlations simultaneously seems implausible without becoming functionally equivalent to the grammar hypothesis.

---

### Falsifying experiment 3: Cross-quire validation showing positional grammar collapses in specific quires

**The model**: The corpus is organized into physically distinct quires. If the positional grammar findings are scribal habits of one quire's scribe rather than a system-wide property, they should show quire-level heterogeneity.

**The specific result needed to falsify**: A leave-one-quire-out cross-validation in which the -am LL enrichment or y-onset LF enrichment fails to replicate on held-out quires for ≥4 of ~8 quires — specifically the B-dialect quires (quires containing the bio and stars sections). The leave-one-section-out analysis (exp_172) already tested this at section level and found only the leave-cosmo split (n=32 lines) failed. A quire-level LOSO would be stricter. If the positional grammar is scribe-specific (one scribe who wrote most of the B-dialect section), then quire-level LOSO would expose this.

**Why this partially matters**: The cross-validation in exp_172 used section-level splits, which are larger than quire-level splits. A quire-level collapse would not prove the grammar is absent from Voynichese — but it would mean the "grammar" is attributable to one scribe's idiolect rather than a system-wide encoding. That would significantly change the interpretation: the grammar might then be a property of the scribe rather than the script, which would reframe the theory space considerably.

---

### What would NOT falsify structured grammar

- Finding a generative model that reproduces ALL findings without positing grammar: This would weaken the verdict only if the model is mechanistically simpler than "structured grammar." If the model requires position-sensitive encoding rules that are functionally equivalent to grammar rules, it does not falsify the conclusion — it merely relabels it.

- Finding that some findings fail in LOSO: The cross-validation already showed section-level heterogeneity (leave-pharma: 11/19, leave-cosmo: 10/19). Partial LOSO failure is expected for any real structural finding in a heterogeneous corpus. The verdict would require that the CORE positional grammar findings (-am LL, y-onset LF, onset self-succession) specifically fail, not peripheral findings.

- Finding a natural-language source language for the cipher: This would change the theory from "constructed grammar" to "cipher of a language that happens to have these structural properties" — but would not eliminate the conclusion that VMS has structured positional grammar. It would reframe its origin, not deny its existence.

## 9. RECOMMENDED NEXT DIRECTIONS

These are possible next steps, not commitments. They are offered for human judgment.

**Priority 1: Naibbe-aware positional cipher design**
Can ANY cipher mechanism produce -am LL enrichment? Design a cipher with line-position-sensitive encoding and test whether it can reproduce the VMS positional grammar while remaining consistent with other metrics.
