## TERMINOLOGY NOTE

Throughout this document:
- **"position-blind cipher"** refers to any homophonic substitution cipher that maps plaintext characters to EVA glyph strings without using information about the word's position within a physical line (e.g., Naibbe, Timm). This is the class of cipher tested in exp_170 and exp_171.
- **"position-sensitive cipher"** refers to a cipher that uses a different encoding table for LF, MID, and LL positions (tested in exp_177). No published position-sensitive cipher for VMS exists.
- **"cipher hypothesis"** refers to the class of homophonic substitution ciphers. Verdicts on "cipher" refer specifically to position-blind ciphers unless otherwise noted.

## 1. THEORIES UNDER CONSIDERATION

Four theories have been operationally defined and tested:

1. **Position-blind cipher hypothesis**: Voynichese is a homophonic substitution cipher of Latin or Italian plaintext, where each plaintext character maps to EVA glyph strings without regard to line position. Operationalized as: Naibbe tarot-deck cipher (exp_170).

2. **Self-citation hypothesis**: Voynichese is generated by copying recent words with occasional modification. Operationalized as: Timm/Schinner algorithm (exp_171, exp_176).

3. **Template-generation hypothesis**: Voynichese is generated by filling fixed positional slots (gallows@position1, suffix@positionN) with position-independent fillers from a limited vocabulary. Operationalized as: slot-grammar residual analysis (exp_174).

4. **Structured grammar hypothesis**: Voynichese is a purposefully designed writing system with morphological rules, onset-class grammar, and positional constraints that are intrinsic to the system. This is the positive hypothesis that has survived discrimination testing by elimination of the first three. It does not imply natural language, decipherability, or any specific origin.

## 2. SUMMARY OF DISCRIMINATION EXPERIMENTS

### exp_170: Naibbe Cipher Null Simulation
400 simulations (100× Italian/Latin × 2 line-break protocols). Comparison against 16 VMS B-dialect metrics.

**Result**: POSITION-BLIND CIPHER HYPOTHESIS SIGNIFICANTLY WEAKENED.
- -am LL enrichment: VMS=6.811×, Naibbe≈1.0× across all 400 runs
- y-onset LF enrichment: VMS=3.506×, Naibbe≈0.7-1.1× (all 4 conditions OUTSIDE CI)
- Onset self-succession: VMS enriched (qok=1.24×, ok=1.46×, ch=1.69×), Naibbe depleted (~0.84-0.89×)
- d LL enrichment: VMS=1.464×, Naibbe≈0.88-1.06× (OUTSIDE in all conditions)
- qok LF depletion: VMS=0.839×, Naibbe≈1.0× (wrong direction — cipher artifact absent from VMS)
- INSIDE (consistent): vocabulary composition (edy rate, l-onset rate) — targets the cipher was designed to reproduce; not independent evidence

**Interpretation**: Naibbe reproduces B-dialect vocabulary composition (by design) but completely fails on positional grammar and sequential structure.

**A-dialect extension (exp_178)**: Naibbe output compared against A-dialect targets (re-analysis of exp_170 data). Result: 7/8 A-dialect positional/sequential metrics ABOVE in all four conditions. The positional grammar failure is dialect-independent. Caveat: Naibbe was designed for B-dialect; comparison against A-dialect is exploratory. A definitive A-dialect null simulation would require an A-calibrated cipher, which does not exist in published literature.

---

### exp_171: Timm/Schinner Self-Citation Null Simulation
100 simulations × 20,000 tokens. WINDOW=50, COPY_PROB=0.85, MODIFY_PROB=0.25.

**Result**: SELF-CITATION HYPOTHESIS NOT VIABLE AS SOLE MECHANISM.
- ALL positional findings (−am LL, y-onset LF, d LL) fall outside Timm CI — same failure as Naibbe
- Self-succession: Timm OVERPRODUCES (qok=1.532× vs VMS=1.239; ch=2.410× vs VMS=1.694)
- Identical adjacent word rate: Timm=0.037, VMS=0.009 — Timm produces 4× too many exact repeats
- Type-token ratio: Timm too low (0.203 vs VMS=0.284)
- Mean word length: Timm too short (5.039 vs VMS=5.768)

**Key diagnostic**: VMS self-succession (1.24-1.69×) sits BETWEEN Naibbe (~0.86-0.96×) and Timm (1.53-2.41×). VMS self-succession is real but not at the level a copy mechanism produces.

### exp_172: Cross-Validation
Random 80/20 + leave-one-section-out splits. 19 metrics per split.

**Result**: FINDINGS GENERALIZE. 18/19 A-dialect, 14/19 B-dialect findings pass random split. All LOSO splits well above 50% threshold (exception: leave-cosmo, n=32 lines, sample-size artifact). The positional grammar findings are not artifacts of the full-corpus analysis.

### exp_174: Slot-Grammar Residuals
Position-controlled onset analysis.

**Result**: TEMPLATE HYPOTHESIS NOT VIABLE AS SOLE MECHANISM.
- Self-succession survives position control: 1.2-1.8× residual enrichment remains after removing positional co-occurrence contribution
- -am is uniquely 6.9× enriched at LL; every other common suffix is near-baseline or depleted
- y/s/t enriched at LF (2.3-4.4×); ch/ch_e depleted at LF (0.29-0.32×) — onset-class grammar, not generic positional slot
- d self-succession rate varies 9-fold across position transitions (2.1% LF→second → 18.0% MID→LL) — position-heterogeneous grammar, not template repetition

---

### exp_175: Long-Range MI Decay
Within-line onset-class MI at lags 1-12. Comparison: VMS B-dialect vs 20 Timm simulations.

**Result**: VMS MI PROFILE INCONSISTENT WITH SELF-CITATION. Two robust findings; lags 10-12 retracted.

| Lag | VMS-B vs Timm | Status |
|-----|--------------|--------|
| 1 | **ABOVE** | Robust — VMS has stronger local onset grammar than Timm |
| 2-7 | **BELOW** | Robust — Timm copy-window creates mid-range MI VMS lacks |
| 8-9 | inside | Transition zone |
| 10-12 | ABOVE Timm CI, BELOW within-line shuffle | **RETRACTED** — sampling artifact |

**Retraction of lags 10-12**: At lags 11-12, within-line shuffled VMS produces *higher* MI than real VMS (VMS/Within = 0.825, 0.873). Genuine long-range sequential structure would show real > shuffle. The lags 10-12 finding is a line-length sampling artifact, not a signal. MI structure beyond lag 7 cannot be reliably estimated from this corpus. (Retraction confirmed by independent Opus review and synthesis audit.)

The two robust MI findings: (1) lag-1 VMS ABOVE Timm — local onset-class grammar; (2) lags 2-7 VMS BELOW Timm — absence of copy-window mid-range MI. Within-line shuffle destroys 63% of lag-1 MI, confirming sequential structure at lag 1 is real.

---

### exp_176: Timm Parameter Sensitivity Sweep (Phase 3)
75 parameter combinations (COPY_PROB × MODIFY_PROB × WINDOW), 20 simulations each. 1,500 total simulations.

**Result**: TIMM FALSIFICATION IS PARAMETER-ROBUST.
- 8/75 combinations meet viability criteria on adj_rate + self-succession
- **0/75 combinations approach positional metrics**: max -am LL = 0.572× (VMS: 6.811×); max y-onset LF = 0.526× (VMS: 3.506×)
- The 8 viable combinations (those that don't over-repeat) perform WORST on positional metrics
- A fundamental trade-off exists: lower COPY_PROB → lower adj_rate BUT lower ss_mean, so no parameter setting simultaneously satisfies adj_rate ≤ VMS AND ss_mean ≥ VMS AND positional metrics ≈ VMS

**Implication**: The Timm falsification for positional grammar is not a parameter-tuning artifact. It is inherent to any position-blind copy mechanism.

---

### exp_177: Position-Sensitive Cipher Simulation (Phase 3)
Polyalphabetic cipher with separate LF/MID/LL encoding tables. Latin source (Cicero De Officiis). Three calibration levels. 100 simulations each.

**Result**: PARTIAL REPRODUCTION. Position-sensitive encoding substantially exceeds position-blind baselines but does not reach VMS levels.

| Metric | VMS (B) | Cipher-strong | Cipher-moderate | Cipher-weak | Timm/Naibbe |
|--------|---------|---------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|
| -am LL | 6.811   | 5.756         | 4.715           | 3.117       | ~1.0        |
| y LF   | 3.506   | 2.395         | 1.950           | 1.509       | ~1.0        |
| d LL   | 1.464   | 1.831†        | 1.483 ≈         | 1.196†      | ~1.0        |
| ss_mean| 1.465   | 0.999         | 0.997           | 0.998       | 1.5-2.4     |

† indicates cipher overproduces (d LL at strong) or underproduces (d LL at weak) relative to VMS CI.

**Calibration note — target-aware design (important limitation)**: The LL table was explicitly target-aware: ALL Latin clause-final morphemes (-um, -us, -em, -is, -ae, -orum, -bus, etc.) were mapped to EVA -am specifically, not to corresponding EVA suffix classes. The code comment labels -am as "the target LL suffix." The LL onset weights were similarly calibrated to VMS LL onset distributions. The LF y-onset mapping was also designed knowing y-onset is the VMS LF target. This means exp_177 demonstrates that a maximally-calibrated position-sensitive cipher can approach -am LL enrichment of 5.756×; it does NOT demonstrate that a naive position-sensitive cipher applied to Latin would do so.

**Key finding**: Position-sensitive encoding is a necessary condition for producing the VMS positional grammar pattern. The jump from position-blind (~1×) to position-sensitive (3.1-5.8×) is the largest single explanatory step even with target-aware calibration — removing position-sensitivity entirely collapses the metric to ~1× regardless of calibration.

**Self-succession not reproduced**: All calibrations produce ss_mean ≈ 1.00 (VMS: 1.465). Substitution alone cannot produce word-class sequential memory — self-succession requires either a copy mechanism or genuine sequential structure in the plaintext.

**Implication for theory discrimination**: Position-sensitive ciphers with appropriate target mapping can approach but not reach VMS -am LL levels. They remain an unruled-out hypothesis class, but any viable position-sensitive cipher must explain both the positional grammar AND the self-succession — two independent constraints the tested mechanism cannot jointly satisfy.

### 3.2 What position-sensitive encoding achieves (exp_177)

The position-sensitive cipher test (exp_177) establishes a critical benchmark. At strong calibration (90% probability for clause-final morpheme detection), it achieves -am LL = 5.756× (vs VMS 6.811×, vs position-blind max 0.572×). This means:
- Position-sensitivity is necessary to approach VMS positional grammar
- Position-blindness alone can explain ~1× of the observed 6.8× enrichment
- The remaining 5-6× requires either position-sensitive encoding or intrinsic positional grammar

### 3.3 Independent statistical validation (exp_173, dual Bonferroni)

All four core positional metrics survive both Bonferroni thresholds (m=30 and m=175):

## 4. THEORY DISCRIMINATION VERDICTS

### Position-blind cipher hypothesis: SIGNIFICANTLY WEAKENED

**Formal status**: The class of position-blind ciphers (of which Naibbe is the strongest available representative) fails on every structural metric. A position-sensitive cipher is not ruled out and shows partial reproduction of the VMS positional grammar (exp_177).

**The decisive finding**: -am LL enrichment is 6.811× in VMS, ≈1.0× across all position-blind simulations (400 Naibbe runs, 1,500 Timm runs across 75 parameter combinations). The maximum any position-blind mechanism achieves is 0.572× — 12× below VMS. No parameter tuning can overcome this failure (exp_176).

**What position-sensitive ciphers achieve**: At strong calibration (90%), a position-sensitive cipher reaches 5.756× on -am LL (exp_177). This is substantially closer to VMS. A position-sensitive cipher is therefore not ruled out on positional grammar metrics alone. However, such a cipher: (a) fails completely on self-succession (ss_mean ≈ 1.0 vs VMS 1.465), (b) underproduces exact word repetition (adj_rate 0.003 vs VMS 0.009), and (c) has no published VMS proposal.

**The remaining cipher space**: Any viable cipher hypothesis for VMS must be position-sensitive, must produce 6.8× -am LL enrichment (stronger than what the Cicero Latin calibration achieves at 90%), and must simultaneously produce VMS-level self-succession without a copy mechanism. This is a narrowly constrained space that no existing cipher proposal occupies.

**What the position-blind cipher correctly reproduces**: Vocabulary composition (edy rate, l-onset rate) — designed-in features of the Naibbe tables. These provide no independent discriminating evidence.

---

### Self-citation hypothesis: NOT VIABLE AS SOLE MECHANISM

**Formal status**: The Timm algorithm fails on positional grammar (like Naibbe), overproduces self-succession (2× too high), and overproduces exact word repetition (4× too high). The parameter sweep (exp_176) confirms no parameter setting resolves the joint constraint failure. Self-citation is not the sole structuring mechanism.

**The decisive finding (quantitative)**: Timm generates identical adjacent word repetition at 4× the VMS rate at the default parameters. At parameters that lower adj_rate toward VMS levels (high MODIFY_PROB, low COPY_PROB), ss_mean drops far below VMS. The two constraints pull in opposite directions and cannot be jointly satisfied.

**The MI finding (exp_175, robust component)**: VMS MI at lags 2-7 is BELOW Timm. Any copy-window mechanism must generate elevated MI within the window. VMS lacks this signature. (Note: lags 10-12 ABOVE Timm result retracted — within-line shuffle exceeds real VMS at those lags. Only lags 1 and 2-7 are robust.)

**What Timm correctly explains**: Self-succession is enriched in VMS. Timm produces self-succession by design. But Timm overproduces it, and VMS self-succession sits at an intermediate level (1.24-1.69×) consistent with grammatical co-selection rather than mechanical copying.

---

### Template-generation hypothesis: NOT VIABLE AS SOLE MECHANISM

**Formal status**: Templates exist and are relevant (Stolfi slot grammar captures the crust-core-crust word structure). But pure template-generation cannot explain the onset-class specificity of positional enrichments.

**The decisive finding**: -am words are 6.9× enriched at LL while every other common suffix is near-baseline or depleted (edy=0.35×, al=0.91×, ol=0.59×). A generic LL slot would not produce this contrast. The slot must have onset-class grammar rules — which is functionally the structured-grammar hypothesis.

**What templates correctly explain**: Word-level entropy profile, productive suffix paradigm structure, positional constraints on -ol (medial) and -edy (medial). These are consistent with a template framework — but the template must be onset-class-aware.

---

### Structured grammar hypothesis: SURVIVING BY ELIMINATION

**Formal status**: This is the positive hypothesis that survives after the three null hypotheses are unable to account for the core positional and sequential structure findings. The verdict rests on elimination of tested alternatives, not on direct confirmation of a specific generative grammar.

**The two remaining hypotheses after exp_179**:

1. **(a) Intrinsic structured grammar**: Voynichese has genuine grammatical rules assigning specific onset/suffix classes to specific line positions and specifying sequential word-class preferences. This is the simplest uneliminated mechanism.

2. **(b) A position-aware hybrid**: A cipher that uses separate encoding tables for LF/MID/LL positions AND selectively restricts copying to non-LL positions (so LL words always come from the cipher table). This would decouple ss_mean (driven by MID copies) from am_ll (protected by LL-exclusive cipher application). This architecture was not tested in exp_179, which used position-blind copying. However, a mechanism that reserves LL positions for cipher output is functionally equivalent to a grammar rule specifying LL word class — making hypothesis (b) a relabeling of hypothesis (a) rather than a genuinely distinct alternative.

These two frameworks converge: any mechanism that jointly satisfies both properties must implement the same structural constraints as a grammar regardless of its mechanical origin.

**What the evidence establishes**:
1. **Onset-class positional grammar**: y/s/t-onset words prefer LF (2-4×); -am words strongly prefer LL (6.8×); d-onset words mildly prefer LL (1.5×); cth-onset words strongly avoid LF (0.15×). Bonferroni-corrected, cross-validated, not reproduced by position-blind mechanisms.
2. **Onset-class sequential grammar**: qok→ch enriched (1.2-1.3×, survives position control); onset self-succession enriched (1.2-1.8×, survives position control).
3. **Two-dialect grammar**: A and B share the same stems but select opposing suffix paradigms and different sequential rules. Consistent with two registers of the same system.
4. **Sequential structure concentrates at lag 1**: Within-line shuffle destroys 63% of lag-1 MI. MI profile inconsistent with copy mechanism (lags 2-7 BELOW Timm). No long-range MI structure confirmed beyond lag 7.

**What the evidence does NOT establish** (see Limitations section):
- That Voynichese encodes a natural language
- That a specific source language exists
- Which subtype of structured grammar (constructed language, code, mnemonic, cipher of a language with unusual morphology)
- That position-sensitive ciphers are ruled out
- That any structural claim extends to semantic or semantic-level organization

## 5. EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS: WHAT THIS ANALYSIS DOES NOT CLAIM

1. **No decipherment**: This analysis identifies structural regularities. It does not decode any VMS text, identify any word's meaning, or establish correspondence between EVA tokens and natural language words. The structured grammar verdict is a characterization of the writing system's statistical structure, not a translation.

2. **No source language identification**: The analysis does not identify the source language, if any. The cipher hypothesis space is narrowed to position-sensitive mechanisms — but no specific natural language has been shown to produce the VMS positional grammar when run through any cipher.

3. **No discrimination among structured grammar subtypes**: The "structured grammar" verdict does not distinguish between: (a) a constructed/artificial language, (b) a cipher of a language with unusual clause-boundary morphology, (c) a mnemonic/memory system, (d) a musical or algorithmic notation, or (e) other structured symbol systems. The evidence is consistent with all of these.

4. **No validity claim for untested cipher mechanisms**: The analysis tests Naibbe (homophonic, position-blind) and a calibrated position-sensitive cipher simulation. Many cipher mechanisms are untested: running-key ciphers, transposition ciphers, Polybius squares, route ciphers, null ciphers, Steganographic systems, and others. The verdict is specifically that position-blind homophonic ciphers of Latin or Italian are insufficient — not that ciphers in general are impossible.

5. **No semantic coherence claim**: The analysis is restricted to surface statistical properties of glyph tokens (onset class, suffix type, line position, adjacency). No claim is made about semantic organization, information content, or whether the writing carries meaningful information. A system with structured positional grammar could in principle be meaningless (e.g., a ritual or decorative script), partially meaningful, or fully meaningful.

## 6. WHAT CANNOT YET BE DETERMINED

**Whether the structured grammar encodes language**: The grammar is real. Whether it encodes natural language information or is a purposefully constructed artificial system cannot be determined from structural analysis alone.

**The plaintext language (if cipher)**: If VMS is a position-sensitive cipher, the plaintext must be in a language that produces clause-final morphemes concentrated enough to generate 6.8× -am LL enrichment. Latin Cicero reaches only 5.8× at 90% detection probability. The plaintext language would need higher morphological density than classical Latin prose.

**Whether dialects A and B are scribal registers or different encodings**: The dialect split is structurally real (vocabulary Jaccard 0.148, suffix paradigm inversion). A and B cannot yet be attributed to scribes, sections, or encoding changes.

**Whether VMS has long-range sequential structure beyond lag 1**: The lags 10-12 MI result has been retracted. No reliable evidence for sequential structure beyond lag 7 is available from this corpus. Whether this rules out natural language as the underlying system, or merely reflects that VMS lines are too short, is unresolved.

**Whether A-dialect null simulations would falsify position-blind ciphers**: The Naibbe A-dialect comparison (exp_178) is exploratory because Naibbe is a B-dialect cipher. A definitive A-dialect test requires an A-calibrated cipher, which does not exist in published literature. The exploratory comparison shows 7/8 positional metrics fail in all conditions, consistent with the mechanism-level failure being dialect-independent.

## 8. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

### Position-blind cipher hypothesis: SIGNIFICANTLY WEAKENED

**#1 (decisive against ALL tested cipher mechanisms): Onset self-succession is 1.465× in VMS and is reproduced by no tested mechanism at VMS levels.**

| Mechanism | ss_mean | Direction | Status |
|-----------|---------|-----------|--------|
| Naibbe (position-blind cipher) | ~0.86-0.96× | depleted | OUTSIDE — underproduces |
| Timm (copy, low COPY_PROB) | ~1.1× | marginal | OUTSIDE — underproduces |
| Timm (copy, high COPY_PROB) | ~1.5-2.4× | enriched | OUTSIDE — overproduces |
| Exp_177 (position-sensitive cipher) | ~1.00× | baseline | OUTSIDE — not reproduced at all |
| **VMS target** | **1.465×** | enriched | — |

Self-succession is uniquely diagnostic because it is the one metric that falsifies both cipher classes simultaneously. Position-blind ciphers underproduce it; position-sensitive substitution ciphers produce no enrichment (ss_mean ≈ 1.0); Timm overproduces it at parameter settings that produce any self-succession at all. No tested mechanism lands in the VMS range (1.2-1.7×). Unlike -am LL enrichment (which a target-calibrated position-sensitive cipher can approach at 5.756×), self-succession has no tested mechanism that produces it at VMS levels. A viable VMS generation model must include a component that produces moderate word-class sequential memory — neither too much (like Timm copying) nor too little (like independent word selection).

**#2 (decisive against position-blind ciphers specifically): -am LL enrichment is 6.811× in VMS, ≈1.0× across all 1,900 position-blind simulations; a target-calibrated position-sensitive cipher reaches 5.756×.**

-am LL enrichment rules out all position-blind mechanisms cleanly: the maximum any position-blind cipher achieves is 0.572× (Timm, 75 parameter combinations). However, exp_177 showed that a position-sensitive cipher with deliberate target-aware calibration (all Latin clause-final morphemes → EVA -am) achieves 5.756×. This weakens -am LL as a discriminator against position-sensitive ciphers specifically. It remains fully decisive against position-blind mechanisms. The metric is now interpreted as: position-sensitivity is a necessary condition for approaching VMS -am LL enrichment; it is not in itself a sufficient discriminator between position-sensitive cipher and intrinsic grammar.

**#3 (convergent): Naibbe and Timm both fail on the same four positional metrics despite being completely different mechanisms, and the Timm failure is parameter-robust (exp_176).**

Convergent failure across a homophonic cipher and a local-copy algorithm at exactly the same four metrics rules out mechanism-specific explanations. The parameter sweep (75 Timm combinations) confirms this is not a parameter-tuning artifact. The convergence points to position-blindness as the common failure cause.

---

### Self-citation hypothesis: NOT VIABLE AS SOLE MECHANISM

**#1 (quantitative): Timm produces identical adjacent word repetition at 4× the VMS rate, and no parameter combination resolves this while preserving VMS-level self-succession (exp_176).**

The two VMS constraints (adj_rate ≤ 0.009, ss_mean ≥ 1.465) pull in opposite directions in Timm's parameter space. Lower COPY_PROB reduces adj_rate but also reduces ss_mean far below VMS. No parameter combination reaches both simultaneously. This is a structural incompatibility, not a parameter-tuning problem.

**#2 (MI profile): VMS MI at lags 2-7 is BELOW Timm's 95% CI — the opposite of what any copy-window mechanism must produce.**

Any copy-window mechanism elevates MI within the window. At within-line lags 2-7, VMS MI is below Timm's. This rules out the specific MI signature that self-citation must leave. (Note: lags 10-12 finding retracted — within-line shuffle exceeds real VMS at those lags, ruling out genuine long-range structure. Only lags 1 and 2-7 are robust.)

---

### Template-generation hypothesis: NOT VIABLE AS SOLE MECHANISM

**#1 (decisive): -am LL suffix specificity — -am is 6.9× enriched at LL while edy (the most common B-suffix at 18% overall) is 0.35× depleted at LL (exp_174).**

A template that assigns words to LL positions from the available vocabulary would not produce this contrast unless the template explicitly excludes edy-suffix words from LL — which is a grammar rule, not a generic slot.

**#2: Self-succession survives position control at 1.2-1.8× (exp_174).**

After controlling for position transition, onset-class sequential effects persist. A pure template model predicts they should disappear. Residual 1.2-1.8× enrichment means onset-class grammar rules operate independently of positional assignment.

---

### Structured grammar hypothesis: SURVIVING BY ELIMINATION

**#1 (base case): All four core positional findings survive both Bonferroni thresholds AND fall outside position-blind simulation CIs AND replicate in cross-validation.**

The triple convergence — Bonferroni m=175, outside Naibbe and Timm CI, passing 80/20 cross-validation — is the strongest evidentiary basis available. No other tested theory accounts for these findings.

**#2 (internal coherence): Positional enrichments, self-succession, and sequential grammar form a mutually consistent grammar system.**

-am words are enriched at LL; d self-succession concentrates at MID→LL transitions; qok→ch enriched at mid-line transitions; y-onset words prefer LF alongside other simple-onset classes. This forms a coherent positional grammar rather than isolated statistical artifacts. Internal coherence is harder to produce by chance and is the strongest argument for intrinsic grammar rather than independent artifacts.

## 9. THE DUAL CONSTRAINT

**Voynichese exhibits two statistically robust structural properties that no single tested generation mechanism can produce simultaneously.**

**Property (1): Position-sensitive onset-class grammar.** -am LL enrichment (6.811×) and y-onset LF enrichment (3.506×) are present in VMS, absent from position-blind mechanisms (~1.0× across 1,900 simulations), and partially reproduced only by a target-calibrated position-sensitive cipher (5.756×). These patterns are Bonferroni-corrected (p < 10⁻⁹⁰), cross-validated, and template-controlled.

**Property (2): Sequential word-class memory.** Onset self-succession (1.465× mean across qok, ok, ch in both dialects) and qok→ch enrichment (1.2-1.3×, surviving position control) require word-class decisions to be correlated across adjacent words. No cipher mechanism tested produces this at VMS levels: position-blind ciphers underproduce (ss_mean ~0.86-0.96×); position-sensitive substitution ciphers produce no enrichment (ss_mean ~1.0×); Timm copy algorithms overproduce at viable parameter settings (ss_mean 1.5-2.4×) and cannot satisfy self-succession and adj_rate simultaneously (exp_176).

**The combination of both properties is the tightest empirical constraint on the hypothesis space**: any viable generation mechanism for VMS must be both **position-aware** AND **sequentially structured**.

- Position-sensitive substitution alone (exp_177): produces Property (1) when target-calibrated, but not Property (2) (ss_mean ≈ 1.0×).
- Self-citation alone (Timm, exp_171/176): produces a version of Property (2) but overproduces it, and produces no version of Property (1).
- Position-blind ciphers (Naibbe): produce neither Property (1) nor Property (2).
- **Position-blind hybrid (exp_179)**: position-sensitive cipher + copy buffer, copying allowed at all positions. 16 combinations (COPY_PROB ∈ {0.2-0.5} × MODIFY_PROB ∈ {0.3-0.6}, 50 sims each). **0/16 viable.** Fundamental incompatibility: copying at LL positions dilutes -am enrichment proportionally to COPY_PROB. At COPY_PROB values needed for ss_mean ≥ 1.2×, am_ll drops to ~3.36× (below threshold). Trade-off is linear and unsolvable by parameter tuning within this architecture.

- **Position-aware hybrid with onset-replacing modification (exp_180)**: Copying restricted to MID positions only; LF and LL always generated by position-sensitive cipher. 18 combinations (COPY_PROB ∈ {0.2-0.7} × MODIFY_PROB ∈ {0.3-0.5}, 50 sims each). **0/18 viable.** Position gating decouples am_ll from COPY_PROB (am_ll flat at 5.71-5.80× across all combinations, eliminating the exp_179 failure mode). However, the modification operation (replace copied word's onset with a random onset) created a new binding incompatibility: onset replacement destroys the very feature driving ss_mean, so any COPY_PROB high enough to produce ss_mean ≥ 1.2× also produces adj_rate > 0.015 (max ss_mean=1.252× at adj_rate=0.036 at cp=0.7). **The "structural failure" claim from exp_180 was subsequently partially retracted by exp_181** — the coupling is structural to onset-replacing modification, not to copy-buffer architectures in general.

- **Position-aware hybrid with onset-preserving modification (exp_181)**: Same position-gated architecture as exp_180, but modification replaces the suffix (onset preserved) rather than the onset. Extended grid: 36 combinations (COPY_PROB ∈ {0.2-0.7} × MODIFY_PROB ∈ {0.3-0.8}, 50 sims each). **4/36 viable** under the ≥5.0×/[1.2,1.7]/≤0.015 viability thresholds. Onset-preserving modification breaks the ss_mean/adj_rate coupling: modified copies contribute to ss_mean (onset unchanged) but not adj_rate (different word). MODIFY_PROB now controls adj_rate without affecting ss_mean — at cp=0.7, ss_mean is 1.572-1.581 across all six MODIFY_PROB values (0.6% spread) while adj_rate drops from 0.039 to 0.019. Viable combinations: (cp=0.5, mp=0.6-0.8) and (cp=0.6, mp=0.8). However, **no viable combination reaches VMS values** (am_ll ~5.76 vs 6.811×; ss_mean 1.26-1.39 vs 1.465×; adj_rate 0.011-0.015 vs 0.009). Under VMS-level adj_rate (≤ 0.009), 0/36 remain viable.

**The structured-grammar hypothesis is consistent with both properties by construction**: a grammar specifies which word classes occupy which positions (Property 1) and which classes prefer to follow each other (Property 2). After exp_181, the position-aware hybrid with onset-preserving modification enters the "not ruled out" zone under lenient viability thresholds — it is no longer categorically falsified. However, it does not reproduce VMS values, and the architecture's position-aware design is partly target-motivated (designed knowing the dual constraint). The remaining alternatives are: (a) intrinsic structured grammar — the simplest uneliminated positive mechanism; (b) a position-aware hybrid with onset-preserving modification and parameters not yet tested (e.g., COPY_PROB=0.7 with MODIFY_PROB > 0.8, which may approach the adj_rate threshold while preserving ss_mean ≈ 1.58). Whether (b) can simultaneously reproduce VMS values on all four core metrics (including the adj_rate gap at VMS level) is an open question.

## 10. WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE STRUCTURED-GRAMMAR VERDICT

### Weakening finding 1 (most important): A position-sensitive cipher fully reproducing all four core metrics simultaneously

A cipher with separate LF/MID/LL tables, applied to a language with appropriate clause-boundary morphology, that simultaneously produces:
1. -am LL enrichment ≥ 6× (not just ≥ 3× — magnitude matters)
2. y-onset LF enrichment ≥ 3×
3. Onset self-succession 1.2-1.7× (not the copy-mechanism level of 1.5-2.4×)
4. qok→ch enrichment 1.2-1.3× at mid-line positions

Why this has not yet been achieved: The exp_177 calibration reaches 5.756× at -am LL (close) but 2.395× at y-onset LF (below VMS 3.506×), and completely fails on self-succession (0.999× vs VMS 1.465×). A mechanism that produces self-succession via substitution alone has not been designed. The four constraints appear jointly unsatisfiable by substitution without a copy component.

### Weakening finding 2: Quire-level LOSO collapse

If -am LL or y-onset LF fails to replicate on ≥4 of ~8 B-dialect quires in leave-one-quire-out cross-validation, the grammar might be attributed to a single scribe's idiolect rather than a system-wide property. The section-level LOSO (exp_172) already survived; quire-level is the stricter test.

### What would NOT change the verdict

- Finding that some peripheral findings fail correction or cross-validation: The verdict rests on the four core metrics only.
- Finding a generative model that reproduces all findings using mechanisms equivalent to grammar rules: If the model requires position-sensitive encoding that is functionally equivalent to grammar, it relabels the conclusion rather than contradicting it.
- Identifying a natural language source: Would reframe the grammar's origin, not deny its existence.
