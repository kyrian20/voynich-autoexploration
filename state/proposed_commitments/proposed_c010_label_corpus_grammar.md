# Proposed Commitment C010: Label Text Constitutes a Structurally Distinct Register

**Proposed date:** 2026-04-12
**Supporting experiment:** exp_580
**Proposing agent:** Cryptanalyst loop

---

## Proposed Claim

**C010**: VMS label text (1,087 label lines, 1,442 tokens, from all label sections in EVA ZL3b) constitutes a structurally distinct register from body text. The differences are large and consistent across label section types:

**Onset profile**: ok (17.2%), ot (16.3%), and o (10.9%) classes together comprise 44.4% of label tokens, vs 14.6% in body text (ratios: ok=2.85×, ot=2.97×, o=3.55× elevated). The q-bench classes (qok+qot+qo+cth) total 1.3% of labels vs 13.5% of body (qok=0.13×, qot=0.13×, qo=0.09×, cth=0.20×: near-absent). ch (0.47×), sh (0.28×), l (0.19×), p (0.13×) are substantially depleted.

**Suffix profile**: The major body-text suffix paradigms are depleted in labels: edy (0.26×), aiin (0.35×), ey (0.60×), ain (0.33×). Shorter suffixes are elevated: dy (1.88×), bare-y (1.83×), am (2.19×), al (1.49×).

**Dialect marker attenuation**: The B-dialect edy marker is near-absent even in B-folio labels (B-folio labels: edy=0.168 vs B-folio body: 0.241). A-folio labels: edy=0.109 vs A-folio body: 0.069. The standard body-text A/B dialect classifier does not apply to label text.

**Multi-word label structure** (N=222, directional only): a-onset words are the dominant label-terminal class (LL: E=3.55×). The bigram [ok/ot/o/y/d]-onset → a-onset is 3–6× more common in labels than body. ot-onset leads at LF (E=1.41×). Body grammar findings hold weakly: -am LL (1.31× vs body 1.76×), y-onset LF (1.73× vs body 3.03×), self-succession (1.27× vs body 1.47×).

**Vocabulary**: 47% of label types appear in body vocabulary; 53% (547 types) are label-exclusive.

---

## Justification for Commitment

1. **Large and consistent differences**: The onset and suffix profile differences are large (factors of 3–10×) and consistent across all 11 label section types characterized. These are not sampling artifacts from low N.

2. **Relevant to the Botanist phase**: The Botanist phase will primarily encounter folio labels (plant labels, star labels) as anchor candidates. A committed claim about label structure prevents the Botanist from incorrectly applying body-text grammar priors to label tokens.

3. **Well-characterized data**: The ZL3b label locus codes (@L*, @Ri, @Ro) are explicit label identifiers in the transcription — not inferred from context.

4. **Directional consistency**: Multi-word label findings (directional only due to N=222) are consistent with the onset and suffix profiles. No contradictions between pooled and multi-word analyses.

---

## Scope Limitations

- Multi-word label positional grammar findings (LF/LL/bigrams) are directional only — N=222 multi-word labels, 355 bigrams; no significance testing is reliable.
- Label-exclusive vocabulary (547 types) has not been cross-referenced with comparison corpora.
- Herbal labels (y/d/a onset profile) and zodiac/star/cosmo labels (ot/ok/o profile) may constitute sub-registers within label text; per-section analysis is preliminary.
- Single-token labels (79.6% of all labels) contribute to onset/suffix profiles but not to positional grammar analysis.

---

## Contradiction Condition

A re-analysis of ZL3b label text using a different locus code classification showing that label text onset/suffix profiles match body text within ±30% on the primary classes (ok, ot, qok, edy) would contradict this claim. A demonstration that the @L* locus codes include substantial body-text runs (not just isolated labels) would require re-analysis.
